#2300. Tuttosport: Back for revenge – why more than three points are at stake for Amorim vs. Bournemouth

📅 26 Oct 2026 | other | Source

Ruben Amorim is gearing up to return to England with AC Milan to face Bournemouth on Matchday 3 of the Europa League.

As Tuttosport (via MilanPress) write this morning, it’s a trip that holds extra significance for Amorim, for a few different reasons. Above all though it is a chance for his side to win a third straight game, and also to move onto six points in their European campaign.

The game goes beyond the league table and European aspirations for the coach. Returning to England also means coming to terms with an experience that – between dashed expectations and internal tensions – ended very differently than he had imagined.

In November 2024, Manchester United paid Sporting CP’s €11m release clause to appoint him as manager and succeed Erik ten Hag. After Ruud van Nistelrooy’s four-match interim spell, Amorim arrived at Old Trafford with high expectations.

In England, he was hailed as a potential ‘New Special One’, but his adventure quickly turned into a disappointment. His relationship with the media, some of the fans and especially with the United management never really took off.

His first season ended with a 15th-placed finish in the Premier League and a defeat in the Europa League final against Tottenham, although it must be remembered that he came in midway through.

The dismissal came midway through the following season, on 4 January 2026, after a draw against Leeds. The decision was driven not only by the results, but also by tensions with the club’s management and some particularly outspoken comments made by the manager.

“I notice you’re getting selective information about everything. I came here to be the manager of Manchester United, not just the coach,” he said.

“I know my name isn’t Tuchel, Conte, or Mourinho, but I am the manager of Manchester United and will be for another 18 months, or whenever the board decides to change things. I want to make this clear. If someone can’t handle the pressure of criticism, then it’s better to change the club.”

These words further ignited the rift with those above him, leading to the eventual separation. The overall record at Old Trafford was not positive: 63 matches, 24 wins, 18 draws, and 21 defeats , with an average of 1.43 points per game.

One of the main points of contention at Manchester United was the tactical approach. Amorim continued to rely on his 3-4-2-1 formation, a system he had also used with conviction during his time at Sporting, despite pressure from the management to change the team’s structure.

The Portuguese coach also clashed with several players, while discussions with management figures like Jason Wilcox and Omar Berrada had fuelled tensions. His stance remained rigid, summed up in a phrase that has become iconic: “No one will ever be able to convince me [to abandon his system], not even if the Pope asked me to.”

Amorim seems to have learned a lesson from it. In his last month at the helm of Milan, the Portuguese has shown greater flexibility, seeking tactical solutions better suited to his players’ characteristics and adapting the team to the needs that emerged on the pitch.

It’s a significant change from the English experience: less ideological rigidity and a greater willingness to find a balance between one’s own beliefs and the quality of the squad.

The away match against Bournemouth therefore represents an opportunity to measure Milan’s progress, but also a chance for Amorim to return to England with a different feeling than he left in Manchester.

Then there’s a personal connection to the Rossoneri colours. Having grown up following football and the great Milan sides, the former Sporting CP coach sees Milanello as a new opportunity to relaunch his career after a difficult spell in the Premier League.

There’s still a long way to go, but the signs of recent weeks suggest a coach who’s more willing to question himself and find alternative solutions. Now, the European challenge offers the opportunity to see how much the team has grown and how much Amorim has managed to turn over a new leaf.

Milan are seeking a third consecutive victory, while their manager wants to put the bitter memories of Manchester United behind them. Beating Bournemouth for the first time would help, given he has drawn twice and lost once against them.

#2294. Ian Rush imparts 5-word advice to ‘fantastic’ Bradley Barcola after £106m Liverpool move

📅 26 Oct 2026 | other | Source

Ian Rush has shared some advice from his playing career on how Bradley Barcola can thrive at Liverpool.

The French winger became the Reds’ second-most expensive player of all time when he joined from Paris Saint-Germain at the end of August for an initial £106m, potentially rising to £123m if all add-ons are triggered (The Athletic).

The 24-year-old had to wait until his seventh appearance for LFC to get off the mark, but then came a hat-trick in the Champions League win over LASK, followed by a horrific one-on-one miss against Brentford in his next match.

Barcola has had an up-and-down start to his Liverpool career, but the legendary Rush has advised him to not become dispirited even if he’s enduring a frustrating game.

Speaking to talkSPORT about the Reds’ number 29, the Welshman said: “We all know he’s a fantastic player. I think the most important thing for me when I was playing was that someone taught me, ‘never be scared to miss’.

“I could miss three or four [chances], but then the fifth one would go in and everyone forgets about the three or four that you missed. He’s just got to be in the right place at the right time and never be scared to miss.”

Duncan Ferguson has been appointed manager of Irish Premiership side Glentoran.

Arsenal's pursuit of back-to-back Premier League titles for the first time ever continues on Saturday, when Everton provide the Gunners' eighth test of the 2026-27 season.

Mikel Arteta's men hold a six-place and eight-point lead over the Toffees in the Premier League table, but David Moyes's charges are yet to lose on the road this season.

Here, Sports Mole runs through everything you need to know about how to tune into the contest.

Arsenal vs. Everton kicks off at 3pm UK time for the UK audience.

The contest is just one of two Premier League games taking place at that time on Saturday, October 24, alongside Coventry City vs. Fulham.

Arsenal are welcoming Everton to their Emirates Stadium home, where the Gunners have won all four games in all tournaments this term, scoring at least twice every time.

The hosts have also prevailed in four and drawn one of their last five home games against Everton, last suffering defeat in April 2021 courtesy of a Bernd Leno own goal.

As Arsenal vs. Everton is a 3pm kickoff, the game is not available to watch on TV in the UK.

This is due to the 3pm blackout rule, prohibiting those kickoffs from being televised in an attempt to encourage fans to attend lower-league games in person.

Arsenal vs. Everton also cannot be legally streamed online in the UK as a result of the 3pm blackout rule.

However, fans can listen to live commentary on BBC Radio 5 Live, as well as the official Arsenal app for members.

Highlights of the game will be available to view on the Sky Sports Premier League, Arsenal and Everton YouTube channels, as well as the Sky Sports website and app.

Alternatively, fans can tune into Match of the Day, which begins at 10.30pm on BBC One on Saturday night.

First place in the Premier League standings will be at stake for Arsenal, if current leaders Manchester City suffer defeat to Aston Villa in the day's early kickoff.

The Gunners trail the Citizens by three points and are six worse off in the goal difference column, but due to Man City's superior goals scored total (19 to 11), Enzo Maresca's men losing 3-0 and Arsenal winning 3-0 would not be enough to take the latter top.

If Arsenal are to end the weekend in first place, they would need to get the better of Everton after Man City lose to Aston Villa, and also hope for a seven-goal swing in their favour.

Meanwhile, Everton could end the weekend as high as fourth with a shock win if other results go their way, as their 10 points puts them within touching distance of Leeds United and Brentford (only ahead on goal difference), Liverpool (12 points) and Chelsea (13 points).

Therefore, if the Toffees win, Chelsea lose and all of Liverpool, Brentford and Leeds fail to triumph, Moyes's men will crack the Champions League spots, although that is a big if.

#2283. Why Brighton are scoring SO many goals

📅 26 Oct 2026 | other | Source

Published: 17:00 BST, 19 October 2026 | Updated: 18:39 BST, 19 October 2026

On par with Real Madrid and behind only Barcelona. Brighton, at last, have found their rightful place in the world.

Fabian Hurzeler’s side, after yet another rout against Crystal Palace, have now racked up more goals across Europe’s top five leagues than anyone bar the Spanish giants. The Seagulls have more than Bayern Munich, too. Who needs Harry Kane when you have Charalampos Kostoulas?

In fact, only three Premier League sides have ever scored more in their opening seven games of a campaign than Brighton’s 23 (27 by Pep Guardiola’s Man City in 2019-20, 24 by Manchester United in 2011-12 and 24 by Arsenal in 2009-10). None of these teams, however, went on to win the title. Can Brighton buck the trend?

It helps, of course, when you get to play sides as incapable at defending as Crystal Palace, who have already conceded 17 goals from their opening seven games with Pierre Sage clinging to his job at Selhurst Park.

When you stick three past Arsenal, three past Chelsea and four past Aston Villa, though, it suggests you’re doing something right.

It also helps when nearly everything you touch turns to goals. For those who bow to the emotionless number crunching of expected goals (xG), Brighton have already scored eight more goals than they should have done based on the quality of chances they have created. In just seven games, that’s more than any side over-performed across the entirety of last season.

Only three Premier League sides have ever scored more in their opening seven games of a campaign than Brighton’s 23

At some point, you imagine, that will prove unsustainable. At some point, Pascal Gross, Maxim De Cuyper, Chema Andres, Jack Hinshelwood and Jaouen Hadjam will stop scoring with every shot on target they muster. Lewis Dunk won't always score from 30 yards, nor Diego Gomez from 50.

And yet none of that should take away from the fact that, despite overperforming it, Brighton are still behind only Manchester City for the xG they have created. Only Manchester United have taken more shots.

No team has created as many ‘big chances’ – defined by data giants Opta as one a player should be reasonably expected to score. They are still one of the most attacking, creative and potent teams in the Premier League.

So, how has Hurzeler managed it? This is a coach, after all, who was under pressure from a section of Brighton's fanbase at the start of the year.

Firstly, as we saw in their dismantling of Arsenal before the international break, their press is relentless. It’s been getting better and better each season under Hurzeler but now it’s in a league of its own. The Seagulls have won possession in the final third 51 times, 14 more than any other side and more than twice as many as 12 Premier League clubs including Arsenal, Liverpool and Chelsea.

They allow opponents the fewest passes before attempting to win it back, a stat known as PPDA (passes per defensive action) which is often used as a barometer for an intense press.

That sees Brighton force opponents into dangerous mistakes. No team has cajoled the opposition into more errors leading to a shot than the 12 by the Seagulls. Their attacks start closer to goal than any other team too, so when they do win it back they are likely to punish you.

And yet for a team that is so deadly out of possession, they are not – like so many modern sides in the anti-Guardiola era – looking to have less of the ball. Brighton average 60 per cent possession, behind only Arsenal and City, and are content to build up slowly from the back, baiting opposition presses, and then playing quickly through the lines at speed.

Centre back Lewis Dunk thumped in a 30-yard strike in the 5-0 win over Coventry last month

Brighton are relentless in their pressing and then immediately turn possession into a dangerous attack

That is why, despite not being a traditional counter-attacking team – one that soaks up pressure and spring the attack – they still rank in the top three so far for fast breaks, shots from fast breaks and goals from fast breaks this term.

Brighton have such great variety in their play. Only City and Liverpool have scored more goals coming at the end of sequences of 10 or more passes, while the Seagulls also have the most goals from both outside the box and from corners. They kill you on the counter, with passing moves and from set-pieces. And anyone can get you. Brighton’s 11 different goalscorers is the highest tally in the division.

They swarm you without the ball but then, importantly, with it. As they break forward they commit so many runners from deep to overload the box, all rotating their positions to make them even harder to track. Take their third goal against Palace.

Kostoulas breaks into the area but blue shirts flood forward behind him from midfield in the form of Gomez, Yasin Ayari, De Cuyper and Gross (image below). Ayari, the No10, is the furthest back. The ball is squared, Gross dummies it and Ayari sweeps in to slot the ball home. After just 10 minutes, it's 3-0 and the contest is over.

Even if Kostoulas’s pass had been intercepted, Brighton have players in prime position to pounce to win it back as usual.

And Brighton have Gross, still pulling the strings at the age of 35, showing he remains one of the most influential and creative players in the Premier League. He has the freedom to roam from central midfield, taking up unorthodox positions, with the pinpoint passing accuracy to open up defences. Only two other players in the league have run more miles than the German.

Only Florian Wirtz has created more chances than Gross this season, though not one of Wirtz’s was classified as a ‘big chance’. Six of Gross’s 19 were. He is making things happen, from both open play and set-pieces.
