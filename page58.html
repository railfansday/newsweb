#2518. West Ham And Moyes: Some Things Are Better Left In The Past

📅 26 Oct 2026 | other | Source

As the old saying goes, absence makes the heart grow fonder — and that is certainly the case with me and former West Ham manager David Moyes. The Scot has been speculatively linked with another return to the London Stadium, which inevitably brings back some wonderful memories of his time in charge.

The speculation follows a slowdown in talks over extending his Everton contract. Moyes himself confirmed to Sky Sports that negotiations have entered something of a lull, although there is currently no credible suggestion that West Ham have approached him or are considering replacing Nuno Espírito Santo.

Nevertheless, the rumour raises an interesting question. Moyes is West Ham’s most successful manager in recent memory, having made it enjoyable to support the club again with three consecutive seasons of European football and, of course, that unforgettable Europa Conference League triumph in Prague. But would bringing him back really be a good idea?

Former West Ham boss David Moyes talks to Warburton

If we remove the rose-tinted glasses, it was clear that his time at West Ham had reached its natural conclusion.

His strange relationship with David Sullivan, disagreements with former technical director Tim Steidten and the departures of trusted coaches Alan Irvine, Stuart Pearce, Paul Nevin and Mark Warburton gradually left Moyes looking like a lonely figure. By the end, he appeared a shadow of the manager who had transformed the club.

Moyes has subsequently looked rejuvenated at Everton, but I have always believed that the importance of Irvine to his success has been underestimated. Draw a line through Moyes’ results with his trusted lieutenant beside him and compare them with those achieved without Irvine, and there is a noticeable difference.

Crucially, Irvine is understood to have no appetite to live and work in London again. A third spell for Moyes at West Ham would therefore be unlikely to recreate the same successful formula.

There is also the question of what Moyes would offer that West Ham do not already have.

He is an experienced, pragmatic manager who prioritises organisation and results. That description could just as easily be applied to Nuno. If the club eventually decides to move on from its current head coach, appointing another manager with a broadly similar approach would make little sense.

Moyes deserves enormous respect for everything he achieved at West Ham. His place in the club’s history is secure and should be celebrated.

But some relationships are best remembered rather than rekindled. West Ham’s next managerial appointment must form part of a coherent, modern footballing structure — not another attempt to look backwards.

Arsenal job cuts: Arsenal have told staff that around 60 jobs are at risk as the club looks to reduce costs. This comes just months after winning their first Premier League title in 22 years.

It’s a blow for staff that follows a week after Arteta inked his latest contract. Reportedly worth around £20m per annum. Keeping the Spaniard coaching at the Emirates Stadium until 2030.

A general review of the Gunners’ operations produced the proposed cuts. Arsenal has employed management consultants from Boston Consulting Group (BCG) to identify areas where savings can be made and improvements carried out.

Some members of staff raised concerns over possible job losses back in September when reports surfaced claiming Arsenal had employed the consultancy to undertake a review of their business.

This includes focusing on the club’s wider workforce without involving the company’s operation relating to their football activities. This will therefore include coaching and performance staff who assist the men’s and women’s first teams and academy.

Arsenal has also stated that it will protect community operations, including the Arsenal Foundation and security department, from the proposed changes. The list of departments affected and the figure for total redundancies, however, remain subject to ongoing consultation. The club hasn’t publicly confirmed the outcome of the consultation process yet.

That’s despite an extremely successful spell on the pitch, Arsenal also made record revenue during the 2024-25 financial year. Reporting £691 million worth of revenue for the year in total – as well as increased commercial income.

However, this came at a cost, with the Gunners’ accounts showing they suffered a loss of £1.4 million for the 2024-25 financial year. That’s up from £147.9 million in 2023-24. Operating costs for the same time frame also saw a rise to £200.8 million from 2023-24 figures. Reports reveal that Arsenal have seen losses over seven consecutive financial years.

But as with much else at the club, they’re looking for ways to maximise efficiencies but remain competitive against the backdrop of the Premier League’s financial regulations.

That’s where this workforce review comes into play.

And it will no doubt raise some interesting questions amongst staff who have seen a lot of money thrown at them over the last year – both their manager and playing squad included.

Last week, Arteta’s contract extension was revealed. This shows just how serious the Gunners are about continuing their run of form on the pitch. Under the Spaniard’s leadership, the Gunners secured the 2025-26 Premier League title, ending a 22-year-long wait for the championship.

They also got to the Champions League final in Budapest this spring. Although they lost out to Paris Saint-Germain (PSG) on penalties following a 1-1 draw. These successes have helped consolidate the Gunners’ standing both at home and abroad. However, the latest news shows the financial ramifications of trying to keep up their current streak.

In another context, Arsenal had already cut some of its staff significantly during the coronavirus (Covid-19) pandemic. Around 55 employees lost their jobs.

Now these proposals would mean that roughly 60 posts will be affected. After the consultation stage, this isn’t set in stone. More on this in due course as we learn more about the proposed changes throughout the process.

#2514. Bolton Wanderers forward has found his feet in the Championship and surprised the doubters

📅 26 Oct 2026 | other | Source

Bolton Wanderers have enjoyed a promising return to the Championship, and Sam Dalby has played an important role in their efforts to establish themselves in the second tier.

Steven Schumacher’s side secured promotion through last season’s League One play-offs, but questions remained over whether their forward line could produce against stronger opposition. Dalby has answered those doubts impressively.

The 26-year-old arrived from Wrexham in 2025 after a productive loan spell at Dundee United, where he scored 15 goals in 35 Scottish Premiership appearances. He then helped Bolton earn promotion, including a spectacular overhead kick in the 4-1 play-off final victory over Stockport County at Wembley.

A step up from League One can expose strikers who have relied on physical advantages at a lower level. Dalby, however, has demonstrated that his imposing 1.91m frame and ability to occupy defenders can translate to Championship football.

He has scored four goals in his first 11 league appearances, becoming Bolton’s leading scorer and finding the net against Preston North End, Sheffield United, Norwich City and Wolves.

His underlying numbers make that return particularly encouraging. According to FotMob, Dalby has registered 4.62 expected goals from 18 shots, all of which have come inside the penalty area.

Eight efforts have been on target, while his 3.04 expected goals on target suggests his finishing has generally placed his shots in less threatening positions than the original chances might indicate. Nevertheless, four goals represent a solid return for a striker still adapting to the division.

Dalby’s greatest strength is arguably his ability to compete physically. He ranks around the 92.2nd percentile among forwards for aerial duels won, succeeding in approximately 49% of his aerial contests.

That gives Schumacher a reliable focal point, whether Bolton need to play directly, relieve pressure or create opportunities from crosses and set-pieces. There are limitations to his game. His dribbling success remains low, and he has yet to register an assist this season.

However, Bolton do not necessarily need him to be an elite creator or a forward who constantly beats defenders one-on-one. His ability to occupy centre-backs, bring teammates into play and consistently find dangerous positions already makes him valuable.

Dalby is not yet among the Championship’s most prolific strikers, but his early performances suggest he belongs at this level. Having progressed from the Scottish Premiership to League One and now the Championship, he is continuing to improve. Bolton will hope this is only the beginning of an impressive second-tier career.

By Seye Omidiora | 22 Oct 2026 23:54, Last updated: 23 Oct 2026 00:16

Fresh from beating Go Ahead Eagles 4-1 in the IJsselderby, PEC Zwolle chase consecutive Eredivisie victories on Saturday when they face FC Utrecht at Stadion Galgenwaard.

The victory over their provincial rivals halted a five-match winless sequence that had led to Henry van der Vegt's dismissal, and the interim pairing of Dennis van der Ree and Maikel van der Werff could remain in situ for gameweek 10.

Van der Vegt was probably on borrowed time after his team suffered a 7-0 home defeat against Feyenoord in mid-September, before that result was followed by a 3-0 reverse away at Groningen on the next matchday.

The return from the international break brought little joy for the Bluefingers, who slipped to another defeat, this time against promoted Cambuur in front of their supporters, marking a third consecutive loss without scoring.

Conceding 11 goals without reply across three games, combined with the team collecting just four of the previous 15 available points, proved the final straw, and Van der Vegt was given the heave-ho in Zwolle.

Van der Ree and Van der Werff were placed in charge for the derby last time out, with the former assuming the senior role, and the pair steered the club to their first victory in nearly two months and only their second league win of the season.

As such, the victory lifted Blauwvingers out of the bottom three and into 15th place in the Eredivisie table, leaving them only four points and as many positions behind this weekend's hosts.

Anthony Correia's men are also experiencing a significant upturn in results following a slow start after the former Telstar boss replaced the retired Ron Jans in the summer.

Correia failed to mastermind a victory in his opening five matches in charge of the club, but he has now collected nine points from Utreg's last four fixtures.

The only defeat during that improved run was a 5-0 beatdown away at the current Eredivisie leaders on matchday seven, but they have since responded by securing consecutive wins over Willem II and FC Twente.

If the 3-1 success over the promoted side was viewed as expected, beating Twente in Enschede, where the Tukkers had won four consecutive Eredivisie matches this term and five stretching back to last season, was doubly impressive.

Now, last season's sixth-placed side aim to claim a third successive league win under their new manager, which would be their first such sequence since the final three matchdays of the 2025-26 regular season and further validate their resurgence.

A home victory may have seemed a near certainty under the previous regime in Zwolle; however, the visiting side now have an opportunity to spring a surprise on the Cathedral Citizens.

Vasilis Barkas missed last week's victory at Twente, with Sergio Padt deputising between the sticks for Utreg, and the veteran goalkeeper may retain his place if the No. 1 is not cleared to return.

Noah Ohio (knee), Alonzo Engwanda (hamstring), Kevin Paredes (knee), Miliano Jonathans (ACL), Artem Stepanov (ankle) and Victor Jensen (knee) are all missing for Correia, who has found ways to secure consecutive wins without five-goal Stepanov.

Angel Alarcon and Yoann Cathline have stepped up during those victories, with Alarcon scoring three goals and Cathline contributing two, and both men should start on Saturday barring late injuries.

Van der Ree opted to drop Koen Kostons for Sydney van Hooijdonk, with the towering striker scoring PEC's first goal and Kostons netting their fourth in the IJsselderby success, giving the interim boss a decision to make this weekend.

Thijs Oosting is expected to retain his place, particularly after his brace in the 4-1 victory opened his account for the season.

Missing out, though, are Younes Namli (hamstring) and Filip Krastev (Achilles) due to injury.

Padt; Horemans, Didden, Panagiotou; Vesterlund, Van Overeem, Agougil, Zagre; Cathline, De Wit, Alarcon

PEC responded impressively to Van der Vegt's departure by hitting Go Ahead Eagles for four, but one encouraging display does little to erase their previous defensive collapses.

Utrecht have found their rhythm under Correia and should underline their resurgence by recording another convincing victory at Stadion Galgenwaard.

Füllkrug has since been performing brilliantly on loan at Werder Bremen and appears to have repaired his career in Germany, at least.

However, when we put it to the agent that West Ham were now willing to accept a minimal fee to make his departure permanent, we were told that no discussions had taken place. Furthermore, the current loan arrangement is still costing the Hammers a considerable amount in wages.

“Listen, there is no agreement between West Ham and the German club, and there’s no agreement between West Ham and Füllkrug. At the moment, West Ham are still paying the majority of his wages. It’s not a good deal.

“So, as things stand, everyone’s hoping for the best, that he can have a good season and get a move away.”

The information casts doubt on suggestions that West Ham have already decided to accept a minimal fee for the striker, although it does not rule out such an arrangement being negotiated in the future.

As we revealed in August, Füllkrug accepted a substantial wage reduction to secure his move to Bremen. However, the club’s continued contribution towards his salary means that the financial problem has not disappeared.

Fullkrug would not be welcome back at West Ham

The prospect of terminating his contract remains a possibility rather than a certainty, while the suggestion of a £10 million offer from Saudi Arabia is purely hypothetical at this stage.

Few days in Sheffield Wednesday’s recent history come close to 29 May 2023.

The Owls headed to Wembley for the League One play-off final against South Yorkshire neighbours Barnsley, with promotion back to the Championship on the line. More than 72,000 supporters packed into the national stadium, and around 44,000 of them were Sheffield Wednesday fans.

That the Owls were there at all felt like a small miracle. Darren Moore’s side had been thrashed 4-0 by Peterborough United in the first leg of their semi-final, and few gave them any chance of turning it around. Instead, Hillsborough witnessed one of the greatest comebacks in play-off history. Wednesday won 5-1 on the night after extra time to level the tie and then held their nerve to win the shootout 5-3.

The final was a far tighter affair. Barnsley had beaten Wednesday home and away during the regular season, and a cagey first half finished goalless. Extra time brought more frustration, with a Sheffield Wednesday goal ruled out for offside, and a penalty shootout looked certain. Then, with the final seconds ticking away, a diving header in the 123rd minute sent the blue half of Wembley into delirium and the Owls back to the Championship.

Moore kept faith with the same starting XI that had pulled off the “Miracle of Hillsborough” days earlier, and those eleven names have been part of Owls folklore ever since.

#2506. One lesson Senne Lammens learned after Tottenham’s late equaliser

Senne Lammens believes Manchester United must learn to finish games off after their late collapse against Tottenham Hotspur.

The result left the goalkeeper frustrated at Old Trafford despite leading the game and with a player more on the field.

Manchester United looked set for three points after Bryan Mbeumo put them ahead in the second half, but Dominic Solanke’s stoppage-time equaliser denied Michael Carrick’s side a much-needed victory.

Speaking to MUTV, Senne Lammens admitted the team had struggled early on but had done enough after the break to put themselves in a winning position.

“Football can be a bit cruel at times,” the Belgian goalkeeper said. “There are a lot of ups and downs, especially in a game like that.”

Senne Lammens felt United failed to take advantage of Tottenham’s red card, allowing the visitors too much space to find a late equaliser.

“We’ve won a lot of games against 10 men [in the past], but you also know there can be a tricky situation when you’re playing against 10 men, because they have nothing to lose and they know that.”

He added that United should have scored again to put the result beyond doubt or, at the very least, prevented Tottenham from creating further chances.

“I think that’s what we did wrong. We gave them a little bit too much breathing room to create those chances, and then one ball has to go right for them, one bounce has to go their way, and they can score like that.”

The goalkeeper also pointed to similarities with United’s earlier disappointment against Everton, where points slipped away late in the match.

“You feel like those games just slip away from you, and there are one or two situations where you don’t really have control,” he said.

“I think there’s a lot to learn from today. It wasn’t all negative, not at all, but especially the way it ended left a very bad feeling.”

“I think the easy answer is that we have to be able to kill the game faster and not put ourselves in that position to give them a chance.”

Despite the frustration, Lammens said there were positives to take from the performance. However, the late goal had left a bitter feeling, and United now need to turn those lessons into better results.

The Norwegian U21 international has certainly captured the eye since joining from Viking FK.

The biggest difference between the two players is their direct goal contribution. Austbø is producing four times the number of goals and assists. Indeed, the young Norwegian’s attacking threat is underlined further by his shooting numbers. Shots on target per 90: Austbø 2.09 and Solomon 0.53.

This is arguably one of the most significant statistics in the comparison. Austbø is regularly testing goalkeepers and putting himself in dangerous scoring positions. Solomon, despite his technical quality, has struggled to generate the same level of threat.

Another statistic favouring Austbø is touches in the opposition box. Austbø: 7.12 v Solomon: 3.72.

The numbers suggest Austbø is constantly arriving in areas where matches are decided. Whether making runs beyond defenders or drifting into pockets of space, he is getting involved in key attacking situations far more frequently.

Austbø also leads in line-breaking passes. Austbø: 3.35 and Solomon: 1.17. For a wide player, this is an impressive return. It suggests Austbø is not simply a finisher but is helping move attacks forward and unlock defensive structures.

Austbø also edges the crossing numbers, 1.26 to Solomon’s 0.53.

However, the comparison is not entirely one-sided.

Solomon leads in chances created (1.91 vs 1.26) and completed take-ons (2.44 vs 1.67).

These metrics highlight the qualities that made Solomon such an exciting player in the first place. He remains the superior dribbler and is more likely to create opportunities through individual skill and creativity. His ability to beat defenders one-on-one remains a valuable asset, particularly against low defensive blocks where moments of invention are often required.

These figures suggest Austbø offers slightly more balance between chance creation and end product, while Solomon’s contributions lean more heavily towards carrying the ball and creating openings.

Based on the figures alone, Nuno would have a strong case for giving Austbø an opportunity from the start. Yet, it’s a nice issue to have as West Ham continue their quest to gain promotion.
