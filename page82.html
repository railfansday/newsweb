#2039. Erik ten Hag accuses Man United chiefs of breaking 'promises' that they would not sell Scott McTominay, made in infamous Ibiza contract summit, as he picks apart their transfer business

📅 26 Oct 2026 | other | Source

By BEN WILLCOCKS, ASSISTANT SPORTS EDITOR

Published: 09:24 BST, 20 October 2026 | Updated: 19:39 BST, 20 October 2026

Erik ten Hag has accused his former Manchester United bosses of breaking promises during his ill-fated spell at Old Trafford, claiming that they sold Scott McTominay against his wishes.

The Dutchman, 56, was sacked by Manchester United in October 2024 after a string of poor results, just months after leading the club to FA Cup glory and signing a one-year contract extension until 2026.

Ten Hag's future had been in doubt after he led United to a disappointing eighth-placed finish in the Premier League, but the club ultimately chose to stick by him after meeting for crunch talks in Ibiza that summer.

According to Ten Hag, United's chiefs made promises and failed to keep them, particularly in the transfer market.

He told The Athletic: 'I wouldn't go on with only one year left. So I said, "I need an extra year and we have to do it together".

'They promised me loyalty, they promised me backing, they promised communication, that we get aligned. We spoke about the issues. But the promises, they didn't keep.'

Erik ten Hag has accused his former Manchester United bosses of breaking promises

The Dutchman claims Man United's chiefs sold Scott McTominay to Napoli against his wishes

Keeping McTominay at Old Trafford was one of those promises, according to Ten Hag.

The Scot was sold to Napoli for £25million in the summer of 2024 and has since gone on to become one of the top midfielders in Italy, becoming a fan favourite at the Serie A club after steering them to the title in his first season.

He even earned a Ballon d'Or nomination and was compared to Argentina icon Diego Maradona by adoring Napoli fans.

On McTominay, Ten Hag said: 'That's why I got disappointed. In Ibiza, we agreed. I said, "No selling Scott McTominay". We would go aligned in the transfer window.

'I make an argument, a reason: we don't have scoring abilities. Scott brings scoring abilities and great mentality. He has United in all his veins. So when they bring up the subject, "No". And in the end they sold him. I got, of course, very embarrassed and disappointed.'

McTominay's transfer to Napoli was used by United's chiefs to raise funds for £42m midfielder Manuel Ugarte, who has struggled to impress since his arrival.

The Uruguayan, who is currently out injured with an anterior cruciate ligament tear he suffered at the World Cup, was mostly used as a squad player under Ruben Amorim and Michael Carrick last season.

Ten Hag's accusation against his former bosses comes just days after Lisandro Martinez explosively hit out at Manchester United's summer transfer strategy.

The defender accused the club's hierarchy of lacking ambition and singled out director of football Jason Wilcox for failing to build a competitive squad after Carrick guided the team to the Champions League as interim manager last season.

McTominay has flourished in Naples and played a key role in the club's 2024-25 title triumph

Asked if Carrick was backed enough over the summer after United's 2-0 defeat by Atletico Madrid last Tuesday, Martinez said: 'I don't think so. He does his best. You can't keep changing, changing, changing the manager.

'What about other areas? Tonight it is easy to say the coach and easy to say the players, but also we have to see other areas.

'I expect more (investment) 100 per cent. I expect more from our technical director. I have to control myself a lot but sometimes is a reflection of the mirror of how is our club at the moment.

'We did a fantastic season last season and now we expect more from the top; more players to compete because many teams bring many players as well.'

#2031. Matthias Jaissle future: Newcastle sack stance ‘revealed’ amid growing concerns

📅 26 Oct 2026 | other | Source

By Oliver Thomas | 23 Oct 2026 17:04

Newcastle United’s stance over the future of head coach Matthias Jaissle has become clear, according to a report.

The 38-year-old was appointed as Eddie Howe’s successor in August, signing a four-year contract, and has since overseen a mixed start to life at St James’ Park.

Two wins, two draws and three defeats have been posted across Newcastle’s opening seven games in the Premier League, leaving the Magpies in 11th place in the table.

After making a promising start to the campaign, Newcastle have lost three of their last four top-flight matches and have conceded nine goals in the process.

Back-to-back 4-1 away defeats to Leeds United and Coventry City were soon followed by a narrow 1-0 home loss to Aston Villa last weekend in a game where “the lucky punch was missing” from Jaissle’s side.

Alarming defensive frailties, growing frustrations on the attacking front and overall team performances have raised concerns over Jaissle’s tactical acumen, with his Magpies team booed off following the loss to Villa.

However, Football Insider claims that Newcastle currently have no plans to sack Jaissle in the near future, as the German retains the board's backing.

Baring in mind that the Magpies expected a transitional period under a new manager working with a new-look squad following an eventful summer transfer window, the club’s board have yet to see anything to spark major concerns at this stage in the campaign.

While there is a feeling that Newcastle need to string together a run of positive results and performances if Jaissle is to silence his growing list of doubters, the manager still retains the club’s support.

Magpies chiefs are said to be prepared to cope with inconsistent results in the early stages of his tenure as they believe that Jaissle is the right man to lead the club forward.

Ahead of Newcastle’s Premier League trip to Crystal Palace on Sunday, Jaissle acknowledged the pressure that comes with the job at a club the size of the Magpies.

He told reporters: "I have always said it is a privilege to be the coach of Newcastle United. It is not a great pressure, I would use another word. It is a great responsibility.

"There is a setback at the moment and we need to overcome it. The disappointment of the fans is part of the game. They are passionate, especially our supporters who are great."

“I already had expectations it would be a tough one purely related to the fact we joined really late and we are on a brave journey,” Jaissle added.

“We are going to have setbacks. This is something you have to calculate. Of course, we hoped it would go smoothly and I am always on the optimistic side when I take a new challenge but we have to face the reality.

“We had a great start, everything was driving in the right direction and now we have a setback, but that makes you stronger, it makes the players stronger. It makes you stronger in life when you overcome setbacks.”

Newcastle will be looking to end a five-match winless run at Selhurst Park and avoid a third-straight Premier League defeat when they take on a Palace outfit who suffered a heavy 5-0 defeat at Brighton last weekend.

While there remains much work left to reach the knockout phase, Inter's strong domestic form has showed no sign of slowing: last year's double winners are undefeated in Serie A this season.

Last weekend, Chivu's men came from behind for the fourth time in seven league games, beating Bologna 3-1 to stay level at the summit with surprise challengers Lazio.

Before they meet AC Milan for the first Derby della Madonnina of the season, Inter must now face two teams stuck near the foot of Serie A.

Ahead of Wednesday's visit to Venezia, the Nerazzuri will reconvene with Fiorentina, to whom they have only lost twice in the last 18 league meetings.

Also beaten by Inter in the 2023 Coppa Italia final, Fiorentina have lost all of the last three clashes in Milan - and by an aggregate score of 9-1.

That surely bodes ill for this trip to the second city, as the Viola are apparently locked in a permanent crisis.

#2021. Forget Michael Carrick - here's who is really to blame for Manchester United meltdown

📅 26 Oct 2026 | other | Source

The back-and-forth volleys of finger-pointing over Manchester United’s tactics between senior players and under-fire manager Michael Carrick after the Como defeat was more reminiscent of an all-time classic on Wimbledon Centre Court than one of English football’s most historic clubs.

And the binary debate produced by those fiery quotes following United’s 4-1 hammering in the Champions League is also a distraction that deviates from where the heftiest portion of the blame ought to be directed.

United’s director of football, Jason Wilcox, and the club’s recruitment team have more to answer for regarding the Red Devils’ latest fall of disgrace than anyone.

Carrick’s results have been abysmal this season. The form of his captain, Bruno Fernandes, has not been much better, yet United were tempting fate right from the moment the summer transfer window shut.

Veteran Casemiro was always going to be a big loss - his experience, his leadership skills, his set-piece threat - but directing 100 per cent of their transfer outlay on midfielders was a grim mistake.

Doing so left an ageing, injury-prone Luke Shaw as the club’s only recognised left-back. It left Diogo Dalot, fine but never anything more, as the starting right-back.

It left Harry Maguire, 33, expected to start in the Premier League and Champions League multiple times a week. It left no nominal striker good enough as back-up for Benjamin Sesko.

With the Slovenian out, Bryan Mbeumo has had to deputise: an excellent player, and versatile - but only to a point. Mbeumo is being asked to learn on the job how to lead a one-man forward line.

It is almost an irony that the way he has taken to that role - one which better squad-builders would have given to an actual striker - is just about the only tactical element to United’s game that is actually working for them right now.

And to assess how those midfielders they did sign have fared is to conclude not only that the funds were directed too heavily towards one area, but that the value for money Wilcox was able to extract once he’d set his mind to the midfield is just as deserving of fierce criticism.

United’s catastrophic nosedive belongs to Wilcox as much as anyone

Youri Tielemans’s big-game experience was an understandably attractive proposition, but he has followed in the footsteps of countless others in the post-Sir Alex Ferguson era in looking a shoddy imitation of the player he was before he joined.

United are a classic example of a club who have multiple directors to direct blame to, because bulging boardrooms and glitzy job titles are so in fashion in the modern game.

But with director of recruitment Christopher Vivell focused more on scouting than how far the budget stretches, it is Wilcox who is the man leading their overall transfer strategy and the day-to-day running of the club, so it is he who must bear the most responsibility.

United spent well last summer on Mbeumo, Sesko and Matheus Cunha to bring proven top-level quality in the final third. They spent poorly this summer to revamp a midfield that looks weaker as a result.

Why is recruitment being done in three chunks? Why must a backline dreadfully short on quality wait until January or, more likely, next summer for its own injection of fresh athleticism and pedigree?

Even in the age of financial regulation, improving the defence this summer was an obvious priority. Failure to do so was the fans' largest grievance once the window shut and the floodgates opened.
