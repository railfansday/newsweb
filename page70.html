#2273. Celtic winger Jota lifts the lid on 18 months of injury anguish... 'I've met some demons and had talks with them'

📅 26 Oct 2026 | other | Source

By CALUM CROWE, SCOTTISH DEPUTY CHIEF SPORTS WRITER

Published: 18:44 BST, 19 October 2026 | Updated: 18:58 BST, 19 October 2026

When Celtic beat Dundee United 5-0 back in April 2025, the overriding emotion at Tannadice was one of euphoria and celebration.

It was a victory which clinched another league title and the fact that Brendan Rodgers’ side had won it in such style would doubtless have made it taste even sweeter.

Yet, for Jota, it turned into a day he will never forget – but for all the wrong reasons. The Portuguese winger went off with a knee injury and so began a long exile and the toughest period of his career.

By his own admission, he did not fully realise the severity of the injury that would ultimately keep him out of action for 18 months.

‘I knew something happened, but to start to think the worst-case scenario, it doesn’t help you,’ said Jota when reflecting on his injury hell.

‘I was just expecting to see how the examinations were going to be. I didn’t want to make the worst from it. But obviously it ended up being a long time.’

Substitute Jota produced plenty of his old tricks in Celtic's 7-1 thrashing of Hearts on Sunday

There were times last season when it felt like Jota had become the forgotten man at Celtic. There were murmurings of a comeback, then further setbacks, with some questioning whether he would ever return at all.

For his part, he never doubted that he would eventually fight his way back. But what made the situation more difficult was the fact that Celtic were toiling so badly for much of the campaign.

Rodgers would walk away in acrimonious circumstances, before the Wilfried Nancy nightmare plunged the club further into the mire. Jota was powerless to help, relying only on the support of family and team-mates to pull him through the bleakest of situations.

The light at the end of the tunnel finally arrived just over a month ago when he was able to take part in full training once again. He made his long-awaited comeback as a substitute in the victory over Motherwell just over a week ago, before coming off the bench at half-time in the 7-1 hammering of Hearts yesterday.

Just a few hours later, a few clips had gone viral on social media of Jota going through his full repertoire of tricks and making some of the Hearts defenders look rather silly with his trademark tricks and dummies.

He would not claim to be back to his best but it was the clearest indicator yet that this is a player who, on his day, remains arguably the finest natural talent in Scottish football.

Opening up on his battle to overcome personal demons during his long lay-off, Jota explained: ‘It’s like a rollercoaster of emotions. We could stay here for two weeks for me to speak about everything that was going on in my head.

‘I mean, in the end, it’s part of everyone’s life, you know? Everyone struggles. The thing is that in our job, I just cannot be out there to play football, which is the one thing I love most.

‘You kind of feel empty. The hardest part is that there is a part of yourself that goes away and you never know when it’s going to come back.

‘But certainly other people, and I always try to see from this perspective, they have worse problems. I always think there’s someone in a worse position than you.

The winger made his long-awaited  comeback in the recent win against Motherwell at Fir Park

‘So I’m just grateful to — and I think I’m privileged to — be in such a wonderful sport and to have all of these experiences.

‘Being able to play against Hearts  and be back at Celtic Park once again, it shows that it matters, like all of the struggle. It matters because these moments are unique.

‘There’s moments where you feel like you’re down and you cannot see any light, but that doesn’t mean that you stop believing.

‘It’s very painful when you cannot help the team (like during last season). You want to be there and help them but you can’t and you don’t know when you will be again.

‘I think I’ve met some demons and I’ve had some good talks with them. I think we developed a good relationship and we are all on the same page now and we can laugh about everything that happened.

‘I think if you don’t grow from a situation like this, it’s because you didn’t learn anything. Now I can see things clearer.

‘In the recovery process, it’s not always a clear trajectory. It’s not a perfect line. Like it’s not every day we’ll get better.

‘Maybe you get better, then you go down again, but then you go up again and you’re already better than what you were before.

‘So it’s just like a mountain where you keep on building and then eventually you reach the peak.

‘My aim was always that I want to come back fit. Whatever time I need to take, because I still have a long career in front of me and I’m still young. So there’s no rush, let’s do everything correctly and just trust the process.’

Given that he is not registered in the Europa League squad, Jota will be unable to play in Thursday night’s clash with Celta Vigo at Parkhead.

Having lost both of their opening fixtures, Martin O’Neill has admitted that it will be a must-win against the Spaniards if Celtic are to have any hopes of progressing to the knockout stages.

Jota leaves the field on crutches after a victory at Tannadice clinched the title 18 months ago

But Jota could feature against Hibs at Easter Road on Sunday as he continues to build his way up to full match fitness once again.

He thanked O’Neill for the patience and support shown during his long lay-off and is confident he will eventually recapture his best form but insists his injury ordeal has proven that nothing can ever be taken for granted in football.

‘I have had a couple of talks with the manager,’ added the Portuguese winger. ‘I think he was just giving me the time to be healthy and I value that because maybe in another place I wouldn’t have that possibility.

‘And here they gave me the possibility to return fit, healthy and clear-minded. I feel ready to go again, but obviously there are metrics and stuff we need to be careful with (in terms of starting games too soon).

‘I didn’t play in a long time. Now I just want to enjoy every single second on the pitch. You learn to really value it, maybe more than before.

‘It teaches you never to take anything for granted. That’s a big thing you learn when you go through something like this.’

Barcelona will enter Sunday's El Clasico off the back of their first defeat of the campaign, going down 4-2 to European champions Paris Saint-Germain in the Champions League on Tuesday evening, and it was a fascinating match between two excellent teams.

Hansi Flick's team will now put their perfect La Liga record this season on the line; an excellent start to the campaign has seen the Catalan outfit win their first nine games to sit at the summit on 27 points, five points ahead of second-placed Atletico Madrid.

Barcelona beat Real Betis 5-2 in Seville last weekend, and a win on Sunday would see them open up a nine-point lead over Real Madrid, which would be substantial, even at this stage of the season.

The Catalan side have won six of their last seven matches against Real Madrid in all competitions, including a 2-0 victory in the corresponding game during the 2025-26 campaign.

Barcelona have not lost at home to Real Madrid since October 2023, with their last two losses to Los Blancos coming at Bernabeu in Spain's top flight.

Real Madrid will enter this match off the back of a 4-1 win over RB Leipzig in the Champions League, while they also beat Sevilla 4-1 in La Liga last weekend.

Los Blancos suffered a damaging defeat to Atletico Madrid before the September/October international break, but they have since overcome Villarreal, Roma, Sevilla and RB Leipzig to post four straight wins in all competitions.

While it would be incredibly difficult to write off a title challenge for Real Madrid in October, Jose Mourinho's side really cannot afford to fall nine points behind Barcelona, as the room for error is likely to be small in Spain's top flight this season.

Real Madrid have four matches before the next international break, facing Barcelona, Racing de Santander and Valencia in the league, in addition to a trip to AEK Athens in Europe.

Sunday's match will see a number of the world's best players go to battle, including Barcelona's Lamine Yamal and Real Madrid's Kylian Mbappe.

Barcelona will definitely be without the services of Dani Olmo (adductor), Roony Bardghji (knee), Andreas Christensen (thigh) and Eric Garcia (thigh) for El Clasico.

Meanwhile, Jules Kounde is a major doubt due to an ankle issue; at this stage of proceedings, it is unlikely that the France international will be involved.

As a result, it should be Joao Cancelo and Xavi Espart in the full-back areas, while Rodri and Fermin Lopez are both likely to be introduced back into the side after the PSG clash.

Yamal and Raphinha will be certain starters in the final third of the field, while Anthony Gordon is expected to keep his spot down the left.

As for Real Madrid, Rodrygo (knee), Ferland Mendy (hip) and Raul Asencio (leg) will miss the game at Camp Nou through injury, while Vinicius Junior is suspended.

Head coach Mourinho has big decisions to make in midfield, with Federico Valverde's services potentially being used at right-back. As a result, Bernardo Silva's performance against RB Leipzig could see him feature in the starting side.

Arda Guler and Yan Diomande are likely to operate in the wide areas for Los Blancos, with Jude Bellingham as the number 10 behind centre-forward Mbappe.

#2263. Now even RED CARDS go digital! Referee uses a 'smartphone' to dish out his punishments - but fans are not convinced

📅 26 Oct 2026 | other | Source

Published: 18:21 BST, 19 October 2026 | Updated: 19:18 BST, 19 October 2026

A referee has gone viral for seemingly showing a player a red card on his phone - in an eerie experiment which could change football forever.

Eduardo Varela has become the first referee in history to send a player off with a digital red card in a trial in a Copa Uruguay clash between Nacional and Rentistas.

And the smartphone-like device - which also displays yellow cards - logs every booking, substitution, goal, and penalty for the wider Uruguayan football system.

That allows the administration to keep better track of statistics and, in due course, hopefully relay them to fans and the media whatever the level of match.

But the initiative has already come under scrutiny due to problems with visibility.

In one test match between Defensor Sporting and Plaza Colonia, the sunlight rendered it almost impossible to interpret the device from a distance, meaning the experiment had to be abandoned.

Uruguay have been trialling digital cards which track and upload data from matches

Referee Eduardo Varela became the first man to send somebody off digitally

The device is only in the trial stage but it has been an idea three years in the making.

Developers REFe are exploring adding LED lighting around the device to make the colours clearer to spectators.

Their ultimate ambition is for the technology to be adopted by South American ​governing body CONMEBOL and eventually FIFA.

'We have seen all kinds of comments, positive and negative. We ⁠have even ​laughed at the memes,' said Mauro Sacco, director of REFe.

'The important thing is that ​people are talking about it. Any innovation takes time.'

Fellow director Diego de la Pena added: 'It's a project with room for improvement. It looked much better live than it did on TV. The challenge we face now is to improve ​it for the whole audience.

'The ⁠yellow card has to be yellow, the red card has to be red, and it has to be visible to people at the ground and on ​television.'

For the most part, fans online have ridiculed the innovation.

'It doesn't generate any improvement to the game or to the spectator… not everything has to be digital,' one fan wrote on X.

'It'll be a problem if the battery runs out,' pointed out one supporter.

Another wrote: 'This is completely unnecessary and probably heavier to carry than two cards, a solution to a problem that doesn't exist!'

One predicted: 'The most unnecessary technological replacement in life, soon we'll see brands on the cards, that's for sure.'
