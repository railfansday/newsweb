#2615. Coventry vs. Fulham: Head-to-head record and past meetings

📅 26 Oct 2026 | other | Source

By Oliver Thomas | 22 Oct 2026 21:45

Coventry City and Fulham lock horns at the CBS Arena for gameweek seven in the Premier League on Saturday afternoon.

Frank Lampard's side were unable to record a third successive top-flight victory as they fell to a 3-1 away defeat against Tottenham Hotspur last Monday.

As for the Cottagers, they celebrated their first Premier League win of the season under new head coach Alvaro Arbeloa last weekend, narrowly beating Hull City 1-0 on home soil.

Ahead of this contest between two teams sitting in the bottom four of the table, Sports Mole takes an in-depth look at the head-to-head record and previous meetings between the two sides.

Coventry City and Fulham have played against each other a total of 39 times across all competitions and it is the Cottagers who marginally lead the head-to-head record with 16 wins to the Sky Blues' 14, while nine draws have also been played out.

October 1919 was the first time these two sides butted heads in the old Division Two, with Fulham securing a slender 1-0 away victory; this was the first of four wins posted by the Cottagers in their opening five encounters with Coventry.

The Sky Blues responded by winning three of their next four league meetings, including their very first against Fulham in September 1921 (2-0 at home).

Between 1929 and 1932, Fulham and Coventry competed in the third division of English football. The Cottagers won three, drew three and lost two of their eight matches. A eventful 5-3 home win for Fulham was secured in August 1931, just five months before the two teams shared the spoils in a thrilling 5-5 draw.

Over a 13-year period up until 1949, Fulham and Coventry played 12 times against each other in the old Division Two and it was the Sky Blues who had the greater share of success, winning seven and drawing one while the Cottagers only registered four victories.

Almost two decades later, the two teams faced off in the 1967-68 Division One campaign, with Fulham collecting four points from their home (1-1 draw) and away (3-0 win) fixtures.

The last time Coventry and Fulham faced off in the league was during the 2021-22 Championship season when the Cottagers secured promotion as champions.

However, Marco Silva's Fulham lost home and away to Mark Robins's Coventry, who secured a 4-1 triumph at the CBS Arena - their joint biggest-ever win over the Cottagers - before celebrating a 3-1 success at Craven Cottage to complete their first league double over the West Londoners for 74 years.

Away from league action, Coventry and Fulham have only ever faced each other three times in domestic cup competitions. Their one and only FA Cup meeting took place in November 1928 when Fulham ran away 4-1 away victors in the first round.

These two teams have also squared off in one EFL Cup tie in October 1982, with Coventry progressing from the second round via the away goals rule after a 2-2 draw was followed by a goalless stalemate in the replay.

#2607. Seven games, 15 points: How Milan’s start under Amorim compares to last eight seasons

📅 26 Oct 2026 | other | Source

AC Milan won their fourth game of the Serie A season so far as they beat Atalanta 3-2 at San Siro in round seven.

The victory over Atalanta looked like being comfortable and comprehensive with the scoreline 3-0 after less than one hour, before La Dea’s late comeback made it a nervy finish. Milan hung on and Ruben Amorim’s side rose to 15 points from seven games, claiming a spot in the top four for now.

It’s a good start for the Rossoneri, but not the best in recent years. In fact, looking at the last eight seasons, this start is the fifth-best. It is ahead of the 14 points earned under Stefano Pioli in 2022-23, the 11 in 2024-25 under Paulo Fonseca, and the nine earned under Marco Giampaolo in 2019-20.

The Diavolo started better last season with 16 points won under Massimiliano Allegri. In 2023-24 they were on 18 points, in 2021-22 it was 19 – the campaign that ended with the Scudetto – and in 2020-21 it was 17, all seasons with Pioli at the helm.

However, it is also worth noting that Amorim (4W, 3D) has become only the third coach in Milan’s history to remain unbeaten in his first seven Serie A games, after Hector Puricelli in 1955 (3W, 4D) and Luis Carniglia in 1963 (4W, 3D).

#2604. Luton Town vs Leicester City - prediction, team news, lineups

📅 26 Oct 2026 | other | Source

By Ben Sully | 22 Oct 2026 22:38, Last updated: 23 Oct 2026 09:33

Leicester City will be looking to claim a third consecutive win when they head to Kenilworth Road for Saturday's meeting with Luton Town.

The Foxes occupy 10th place in the League One standings, while the Hatters are one point worse off in 13th position.

Mid-table Luton have struggled for consistency this season, having recorded four victories, two draws and four defeats in 10 League One games this season.

They are at least showing signs of building momentum after taking maximum points from two of their last three outings.

The Hatters cruised to a 4-1 away victory over Wycombe Wanderers, before they fell to a narrow 2-1 defeat to MK Dons at Stadium MK.

Jack Wilshere's side responded to that disappointing result with a 2-1 home win in Tuesday's meeting with Barnsley, which came courtesy of first-half efforts from Mads Andersen and Nakhi Wells, the latter of whom has scored six goals in 10 appearances this season.

Luton, who are four points adrift of the top six, will now be aiming to record back-to-back home league wins for the first time this season.

The opening could prove crucial to those hopes, considering they have lost all four league matches in which they have conceded first this term.

Leicester boss Russell Martin came under scrutiny after starting his tenure with just two victories in the first seven matches of the League One season (D3, L2).

Fortunately for the former Rangers boss, some of that pressure has alleviated with back-to-back victories, including a 2-1 home success against Doncaster Rovers last weekend.

The Foxes backed up that result with an emphatic 4-0 triumph in their midweek away clash against newly-promoted Bromley, thanks to goals from Will Alves, John Lundstram, Luke Thomas and Emil Riis.

While those two wins have acted as a welcome confidence boost, Martin will be fully aware that his side cannot afford to rest on their laurels in their efforts to secure an immediate return to the Championship, with the Foxes sitting three points adrift of the playoffs and seven points away from the automatic promotion spots.

They now head on their travels for their first meeting with Luton since running out 4-0 winners in an EFL Cup tie in September 2019, and their first league encounter between the two sides since a 1-1 draw in the Championship in February 2007.

The Foxes enter the game as one of two teams yet to lose a League One away match this season, having recorded two wins and three draws across their five road trips.

Luton are expected to be without the services of Sverre Sandal, Benedict Benagr, Shayden Morris, Shandon Baptiste and Gideon Kodua.

Hakeem Odoffin is unlikely to feature after being forced off in the closing stages of the first half against Barnsley.

As a result, Isiah Jones, who replaced Odoffin as a substitute on Tuesday, is likely to start on the right side of a back four made up of Mads Andersen, George Johnston and Kal Naismith.

As for the Foxes, goalkeeper Jakub Stolarczyk and Ben Nelson are out for the long term with anterior cruciate ligament injuries.

They are also unable to call upon defender Jayden Joseph, who has missed the last six matches through injury.

Midfielder Will Smallbone will provide a useful option off the bench after making his Leicester debut as a substitute in midweek.
