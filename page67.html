#2340. The greatest players never to win the Ballon d'Or: Thierry Henry, Andres Iniesta and more

📅 26 Oct 2026 | other | Source

By Andrew Delaney | 23 Oct 2026 09:29, Last updated: 23 Oct 2026 09:29

Since its foundation in 1956, the Ballon d'Or has been one of the most illustrious individual honours in football, crowning the best player for each calendar year in Europe (and more recently for each season).

From 1995, the award was opened up to all players around the world, after the likes of Pele and Diego Maradona missed out due to being ineligible in their prime, and has grown in significance ever since.

Out of all the players who were eligible, though, there are some whose greatness certainly demanded more recognition from those journalists, players and coaches who vote for the award every year, but never did so.

The 2026 award will be handed out in London on Monday, with Harry Kane and Lamine Yamal expected to go head-to-head for the main prize.

Kane is seen as the slight favourite ahead of Yamal, and it might well be the former's final chance to secure the Ballon d'Or.

Yamal, on the other hand, will surely have more opportunities in the coming years given his age - 19 - and the fact that he is already one of the best players in the world.

Kane continues to influence matches at the highest level, but it is a big ask for him to enjoy an even better season in 2026-27.

Yamal helped Barcelona win La Liga last season before securing the World Cup trophy with Spain, while Kane had an outstanding 2025-26 campaign at Bayern, winning a domestic double, before helping England to finish third at the 2026 World Cup.

Here, Sports Mole looks at some of the finest players who have never won the Ballon d'Or.

Had the award been around a few years earlier, then there is no doubt that Ferenc Puskas would have been a recipient, as the Hungarian legend, part of the trailblazing 'Golden Team' of the 1950s, remains one of the greatest strikers ever to play the game.

One of the biggest shocks in footballing history was Hungary's failure to win the 1954 World Cup, just before that iconic team was split up due to the revolution that took place in the country a couple of years later.

That is what saw Puskas move to Real Madrid in 1958, going on to score 242 goals in 262 matches, winning five La Liga titles and three European Cups, despite playing up until his late 30s, but his performances were only enough to grant him second place in 1960.

Often referred to as the greatest striker in Premier League history, Thierry Henry spent less time in the English top flight than many of his peers, but can more than match them for strike rate, before moving to Barcelona and winning the Champions League in the latter part of his career.

A member of the Arsenal side that went invincible in 2004, Henry also dragged the Gunners to their first ever Champions League final in 2006, and the legendary forward enjoyed unparalleled success at international level too, winning the World Cup and European Championship with France.

Zinedine Zidane got the acclaim ahead of Henry, though, and despite being named FWA Player of the Year three times, and winning the European Golden Boot twice, the Arsenal great could only manage second place in 2003 and third in 2006 - with France missing out on the World Cup being fatal to his chances.

For 14 years, Andres Iniesta was a staple of the classy Barcelona midfield that conquered Europe on numerous occasions, and the Spain side that dominated international football in the late-2000s and early-2010s.

In 2009 and 2011, Iniesta was crucial in one of the greatest club sides of all time claiming Champions League glory, while he was instrumental in Spain's major tournament success in 2008, 2010 and 2012, but playing in the era of Lionel Messi and Cristiano Ronaldo saw him denied individual success.

Barca teammate Messi often pipped him to the award, as Iniesta came second in 2010 thanks largely to his World Cup-winning goal, and he also came third in 2012 after Spain cruised to a second straight Euros crown.

Another player who suffered during the Messi-Ronaldo monopoly was Luis Suarez, who remarkably never made the top three in any year, but will go down as one of the most talented players of his generation.

Winning Golden Boots in Holland, England and Spain, Suarez almost dragged Liverpool to an unlikely title in 2014, before moving to Barcelona and winning the treble in his first full season as one-third of the greatest modern-day strike force in football.

With over 500 goals in roughly 800 club appearances, Suarez was also prolific at international level, lending a helping hand (literally) in Uruguay's run to the semi-final at the 2010 World Cup, before winning the Golden Ball in their Copa America success in 2011, going on to become the country's all-time record goalscorer.

In an award often dominated by attackers, some love has to be shown to one of the greatest defenders of all time, and possibly the most loyal one-club man in the game, Paolo Maldini.

The AC Milan great came third in the 2003 vote after captaining the Rossoneri to the Champions League, and he led his club to the summit of European football again in 2007, having already won the competition three times during the 1980s and 1990s.

Never winning a major honour with Italy is what almost certainly denied him though, as he would go on to see his compatriot Fabio Cannavaro win it in 2006, but Maldini's retirement in 2002 handed the opportunity to Fabio Grosso to take his place at left-back and be the cult hero that guided the Azzurri to their fourth global crown that year.

The one player in this list who still has a huge opportunity to win it in the future is Kylian Mbappe, whose recent move to Real Madrid is often attributed to his desire to win this particular accolade, as well as the Champions League.

In previous eras, what Mbappe has done at international level would have certainly won him the award by now, as he was the Young Player of the Tournament at the 2018 World Cup, becoming the second teenager after Pele to score twice in a World Cup match, and to net in the final, on the way to France winning it for the second time.

Had it not been for the fairytale story of Messi winning the World Cup in 2022, Mbappe would have been in serious contention for the award again too, as his astonishing hat trick in the final was overshadowed by the Argentine great finally clinching the Holy Grail, but at 25, there is still plenty of time for the Frenchman to add his accolades.

#2336. 'He's like a Formula One car on the pitch': Inside the rapid rise of Victor Munoz - and how electrifying talent who trains in mindfulness, rejected Real Madrid and has 'Spartan' discipline became the perfect winger for Andoni Iraola

📅 26 Oct 2026 | other | Source

Published: 17:00 BST, 19 October 2026 | Updated: 17:07 BST, 19 October 2026

Ask anyone in Spain about Victor Munoz and the replies all centre on the same thing. His speed.

‘He was the Formula One car of Real Madrid’s academy,’ one source tells us, while another opens with a word that needs no translation: explosivo.

But Munoz, who sped away from Brentford’s back four like a racecar on Saturday to score a thumping goal, has not always enjoyed life in the fast lane.

Indeed, as a Real Madrid fan who spent his formative years in Barcelona’s famed La Masia academy, the 23-year-old’s journey has been far from linear.

After being dumped by Barca at 14, he was picked up by Real at 18 but made just four first-team appearances. He was deemed surplus to requirements and shipped off to Osasuna for around £5million in 2025.

Munoz hasn't looked back since. Just over a year later, he is a World Cup winner, and excelling back at one of the biggest clubs in world football.

The winger has taken to life in the Premier League with ease. While pundits have criticised some of Liverpool’s big-money buys, this £34.5m summer signing has gone above and beyond expectations.

Victor Munoz has made an impressive start to life at Liverpool, scoring vital goals already against Nottingham Forest and Brentford

While pundits have criticised some of Liverpool’s big-money buys, this £34.5m summer signing has gone above and beyond expectations

‘After leaving Barcelona, he excelled at Damm, a respected development club in Catalonia,’ explains Fernando Sanchez Tavero, a Spanish football expert for the newspaper AS. ‘His performances were so impressive that Barcelona wanted him back.

‘But Real Madrid also came calling, and he did not hesitate. At La Fabrica (their academy), he always stood out for two things: his professionalism and his extraordinary athleticism. Other players attracted more headlines and attention along the way, but Victor never stopped working.

‘He never complained and never gave up. He just kept improving. In his final season with Castilla, everything clicked. He looked like a Formula One car on a football pitch. He was clocked at speeds of up to 23 miles per hour.

‘Suddenly people began to realise just how special his physical qualities were.’

Funnily enough, Munoz’s debut for Real came against his old employers in El Clasico. It was a 4-3 defeat where he missed a one-on-one chance to equalise. He ended up deactivating his social media accounts following the inevitable and depressing online backlash from Madridistas.

AC Milan came knocking but Munoz instead chose the project at Osasuna, which he saw as being built around him rather than just being a spare part at San Siro.

‘That decision proved crucial,’ adds Tavero. ‘Step by step, he evolved into an international player, earned his place with Spain and became a world champion. None of it happened by accident.

‘For years, he has lived with almost Spartan discipline. While other young players struggled with routines, nutrition or consistency, Victor followed every detail relentlessly. Those habits are a major reason why he has been able to thrive once he reached the highest level.

Munoz made his Spain debut in March of this year and was part of the squad that lifted the World Cup four months later

The 23-year-old's pace is an invaluable asset for a winger and has seen him compared to a 'Formula One car'

‘His biggest attributes are his pace, power, intensity and ability to attack space, but there is also a maturity to his game that people sometimes overlook. He knows how to wait for the right moment, how to take his opportunities and how to develop without rushing the process.

‘He never tries to run before he can walk. And when he does run, he usually does it faster than anyone else! That combination of elite athleticism, professionalism, humility and constant improvement is what makes him such a valuable player.’

Liverpool saw all of the above and Munoz became Andoni Iraola’s first signing. He has already surpassed the first buys of Iraola's two predecessors: Arne Slot’s first purchase was Federico Chiesa, who has still not really settled, and Jurgen Klopp’s was Marko Grujic, who made just eight Premier League appearances in four years.

But the fact Munoz was Iraola’s maiden buy is notable. Liverpool, remember, were planning for this campaign with Slot in charge until a late change of heart in the week following last season – so the early recruitment meetings with the new manager saw some realigning.

Iraola, as a La Liga obsessive, knew Munoz well and when his name was flagged by the recruitment team, demanded Liverpool go for him. Iraola knew the Osasuna manager Alessio Lisci and received a strong reference.

The Reds’ scout Kiko Espinar is said to have played a part in the signing, too.

Real Madrid had an option to sign Munoz on a buyback clause but he was less keen to return to the club he supported as a boy.

Newcastle were front-runners to sign him. They had agreed a fee and personal terms but then Liverpool swooped in. It had echoes of a similar saga the summer before, when the Reds hijacked the North East club’s deal for Hugo Ekitike.

'His biggest attributes are his pace, power, intensity and ability to attack space, but there is also a maturity to his game'

Reds boss Andoni Iraola pushed for the signing of Munoz, and the winger is a perfect fit for his high-intensity style of play

‘Victor is a player we knew very well,’ said Iraola ahead of Liverpool’s Champions League tie with Villarreal.

‘Attitude wise, he is ready to help, he plays right, left, the other day he played right back. He is a player with hunger, he has not played professionally in a big club like Liverpool but he has been in the academy of Barcelona and Real Madrid.

‘He trained with those teams and knows the level that is required. With the national team, he has experience and he is a player that is adapting really well.’

Munoz won possession in the final third of the pitch on 22 occasions in La Liga last season – only six players did so more. That suits Iraola, who demands a high-intensity style.

So far at Anfield, he has drifted in and out of the starting XI, with huge competition on the wings. Cody Gakpo, Bradley Barcola, Rio Ngumoha and Munoz all have different traits and competition is fierce.

Munoz has been making 23 sprints per 90 minutes in the Premier League: for comparison, Mohamed Salah last season made 13 per 90. Barcola is averaging 16, Gakpo nine and Ngumoha 13 this term. He has reached a top speed of 22mph, which makes him Liverpool's second-fastest player, behind only Barcola.

Munoz's goals per 90 minutes in the League is 0.42, and he is Liverpool’s most lethal winger. Barcola and Ngumoha are yet to score, while Gakpo is on 0.24. Last season, Salah finished with 0.29 goals per 90 minutes, Gakpo 0.23, Ngumoha 0.33. It's a small sample size, but impressive nonetheless.

Off the pitch, Munoz, whose mother was a hairdresser and father worked in a hospital, is said to have settled well.

Munoz (pictured at Liverpool's AXA training centre with Alexander Isak) is said to have settled in well at his new club

He is a quiet personality off the pitch, who likes going for walks and homecooking (Source: Victor Munoz/Instagram)

He practises ‘mindfulness’ and takes online sessions on this topic. ‘At first, I went to in-person sessions, and now we continue online,’ he told Pamplona newspaper Diario de Navarra before the World Cup.

‘It helps me a lot to manage pressure, injuries, and even the good times, which can sometimes be hard to process. You have to train your mind just like you train your legs.’

Munoz is said to be a quiet personality who likes the music of American rapper Eladio Carrion and enjoys going for walks and cooking.

He certainly doesn't do much walking on the pitch – this electrifying talent has set Liverpool’s attack alight.

In general, the most concrete interest came from England, where Estupinan had shown very positive times during his first season or two at Brighton. These signs have not been seen at Milan, or at least not often enough.

In the league, Estupinan has started five of seven games under Amorim, but he almost never gave the impression of being an added value on the flank. Instead, he made individual errors and was often tactically disorganised. It was almost as if he were playing with the handbrake on.

Last year, Massimiliano Allegri preferred Davide Bartesaghi, who was able to exploit the Ecuadorian’s hesitations to carve out space and shine. This year, however, Amorim continues to rely on Pervis, while also starting to use Diego Moreira in that role.

At this point, one wonders whether Amorim has seen something that others are missing. Or, is this is simply one of those cases where the coach decides to go all the way with his own convictions, even at the cost of testing the patience of a less-than-enthusiastic fanbase?

Amorim wants to prove the doubters wrong. After the match against Atalanta, he declared: “We have to help him. We have to help the fans understand who Estupinan is.” A misunderstood talent? A diamond in the rough? Or simply a player who, for now, hasn’t quite found his feet at Milan?

The pitch, as always, will have the final say. Also because at a certain point, more than explaining a player, you have to be able to make him play well and to do so consistently.

Amorim has certainly shown he knows how to work on individuals, though. Just look at Alphadjo Cisse, this year’s surprise package, as well as the exponential growth of Koni De Winter and Yunus Musah. Moreira is gradually emerging, while Adrien Rabiot has become even more indispensable.

In short, these first two months of the season have seen some positive developments. Estupinan, for now, remains a unique case. In the two Europa League matches in which Milan gave space to the second string, he was rested: a sign that Amorim wants to preserve him above all for the league.

It is faith that, at least for now, hasn’t been repaid on the pitch. Amorim will therefore have an additional task, beyond that of leading Milan back into the Champions League: convincing everyone that Estupinan is a player capable of making a concrete contribution to the team.

Between the two missions, one wonders which is the more complicated. The Champions League is a difficult objective, of course. Convincing the Milan fans that the former Brighton player is truly the right man on the wing, after what we’ve seen so far, risks becoming a real challenge.
