#3160. Scott Boras decries MLB owners who wear 'floaties' as Brewers-Rays World Series comes with salary cap debate

📅 26 Oct 2026 | other | Source

If not listed, please contact your TV provider.

MILWAUKEE — The Brewers and Rays have bullied their way through the postseason. Sure, they've had their share of fights. Tampa Bay needed six games to get past the Guardians. Milwaukee's NLDS win was tight. But for the most part, these two clubs have imposed their will on the opposition, leaving little doubt about their place on baseball's biggest stage.

Now, they'll meet in the 2026 World Series, beginning Friday night at American Family Field.

Yet while their presence here is widely viewed as a triumph for two of baseball's smaller spenders, who entered the postseason with a combined payroll of just $241.8 million, their success comes against the backdrop of a brewing labor war.

And depending on which side of the bargaining table you sit, this World Series is being viewed as either an argument for a salary cap or evidence that baseball doesn't need one.

"I think the fact that two teams like the Brewers and the Rays, we hear all the time that we play baseball the right way, that it's exciting to watch," Brewers owner Mark Attanasio said during Thursday's news conference when asked about a salary cap. "I think they probably hear the same. I think that's good for baseball.

"So whatever that means relative to revenue systems or otherwise, I think if you have a more competitive set, you can have more exciting baseball."

"This has been our goal for 22 seasons, to compete," Attanasio added. "And that said, I think the system that's being proposed, which is similar to other leagues in terms of revenue-sharing system, not only will help us compete but more importantly all of the teams with lower payrolls too, which is something also I think the fans want."

Mega-agent Scott Boras sees things differently. He believes the current system works, as evidenced by the success of the Brewers and Rays. A salary cap, in his view, isn't the answer. Rather, it's about ownership groups making smart decisions, investing in talent and fully committing to building a winner, regardless of market size.

"Look, in the major-league pools, there are a lot of ownership groups that swim, and there's a lot of owners that just choose to wear their floaties all the time," Boras said. "And so, how do they get rid of their floaties? And the answer is, they either have to do it through talent, or they have to do it through investment and acquisition of talent to go out and compete."

Boras pointed to the NBA as a cautionary tale, arguing that basketball's salary-cap system has limited owners' ability to reach deep into their pockets to retain star players, even homegrown talents such as former Celtics star Jaylen Brown, who was traded to the 76ers in July in part because of salary-cap constraints and the financial implications of keeping multiple stars on massive contracts.

"We don't want things like what happened in the NBA, where LeBron James is making $4 million, worth $60 or $70 million," Boras added. "And the Boston owner wants to keep his stars, but he has to sell them, and they can't reward their talent accumulation and intellect as owners because of a false system."

Investment goes beyond the product on the field. The Rays and Brewers have shown that establishing the right culture and organizational infrastructure is just as important to sustained success.

The Rays, for example, have Kevin Cash, a Tampa Bay native and the longest-tenured manager in baseball, in their dugout. Then there's Pat Murphy, who spent eight years in the Brewers organization before taking over as manager ahead of the 2024 season. The culture of both organizations runs through those two men.

But the identities of these franchises extend well beyond their managers. Both teams know exactly who they are, and their commitment to scouting and analytics reflects that. According to MLB Network, the Rays (80) and Brewers (64) have two of the largest scouting departments in baseball, while both rank among the top seven in analytics department size. Those figures don't necessarily reflect how much each organization spends, but they underscore the emphasis both place on identifying, evaluating and developing talent.

"It starts with people and process," said Matt Arnold, the Brewers' president of baseball operations, who spent nearly a decade with the Rays before joining Milwaukee in 2015. "[The Rays] have had an incredible run. Really good people that are scattered all throughout the industry at this point. We have a little bit of a different formula here, and they have a really good formula there obviously, and we learned a lot from each other."

The two sides have learned plenty from one another. And in the looming labor war, they'll find themselves at the center of the debate, too.

For Boras, the heat should be on the owners wearing floaties, not the economic structure of the sport.

"The mere fact that some owners choose not to invest, and they're profiting at high levels, making $100 million a year, and owning a franchise and not being successful. That is not a reason to change the system," Boras said.

Enzo Maresca has provided a fresh update on the fitness of his Manchester City squad ahead of Saturday lunchtime’s Premier League trip to Aston Villa.

The Citizens manager named a much-changed starting lineup that cruised to a 5-1 home win over AEK Athens in the Champions League on Wednesday night.

Erling Haaland, Nico O'Reilly, Mateo Kovacic and Matheus Nunes were four notable omissions from the side, with the latter three all left out of the squad entirely while Haaland watched on as an unused substitute.

Haaland was pictured with a swollen foot after taking a painful blow in the closing stages of City’s 3-2 Premier League win over Ipswich Town last weekend, and he did not warm up with the rest of his teammates before or during the match against AEK Athens.

Afetr the match, Maresca allayed fears over Haaland’s fitness and said that he would be “okay to start” against Villa this weekend.

The Italian coach has since confirmed that Haaland is “100% available” for the trip to Villa Park, while a late call will be made on the fitness of O’Reilly, Kovacic and Nunes.

Speaking about all four players at a press conference on Thursday, Maresca said: "We have a session this afternoon. They can be all available or not.

“Yesterday they were all improving, and we'll see this afternoon. Erling is the only one 100% available, and the other three we need to wait for today's session."

City have heavily relied on Haaland's prolific form once again this term, with the Norwegian goal machine netting 12 times in just 10 appearances across all competitions, including a brace against Ipswich.

O'Reilly was taken off in the second half against the Tractor Boys after aggravating a niggling back issue, while both Kovacic (knock) and Nunes (muscle) were also withdrawn early with minor issues.

The only confirmed injury absentee for Man City in attacker Antoine Semenyo, who is facing around eight weeks on the sidelines with a knee injury picked up in the closing stages against Ipswich.

Man City are one of only three Premier League teams in history to win their opening seven matches of a campaign and they unsurprisingly sit top of the table with a maximum 21 points.

However, the Citizens have failed to win any of their last four visits to Villa Park, losing their last three, and Maresca is expecting another challenging encounter for his team on Saturday.

"I have huge respect for Unai [Emery]; he's done well in all clubs he's been! He's a fantastic manager, they're a very good team, you can recognise the team, see clearly what they want - very intense off the ball.

“They are also, in this moment, in a good mood, winning games. I have respect for Unai and I'm 100% sure that it's going to be a very tough game!"

Man City will move six points clear at the summit with a win against Villa before champions Arsenal in second place take on Everton at 3pm on Saturday.

Lionel Messi has sent an emotional message to Barcelona fans during his return to Spain. The Inter Miami star is in Oviedo to receive the Princess of Asturias Award for Sports, and in an interview with EFE, he spoke about the club and the city where he spent most of his career.

“Barcelona means a lot to me. I feel special love for the city, the club. It will always be like this,” Messi said. He admitted that it is hard to sum up in a few words what the club means to him, because it is a part of his life, adding that he lived “many good things” in Barcelona, both at the club and in the city.

Messi then looked back on the success he enjoyed in Catalonia. “We were lucky to win everything, also in the way we did it, in how we enjoyed playing, winning, achieving important things, but above all in how we enjoyed the day-to-day, the training sessions. We had a great time,” he said. He also called it a privilege to share a dressing room with Xavi, Iniesta and Puyol, “and many more”.

Messi will receive the award on Oct. 23 at the Teatro Campoamor in a ceremony presided over by the Spanish royals, and he described it as an honor to join a list of winners that includes Serena Williams, Rafael Nadal and Michael Schumacher, among other sports icons.

The trip raised some doubts about Messi’s availability for Inter Miami’s next match. The ceremony in Oviedo takes place on Oct. 23, and Inter Miami visit Red Bull New York in Harrison, New Jersey, on Oct. 24 at 5:30 p.m. ET, less than a day later. Sin embargo, Messi is expected to fly to New York on the night of the ceremony and rejoin the team immediately.
