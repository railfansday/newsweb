#3914. Meta announces $95 billion stock offering to fund AI expansion

📅 26 Oct 2026 | technology | Source

Meta said Monday it is planning to raise a total of $95 billion to support its investments in AI infrastructure and superintelligence.

The stock was up 1.6% in premarket trading after the announcement. Nvidia, AMD and Broadcom were interested in purchasing up to $5 billion in shares, Meta said in a statement.

The tech giant will initially raise $35 billion in underwritten public equity offerings, before raising an additional $60 billion with an at-the-market program.

The news comes as hyperscalers pour record sums into the AI infrastructure buildout. Goldman Sachs estimates that AI-related capital spending could total about $7.6 trillion from 2026 through 2031, across compute, data centers and power.

Meta's planned investments will support "emerging AI opportunities" including its family of apps, its AI agent Muse and its data center business, the company said Monday.

It plans to balance funding for these investments through a mix of operating cash flow, investment-grade debt, partnerships and equity capital.

Meta is among the major hyperscalers that are increasingly tapping debt to fund the expensive infrastructure required for the AI boom.

Last week, Meta posted third-quarter results showing revenue jumped 26% year-on-year: $64.60 billion versus the $63.37 billion analysts expected. However, investors focused more on the tech company's negative free cash flow and its higher full-year capital expenditure forecast.

It now expects its full-year capex to range from $135 billion to $145 billion, raising the bottom of the range from $130 billion. That sent shares lower on the day.

Meta is building four "superclusters" with more than one gigawatt of capacity each and is on track to nearly double total infrastructure capacity in 2026 on a year-over-year basis, the company said.

The decision to raise more money "reflects the eye-watering levels of investment required to keep up in the AI arms race," Ben Wood, senior partner at CCS Insight, told CNBC.

"Meta offering more stock comes at a time when numerous companies, such as Google, SpaceX and soon, Anthropic, are going to the markets to raise funds."

Alphabet, Google's parent company, said it would look to raise $85 billion in June. SpaceX has closed a $40 billion debt financing to purchase graphics processing units. Anthropic is preparing for an IPO.

TikTok U.S. is looking to secure multiple gigawatts of computing capacity over the coming years, CNBC reported on Friday.

#3934. Apollo to buy nearly 10% stake in Abu Dhabi-backed alternative asset manager Mubadala Capital

📅 26 Oct 2026 | technology | Source

Investing giant Apollo Global Management is finalizing a deal to buy nearly a 10% stake in Mubadala Capital, the alternative asset manager subsidiary of Abu Dhabi's sovereign wealth fund, Mubadala Investment Co., according to people familiar with the matter who spoke on condition of anonymity.

The deal is expected to be announced later Monday morning, they said. Financial terms were not immediately known.

The transaction is significant on multiple fronts. It further advances Apollo's ambitions in Abu Dhabi often called the "capital of capital." The firm has spent the past 15 years building key strategic relationships there, with both the country's investment arm and its core leadership.

Apollo already has a multibillion-dollar partnership with Mubadala Investment Co., first established in 2022 and extended two years ago. At the time of that extension, Apollo said the agreement would strengthen the firm's Capital Solutions platform by enabling the firms to jointly source and provide customized debt and equity financing to companies worldwide.

In 2023, the institutions formed a $2.5 billion joint venture designed specifically to co-invest in global private credit opportunities over a five-year horizon.

For Mubadala Capital specifically — the region's only large-scale asset manager, advising and administering more than $755 billion in assets — the deal further advances its growth strategy under the direction of CEO Hani Barhoush, and its relevance on the global investing stage. It also marks an inflection point for the company's core alternatives business, which has grown to more than $60 billion.

John Zito, co-president of Apollo's Asset Management arm, and Barhoush have worked closely together over the past several years and, according to sources, their close-knit relationship and the success of prior partnerships led to talks of deepening ties. So did the belief that scale in the asset management business matters more than ever.

"Capital as a moat is a big thing," said one person familiar with the deal.

Importantly, the deal also continues a notable shift in the relationship between American investment firms and the Middle East. While the region, and Abu Dhabi specifically, has long been a sought-after source of capital, this deal further reverses that dynamic, with Apollo making a significant direct investment in the country and its investment vision.

Mubadala Capital's international offices include London, New York, Rio de Janeiro and San Francisco.

Apollo's investment in Mubadala Capital is similar to deals the firm has done with other asset management platforms, including Diameter Capital, Thoreau, Motive Partners and others.

Spokespersons for both firms declined to comment.

Australians should prepare for more basic and important online services to go offline unexpectedly, the nation’s signals intelligence agency has warned, urging that “system security must be prioritised over system availability”.

The Australian Signals Directorate (ASD) also says businesses and critical infrastructure must use cutting-edge AI models to defend against digital threats, raising alarm that artificial intelligence is enabling more dangerous threats than ever.

In its annual cyber threat report, ASD warns hostile foreign governments, criminals and hackers are using AI to gather intelligence, disrupt critical infrastructure and steal money, and that Australians should fight fire with fire.

“We need AI tools to help automate our defences to stay ahead of AI-enabled attackers,” the ASD director general, Abigail Bradshaw, said on Monday.

“Australia must accelerate the modernisation of its technology and cybersecurity foundations to protect the systems we rely on every day.”

The annual threat report, released on Tuesday, revealed that ASD had responded to 1,500 cybersecurity incidents in the 2025-26 year, an increase of 20% from the previous year. It also notified external entities – including businesses, governments and critical infrastructure – of more than 4,400 potential malicious cyber incidents, up 80% in a year. AI is expected to accelerate those dangers in future.

ASD said government and private networks in Australia experienced “persistent” breach attempts from state-based actors last year, and claimed such actors “in the Indo-Pacific” could be launching attacks to prepare for potential future conflicts – including “future intelligence collection or disruption activities”.

The agency said the increasing availability of AI was creating positive opportunities, but also allowing cybercriminals and state-based actors to find vulnerabilities and launch attacks faster and more effectively. ASD warned “all network owners are facing elevated threat levels”, saying cybersecurity was now a “core business risk and leadership responsibility” for all organisations, no longer only a technical concern.

The report said integrating AI tools into cybersecurity functions could help find potential weaknesses or suspicious behaviour, and improve responses.

“To strengthen our cyber defences, we must harness the technologies that are reshaping the threat environment,” Bradshaw said.

She has spoken previously about ASD having access to Anthropic’s Mythos model, the powerful AI tool which the frontier lab said it couldn’t release publicly because it could identify unknown flaws in IT systems. While the use of AI by malicious actors is on the rise, Australian government agencies believe having access to such frontier models and collaborating with leading AI labs is critical to bolstering cybersecurity.

“To stay ahead, Australians must enhance their cyber defences while harnessing AI’s benefit,” the defence minister, Richard Marles, said on the report’s release.

Tony Burke, the minister for home affairs and cybersecurity, said the government was “positioning Australia to respond to the evolving threats and opportunities presented by emerging technologies”.

#3452. Serbia after Vucic: Who will be PM and when will a government be formed?

📅 26 Oct 2026 | technology | Source

Uncertainty remains over key positions after student-led movement deals blow to longtime leader’s political dominance.

A student-led opposition movement has emerged victorious in Serbia’s snap parliamentary election, ending former President Aleksandar Vucic’s long grip on power.

The Student List’s win on Sunday followed nearly two years of youth-led protests triggered by the death of 16 people in a railway station canopy collapse in the city of Novi Sad in 2024.

Demonstrations demanding accountability quickly evolved into a wider political campaign against alleged corruption and capture of democratic institutions. In its electoral programme presented in September, the Student List said fighting corruption and organised crime were among its main priorities. It also pledged to raise living standards, boost employment, improve education, ensure media freedom and the independence of Serbia’s institutions.

After declaring victory on Sunday, the movement also said it would work to improve relations with neighbouring countries following a period of tension during Vucic’s more than decade-long rule.

The Republic Election Commission (RIK) announced on Monday that the Student List had won 52.54 percent of the vote, based on 8,077 processed polling stations out of 8,392.

The figures also showed the United Serbia list, led by Vucic’s Serbian Progressive Party, had secured 39.29 percent of ballots cast.

Voter turnout stood at 68.78 percent, a record high in more than 30 years, according to RIK data on Sunday.

While the final election result is not yet known, the figures mean that the Student List will be able to form a majority in Serbia’s 250-seat parliament on its own.

The bloc is projected to win 140 seats, well ahead of the 126 mandates required for a majority, while United Serbia is expected to have 103.

The remaining seats are set to go to the Alliance of Vojvodina Hungarians (three); the Albanian political bloc (two); and the Rasim Ljajc-Ljudski and SDA Sandzak lists, with one each.

Unlike United Serbia, which named Vucic as its candidate for prime minister before the election, the Student List made no such announcement during the campaign.

Since its formal creation, the list has avoided highlighting single candidates, eager to emphasise the collective nature of the movement that brought together academics, doctors and workers.

During the campaign, the movement was led by Ilija Srdanovic, a professor at the Faculty of Medicine at the University of Novi Sad. The cardiologist is now seen as the most likely candidate to become the next prime minister, but it remains unclear who will take over key ministries, including those of internal affairs, finance and justice.

This depends on when the final election results will be announced and when parliament convenes.

The new National Assembly must be established within 30 days of the final results being announced.

A government must be formed within 90 days after that – but the Student List’s clear majority removes the need for lengthy coalition talks.

Vucic has dominated Serbia’s politics – first as prime minister and then as president – since 2014.

Following his call for the snap parliamentary vote last month and his resignation as president, the post has been held temporarily by Ana Brnabic, the president of the National Assembly.

Brnabic is obliged to call for an extraordinary presidential election which must be held no later than three months after the end of Vucic’s mandate.

Vucic resigned on September 27 , so the deadline to hold a vote is December 27 .

The president of Serbia is elected directly by the citizens, not by the representatives in the assembly, but the post is largely ceremonial.

#4101. Scientists Thought This Venomous Primate Might Be Extinct. It Was Turning Up in People’s Gardens

📅 26 Oct 2026 | technology | Source

Missing from science for nearly 90 years, this unusual primate was hiding in plain sight.

How does one stay off science’s radar for nearly 90 years?

The Bangka slow loris did. This small primate has practically mastered the art of going unnoticed. With its nocturnal lifestyle and a silent, deliberate way of moving through the trees, this elusive Indonesian mammal is remarkably good at keeping a low profile.

But here’s the funny thing: it wasn’t even trying that hard to hide. While scientists feared it might have gone extinct, the little primate was casually wandering into people’s houses, crossing roads, climbing power lines, and even helping itself to guavas in someone’s garden.

Now, a new paper has brought the species back into the scientific spotlight. Researchers examined records of more than 90 slow lorises rescued on Indonesia’s Bangka Island between 2015 and 2025, including 86 that were eventually returned to the wild.

The Bangka slow loris was first described in 1906 but only recognized as a distinct species in 2013. By 2020, the International Union for Conservation of Nature (IUCN) had classified it as critically endangered and possibly extinct.

The last historical scientific account dated to 1937, although the new paper notes that the animals described in that account may have been collected several years earlier.

Study co-author Timon Glaw, a researcher at Vienna’s Haus des Meeres Aqua Terra Zoo, told Reuters that he was astonished such a distinctive primate had received so little scientific attention for nearly a century. He hopes the discovery might encourage searches for other species feared extinct.

So how did such a distinctive animal slip through the cracks?

At the Alobi Wildlife Rescue Center, a local conservation organization on Bangka Island, slow lorises had been turning up for years. When Glaw connected with conservationist Jochen Menner, who put him in touch with Alobi, he discovered that local rescuers possessed something the scientific literature lacked: years of documented encounters with the supposedly lost primate.

The animals were well known to local rescuers, even as the scientific community continued to regard the species as possibly extinct.

Some evidence had previously surfaced in photographs, online observations, and an unpublished university thesis. But the rescue records had never been comprehensively documented in a peer-reviewed scientific study. Until now.

The researchers examined detailed circumstances for 43 rescued animals. Sixteen had entered houses or gardens. Seven had burns consistent with electrical shocks, most apparently involving power lines. Six were found on roads, five came from captivity or the wildlife trade, and three were discovered in plantations.

One turned up at a school. Another was found injured in the back of a pickup truck returning from a banana plantation. At least three of the rescued animals died.

Perhaps the most charming encounter came in October 2024, when someone found a slow loris eating guavas in a garden. For an animal considered possibly extinct, it wasn’t being particularly discreet.

But there’s a catch. Finding animals across an island doesn’t really tell you how many are left.

The Bangka slow loris looks adorably cartoonish. Measuring roughly 26 centimeters (10 inches) long, it has reddish-brown fur, a round head, tiny ears, and enormous forward-facing eyes surrounded by dark markings that resemble a mask.

They live a nocturnal lifestyle. Unlike monkeys that leap between branches, slow lorises move with extraordinary deliberation, gripping branches with powerful hands and feet. They can hang beneath a branch while reaching for food, moving almost silently through the forest canopy.

But don’t let the adorable appearance fool you. Slow lorises are the only venomous primates that we know of, and their method of delivering venom is unlike anything else in the animal kingdom.

When threatened, a slow loris raises its arms above its head and licks a specialized gland near its elbow. The secretion mixes with saliva to produce venom that it can deliver through a powerful bite. In humans, such bites can cause intense pain, swelling, and occasionally life-threatening allergic reactions.

A 2020 study published in Current Biology, which followed wild Javan slow lorises for eight years, found that they use venom in fights with one another, apparently to defend territories and mates. Researchers also suspect that venom may help deter predators and parasites.

Whether Bangka slow lorises use their venom in exactly the same ways hasn’t been studied. In fact, we know remarkably little about how these animals behave in the wild.

Slow lorises have another peculiar adaptation: a comb of forward-pointing lower teeth. Besides grooming their fur, they use these teeth to scrape bark and extract tree gum. A specialized second tongue helps keep the toothcomb clean.

Other slow loris species feed on insects, nectar, tree gum, and various plant materials. What the Bangka slow loris eats in the wild, however, remains largely a mystery. The guava-eating visitor offers one small clue, but hardly enough to establish the species’ natural diet.

Even basic questions about its population, behavior, and reproduction remain largely unanswered. For a primate that has lived alongside people for generations, scientists are only beginning to get acquainted.

Unfortunately, the abundance of encounters with humans isn’t necessarily good news.

Bangka, home to more than a million people, has experienced extensive deforestation driven by tin mining, agriculture, and expanding oil palm plantations.

A 2017 study of deforestation in Indonesia estimated that the Bangka slow loris lost 62% of its forest habitat between 2000 and 2012 alone.

The new paper paints a troubling picture of the remaining landscape: mining pits, plantations, and isolated forest fragments. During a visit to one potential habitat in 2023, researchers encountered a deliberately set forest fire and small-scale tin mining operations. They found no lorises, although their brief search was not a systematic survey.

For an animal that depends on trees to move around, this transformed landscape can be treacherous. Roads and open spaces interrupt the canopy, while power lines can become deadly obstacles.

The scientists also caution that the rescue records cannot reveal how many animals survive in the wild. Most reports came from areas close to the rescue center, where people were more likely to encounter and report injured animals.

Some of the apparent geographic distribution may also reflect animals that had previously been captured or transported by people, rather than where they originally lived.

Even the 86 successful releases leave an important question unanswered: Did the animals survive afterward? No follow-up survival data exist.

That uncertainty matters. In a separate 2026 study of Bengal slow lorises in Bangladesh, only two of nine released animals survived beyond six months. Four died after fights with other lorises. Those findings cannot establish the fate of Bangka’s animals, but they show why releasing rescued wildlife isn’t necessarily the end of the story.

Researchers now recommend systematic population surveys, genetic testing, monitored releases, a conservation breeding program, and stronger protection for the remaining forests.

The genetic work is particularly important because the species’ distinct identity still rests on physical characteristics rather than direct genetic confirmation from Bangka animals. Such testing could also help determine how the island’s slow lorises are related to populations on Borneo and whether the animals reported from neighboring Belitung belong to the same species.

“We are glad to contribute to the scientific record to show that the Bangka slow loris is not lost, but the species is still very close to extinction,” said Langka Sani, director of the Alobi Wildlife Rescue Center. “Its natural habitat on Bangka Island has shrunk significantly because of illegal tin mining and massive land clearing for oil palm plantations. We ask people around the world to support us in saving the Bangka slow loris while we still can.”

After nearly a century of scientific neglect, the Bangka slow loris is finally getting attention. The question now is whether that attention has come soon enough.

The study was published in the journal Spixiana.

#3770. PHP warns of a third PHP2Shell flaw but won’t rush out a fix

📅 26 Oct 2026 | technology | Source

A North Korea-linked group is scanning 216 servers for the first two flaws, CYFIRMA says. Palo Alto Networks has withdrawn its claim of an attack on a European agency.

PHP has warned of a third flaw that could let an attacker run code on a web server, two days after its emergency fixes for the PHP2Shell bugs. This time it will not rush out a fix.

In a short advisory on 23 October, PHP said the new bug, CVE-2026-115560, needs an unusual mix of settings and application code. An application would have to switch its session serialize handler to ‘php’ at runtime before starting a session. Session upload progress would also have to be on.

Until the next routine release, which will carry the fix, PHP suggests turning off session upload progress. That setting was already a fallback for one of the first two flaws, so admins who applied it need do nothing more. Elizabeth Barron, the PHP Foundation’s executive director, told The Register that the foundation does not expect that setup in practical use.

PHP fixed the first two flaws on 21 October, after researchers using AI reported them. The fixes are in versions 8.5.12, 8.4.27, 8.3.36 and 8.2.35.

Meanwhile, a Korean-speaking group linked to North Korea is quietly scanning 216 servers for those first two flaws, CYFIRMA said. The threat intelligence firm says the targets include government agencies, banks, airports and online transaction platforms. They sit in the US, Japan, South Korea, India, Australia, the EU and elsewhere.

CYFIRMA published its advisory on 23 October and updated it on 24 October. It says the operators work from 21 hijacked machines. It has not confirmed any mass compromise by this group. Its founder and chief executive, Kumar Ritesh, described the method to The Hacker News.

“The scanning is deliberately low-volume and paced. Nodes are rotated and abandoned at the first sign of blocking or rate-limiting, so threshold-based alerting is unlikely to catch it.” Kumar Ritesh, CYFIRMA, to The Hacker News

The firm ties the operators to North Korea with high confidence. It points to their language, their talk in a closed I2P channel and their targets. It rates their link to the Lazarus Group at moderate confidence. Next, the advisory says, the operators will pick the most promising hosts and decide which public methods are worth adapting.

A separate report no longer stands. On 23 October, Palo Alto Networks’ Unit 42 said attackers had used PHP2Shell against a government body in Europe. Within two hours, it said, they reached password hashes and multi-factor authentication secrets. It saw signs that an AI agent was doing the work.

On Sunday, Unit 42 retracted that update.

“We are unable to confirm that initial access is associated with the specific PHP2Shell CVEs. We have retracted our previous update from October 23.” Unit 42, Palo Alto Networks

Attempts still run into the millions, as Cloudflare has reported. Officials in Germany and Japan had already said they saw no confirmed break-ins. As of Friday, nobody had publicly confirmed a successful break-in through PHP2Shell, The Register reported. Barron said this of the first two flaws:

“We currently have no evidence of any exploitation of these vulnerabilities on patched PHP versions.” Elizabeth Barron, to The Register

The flaws also reach network storage devices, which run PHP in their own software. Synology has shipped updated PHP packages, AppleInsider reported on 22 October. QNAP confirmed a range of affected products and was still working on fixes, with no date given. It reported no attacks on its products. TerraMaster said it was working toward a fix.

As the brain ages, protective and harmful biological processes compete to influence its health. When harmful changes become dominant, synapses deteriorate, inflammation increases, and problems with lipid metabolism can interfere with normal brain function.

“As humans age, both protective and harmful biological processes shape brain health. When the balance shifts in favor of harmful processes, age-related changes in the brain can accelerate, including synapse loss, disruptions in lipid metabolism and chronic inflammation,” said Vicidomini.

The mice also experienced disruptions in lipid metabolism, accumulation of fat droplets in brain cells, and greater memory impairment.

Increasing Pla2g2f activity had the opposite effect. It protected against many of these changes, helped maintain healthy communication between neurons, and preserved memory.

“Remarkably, boosting Pla2g2f in middle-aged mice before they developed substantial cognitive decline altered the trajectory of age-related memory loss,” said Sahay.

The results suggest that strengthening this protective mechanism before significant damage occurs may be especially valuable.

The researchers also uncovered a connection between Pla2g2f and the regulation of fats in the brain.

Microglia, the brain’s immune cells, help clear waste, maintain synapses, and support neurons. During aging and Alzheimer’s disease, however, these cells can accumulate lipid droplets, making them less effective and contributing to chronic inflammation.

Experiments using human microglia derived from induced pluripotent stem cells showed that neuronal PLA2G2F could influence these immune cells. Increasing its activity reduced the accumulation of lipid droplets and cholesterol esters, a form of stored cholesterol. Together, the findings point to PLA2G2F as a protective signal released by neurons that helps maintain healthy lipid metabolism and limit inflammation across brain cells.

Aging is the greatest risk factor for Alzheimer’s disease, making the discovery of natural mechanisms that preserve memory particularly important.

“Additional studies will be needed to determine whether this approach can be safely translated into therapies for patients,” Sahay said. “We are actively exploring potential ways to safely increase Pla2g2f levels in the aging brain and in Alzheimer’s disease as a possible therapeutic strategy.”

#4050. A 2,700-year-old solar eclipse just revealed something surprising about Earth

📅 26 Oct 2026 | technology | Source

An international research team has revisited one of the oldest precisely dated records of a total solar eclipse, uncovering new details about how Earth's rotation has changed over thousands of years. By combining historical geography with modern astronomical calculations, the scientists reconstructed an eclipse that occurred in 709 BCE and determined how it would have appeared from Qufu, the ancient capital of China's Lu Duchy.

The researchers also examined an early account that may describe the solar corona, the Sun's faint outer atmosphere that becomes visible to the naked eye during a total solar eclipse. Their analysis of its possible shape suggests that the Sun was returning to a more active phase following an extended period of unusually low activity.

Published in Astrophysical Journal Letters, the findings provide more precise evidence of Earth's rotation speed in ancient times. They also offer potential independent support for previous reconstructions of solar activity based on radiocarbon measurements.

An Ancient Chinese Eclipse Record Holds a Crucial Clue

On July 17, 709 BCE, observers at the court of the Lu Duchy witnessed a total solar eclipse. The event was preserved in the "Spring and Autumn Annals," a historical chronicle compiled approximately 200 to 300 years later. The original account stated that "the Sun was totally eclipsed."

An additional description appears in the "Hanshu" (Book of Han), which preserves a passage written about seven centuries after the eclipse. This later account contains an unusual observation that may refer to the Sun's corona.

"What makes this record special isn't just its age, but also a later addendum in the 'Hanshu' (Book of Han) based on a quote written seven centuries after the eclipse. It describes the eclipsed Sun as 'completely yellow above and below.' This addendum has been traditionally associated with a record of a solar corona. If this is truly the case, it represents one of the earliest surviving written descriptions of the solar corona," explained lead author Hisashi Hayakawa, an assistant professor at Nagoya University's Institute for Space-Earth Environmental Research and Institute for Advanced Research.

Initially, however, the historical account appeared to conflict with modern astronomy. When the team calculated the eclipse's path using existing estimates of Earth's ancient rotation, their results indicated that a total eclipse should not have been visible from the previously assumed location of the Lu court in Qufu.

The researchers traced the discrepancy to a mistake in the city's geographic coordinates. By examining archaeological excavation reports and historical geographic evidence, they determined that earlier calculations had placed ancient Qufu approximately eight kilometers from its actual location.

Correcting this relatively small error made it possible to reconstruct the eclipse more accurately and extract valuable information about Earth's rotation and the Sun's appearance.

"This correction allowed us to accurately measure the Earth's rotation during the total eclipse, calculate the orientation of the Sun's rotation axis, and simulate the corona's appearance," explained Hayakawa, who holds doctorates in solar physics and oriental history.

Why Ancient China Preserved Such Detailed Astronomical Records

The survival of this remarkable observation reflects the importance of astronomy in ancient Chinese society. Successive dynasties employed specialists to monitor celestial events because unusual phenomena in the sky were often interpreted as omens.

This practice, known as omenology, was closely connected to political beliefs. Eclipses, auroras, and other extraordinary astronomical events were sometimes considered warnings that an emperor had acted improperly.

As a result, court astronomers carefully documented changes in the sky. The records maintained across successive dynasties eventually became some of the world's most valuable historical sources for studying ancient eclipses.

Even so, the researchers emphasize an important distinction between the original eclipse observation and the later description of the corona.

The 709 BCE event is regarded as the earliest explicit written record of a total solar eclipse that can be securely dated. It may also be associated with one of the oldest surviving descriptions of the solar corona, but that interpretation remains uncertain because the relevant passage appears in a source written centuries after the event.

Although the corona account requires caution, the established timing of the eclipse provides a reliable basis for investigating Earth's historical rotation. The possible corona description also offers a valuable comparison with other studies of ancient solar activity.

How a 2,700-Year-Old Eclipse Reveals Earth's Faster Rotation

Earth does not rotate at a perfectly constant rate. Over long periods, its spin has gradually slowed, partly because of friction associated with ocean tides generated by the Moon's gravitational pull.

Consequently, Earth rotated slightly faster 2,700 years ago than it does today.

Using the revised position of ancient Qufu, the researchers obtained improved constraints on Earth's rotation during the period spanning the eighth through sixth centuries BCE.

Their calculations placed delta T (ΔT), a measurement used to describe variations in Earth's rotation relative to a uniform time scale, between 20,264 and 21,204 seconds for the eclipse of 709 BCE.

"This new dataset fixes coordinate errors in previous Earth rotation studies. Additionally, it improves the accuracy of dating and reconstructing historical astronomical events," said coauthor Mitsuru Sôma of the National Astronomical Observatory of Japan.

These measurements help scientists better understand changes in the length of Earth's day over thousands of years. They can also improve calculations of when and where other ancient astronomical events would have been visible.

Evidence That the Sun Was Awakening From a Long Quiet Period

Beyond revealing Earth's rotational history, the eclipse may provide evidence of an important transition in the Sun's activity.

Scientists have previously reconstructed ancient solar cycles by studying radiocarbon preserved in tree rings. The possible corona description from 709 BCE offers another way to examine those findings.

"This unique historical addendum for the possible solar coronal structure is critical for providing a spot reference on solar activity reconstructions from tree rings and ice cores, as well as providing independent validation of solar activity models," explained coauthor Mathew Owens, a professor of space physics at the University of Reading.

Trees absorb carbon from the atmosphere during photosynthesis, including small amounts of radioactive carbon known as radiocarbon. Some of this material becomes locked into annual growth rings, creating a natural record that researchers can examine thousands of years later.

Radiocarbon levels are influenced by cosmic rays reaching Earth's atmosphere. When the Sun becomes more active, its magnetic influence generally reduces the number of cosmic rays that reach Earth, affecting radiocarbon production.

By measuring changes in radiocarbon concentrations across successive tree rings, scientists can reconstruct how solar activity varied long before modern instruments existed.

The Sun normally follows an activity cycle lasting approximately 11 years, during which the number of sunspots and the strength of solar magnetic activity rise and fall.

Occasionally, however, the Sun enters much longer periods of reduced activity called "grand minima." During these intervals, sunspots become exceptionally rare, and the usual cycle is much less pronounced.

One such interval, known as the "Neo Assyrian Grand Minimum" or "Homer Grand Minimum," lasted from approximately 808 to 717 BCE.

The eclipse of 709 BCE took place shortly after this prolonged quiet period ended.

According to the researchers, the possible description of the solar corona is consistent with a Sun that had resumed its regular activity cycles. Its apparent structure suggests substantial magnetic activity and indicates that the Sun may have been near the peak of its approximately 11-year cycle at the time.

This interpretation agrees with earlier solar activity reconstructions derived from radiocarbon measurements in tree rings, although the uncertainty surrounding the historical corona description means it cannot be considered definitive proof.

Ancient Astronomers Are Still Helping Scientists Understand the Universe

The findings demonstrate how observations preserved for thousands of years can continue to contribute to modern scientific discoveries.

By bringing together historical documents, archaeological evidence, geographic research, and sophisticated astronomical models, scientists can recover information that would otherwise be impossible to measure directly.

In this case, a correction of just eight kilometers helped transform an ancient eclipse record into a more precise source of information about Earth's rotation and a potential window into the Sun's distant past.

"Some of our ancestors were very skilled observers," noted coauthor Dr. Meng Jin of the Lockheed Martin Solar and Astrophysics Laboratory. "When we combine their careful records with modern computational methods and historical evidence, we can potentially find new information about our planet and our star from thousands of years ago."

'The feeling surrounding Harry Kane is that, all things considered, his candidacy is the strongest,' write Spanish outlet Marca.

Kane helped fire England to the World Cup semi-finals and also won the Bundesliga, DFB-Pokal and German Supercup with Bayern.

The most prestigious individual honours in world football will be awarded tonight, with the likes of Lionel Messi, Jude Bellingham, and Rodri also in contention.

Presented by magazine France Football since 1956, it recognises the player judged the best in the world across the previous season. And since 2018, there has been the equivalent prize for the women’s game. Here’s all you need to know about tonight’s ceremony…

Kane is the bookies' favourite to win the Ballon d'Or tonight after scoring 73 goals in 65 games

With the exception of 2010-2015, when Zurich took the honour, the Ballon d’Or ceremony is held in France – with the last seven years in the Theatre du Chatelet in Paris. But this year the Palladium in London’s West End will host – perhaps a good omen for Kane. UEFA explained in May that the choice of venue is symbolic in tribute to inaugural winner Stanley Matthews, who received his award at Blackpool Town Hall. Seventy years on, and having accumulated a little more glitz and glamour over the decades, it returns to England.

Tonight’s show will be hosted by Arsenal and France legend Thierry Henry for the first time, alongside presenter Kate Scott, who returns for a fourth year. The 45-year-old is best known for her work on Champions League nights at US network CBS Sports, where she is a regular host alongside pundits Henry, Jamie Carragher and Micah Richards.

Henry – who was nominated for the award nine times – said: 'London is the city I call home, and I have a deep connection to its football culture and the passionate fans here, so to be hosting here alongside my friend and colleague Kate feels like a natural fit.’

For the men’s and women’s awards a shortlist of 30 players is decided by journalists from both France Football and L'Equipe newspaper. This shortlist is then ranked by journalists from around the world – 100 for the men’s, 50 for the women’s – with their rankings combined to decide the winner.

Jurors are instructed to consider these factors: individual performances and impressive character; collective performances and titles won; class and fair play.
