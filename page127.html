#1138. Germany's Franz Reindl elected new IIHF president

📅 26 Oct 2026 | other | Source

Franz Reindl has been elected president of ice hockey's world governing body. The former forward was a member of West Germany's Olympic bronze medal-winning team.

Franz Reindl came from behind to narrowly beat Petr Briza of the Czech Republic in a run-off vote to become the International Ice Hockey Federation's new president on Friday.

Reindl had trailed Briza 48-36 in the first round of voting but picked up crucial votes after Aivaz Omorkanov of Kyrgyzstan was eliminated. He then beat Briza 60-57 in the runoff held on the Spanish island of Tenerife. Six of a total of nine candidates had withdrawn prior to the election.

The 71-year-old German, who had been a member of the IIHF Council since 2016, succeeds Canadian-born Frenchman Luc Tardif, who had announced in March that he would not seek a further term in office. Reindl's election comes 5 years after he had finished as runner-up to Tardif. His term runs until 2030.

Reindl has done it all in German ice hockey, having been a player and coach, as well as serving as the first CEO of the top-flight professional league, the Deutsche Eishockey Liga (DEL), from 1994 to 19<96. He also headed the organizing committees for three World Championships — in 2001, 2010 and 2017. From 2014 to 2022 he served as president of the German Ice Hockey Association (DEB), overseeing an upturn in the fortunes of the men's national team, which won the silver medal at the Pyeongchang Winter Olympics in 2018.

"Franz Reindl has shaped German ice hockey for decades—as a player, but above all through the various key roles he has held within our federation," the current DEB chairman, Frank Lutz, said.

"The fact that the international ice hockey family is now entrusting him with the leadership of the IIHF is a great tribute to his work."

Like Briza, who was a goalie for the Czechoslovakia team that won bronze in Albertville in 1992, Reindl was also an Olympic bronze medalist, having been a member of the 1976 West Germany team who beat the United States and Finland to finish third in Lake Placid.

Domestically, Reindl played 669 games and scored 423 goals for SC Riessersee and SB Rosenheim over 16 seasons in the Eishockey-Bundesliga, the predecessor to the DEL.

Tardif's years as IIHF president were marked by two major events: the return of National Hockey League players to the Olympic tournament in Milan Cortina this year, and the exclusion from international play of Russia in 2022 over the country's full-scale war on neighboring Ukraine.

Reindl is the second German to be elected IIHF president after Günter Sabetzki, who held the post from 1975 to 1994.

#1135. Man United ask fans for views over new £2billion Old Trafford - indicating that original 'circus tent' design could be ditched

📅 26 Oct 2026 | other | Source

Published: 10:09 BST, 23 October 2026 | Updated: 10:39 BST, 23 October 2026

Manchester United have invited fans to have their say over the club’s new £2billion stadium in the clearest indication yet that the controversial ‘tent’ design unveiled last year is not final.

Supporters have two weeks to take part in a survey that will gather their responses on a range of topics including creating a world-class football stadium and match-day experience, protecting United’s heritage and legacy, atmosphere within the ground, the wider area around it and access to the stadium.

It follows United’s largest-ever survey of 50,000 supporters over the future of Old Trafford in September 2024, in which 52 per cent voted in favour of a new 100,000-seater stadium.

As a result, Foster + Partners were asked to design Sir Jim Ratcliffe’s ‘Wembley of the North’, and unveiled a futuristic design last year which was likened to a circus tent.

Fosters have since been formally appointed as architects for the project but Collette Roche, United’s CEO of new stadium development, said in July that the design was ‘not set in stone’.

Sources have confirmed to Daily Mail Sport that it was conceptual, and the club are now going through the actual design process which is committed to giving fans their say.

Man United's plan for a new 100,000-seat stadium may not include the controversial canopy

Announcing the launch of the survey – which will be sent to season ticket holders, cup season ticket holders, executive club members, official members and supporters who have purchased hospitality at Old Trafford within the past three years – Roche said: ‘Our proposed new stadium is one of the most significant projects Manchester United will undertake in the next 100 years, and it is vital that we shape it together with our fans.

‘Above all else, this is a football stadium built for them, so getting the details right matters.

'That’s why we are committed to thorough and transparent engagement, ensuring supporters help inform key decisions on atmosphere, affordability, accessibility and the traditions that have made Old Trafford so special for so long.

‘Our engagement with supporters since 2024 has shown there is genuine excitement about the prospect of a new stadium, but we also recognise that some fans will have important questions and concerns.

‘Listening to those views and ensuring they are shaping the design is a vital part of the process. By working together, we can create a world-class football stadium that supports success on the pitch, reflects the identity of Manchester United, and gives fans a home they can be proud of for generations to come.’

The stadium rebuild and wider regeneration project is expected to deliver around 15,000 new homes, including affordable housing, create 48,000 new jobs locally and over 90,000 nationally, and add more than £7bn a year to the UK economy.

📅 26 Oct 2026 | other | Source

The Gunners have made their worst-ever start to a Women’s Super League season and are already out of the title race, with the pressure mounting on Renee Slegers and the club struggling to match their ambition off the pitch

Want to bookmark your favourite articles and stories to read or reference later? Start your Independent Membership today.

The post-match huddle at the Emirates, designed to project an image of togetherness, unity and collective purpose, instead suggested the complete absence of any of the above. The Arsenal squad stood around in a circle appearing shell-shocked. Some senior players avoided looking each other in the eye. The season is not even two months old and Arsenal are out of the title race, a painful reality sinking in with familiar acceptance, the pressure growing on Renee Slegers.

Arsenal have made their worst ever start to a Women’s Super League season. They are without a victory in five games since beating Brighton on the opening weekend, which adds up to the club’s longest-ever winless run in the league. There were boos at the Emirates following the 1-1 draw with newly-promoted Birmingham on Saturday, a result which left the Gunners eighth in the table and already 12 points behind leaders Manchester City.

It is approaching eight years since Arsenal were last champions, a run of underperformance that goes against the club’s historic success and place at the forefront of the game’s growth. They have finished runners-up in the last two seasons but without putting up a proper challenge, ultimately paying the price for too many dropped points in the opening weeks. It was two years ago that former head coach Jonas Eidevall departed just four league games into the season, at which stage the Gunners were five points behind leaders Manchester City. As the wheels fell off and supporters turned, many of the issues the Swede faced – inconsistency, struggles in front of goal and vulnerability in defence – remain strikingly apparent now.

Yet Slegers is under pressure less than 18 months on from taking Arsenal to the greatest heights, the former Eidevall assistant whose interim spell in charge led the club to winning the Champions League with victory over dominant Barcelona. After setting the standards off the pitch, with Arsenal’s average attendance at the Emirates ranking among the highest in England, for men’s and women’s, they have stalled at the next steps. Arsenal were right to back Slegers after the Dutchwoman won the Champions League at the age of 36. But if they had to simply trust their eyes then – observing the tactical sophistication of their Champions League triumph and the response of her players during their unlikely run – the same assessment of performances now could lead to the conclusion that Arsenal are in need of fresh ideas.

Perhaps that is unfair on Slegers. In the WSL this season, Arsenal have scored just five goals from 121 shots while just 14 per cent of their shots on target have found the back of the net. But while they have been guilty of poor finishing, Slegers’ side have also continued to struggle against low-blocks. Arsenal have not only lost away games against the teams who should be their closest competitors, in champions Manchester City and rivals Chelsea at the Etihad and Stamford Bridge respectively, but Crystal Palace, Manchester United and Birmingham have found it all too easy to arrive at the Emirates, face a barrage of possession and shots, but then depart with a point. Slegers and her coaching staff had a full international break to prepare for the visit of Birmingham but then could not find a way to break them down.

Arsenal arriving back at the point they were at two years ago has brought others into the line of questioning, too. If winning the Champions League was supposed to act as the springboard then their recruitment, led by director of women’s football Clare Wheatley, was required to play a pivotal part. Arsenal have deliberately looked to lower the average age of their first-team squad. The signings of forward Olivia Smith, 22, right back Smila Holmberg, 20, and winger Lisa Baum, 19, in consecutive windows has contrasted to the departures of the long-serving Katie McCabe, Beth Mead and Lia Walti after they had all turned 30. This past summer was designed to bring further strengthening with the arrivals of established internationals like Georgia Stanway, Selina Cerci, Geraldine Reuteler, Ona Batlle and Elise De Almeida, but it is all yet to click together.

Despite the departures of McCabe and Mead this past summer and the focus on reducing the age of the first-team squad, there should not be an absence of on-field leadership from a group that still includes Kim Little, Leah Williamson, Lotte Wubben-Moy, Steph Catley and Alessia Russo. But the disconnect that felt so apparent following Saturday's draw with Birmingham, added to Arsenal’s early-season underperformance, means patience is wearing thin and frustration is building.

Defeat at Tottenham, who Arsenal have beaten in 11 of their previous 13 meetings, in Saturday’s north London derby would leave the Gunners seven points behind their neighbours. Such a position would be embarrassing enough without considering how far off the pace they would be to the current top three of Manchester City, Chelsea and the big-spending upstarts London City Lionesses.

That was not the ambition Arsenal wanted to project as they basked in the glow of the Champions League in Lisbon 16 months ago. Slegers could yet turn the ship around and salvage the season by reaching an FA Cup final or embarking on another European run, but they will be left to regret another wasted WSL campaign. As it is, the club are already running out of time to maintain the idea that they should be doing much, much more.

One regular day of LaLiga, that’s all they are asking for. It did not require fluent Spanish to understand the gravity of Real Madrid’s ‘Comunicado Oficial’ last Friday afternoon. A press release containing mention of Javier Tebas, the LaLiga president, alongside Joseph Goebbels and the “ministro de Propaganda del régimen nazi” signalled a drastic escalation in their long-running dispute with the Spanish league and that tensions with rivals Barcelona had reached a peak ahead of this weekend’s Clasico at Camp Nou.

In the statement, Real Madrid demanded Tebas to resign after he compared the club’s criticism of referees through their in-house TV channel to “propaganda techniques” used by the Nazis during the second world war. The 15-time Champions League winners called the language used by Tebas “deeply offensive” and “morally repugnant”. Tebas, in turn, issued his second lengthy social media post of the day in which he largely doubled down in accusing Real Madrid of “intimidation” and seeking to “destroy” the Spanish league.

Barcelona, unsurprisingly, also had their say as club president Joan Laporta criticised Real Madrid for “constantly attacking” LaLiga. Laporta also praised Tebas for being a “counter’ to Madrid’s accusations, which will have done little to dampen the fire building at the Bernabeu. The Madrid supporters had their say during last weekend’s home win over Sevilla, unveiling a banner that played on a LaLiga marketing slogan with the message: “It’s not football, it’s Tebas and Negreira”.

Once again, the Negreira case is likely to feature prominently as Barcelona host Real Madrid in El Clasico this weekend. Real Madrid have demanded a “full clarification” as to why payments of €7.3m (£6.5m) from Barcelona to Jose Maria Enriquez Negreira, the former vice-president of the Spanish football federation’s technical committee of referees, were made between 2001 and 2018. Earlier this month Real Madrid sent Uefa a “50,000-page” dossier of documents which they say relate to the “integrity of the competition and confidence in the refereeing system”.

Barcelona and Negreira deny wrongdoing and were cleared by Uefa investigators in 2023. Laporta said the payments related to consultancy work and providing reports on referees – but Real Madrid president Florentino Perez ramped up the pressure in May by accusing Barcelona of “stealing” seven league titles from his club. Relations between the historic rivals are usually fraught but they took a further turn when Barcelona finally withdrew from the European Super League in February, leaving Real Madrid as the only founding member of the project to remain on board.

It’s an interesting time, then, for the first Clasico of the season to roll around, particularly when Camp Nou will also welcome the return of one of its fiercest opponents: Jose Mourinho.

It’s been 15 years since Barcelona and Real Madrid’s modern rivalry arguably reached its peak: when El Clasico offered the contrast of Pep Guardiola and Jose Mourinho and their clash of styles, the two best managers in the world on the touchline as much of a draw than the best players in the world on the pitch in Lionel Messi and Cristiano Ronaldo. Mourinho would prepare his players for “war”, a tactic that will not be forgotten at Camp Nou and could be deployed again now as Real Madrid attempted to halt a Barcelona side who have scored 50 goals in their first 12 matches of the season. Hansi Flick’s team won their first 11 in a row, until Tuesday’s Champions League defeat at Paris Saint-Germain, and lead Real Madrid by six points in LaLiga. Mourinho can’t afford another defeat now.

It did not take Mourinho long on his return to Spain, however, to play into the narrative that Real Madrid have been up against something bigger than just Barcelona. Having suffered their first defeat of the season at Real Betis, Mourinho arrived at his press conference following the heated 2-1 derby defeat at Atletico Madrid holding printouts of the “two clear red cards” Diego Simeone’s team should have received in the first half. Given the attention Mourinho looked to draw towards the two incidents – a studs-up challenge from Cristian Romero on Jude Bellingham’s knee and Lee Kang-in catching Federico Valverde on his ankle – any late challenges in the Clasico which Real Madrid feel are dealt with too leniently or too strongly could be the match that sparks the dynamite.

In between the controversy, it’s easy to forget what the selling point of El Clasico really should be about: the stars. The Premier League may be the most dominant domestic competition in the world but it does not have a fixture that comes close to matching the individual talent on display when Kylian Mbappe faces Lamine Yamal and the midfield battle is decided by either Bellingham or Rodri. The prospect of Yamal enjoying a potential crowning moment at Camp Nou, a night before the Ballon d’Or ceremony in London and in front of a Real Madrid side who were left seething by Vinicius Jr missing out on the award two years ago, adds another delicious storyline into the mix. But the sharp edge to world football’s biggest club fixture is back as well.

#1123. "NAZA": Israeli military issues statement on film

📅 26 Oct 2026 | other | Source

The award-winning documentary "NAZA" continues to spark controversy in Israel. The Israeli military has now published a multi-page statement calling the film's portrayal "distorted."

When it premiered at the Venice International Film Festival in mid-September, "NAZA" received a 25-minute-long standing ovation and the Special Jury Prize. But in Israel, the documentary has fueled heated debate.

Israeli Culture Minister Miki Zohar described the work of filmmakers Rachel Szor and Yuval Abraham "treason against the state" and urged that their citizenship be revoked. Prime Minister Benjamin Netanyahu has also sharply criticized the documentary.

Now the Israel Defense Forces (IDF) has also rejected the film's allegations about its conduct during the war in Gaza. In a multi-page document, the military accuses the filmmakers of presenting a "distorted, misleading and disingenuous picture." The film, it says, is based on "substantial factual errors" resulting from "manipulative misrepresentations of reality."

The 80-minute documentary examines the Israel Defense Forces' operations during the war in Gaza. It is based on interviews conducted with 24 anonymous military and intelligence personnel, who describe internal procedures and decision-making processes involved in selecting targets in the Gaza Strip. According to the interviewees, high civilian casualty estimates were, in some cases, deliberately factored into these decisions.

The IDF rejects those claims. It says it has never planned or carried out attacks while anticipating hundreds of civilian deaths. It also disputes the film's claim that artificial intelligence selected targets without human oversight, saying people have always made decisions about targets and attacks.

The military also accuses the filmmakers of omitting important facts. According to the IDF, the film fails to show the role of military legal advisors and commanders in reviewing and authorizing attacks or the measures it says it takes to protect civilians. It also argues that the film does not adequately address the Hamas attacks on Israel on October 7, 2023, in which over 1,200 people were killed and 250 people were taken hostage.

Several human rights organizations and a UN commission have accused Israel of committing acts of genocide in Gaza. According to health authorities in Gaza and other sources, at least 74,000 Palestinians have been killed since the war began.

Speaking at the awards ceremony in Venice in mid-September, co-director Yuval Abraham said: "We believe that the denial of a crime is what helps it persist. That is why it's really, really especially important to us that Israelis watch this film."

#1122. Inside Norwich City's slump under Philippe Clement: How Belgian's 24/7 intensity is leaving players running on empty and staff mentally drained - with 'absentee landlord' Mark Attanasio adding to toxicity

In the early days of Philippe Clement’s reign at Norwich City, the Belgian worked so late into the night at the club's training ground that when he returned to his hotel, he had to plead with the staff to reopen the kitchens and bung a pizza in the oven.

Clement, described by sources as an ‘all-consuming perfectionist’, was simply too busy transforming a club that had become so toxic supporters were setting off flares outside Carrow Road and lobbing tennis balls on to the pitch.

When he arrived almost a year ago, only Sheffield Wednesday, a club hit with a 12-point deduction, sat below Norwich in the Championship. The Canaries had lost all eight home games of the season and were facing their first relegation to League One in 15 years.

And yet the 6ft 3in Clement, through what club insiders called his ‘aura’ and relentless work ethic, hauled them from the doldrums and into the play-off hunt. From the start of January to the end of the season, only Southampton won more points than Norwich.

In the summer, American billionaire owner Mark Attanasio declared that all the talk inside the club was how to ‘get the team into the top two next year’.

Only, Norwich are nowhere near the top two. They are back in the doldrums. Their 1-0 defeat by Portsmouth last weekend was their fifth in a row in the Championship, their eighth in 11 league matches this season, and left them just a point outside the relegation zone.

Philippe Clement is feeling the heat at Norwich, with his side losing eight of their 11 league matches this season

The fanbase may not be hurling tennis balls in fury again just yet but as the Norwich players completed their customary lap of appreciation around Carrow Road on Saturday, the supporters sent them back down the tunnel with chants of ‘You’re not fit to wear the shirt’ ringing in their ears.

Norwich are shipping goals at a faster rate than even under predecessor Liam Manning this season but, in trying to shore up the defence in recent weeks, it has come at the expense of goals for Clement’s side.

The Belgian's magic appears to have worn off. That round-the-clock drive, something he expects of everyone else at the club too, has stopped reaping its rewards.

Clement gave his charges one day off last December and it wasn’t Christmas Day. A week after the season ended, he had his players return for a 10-day boot camp. While other clubs gave their squad time off during the recent three-week international break, Clement’s men had just the odd day here and there.

Clement joked himself that the only time he wasn’t spending every waking second thinking about fixing Norwich’s plight, was the two hours he was hitting golf balls with his wife at the local driving range.

During the break, Clement held a meeting with the entire football department at their Colney training ground – from the data analysts, to the technical staff, to sporting director Ben Knapper – that lasted for nearly seven hours as they tried to figure out what was going wrong. He is known for not being shy in holding frank exchanges of views with his staff.

That intensity – and the demand for everyone else to buy into it – is fine and dandy when you’re winning matches. It inspires everyone around you, sweeps them up, and carries them with you. It soon starts to drag when you’re not winning anymore.

With no time for rest and recuperation, Norwich are running on empty and anyone who has watched their recent performances can see it.

Norwich players are running on empty and anyone who watches them can see it

Sporting director Ben Knapper faced chants calling for his dismissal towards the end of Norwich's loss to Portsmouth last weekend

Injuries have, again, hurt Norwich. At times last season, the Canaries were playing matches with as many as 14 senior players missing, with one fit striker and a defensive midfielder on the right wing.

It prompted the club to hire a firm to audit their practices, take advice from Liverpool and NBA teams and completely overhaul the medical department. A new head of physiotherapy and chief medical officer joined over the summer following the appointment of Dr Paul Ford from the British Olympic Association as the new performance director overseeing all elements of athlete management, including medical, performance, nutrition and psychology.

Top scorer Mo Toure, who’s bagged 16 goals from his 21 matches since joining in January, is now out too after needing surgery on a shoulder injury while winger Ali Ahmed is likely to miss most of the season with a torn ACL.

While some fear Clement's time at Carrow Road is running out, the Belgian has credit in the bank for the miracles he worked last season

While there is naturally scrutiny starting to come Clement’s way, the general feeling is that the 52-year-old has enough credit in the bank for the miracles he worked last season. Much more of the anger, instead, is being directed at sporting director Knapper, who faced chants calling for his dismissal towards the end of the Portsmouth defeat.

Knapper, the former Arsenal loan manager, is on to his fourth permanent manager in three years after inheriting then sacking David Wagner before appointing Johannes Hoff Thorup and Manning.

When he first came to the club, Knapper talked about the need to lower the age of a squad which included the likes of Shane Duffy and Ashley Barnes, but the fear is Norwich have now gone too far the other way. Only Watford average a younger starting XI in the Championship this season. It was understood the Canaries wanted an experienced winger in the summer only to spend £8million on 23-year-old Andre Brooks from Sheffield United.

The frustration, more than anything, centres on who is holding the decision-makers to account and what is the plan for supporters to get behind. Norwich are into their fifth year in the Championship since relegation from the Premier League in 2021-22 and continue to drift.

Attanasio, the long-time owner of the Milwaukee Brewers who took over from Delia Smith and her late husband Michael Wynn-Jones, is rarely at matches. There are regular Zoom meetings but he leaves the everyday running of the football side to Knapper.

A host of Norwich legends returned to Carrow Road last Saturday to pay their respects to former owner and honorary life president Wynn-Jones, who died last month aged 85. All the board and ownership group were there except for Attanasio.

Across the Pond, he was celebrating the Brewers reaching the World Series for the first time in 44 years. The Brewers have enjoyed great success due to their use of data, punching above their weight significantly as one of the smaller teams in Major League Baseball, and Attanasio wants Norwich to do the same.

To make matters worse, his son, Mike, started blocking Norwich fans on X when they sent him their thoughts on the matter. ‘He’s put in more blocks than (defender Jose) Cordoba this season,’ remarked one supporter.

This did nothing to dispel the grumbles that Norwich are in the hands of an absentee landlord.

Attanasio claims his success at the Brewers is built on his four cornerstones for how to run a club: fielding a perennially competitive team, delivering the best fan experience, remaining a leader in the community and being an organisation where people want to work.

Norwich began to charge for parking at Carrow Road this summer. They were forced to shelve plans for the stadium which would have involved 3,000 season-ticket holders moving to new seats. Redundancies towards the end of last season affected morale.

And they are, most certainly, not perennially competitive. They are, if anything, perennially treading water and find themselves doing so once again, no matter how tirelessly Clement wants everyone to keep kicking.
