#1600. Kyle Walker Issues Statement Addressing Man City Tattoo Removal Comments

📅 26 Oct 2026 | other | Source

Kyle Walker has now spoken out again after causing a stir with his initial comments relating to former club Manchester City.

The English defender, now of Championship club Burnley, spent eight years at the Etihad between 2017 and 2025. During that time, he established himself as a club legend, winning six Premier League titles, two FA Cups, four League Cups, and the Champions League.

However, his legacy, as well as those of his teammates, may well now be tarnished. This is because Man City have been found guilty of breaking the Premier League's financial rules. While City have appealed the call, big sanctions are expected, such as titles being stripped.

Simon Jordan thinks one Premier League team is 'bigger and worse offenders' than City.

Walker gave his thoughts on the guilty verdict while appearing on TalkSport this week, via ESPN. The 36-year-old has a tattoo which reads "4 in a row" – referring to City's streak of league titles from 2020-21 to 2023-24.

He was directly asked whether he would have a tattoo removed if his former side were stripped of their leagues. Walker was perhaps a little too candid, as he suggested he might have to:

"Well, I'm probably going to have to do that bit. I'll try and keep it on, but hopefully we don't get things taken away.

"It doesn't look great. Let's not hide from the fact that they've been found guilty, but they're going through an appeal.

"As a player all I can say is that all we did is go out and play our best football as a team and that I'll defend the players for the rest of my life."

It's little wonder that the comments sparked quite a strong response online, with many Man City fans upset with the comments from a former club captain. Walker, who left for Burnley in 2025 after a brief loan spell with AC Milan, has since taken to social media to address the backlash.

He distanced himself from remarks in a statement on Instagram, which read:

"Just to clear up the misleading headlines today. I've never had any intention of removing my Manchester City tattoos.

My time at City was a huge part of my career and journey as a player, and something I'll always be incredibly proud of.

"I shared some unbelievable moments with some great teammates, people at the club and of course the City fans, who supported us throughout.

"Those memories will always stay with me. As will the tattoos."

While Walker has now nailed his colours to the mast, claiming he won't touch his tattoos, it will be very interesting to see how players react if Man City are stripped of any titles. The entire appeal process is said to take up to 12 weeks, at which point we will know if punishments can be expected.

Still, it's unclear as of yet what exact sanctions would be dished out if the appeal is unsuccessful.

#1597. It is Michael Carrick's turn to go now, but who will be stupid enough to follow him? The top job at rotting Man United is a red cyanide pill at a club where nobody takes responsibility, writes IAN LADYMAN

📅 26 Oct 2026 | other | Source

Published: 09:05 BST, 22 October 2026 | Updated: 10:13 BST, 22 October 2026

So the Manchester United job has ruined Michael Carrick just like it ruined Ole Gunnar Solskjaer.

Carrick's sad, Mea culpa walk across the turf towards his club's supporters at Como's Stadio Guiseppe Sinigaglia last night bore a dismal resemblance to that made by one of his many predecessors at Vicarage Road, Watford, almost five years ago.

Solskjaer was to say goodbye to United in tears a couple of days later, a coach broken by a club so dysfunctional and a playing squad so riddled with rot that it had become impossible to manage.

And now it is Carrick's turn. Another good man on the way, another servant of the club to feel that miserable cocktail of regret, failure and resignation. Another football man with United in his veins who must look around and wonder what on earth a once great club has become.

The question now is who will be stupid enough to put their hand up to follow Carrick into a post he will surely be invited to leave before too long.

The job at Old Trafford used to be one of English football's most coveted postings. Now it merely represents a fast track to pain, suffering and failure.

Michael Carrick will surely be invited to leave the Manchester United job shortly

Only a football junkie looking for a cheap fix of glamour and status would take it. It still gives you that. Managing United will still provide a man with that quick jolt of adrenaline.

But the reality is that it is a cyanide pill coloured red. Nobody since Sir Alex Ferguson's day gets out alive. That's the reality of working at a place where if the lack of football smarts above you don't get you then the lack of heart in the dressing room surely will.

The thing about United is that the expectation will always be the same. Despite its reduced circumstances, no club expects you to win so regularly and monotonously as this one does.

The modern United is not set up to succeed. The squad is not good enough, the ownership continues to milk its revenues from within and its executive structure makes bad decisions.

Still, though, their managers are expected to win and to compete with proper, well-run football clubs like Arsenal at the top of the Premier League. It's impossible. The road back to the top of the English and European game is longer than it has ever been for a United manager but that's the remit nevertheless.

Carrick should never have been given the job last summer. The red flag comparisons with Solskjaer were so numerous, it was terrifying. But whoever succeeds him is a fool to think success can come quickly to this club in its current form.

It's a place that pays footballers who don't want to run and who talk a better game than they play. A place where even the rehabilitation of a captain like Bruno Fernandes has now been derailed by an environment where not a single person in the building bar the manager seems willing to take responsibility for failure.

To listen to Bruno Fernandes talk after last night's dismantling by Como in Italy was to be granted a snapshot of United's rotting soul. Back in the day when United did things properly on and off the field, Fernandes would have spent the following few days juggling footballs on his own in the car park. On Saturday, when United face Bournemouth, he will doubtless lead the team out wearing the captain's armband.

Eddie Howe would be a fool to go anywhere near Old Trafford in the current climate

The Premier League's latest young tyro Fabian Hurzeler of Brighton should avoid United

In terms of their future, United are known to have feelers out for replacement candidates. The former German national team and Bayern Munich coach Julian Nagelsmann is thought to be near the top of a provisional list. Antonio Conte – once of Tottenham and Chelsea – would like to be on it.

Both will back themselves to be the man to work the miracle simply because managers always do. It's the way they are made. The job would play to Conte's ego and he would come with nothing to lose. He would also fail, and do so quickly, but it hasn't stopped United making bad appointments before.

But as for men like Nagelsmann, Eddie Howe and the Premier League's latest young tyro Fabian Hurzeler of Brighton, they would be fools to go anywhere near Old Trafford.

Howe may be a future England coach. Why jeopardise your chances? Hurzeler is a former Bayern Munich B team player. They, along with the rest of the Bundesliga, will be watching the 33-year-old. Why risk a reputation on a basket case club? All three are coaches whose next posting will determine their career trajectory. They need to go somewhere that provides them with a platform to develop, grow and succeed. They need foundations. United presents only a trapdoor without a latch.

In the 13 and a half years since Sir Alex Ferguson left, seven have walked through the door thinking they had reached the top of the mountain. Seven have crawled out again on their knees.

This is what United does to people these days. There are some with Carrick's best interests at heart who feared for him when he took the job. They had seen the evidence and followed the trends.

Bruno Fernandes captains a club where not a single person bar the manager seems willing to take responsibility for failure

What will come of him is painful to consider. Once he has done the rounds of the podcasts and told his stories of an environment where self-harm has become a culture, he will look down at his feet and see a career in management lying in ruins.

He is not alone in that. Others, meanwhile, count their blessings of a lucky escape.

The England manager Thomas Tuchel, for example, spoke to United more than once when they looked to replace Erik ten Hag in the summer of 2024. He ultimately withdrew from the process.

United failed the sniff test. He didn't like the rather unpredictable nature of what he saw. And this from a bloke who once worked at Chelsea.

#1596. Vast new fossil record challenges story of Earth’s ‘big five’ mass extinctions

Large language models (LLMs), such as the one underlying OpenAI’s ChatGPT, are trained to recognize patterns in written texts, helping them to choose a statistically probable word to complete a sentence. Palaeontologists have now trained a similar model on fossil records instead of words, then used it to reconstruct a timeline of changes in Earth’s biodiversity over hundreds of millions of years in unprecedented detail.

This big-data approach to interpreting the rock record — the field known as stratigraphy — could spur a rethink of major upheavals in the history of life, the model’s developers say. For instance, the model suggests that three of the ‘big five’ mass-extinction events were gradual declines in biodiversity, rather than the sudden cataclysms described by other reconstructions.

Supercomputer scours fossil record for Earth’s hidden extinctions

Supercomputer scours fossil record for Earth’s hidden extinctions

The model is “a real revolution for studying stratigraphy and past diversity change”, says Shuzhong Shen, a palaeontologist at Nanjing University in China, who presented the research at a meeting of the Geological Society of America in Denver, Colorado, on 12 October.

Other researchers say that although the model’s reconstruction — called OneStrata — seems to be the most detailed global record so far, scientists already have high-resolution reconstructions of changes in biodiversity at specific locations, including many that shed light on mass extinctions.

“We could do some extra things with it, but I don’t know if it would be Earth shattering,” says Matthew Clapham, a palaeontologist at the University of California, Santa Cruz.

The history of life is written in the fossil record. Researchers can reconstruct the grand story of adaptation and extinction across Earth’s history by marking the depth at which species occur in layers of sedimentary rock, called strata; depth corresponds to when a fossil was deposited in geological time. However, it is a steep computational problem to combine sequences from many different sites, involving tens of thousands of species, into a single coherent timeline of changing biodiversity.

To simplify this task, stratigraphers divide sequences of fossils for each species into time intervals of millions of years. This makes it easier to assemble many sequences into a unified whole. But it produces a blurry view, obscuring changes in biodiversity that occur over shorter time intervals.

For the past two decades, Shen and his colleagues have worked to get a sharper picture by compiling a huge data set of fossils and other geological information called OneStratigraphy. This includes data on the depth of millions of fossil specimens in sequences of rock from tens of thousands of sites around the world.

In 2020, Shen and his colleagues ran an algorithmic method on a supercomputer to piece together thousands of these sequences, enabling them to estimate when a species appeared and went extinct with a resolution of 26,000 years — much higher than the resolution of previous estimates. They could then reconstruct a high-resolution timeline for 11,000 marine species over about 300 million years1. However, including more species at shorter intervals requires an impractical amount of computational resources.

Biodiversity theory backed by island bird data

Biodiversity theory backed by island bird data

To get around that computational limit, Shen’s colleagues at the Zhejiang Lab — an artificial-intelligence-focused research institution in Hangzhou, China — used data from OneStratigraphy to train a machine-learning model on statistical patterns in the fossil record. Just as an LLM uses its training to predict the next word in a sentence, the OneStrata model uses those patterns to predict when a particular fossil is most likely to have been deposited.

#1592. Milan condemn ‘unacceptable’ racist abuse of Maignan after Bournemouth red card

📅 26 Oct 2026 | other | Source

Milan have condemned the racist abuse aimed at Mike Maignan on social media following his red card in Thursday’s 3-0 Europa League defeat at Bournemouth.

According to MilanPress, the club released an official statement on Friday afternoon, after the goalkeeper was targeted online in the hours after the game.

“Following yesterday’s match against Bournemouth, Mike Maignan was the target of serious and unacceptable racist insults on social media,” the statement read. “AC Milan firmly condemns every form of racism and discrimination, which have no place on or off the pitch. The club expresses its full solidarity and support to Mike.”

It is not the first time Maignan has suffered racist abuse. In January 2024 he led his teammates off the pitch at Udine after being targeted from the stands, briefly halting the game.

The club also appealed to supporters frustrated by Thursday’s result to direct that frustration properly.

“We must remain united, as Milanisti and as Italian football,” the statement continued. “We all want to win, but we must do it the right way, with dignity, mutual respect and class.”

#1591. 'Arsenal Didn't Want Me Because I Was Chubby - Now I Could Win the 2026 Ballon d'Or'

📅 26 Oct 2026 | other | Source

In football, there is no greater individual prize than the Ballon d'Or. The award is given out every year to recognise the very best player on the planet.

To even be in the conversation, a footballer needs to have had an incredibly impressive past 12 months. It's funny to think, then, that one of the favourites in the 2026 Ballon d'Or hasn't always looked destined for greatness.

In fact, for a long time it didn't look as though they'd make it at the elite level. Especially as a youngster, there were major doubts over their talents, with Arsenal famously rejecting them. Many years on, though, and they may well be the best footballer in the world.

The player in question is Harry Kane. Now at Bayern Munich, the Englishman is in the form of his life. In 2025/26, he scored 61 goals in 51 games for the German giants, while also claiming seven assists as the club won both the Bundesliga and DFB-Pokal.

He then scored six times for England at the World Cup, extending his record as the Three Lions' all-time top-scorer. Amid that scintillating form, there are many who feel as though Kane should lift the 2026 Ballon d'Or. The 33-year-old has spoken about his chances to the press ahead of the ceremony, noting:

“I think my wife would prefer to know and my family … it’s very stressful for everyone. I can see why they don’t tell people. They want everyone to go, all the big names, and there will be more people watching it if they don’t know so I can understand why.

“From my point of view, I’d love to know to just kind of see and not have to wait throughout the whole night. But it is their decision, I can’t control that. We’ll have to just enjoy it and try not to stress out too much.

“I try not to think about it. ­Obviously it is hard not to. There are always rumours and stuff flying around so you try to stay away from it.”

There's no shock these days to see Kane's name among the Ballon d'Or favourites, but few of his coaches would have predicted that when he was starting out as a youngster. When he was just eight years old, the striker was signed by Arsenal from local club Ridgeway Rovers.

Kane, though, lasted just 12 months with the Gunners. Former academy director for the Gunners Liam Brady explained that the North London club were not convinced that he had the right physical profile to make it as an elite footballer. He recalled:

"He was a bit chubby, he wasn’t very athletic but we made a mistake.

"Tottenham sent him out on loan to lower-division clubs three or four times as well. But through his determination he has carved out an amazing career and he deserves it. He has a character that drives him to improve all the time."

It's true that even Tottenham took convincing before they were ready to give him a shot at senior football. Spurs loaned him out to the likes of Leyton Orient, Millwall, Norwich, and Leicester City, but he failed to really deliver anything that special during that time.

Even so, Kane would eventually get a shot as Spurs, really breaking through in 2014/15 and then winning PFA Young Player of the Year. From there, he never looked back, as he went on to score a club-record 280 for Tottenham before moving to Bayern in 2023.

Harry Kane is England's greatest-ever goalscorer, but Arsenal released him as they felt two strikers had more potential.

Some of Kane's best career performances have come against Arsenal, and the player has even admitted that the rejection definitely helped light a fire within. He told The Players' Tribune:

"It might sound ridiculous. I was only eight when they let me go, but every time we played them, I thought, ‘Alright, we’ll see who’s right and who’s wrong’.

"Looking back on it now, [being released by Arsenal] was probably the best thing that ever happened to me because it gave me a drive that wasn’t there before."

Even since leaving Tottenham, Kane has still managed to get some joy out of facing Arsenal. In four Champions League meetings with the Gunners since his move to Germany in 2023, he's scored on two occasions, while also getting one assist.

After bagging against the Gunners in October 2026, he could help but joke at the club's expense: "I always seem to get a penalty against Arsenal." Kane has scored nine spot-kicks against his old North London rivals in 23 games, never missing once.

Talk about a player coming back to haunt you.
