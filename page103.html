#1617. How Bournemouth became favourites to beat AC Milan: From copying the Italians' famous kit to just £100,000 saving them from extinction... now the Cherries are ready to beat their idols

📅 26 Oct 2026 | other | Source

Published: 07:00 BST, 22 October 2026 | Updated: 08:34 BST, 22 October 2026

Another club might have ditched the self-deprecation by now, but Bournemouth are a conscious bunch. They know the depths from which they rose to reach this European stage and so their social media manager could not resist adding a note when advertising their next home fixture.

‘AC Milan (H)… still feels a bit mad typing that,’ the admin tweeted. While Bournemouth are now an accomplished Premier League club, they recognise their history and respect that this is a Europa League fixture at the Vitality Stadium which will carry no shortage of nods.

There is the nod to Milan themselves with Bournemouth’s red-and-black striped kit adopted in 1971. That rebrand was inspired by the Italian giants’ swashbuckling greatness, while they also added ‘AFC’ to the beginning of their name to ensure they would appear first in alphabetical lists.

There is the nod to how as recently as 2008, they were starting their season in League Two with a 17-point deduction. Bournemouth’s supporters unveiled an enormous banner before their last Europa League home game against Sturm Graz which read ‘Minus 17 to European dreams’ as the DJ pressed play on Andy Williams’s The Impossible Dream. We can expect to see more references to where they came from before this marquee showdown with Milan, a club that have seven European Cup/Champions League trophies in their cabinet.

But the biggest nod is the literal one provided by Jeff Mostyn while standing at the back of a room. As detailed by Mostyn, who wrote a memoir called The Chairman: The Man Who Saved AFC Bournemouth, he led the takeover of the club in 2007 at a time when the club was flirting with ceasing to exist.

Mostyn’s lawyers did their due diligence at the time, and, in his own words, advised him to get back in the car and drive as fast as he could out of Bournemouth.

Yet Mostyn saw a club in need. There was a famous moment when administrator Gerald Krasner conducted a press conference. He had warned Mostyn beforehand that at some stage, he would ask him the question whether he would fund the additional £100,000 required to save Bournemouth from liquidation. It would then be over to him to either nod, or shake his head. The room turned to Mostyn, who nodded, his heart ruling his head in that moment.

Were it not for Mostyn and that nod, Bournemouth would not be where they are today, as a Premier League club about to face Milan in a European fixture.

It is a clash worth celebrating because of that. It allows those supporters of sides lower down the pyramid to dream themselves. Yet once Marco Rose's players are on the pitch, Bournemouth should not see this as a ceremonial clash.

They have snapped out of their Premier League slumber, finally securing their first win under Rose by beating Sunderland on Sunday. Milan are unbeaten in Serie A, and managed by Ruben Amorim, formerly of Manchester United, but the bookmakers have Bournemouth as favourites to win amid all the romanticism. And then they visit a troubled United on Sunday.

They are perfectly capable of competing, and winning. Bournemouth already carry a perfect record in the Europa League, having won away at Real Sociedad and at home against Sturm Graz. Adam Smith, who first joined the club on loan in 2010 when they were in League One, set up Evanilson for their opener in that latter victory.

Fifteen years ago on this night, Bournemouth were still toiling in football's third tier, losing at home to Bury. That was during a six-game winless streak which included being beaten by Hartlepool, and drawing with Tranmere, Rochdale, MK Dons and Colchester.

Bournemouth’s mayor, Roberto Rocca, was not living here then. He moved to the town 13 years ago as a Milan-born Italian. Yet he is now backing his local club.

‘Milan by birth, Bournemouth by choice,’ Rocca said, and there is every chance that he will have bragging rights over his Milan-supporting family members come full-time. They have endured the dark days, but this will be up there with Bournemouth’s best ones.

Scientists say long-term funding makes it easier to pursue high-risk research. Credit: Weiquan Lin/Getty

China has selected its first group of young researchers to receive funding for up to 15 years, in one of the longest research grants on offer to scientists globally.

Most research funding is awarded in grants that last for five years or less, says Nicole White, a statistician at Queensland University of Technology in Brisbane, Australia. But short funding cycles favour projects that are able to deliver results quickly, which can disadvantage high-risk but potentially ground-breaking research and cut promising work short before its impact is fully realized, she says. “Some of the most impactful scientific discoveries have taken decades, not years, to achieve,” White adds.

China boosts prestigious grants for young scientists — will it ease competition?

China boosts prestigious grants for young scientists — will it ease competition?

Dou Xiankang, president of the National Natural Science Foundation of China (NSFC), described the agency’s latest initiative at a forum held by the New Cornerstone Science Foundation in Shenzhen on 19 September. He said that 44 researchers under the age of 35 (women under 38) had been chosen, including 9 from outside China, as part of the National Distinguished Young Scientists Fund. Their names and projects have not been released.

At the forum, Dou said the 15-year grants would work in a “five-plus-five-plus-five arrangement”. That means, recipients whose initial five-year grants were about to end could be considered for a second five years and after ten years, another portion would be selected for a third five years of funding.

“During those 15 years, we will not conduct any short-cycle, short-term target assessments. We will assess only one thing: whether you are there in the laboratory,” Dou told the forum.

The NSFC first announced plans to create 15-year grants in 2023, stating that initial 5-year grants would be worth 4 million yuan (US$596,000), they would then double to 8 million yuan for the second 5 years, and then double again to 16 million yuan for the final 5 years.

The NSFC has been ramping up funding for young researchers. Earlier this year, it pledged to increase the number of grants it provides for early-career scientists by 50%, adding 12,000 projects through its Young Scientists Fund.

#1611. Who are Ravenna? Everything you need to know about Ronaldinho’s Serie C club

📅 26 Oct 2026 | other | Source

Ronaldinho will make his long-awaited Ravenna debut in the Serie C derby against Forlì on November 15, but who exactly are the club bringing the 46-year-old Brazilian back to competitive football?

According to the club’s announcement, the former Ballon d’Or winner will take to the field at the Stadio Bruno Benelli at 17:30 local time. It will be his first competitive match since leaving Fluminense in 2015. Ravenna announced his signing in June, unveiled him in Miami, and then presented him to around 6,000 fans at a beachside event in Marina di Ravenna in August.

Ronaldinho also holds a minority stake in the club. According to Goal, the dream is for him to score the final goal of his career at the Benelli, while also launching his R10 brand. A limited-edition Ravenna x Ronaldinho shirt is on sale for €129.

Founded in 1913, they are based in the Emilia-Romagna city better known for its Byzantine mosaics and Adriatic beaches. The club spent several seasons in Serie B in the 1990s and 2000s, but went bankrupt in 2012 and had to rebuild from the amateur leagues before working their way back to Serie C.

The president is Ignazio Cipriani, of the famous Cipriani restaurant and hospitality family. Former Milan sporting director Ariedo Braida is vice-president.

The coach is Andrea Mandorlini, a Ravenna native and former Inter defender who won the Scudetto in 1989. As a coach, he is best known for taking Hellas Verona from the third tier to Serie A. He took charge in February 2026 after Marco Marchionni was sacked, and was kept on for this season. His son Davide is the club’s sporting director.

Ravenna make no secret of their ambition to reach Serie B, and they are in the race. After 10 games they are second in Serie C Group B with 20 points from six wins, two draws and two defeats.

However, leaders Spezia, who were relegated from Serie B, have already opened up an eight-point gap. They won 1-0 at the Benelli last Sunday. Grosseto are a point behind Ravenna in third.

Only the group winners earn automatic promotion to Serie B, with the rest of the top places going into the promotion playoffs.

Ronaldinho’s debut opponents Forlì, from the neighbouring city, sit 14th. The derby looks certain to be the most watched game in Serie C history, though Mandorlini will decide how many minutes the Brazilian gets.
