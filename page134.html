#1000. Capcom data breach confirmed: More than 11 million accounts impacted

📅 26 Oct 2026 | other | Source

Do you play video games like Street Fighter, Resident Evil, or Monster Hunter online? If so, you probably have a Capcom ID — and you should know that there has been a major data breach that has affected millions of accounts.

Capcom, the developer of the aforementioned titles, posted a notice on Thursday informing users of a major data breach affecting more than 11 million accounts.

"Capcom Co., Ltd. has determined that certain customer information associated with CAPCOM ID, an online membership account service operated by the company, was subject to unauthorized access by a third party," the company said in a statement. "Capcom sincerely apologizes for the complications and concern this has caused its customers and other stakeholders."

Data breaches are seemingly becoming a regular occurrence, which may result in some becoming desensitized to this news. However, the specific information Capcom has shared should be concerning to those with a Capcom ID, as the company has confirmed that some accounts have experienced unauthorized changes due to the breach.

According to Capcom, the company first noticed suspicious activity on Capcom IDs on Oct. 19, then began blocking access and opened an investigation. Capcom's investigation found that approximately 11,774,247 accounts were impacted in a data breach. The account information exposed to the unauthorized party includes email address records, the date and time an account was created, the total number of logins, the date and time of the last login, and the IP address used for that last login.

Of those more than 11.7 million accounts, 43 had their date of birth, gender, country/region, and nickname affected, and 196 had unauthorized changes to their Capcom ID login password.

Capcom says it has already reset the passwords associated with those 196 accounts.

One positive piece of news is that Capcom says it has yet to find any evidence that the passwords originally set by its users were leaked in the data breach. In addition, Capcom shared that it does not appear that its games were affected in the data breach.

While Capcom continues its investigation, it is warning its users to be wary of phishing emails that weaponize data leaked in the breach.

"If you receive any suspicious email or message, please exercise caution and refrain from accessing any URLs contained therein or opening any attached files," Capcom said in its breach notice.

#998. Nobel Prize in chemistry goes to Henri B. Kagan and Kenso Soai

📅 26 Oct 2026 | other | Source

Henri B. Kagan and Kenso Soai won the Nobel Prize in chemistry for their groundbreaking discoveries related to the origin of chirality and life's building blocks.

The Nobel Prize in chemistry on Wednesday was awarded to Henri B. Kagan and Kenso Soai "for the discovery of non-linear effects and autocatalysis in asymmetric organic synthesis."

Kagan, who is 95 years old and was born in France, is affiliated with France’s Université Paris-Sud, and Soai, who is 76 years old and was born in Japan, is based at the Tokyo University of Science.

The scientists share the Nobel Prize for answering longstanding questions in chemistry about life's origins.

Specifically, the duo solved an age-old mystery that has long puzzled chemists: some molecules, like amino acids, exist as two variants that are mirror images of one another, but just one of the variants out of the two is found in living organisms.

The two answered questions about why life chooses one variant over another.

The Royal Swedish Academy of Sciences announced the winner in Stockholm.

In chemistry, life's building blocks are what scientists call homochiral, after the Greek words for "same" and "hand."

The Nobel Committee said the chemistry prize recognizes discoveries that have enabled chemists to drive chemical reactions that lead to homochirality.

"Henri Kagan and Kenso Soai have provided a solution to a chemical mystery that is over a century old: how homochirality can emerge spontaneously," Heiner Linke, chair of the Nobel Committee for Chemistry, said in a news release. "The chemical reactions they have developed are spectacular."

The duo's discoveries, the news release said, "have been decisive for chemists who design reactions for the manufacture of pharmaceuticals."

#995. Do Manchester City have any grounds to win their appeal?

📅 26 Oct 2026 | other | Source

BBC Sport's Dale Johnson, Dan Roan, Laura Scott and Sami Mokbel assess Manchester City's position

Manchester City face "substantial hurdles" if they are to have any chance of winning their appeal after being found guilty of breaking the Premier League's financial rules, a leading lawyer has told BBC Sport.

City have been found guilty of all charges related to breaches of the Premier League's financial rules between the 2009-10 and 2017-18 seasons, and creating "sham contracts" to inflate sponsorship deals by Â£830m.

The club have said they are "innocent of the accusations made by the Premier League" and they have "irrefutable evidence" the ruling "contains clear material errors, of law, principle and fact, and is unsafe".

In their appeal, City will argue the payments were made by the United Arab Emirates government as a shareholder of the sponsors, while there are inconsistencies in the Commission's judgement.

Therefore, City say the sponsorship amounts were legitimate and not topped up by the owners.

James Severn, Member of the London Solicitor Litigation Association (LSLA) and Partner at Penningtons Manches Cooper, told BBC Sport the appeal is "not simply an opportunity to invite a new panel" to get the verdict overturned.

"City will need to identify a material error of law, principle or fact, or serious procedural unfairness, which materially affected the Commission's decision," Severn said.

"The Appeal Board nevertheless has broad powers to allow or dismiss the appeal, vary the decision or make any other order it considers appropriate."

Manchester City have been sponsored by Etihad since May 2009

Etihad Airways, one of City's principal sponsors at the centre of the case, had previously stated that the Abu Dhabi government had not paid for its sponsorship of Manchester City.

That came in court documents as part of a US investigation in New York in 2015.

City will argue this does not have the full context of the situation and is not proof they have been inconsistent with evidence.

The club are adamant government documents fully support their case - but, Severn said, if these were already available to the Commission they may not be much use in an appeal.

"The bank statements, government records and contemporaneous payment documents could be powerful evidence if they establish a clear audit trail showing the disputed funds came from the Abu Dhabi government rather than the owners," Severn added.

"However, if that evidence was already before the Commission, which nevertheless concluded that the arrangements were a sham, City will face an uphill struggle to demonstrate the finding was materially wrong."

Shortly before City's appeal was confirmed, Etihad Airways issued a statement saying it had never been "given the opportunity by the Premier League to be represented, to provide relevant information, or to ensure the accuracy and fairness of information relied upon".

Severn said Etihad Airways' complaint "does not, of itself, establish procedural unfairness".

"Etihad was not the respondent and is unlikely to have had an independent right to participate in the proceedings," Severn said. "It is likely to have been open to either party to obtain and present such evidence to the Commission.

"If the Appeal Board is prepared to consider new evidence from Etihad, it is likely to carry greater weight if supported by contemporaneous documents."

#991. PHP vulnerability: PHP security issues today explained

📅 26 Oct 2026 | other | Source

Updated on Thursday, Oct. 22 at 12:25 p.m. ET — The PHP security issues known as PHP2Shell sent the cybersecurity world into a frenzy yesterday, and now we have some data on the fallout.

VirusTotal, a cybersecurity tool owned by Google, reported that there are about 5.1 million IP addresses running PHP, showing just how widespread the vulnerability is.

Cogent Security, an agentic AI cybersecurity company, released data on how the issue affected big companies. Cogent reported that the average Fortune 500 company had 2,266 affected assets, with an average of 16 assets demanding immediate action. As of 8 p.m. ET on Wednesday, Cogent reported that less than 1 percent of affected assets had been patched.

Cloudflare CEO Matthew Prince reported a surge in attempts to exploit PHP2Shell across the company’s network: thousands in the 10 hours before the public announcement, tens of thousands within 45 minutes afterward, and hundreds of thousands within two hours. By the time of his post, the count had reached millions.

PHP2Shell caused outages across the web, including at Wikipedia, and many government, gaming, and business websites required emergency maintenance. By Thursday morning, most services had been restored.

The PHP Foundation, the non-profit team behind the PHP coding language, released emergency security updates on Wednesday to fix two critical vulnerabilities, a day after details about the flaws leaked online.

The UK’s National Cyber Security Centre (NCSC), the Prime Minister of Japan, and the U.S. National Security Agency all urged organizations to install the fixes immediately.

The newly exposed PHP vulnerability led to a number of outages on Wednesday, as webmasters around the world scrambled to update their websites.

PHP is a programming language that runs on web servers. When you load a webpage, PHP often runs behind the scenes, retrieving data from databases and building the page before it reaches your browser. BleepingComputer notes that it powers many websites, including content management systems, online stores, and custom web services. According to the NCSC, PHP is also built into a range of other products.

The NCSC says the two flaws, tracked as CVE-2026-109618 and CVE-2026-109619, could allow an attacker to run their own commands on a PHP-based server without logging in. This is called remote code execution, and it’s one of the most serious kinds of security bugs.

According to the NCSC, an attacker could overload PHP’s memory to corrupt it, then, under certain conditions, use that corruption to take control of the server. One of the flaws involves how PHP processes form submissions and file uploads.

PHP rated each flaw 9.2 out of 10 on CVSS, the industry’s standard severity scale. Security firm watchTowr described them as a perfect 10, BleepingComputer reported, and so did an anonymous Hacker News post that has since been deleted. Researchers have started calling the bugs "PHP2SHELL."

The fixes were originally planned for a coordinated release later this week, so companies would have time to prepare. Then, information about the flaws leaked early. Speaking to BleepingComputer, watchTowr CEO Benjamin Harris said the vulnerabilities are "indeed real" and affect PHP versions 5 through 8. The firm said it had reproduced one of them.

PHP moved its release up to 10 a.m. ET Wednesday. As of Tuesday, watchTowr said it hadn’t seen anyone exploiting the flaws. By Wednesday, Cloudflare CTO Dane Knecht said on X that the company was seeing early attacks, according to BleepingComputer.

The NCSC says organizations running PHP 5 or later are affected and should update to the latest fixed version. Anyone still using an older, unsupported version should move to a supported one.

Wikipedia went down for a time on Wednesday, and the Wikimedia Foundation, the nonprofit that runs the site, confirmed to Mashable that the outage was tied to the PHP vulnerabilities.

In a statement to Mashable through email, the foundation said it is working to address the flaws in PHP, which it described as the programming language behind most wikis, including its own projects. "This issue affects all known versions of PHP, and is not specific to the Wikimedia Foundation," the statement said.

The foundation added that all of its PHP-based systems are affected, along with volunteer-run systems that use PHP. It has also taken several other systems offline as a precaution until they can be safely patched. Wikipedia was back up as of this writing.

If you only browse the web, there’s nothing for you to install. The fix is up to the people who run websites.

If you run a website, blog, or online store, check whether it uses PHP and make sure it’s up to date. If a hosting company manages your site, contact them to confirm they’ve applied the patch.

Taking systems offline if they can’t be fixed quickly.

More than 700,000 Brazilians died during the pandemic. Now his son, Flávio, is standing against the 80-year-old incumbent leftwing president, Luiz Inácio Lula da Silva.

“I didn’t just think of [my grandmother] when I gave that interview, I think about her and miss her every single day,” Carlinha tells Moving the Goalposts. “I felt relieved after talking about my personal opinion in such an important moment of Brazilian democracy. I’m a journalism student and I know the impact if the right wins the elections.”

Carlinha is one of several female football players who have used social media and their visibility to speak up before and during the presidential election, which will come to an end on Sunday with a second-round vote. The father of the Bolsonaro dynasty is currently under “humanitarian house arrest”, serving a 27-year sentence for a coup attempt.

Carlinha’s club and international teammate, Tayla Carolina, went viral the weekend after discussing her family’s background and how public policies implemented during Lula’s government were important for her mother and grandmother, who have worked as cleaners throughout their lives and have since passed. “I’m from a very humble family, I’m a Black woman. I’ve studied in public schools and used the national healthcare system … my mum, dona Dora, worked for over a decade for someone who never paid her labour rights … and she worked until she couldn’t walk any more due to the cancer that took her from us. She never saw me play because she never had the time, she never rested,” she wrote. Her story resonates with many Brazilians who fought for shorter working weeks, approved earlier this week in congress.

Tayla elaborates her point: “I’m from a country where most of the people are poor but the country is not seen as poor. It shows there are huge inequalities, which come from exploitation. Years have passed and there’s still a lot of inequality and I come from it … as a public figure, and someone who has lived similar lives to the majority of Brazilians, I feel the responsibility to speak up.”

The player organisation Fifpro has just released a new study showing that nearly half of female football players think of early retirement owing to the psychological and financial pressures of the women’s game (800 professional players took part). With the Women’s World Cup being staged in Brazil next year both Carlinha and Tayla feel the election will have a huge impact on the game going forward.

“There is one side that makes it quite clear how they feel about women’s rights,” says Carlinha, referring to, among other things, politicians such as Eduarda Campopiano, one of the most voted members of the São Paulo Assembly, who calls herself an “enemy of feminists” and has said she does not believe in femicide – that women and girls can be killed because they are women. Tayla also feels the need for policies to support the women’s game at a crucial moment. “We still need government incentives, so it may not be immediate, but the game will be affected, positively or negatively, depending on how the elections go.”

Lula approved the World Cup law in June 2026, which regulates many aspects of the tournament, including how ministries, the Brazilian FA (CBF), state federations and clubs will commit to developing a legacy and supporting the women’s game in the country. The legacy plan, divided into four strands, proposes supporting access and permanence of girls and women in football, developing and professionalising the game, fighting gender- and racial-based discrimination, and enhancing the economic and cultural aspects of women’s football in Brazil. The commitment to the tournament is stated in Lula’s government plan. Bolsonaro’s proposals for women’s sport do not mention the World Cup; instead, he proposes “ensuring that women’s categories, from youth to elite, are played by athletes of the female sex, protecting women and tournaments from ideological agendas”.

Brazilian football, men’s and women’s, has been heavily politicised for decades, and the rise of social media has led to an increase in players publicly supporting their political candidates. In the men’s game, the far right is often championed by the Santos and Brazil forward Neymar, who has showed support for the Bolsonaro family on many occasions. Tottenham’s Richarlison, meanwhile, is positioned on the left and this week accused Flávio Bolsonaro of “taking possession” of an island he says he bought a few years back.

The country’s female players do not have the same number of followers on social media and cannot influence a big audience in the same way as the men. However, it is true that the result of the election is likely to have more of an impact on the women’s game than on the men’s.

Kely Nascimento, the daughter of late football legend Pelé and founder and executive director of the Nascimento Foundation, says: “Female players, female athletes, they don’t have the protection of wealth. What feeds the brilliance of Brazilian football are the players, and they mostly come from communities, favelas and other places that are going to be hugely affected by these elections. Women are more affected by all this, right?”

Carlinha, who played in one of the two recent friendlies against Argentina, says playing for the national team at home is “one of the best feelings ever”. At both stadiums, in Porto Alegre and Recife, the crowd predominantly chanted in support of Lula, which could be heard over the TV commentary during the broadcast.

No topic is too small or too big for us to cover as we deliver a twice-weekly roundup of the wonderful world of women’s football

The politicisation of women’s football is not new, as Brazilian women’s football historian and author Aira Bonfim explains. “This is a hundred-year-old history, because we have episodes of women playing football in Brazil since 1915, and the women’s game reveals different perspectives of gender inequalities, racial inequalities, class, territory, which will affect the progress of the women’s game,” she says. “By knowing women’s football history, we can look at episodes which are equally as political, such as the ban on the women’s game [between 1941 and 1979], the persecution of women, and lies that were told so that female players would give up on it.”

One of the pieces of history that she has uncovered in her research became a short film, which debuted earlier this month at the Rio Film Festival, The House of Dona Carlota, produced by Mymama Entertainment and directed by Lili Fialho and Bruna Linzmeyer, which tells the story of a woman who was arrested and persecuted because she led a women’s football team, Primavera Atlético Clube.

It shows women’s football as a space of socialisation, sport and resistance to the Estado Novo dictatorship in the late 1930s and early 1940s. The women’s game was banned in Brazil for decades, and the World Cup in 2027 is where many hope its renewed development will be consolidated.

“What’s at risk [in this election] is a waste of all of the human investments in building social legacy plans. There is also the risk of dismantling all political communication that has been done to support [the World Cup] through the ministries, cities and state governments. If you elect someone who did not make any commitments towards it, who did not commit to the World Cup or what it represents to women, it can be a huge loss,” Bonfim says. “Just the fact that during Jair Bolsonaro’s government he removed the status of the ministry of sports reveals how he feels about the sector.”

The hosting of a Women’s World Cup is key in developing public policies and attracting investment and support. As Nascimento says: “There are girls all over Brazil for whom football is not just their dream, but it’s also how they get out of very awful situations. And there are amazing people doing God’s work all over Brazil just to get girls to play, to be educated in another way … for some reason, Brazil just makes football magic, and that’s not gender-specific. And there’s an enormous Brazil that is being ignored and taken advantage of.”

There are many reasons for the international community to look closer at the second round of Brazil’s elections on Sunday, from how it can affect climate change, foreign policy and financial markets to many other issues. Women’s football, and all it represents, is one of them.

WEE1 phosphorylates and inhibits the activity of cyclin-dependent kinases to prevent mitotic entry and establish a G2 DNA damage checkpoint1,2,3. Consequently, WEE1 inhibitors have been developed to be synthetically lethal in TP53-mutated tumours that become reliant on this WEE1-mediated checkpoint, and such inhibitors are undergoing clinical trials. However, WEE1 inhibitors also have efficacy in TP53-wild-type tumours, and many trials have been terminated owing to high levels of toxic side effects, suggesting that WEE1 has unknown functions. Here we show that WEE1 promotes cell cycle re-entry from quiescence (G0) by opposing the activity of the E3 ubiquitin ligase, anaphase-promoting complex–cyclosome bound to CDH1 (APC/CCDH1). WEE1 phosphorylates CDH1 (FZR1) at key residues that mediate the interaction between CDH1 and APC/C. Cells with loss of function of WEE1 during G0/G1 have delayed S phase entry, impaired G1/S transition, abnormal S phase accumulation of cyclin-dependent kinase inhibitor p21Cip1/Kip1 and a p21-dependent G2 arrest. Reduced expression of APC/CCDH1 or p21 renders cells more sensitive to acute WEE1 inhibition, and both pathways are downregulated in cells with acquired WEE1 inhibitor resistance. These results reveal a mechanism of cell cycle control that has implications for how WEE1 inhibitors should be used in the clinic.

Cyclin-dependent kinases (CDKs) control passage through the eukaryotic cell cycle. WEE1 (ref. 4) is a tyrosine kinase that inhibits CDK1 by phosphorylating Tyr15 (refs. 1,2,3). Late in G2, CDC25 phosphatases dephosphorylate Tyr15, promoting mitotic entry1,5,6,7,8. This mechanism forms a G2 DNA damage checkpoint, which becomes vital in TP53-mutated cancer cells9. Thus, WEE1 inhibitors (WEE1i) have been developed to be synthetically lethal in TP53-mutated cancers and are being trialled across several indications10,11. However, toxic side effects of unknown origin11 have led to trial suspensions. WEE1 also inhibits CDK2, a key driver of G1/S progression and proliferative commitment. Consistently, non-phosphorylatable CDK2T14A;Y15F knock-in mutant mice and human colorectal cancer cells enter S phase faster when returning to the cycle from quiescence12,13. However, no direct cellular evidence of the impact of WEE1 on CDK2 is available14, and exogenous WEE1 expression in IMR90 human fibroblasts has no effect on G1/S timing15. Therefore, we sought to clarify the function of WEE1 in early cell cycle control. We demonstrate a role for WEE1 in suppressing the anaphase-promoting complex–cyclosome bound to CDH1 (APC/CCDH1), a key antagonist of G1/S progression, to promote timely cell cycle re-entry following release from quiescence. We identify CDH1 (FZR1) as a WEE1 substrate relevant to cell cycle control. Our findings shed light on the pleiotropic impact of WEE1i in cells.

To understand the potential roles of WEE1 in regulation of CDK2 activity during G1 and S phase entry, we constructed an influence diagram describing the known interactions of WEE1 with the G1/S regulatory network (Fig. 1a). WEE1 inhibits cyclin E–CDK2 and cyclin A–CDK2 (ref. 16). We converted this influence diagram to a series of ordinary differential equations and parametrized the model to reproduce the known dynamics of the G1/S regulatory network (Fig. 1b). We simulated the depletion of WEE1 levels to 50% of baseline and observed that CDK2 was activated earlier and S phase entry was faster than in the presence of 100% WEE1 activity.

To experimentally test whether we could observe accelerated S phase entry in cells when WEE1 activity was reduced, we used AZD1775, a WEE1i previously used in clinical trials (Extended Data Fig. 1a–c). To investigate the role of WEE1 in S phase entry while avoiding confounding effects from WEE1 perturbation during the preceding G2 phase, we synchronized hTert-RPE1 mRuby–PCNA cells in early G0/G1 phase with a CDK4–CDK6 inhibitor (CDK4/6i) (Extended Data Fig. 1d). Labelling of PCNA at the endogenous locus with mRuby generates a robust marker to define the G1/S transition (Methods). Subsequently, we washed out the CDK4/6i, releasing cells into G1, in the presence or absence of WEE1i and quantified G1 length by time-lapse imaging of mRuby-labelled PCNA (Extended Data Fig. 1e). Contrary to our model predictions, WEE1 inhibition increased both G1 length and the fraction of cells that did not enter S phase during the 48 h imaging period (Fig. 1c). To confirm that this was not an artefact of CDK4/6i treatment, we also arrested cells in G0 by contact inhibition, followed by serum starvation (Extended Data Fig. 1f), then replated cells at low density, releasing the cells into G1 in the presence or absence of WEE1i, and again performed time-lapse imaging. Again, we observed increased G1 length and an increased fraction of cells not entering S phase after WEE1 inhibition (Extended Data Fig. 1g).

As kinase inhibitors can have off-target effects, we depleted WEE1 from CDK4/6i-arrested G0/G1 cells using short interfering RNA (siRNA) (Extended Data Fig. 1h), then released the cells back into the cell cycle and quantified G1 length by time-lapse imaging. WEE1 depletion had a more severe impact than WEE1 inhibition, with very few cells entering S phase over the 48 h imaging period compared with control-depleted cells and those that did so having an increased G1 length (Fig. 1d). To be certain that the delay in entering S phase was due to loss of WEE1 function, we generated a cell line with an inducible WEE1–GFP degron (Extended Data Fig. 2) to remove endogenous WEE1 in a highly specific manner. We validated WEE1–GFP localization and normal growth of this cell line and showed that WEE1 could be degraded within 1 h of addition of doxycycline and indole-3-acetic acid (IAA) (Extended Data Fig. 2b,d,e). Degradation of endogenous WEE1 upon release from CDK4/6i-mediated G0/G1 arrest again led to most cells not entering S phase; of those that did enter S phase, the length of G1 was significantly longer in the absence of WEE1 (Fig. 1e).

These data show that, contrary to our predictions and previous understanding of the functions of WEE1, WEE1 inhibition does not accelerate S phase entry. Instead, WEE1 is required for timely S phase entry in cells returning to proliferation from quiescence.

Our model (Fig. 1a,b) predicted an earlier and more rapid increase in CDK2 activity upon WEE1 inhibition. To test this, we used an exogenously expressed fluorescent live-cell reporter (CDK2L–GFP) that measures cyclin E–CDK2 activity in real time during G1 (ref. 17) (Fig. 2a). hTert-RPE1 mRuby–PCNA CDK2L–GFP cells were released from CDK4/6i G0/G1 arrest in the presence or absence of WEE1i, and CDK2 activity was tracked by time-lapse imaging. We used a WEE1i to perturb WEE1, as it was a clinically relevant drug, and because a higher fraction of cells entered S phase with this approach compared with siRNA or degron approaches, allowing us to make statistically meaningful comparisons. When WEE1 was inhibited, CDK2 activity started to increase earlier during G1 and reached a higher activity than in dimethyl sulfoxide (DMSO)-treated cells (Fig. 2b and Supplementary Video 1). Cells treated with WEE1i therefore entered S phase at a higher level of CDK2 activity. The observation that cells could remain in G1 with high CDK2 activity was surprising; we repeated the experiment using a similar, shorter version of the CDK2 sensor (DHB–mCherry) and observed the same result18 (Extended Data Fig. 3a,b).

Two key events drive the G1/S transition: an increase in CDK2 activity and inhibition of APC/CCDH1 E3 ubiquitin ligase activity19 (Fig. 1b). CDK2 can phosphorylate CDH1 and inhibit APC/CCDH1 activity20,21,22. Therefore, we tested whether there was any change in the timing and/or rate of APC/CCDH1 inactivation relative to the G1/S transition in WEE1i-treated cells that had abnormally high CDK2 activity in G1. We used hTert-RPE1 cells expressing mCherry–geminin(1–110), constitutively expressed from an exogenous promoter, as a live-cell reporter of APC/CCDH1 activity21. Geminin is very effectively degraded by APC/CCDH1 during G1, and geminin protein only starts to accumulate after the G1/S transition (Extended Data Fig. 3c). Therefore, mCherry–geminin, together with coexpressed mTurquoise2–PCNA (under the control of an exogenous promoter), is a useful reporter for determining the timing and rate of APC/CCDH1 inactivation relative to the G1/S transition. Using this reporter, we identified that in cells released from CDK4/6i G0/G1 arrest, APC/CCDH1 activity was switched off just after the G1/S transition in WEE1i-treated cells, with timing and rate indistinguishable from those observed in DMSO-treated cells (Fig. 2c and Extended Data Fig. 3c). Our data suggest that upon WEE1 inhibition, CDK2 activity increases to a higher level in G1, but the timing of APC/CCDH1 inactivation relative to the G1/S transition remains similar to that in DMSO-treated cells (Fig. 2d). Consequently, APC/CCDH1 inactivation is more important than reaching a threshold of CDK2 activity with respect to establishing the timing of S phase entry.

Low CDK2 activity during G1 provides a period for licensing of DNA replication origins23. An early increase in CDK2 activity in G1 in WEE1i-treated cells could decrease DNA replication origin licensing. CDC6 is an essential replication origin licensing factor that is required for formation of prereplicative complexes at replication origins24. We used an hTert-RPE1 cell line expressing mTurquoise2–PCNA, DHB–mCherry and CDC6–mVenus25, all under the control of exogenous constitutively active promoters, to track the dynamics of nuclear CDC6 protein relative to the G1/S transition. In cells released from CDK4/6i G0/G1 arrest in the presence of WEE1i, the licensing window (the time from the rise to the peak in nuclear CDC6–mVenus intensity25) was reduced compared with that in DMSO-treated cells (Fig. 2e). We also quantified replication origin licensing during G1 by quantifying chromatin-bound MCM protein in cells released from CDK4/6i G0/G1 arrest. We observed a decrease in chromatin-bound MCM7 protein in WEE1i-treated G1 cells compared with DMSO-treated cells (Fig. 2f).

In summary, these results demonstrate that WEE1 inhibits CDK2 activity during G1, and that an early and rapid increase in CDK2 activity after WEE1 inhibition can lead to reduced DNA replication origin licensing but not inhibition of APC/CCDH1 activity. Increased CDK2 activity is insufficient to accelerate the transition of WEE1i-treated G1 cells into S phase, suggesting that WEE1 has other roles during G1.

As progression through G1 was abnormal in WEE1i-treated cells, we examined whether such cells entered S phase normally. We observed that mRuby–PCNA localization at the start of S phase was abnormal in WEE1i-treated cells, with low PCNA intensity and less well-defined PCNA foci compared with DMSO-treated cells (Fig. 3a). In WT cells, as S phase progresses, mRuby–PCNA localization changes, with early DNA replication factories (small puncta) coalescing into larger DNA replication factories (large foci). Thus, the standard deviation of mRuby–PCNA intensity normally increases throughout S phase. We quantified the standard deviation of mRuby–PCNA nuclear intensity; this showed a clear reduction at the G1/S transition in WEE1i-treated cells compared with DMSO-treated cells (Fig. 3b). This decrease also remained throughout S phase, indicating altered S phase progression in the presence of WEE1i, consistent with previous reports26,27,28.

PCNA recruitment to chromatin in S phase brings substrates, including DNA licensing factor CDT1 and CDK inhibitor protein p21, in contact with the CRL4CDT2 E3 ubiquitin ligase. This occurs through substrate binding to PCNA through a PCNA interaction protein (PIP) motif29, which leads to rapid substrate degradation and S phase progression. We assayed whether the change in PCNA chromatin recruitment in WEE1i-treated cells affected PIP-containing substrate degradation using an hTert-RPE1 cell line expressing the PIP-FUCCI reporter from an exogenous promoter and time-lapse imaging. We tracked the dynamics of the degradation of this construct, which contains the PIP motif of CDT1 fused to an mVenus fluorophore, at the G1/S transition. In WEE1i-treated cells, the degradation of PIP-mVenus was slower compared with that in DMSO-treated cells (Fig. 3c), suggesting that impaired recruitment of PCNA to chromatin at the start of S phase affects the rate of degradation of PIP-containing substrates.

p21 contains a PIP motif; therefore, p21 protein is normally rapidly degraded at S phase entry30,31,32, remains low throughout S phase and reaccumulates at the S/G2 transition when CRL4CDT2 is inhibited by CDK1 (ref. 33). As PIP-mVenus was more slowly degraded at the G1/S transition after WEE1 inhibition, we proposed that normal p21 protein degradation might also be impaired. To test this hypothesis, we used an hTert-RPE1 mRuby–PCNA cell line expressing endogenously labelled p21–GFP34. We observed that in WEE1i-treated cells, p21–GFP protein accumulated abnormally during S phase (Fig. 3d, Extended Data Fig. 4a,b and Supplementary Video 2). This suggested continuing impaired p21 degradation in S phase, not just at the G1/S transition, upon WEE1 inhibition. We further proposed that WEE1 inhibition in S phase would lead to increased CDK1 activity and premature inactivation of CRL4CDT2 (ref. 33). We tested whether treating cells with a CDK1 inhibitor would restore CRL4CDT2 activity during S phase and prevent accumulation of p21. Combining WEE1 and CDK1 inhibition prevented accumulation of p21–GFP in S phase cells treated with WEE1i alone (Fig. 3e and Extended Data Fig. 4c,d).

If p21 is not degraded during S phase, it can cause severe disruption to DNA replication and DNA damage35. Cells treated with WEE1i had increased levels of DNA damage compared with DMSO-treated cells (Fig. 3f and Extended Data Fig. 4e). These results indicate that abnormal p21 protein accumulation may contribute to DNA damage in WEE1i-treated cells.

High levels of p21 protein can cause cell cycle arrest in G2. We tracked the cell cycle fate of hTert-RPE1 mRuby–PCNA cells released from CDK4/6i G0/G1 arrest and treated with WEE1i. We observed an increase in the fraction of cells arresting in G2 in WEE1i-treated cells compared with DMSO-treated cells and determined that this was p21-dependent (Fig. 3g,h), whereas the increase in G1 arrest after WEE1i treatment was not p21-dependent (Fig. 3g,h). WEE1i-treated cells do not completely replicate their DNA during S phase; therefore, more accurately, this was a ‘G2-like’ arrest36. We confirmed that this was the case by examining cellular DNA content: we observed a large accumulation of cells between 2n and 4n specifically in WEE1i-treated cells (Extended Data Fig. 4f,g).

These data show that WEE1 inhibition leads to an abnormal G1/S transition that is exacerbated by the detrimental effects of WEE1 on DNA replication.

We next aimed to determine the mechanism preventing WEE1-inhibited G1 cells with high CDK2 activity from entering S phase prematurely. As APC/CCDH1 seemed to be inactivated with normal rate and timing around the G1/S transition in WEE1i-treated cells, we considered whether WEE1 inhibition could cause increased or prolonged APC/CCDH1 activity during G1 that could lead to a delay in S phase entry. This hypothesis was consistent with a previous report showing that WEE1 physically interacts with and can inhibit the ubiquitin ligase activity of APC/CCDH1 (ref. 37).

First, we revised our ordinary differential equation model to test the impact of WEE1 inhibition of APC/CCDH1 on the G1/S regulatory network (Fig. 4a). We considered three hypotheses for this interaction: first, that WEE1 inactivates CDH1 through a mechanism identical to that of CDK2 (‘CDK2-like model’), that is, WEE1 phosphorylates the same sites in CDH1 as in CDK2 (Extended Data Fig. 5a,b); second, that WEE1 does not inactivate CDH1 directly but rather increases the affinity of cyclin E–CDK2 for APC/CCDH1 (‘threshold model’), which has otherwise been shown to be negligible in vitro20,38 (Extended Data Fig. 5e,f); and, third, that WEE1 inactivates CDH1 independently of CDK2, by phosphorylating separate amino acid residues in CDH1 (‘independent model’) (Extended Data Fig. 5i,j).

All three models reproduced G0 arrest upon complete WEE1 depletion (Fig. 1d,e and Extended Data Fig. 5d,h,l). However, they differed markedly with respect to the emergent APC/CCDH1 activity during G1 and at G1/S upon partial (50%) WEE1 inhibition. Thus, each set of assumptions made distinct, experimentally verifiable predictions. The threshold model predicted a gradual inactivation of APC/CCDH1 around the G1/S transition (Extended Data Fig. 5f), which was inconsistent with our time-lapse data (Fig. 2c). This left the CDK2-like and independent models of CDH1 inhibition by WEE1. These two models differed principally in terms of the change in APC/CCDH1 activity during G1 after WEE1 inhibition (Extended Data Fig. 5a,b,i,j). In the CDK2-like model, APC/CCDH1 activity remained unchanged after WEE1 inhibition, although it was prolonged, whereas in the independent model, APC/CCDH1 activity increased after WEE1 inhibition. To differentiate between the two possibilities, we quantified levels of APC/CCDH1 activity during G1 in WEE1i-treated cells.

To estimate APC/CCDH1 activity in G1 cells, we used time-lapse imaging to quantify the levels of two APC/CCDH1 substrates that accumulate during G1: CDC6 (ref. 39) and CDC25A (ref. 40). We considered using the mCherry–geminin reporter, but as geminin is very efficiently degraded by APC/CCDH1, the mCherry–geminin signal intensity was negligible in G1 (Extended Data Fig. 3c). Quantification of the peak intensity of nuclear CDC6–mVenus protein from our previous analysis showed that CDC6–mVenus protein levels were decreased in WEE1i-treated G1 cells compared with DMSO-treated cells (Fig. 2e). This was owing to decreased CDC6–mVenus protein stability in WEE1i-treated cells (Extended Data Fig. 3d,e). To analyse CDC25A protein levels in real time, we generated an hTert-RPE1 mRuby–PCNA cell line in which we introduced a GFP fluorophore into the carboxyl terminus of the endogenous CDC25A gene (Extended Data Fig. 6a). We validated these cells (Extended Data Fig. 6b–f). In unperturbed cells released from CDK4/6i G0/G1 arrest, CDC25A–GFP showed accumulation around the G1/S transition (Fig. 4c and Supplementary Video 3). We confirmed that CDC25A–GFP was a reliable readout of APC/CCDH1 activity in G1 by depleting CDH1 using siRNA, then quantifying CDC25A–GFP levels and showing that they were increased (Extended Data Fig. 6g). After WEE1i treatment in cells released from CDK4/6i G0/G1 arrest, CDC25A–GFP levels were much reduced, compared with those in DMSO-treated cells, around the G1/S transition (Fig. 4c). This reduction was due to a change in CDC25A protein stability, rather than a change in CDC25A transcription (Extended Data Fig. 6h,i). The decreased CDC6 and CDC25A protein levels after WEE1i treatment during G1 were consistent with increased APC/CCDH1 activity after WEE1 inhibition and thus the independent model of WEE1 inhibition of CDH1 (Fig. 4b and Extended Data Fig. 5i–l).

To test whether increased APC/CCDH1 activity caused a longer G1 phase in WEE1i-treated cells, we used siRNA to deplete CDH1. This led to a short G1 length (Fig. 4d and Extended Data Fig. 7a). CDH1 depletion and simultaneous inhibition of WEE1 also led to a short G1 length, consistent with our model and supporting the hypothesis that in WEE1i-treated cells, APC/CCDH1 causes a prolonged G1 phase (Fig. 4d). Using an hTert-RPE1 cell line expressing exogenous, doxycycline-inducible EMI1 to inhibit APC/CCDH1 (ref. 41), we observed a robust decrease in CDH1 protein levels and a decrease in the proportion of G0/G1-arrested cells in the presence of WEE1i (Fig. 4e and Extended Data Fig. 7b). Notably, we did not observe any increase in CDH1 protein levels or change in CDH1 stability in G1 cells after WEE1i treatment (Extended Data Fig. 7c–e), suggesting that WEE1 does not regulate CDH1 protein stability.

To test whether WEE1 phosphorylated CDH1 directly, we used in vitro kinase reactions with purified WEE1 and CDH1 proteins. WEE1 phosphorylated tyrosine(s) in CDH1 (Fig. 4f and Extended Data Fig. 7f) in a time-dependent manner, whereas phosphorylation of related APC/C coactivator CDC20 was negligible (Extended Data Fig. 7g). Mass spectrometry analysis showed that Tyr91 and Tyr130 of CDH1 were phosphorylated by WEE1. We re-analysed the samples using a targeted liquid chromatography–mass spectrometry approach, focusing acquisition on the identified phosphorylated peptides. This provided exceptionally high-quality fragmentation data, allowing unambiguous localization of the phosphorylation sites and demonstrating phosphorylation of Tyr91 and Tyr130 of CDH1 by WEE1 (Extended Data Fig. 7h,i). Tyr91Phe–Tyr130Phe CDH1 mutants had substantially reduced WEE1-driven pTyr phosphorylation, indicating that these sites contribute substantially to CDH1 phosphorylation, although WEE1 may phosphorylate other sites (Extended Data Fig. 7j). These sites, particularly Tyr130, are conserved across species (Extended Data Fig. 8), and both phosphorylation events have previously been detected in cells, although the kinase responsible was not identified42,43.

Scrutinizing the structure of human APC/CCDH1 (ref. 44), we found that the Tyr91 and Tyr130 sites were part of a CDH1 amino-terminal extension that interacted extensively with APC/C. Tyr91 sat at an interface between CDH1 and APC1 and formed a hydrogen bond with His1231 in APC1 (Fig. 4g). Tyr130 formed intramolecular hydrogen bonds with Asp46 and Arg47 and seemed to stabilize the C-box motif in CDH1 (ref. 45) (DRFIP), which has a crucial role in maintenance of the interaction with APC8b (ref. 46) (Fig. 4h). These observations suggest that phosphorylation of CDH1 at Tyr91 and Tyr130 could weaken the engagement of CDH1 with APC/C and thereby reduce APC/CCDH1 activity (Fig. 4i). To test this, we examined whether phosphomimetic substitution of the WEE1-targeted residues affected APC/C binding by CDH1. In APC/C binding assays in Xenopus extract, both CDH1 Tyr91Glu and CDH1 Tyr130Glu showed reduced association with APC/C, with Tyr130Glu consistently causing the stronger defect. Notably, the CDH1 Tyr91Glu–Tyr130Glu double mutant was almost completely defective in APC/C binding (Fig. 4j). Consistent with this binding defect, in vitro cyclin destruction assays showed that CDH1 Tyr130Glu was impaired in APC/C activation, whereas CDH1 Tyr91Glu caused only a modest defect. The double phosphomimetic mutant was severely compromised (Fig. 4k). These findings support a model in which WEE1-mediated phosphorylation of CDH1 inhibits APC/CCDH1 by weakening CDH1 engagement with APC/C.

On the basis of our data and modelling, we propose a mechanism of G1/S control whereby WEE1 phosphorylates and inhibits APC/CCDH1, independently of CDK2-mediated inhibition of CDH1, to control G1 length and promote robust S phase entry.

Having shown that WEE1 may suppress APC/CCDH1 activity and maintain low levels of p21 protein during S phase, we next investigated the implications of these findings for WEE1i treatment in the clinic. To determine whether WEE1 inhibition resulted in similar phenotypes in cancer cells to those observed in non-transformed hTert-RPE1 cells, we repeated experiments in TP53-wild-type (WT) KU1919 bladder cancer and MCF7 breast cancer cell lines engineered to express endogenously labelled mRuby–PCNA. These cell lines were chosen as they have a functional p53–p21 pathway and are sensitive to CDK4/6i, which enabled us to robustly arrest cells in G0/G1. G1 length increased in WEE1i-treated KU1919 and MCF7 cells, and cells of both lines underwent significant G1 and G2 arrest after treatment with WEE1i, similar to phenotypes of hTert-RPE1 cells (Fig. 5a,b and Extended Data Fig. 9a–f). G2 arrest was p21-dependent, and we observed increased cell death during G2 in WEE1i-treated cells compared with DMSO-treated cells in the absence of p21 in all cell lines tested (Figs. 3g and 5a,c and Extended Data Fig. 9a). These data indicate that cells lacking p21 may be more sensitive to WEE1 inhibition. CDKN1A (encoding p21) is rarely mutated in cancers, except in bladder cancer, for which between 10% and 15% of patients have predicted loss-of-function mutations of p21 (ref. 47). These mutations occur early and can be present in normal bladders48. Therefore, we investigated whether WEE1i treatment could be used to eradicate pretumorigenic CDKN1A mutant lesions to prevent a subset of bladder cancers. We generated p21-knockout cell lines in the non-transformed bladder urothelial cell line HBLAK and found that these cells were indeed more sensitive to WEE1i than their WT counterparts (Fig. 5d and Extended Data Fig. 9g).

We reasoned that depletion of CDH1 might also sensitize cells to WEE1i by allowing the cells to enter S phase and incur WEE1i-mediated DNA damage. Indeed, siRNA-mediated depletion of CDH1 increased sensitivity of hTert-RPE1 cells to WEE1i (Fig. 5e).

To identify changes that could drive acquired resistance to WEE1i, we generated a WEE1i-resistant MCF7 cell line (Fig. 5f). We profiled these cells by RNA sequencing and found that WEE1i-resistant cells showed upregulation of nucleotide biosynthetic pathways, consistent with previous work showing that nucleoside supplementation reduces double-strand breaks caused by increased replication initiation in WEE1i-treated cells26 (Extended Data Fig. 9h–j and Supplementary Data Table 1). WEE1i-resistant cells also showed downregulated TP53 and CDKN1A expression, as well as components of APC/CCDH1 (ANAPC7 and ANAPC10, encoding APC7 and APC10, respectively) (Fig. 5g). These observations were consistent with our mechanistic findings, as cells would need downregulation of the p53–p21 and APC/CCDH1 pathways to enable continued proliferation in the presence of WEE1i. However, loss of these proteins would lead to increased sensitivity to acute WEE1i treatment, as we observed for p21 and CDH1 (Fig. 5c,e,h). Analysis of DepMap for genes that synergized with WEE1 loss identified several APC/CCDH1 components (Extended Data Fig. 9k).

In conclusion, our data suggest that CDKN1A mutation status or APC/CCDH1 subunit expression could be used to improve patient selection and potentially reduce toxicity in WEE1i clinical trials.

The substantial side effects associated with WEE1i treatment reported in clinical trials, including myelosuppression, gastrointestinal issues and fatigue, have been attributed to potential off-target effects on PLK1. An alternative explanation is that WEE1 has other functions. We report a role for WEE1 in the regulation of re-entry into the cell cycle from quiescence. This could account for some of the effects of WEE1i observed in healthy tissues.

Our findings lead to an apparent conundrum, in that WEE1 inhibits CDK2 to prevent S phase entry yet also inhibits APC/CCDH1 to promote S phase entry. We propose that WEE1 overall promotes S phase entry. By inhibiting APC/CCDH1 activity, WEE1 permits CDC25A protein to accumulate; this in turn reverses WEE1 inhibitory phosphorylation of CDK2. WEE1 may be required for initial inhibition of APC/CCDH1 before CDK2-mediated CDH1 phosphorylation and degradation. Future work involving mutation of WEE1 and CDK phospho-sites in CDH1 and identification of tyrosine phosphatases that act on CDH1 could determine the relative importance of each pathway with respect to S phase entry. mTOR-mediated phosphorylation of CDH1 Thr129 transiently inactivates APC/CCDH1 (ref. 43). This site is adjacent to CDH1 Tyr130, which is phosphorylated by WEE1, and phosphorylation of Ser131 has also been detected in cells42; these findings indicate a potential key role for this motif in regulation of APC/CCDH1 activity, probably involving stabilization of the interaction of the C-box in CDH1 with APC8. mTOR inhibition decreased Thr129 and Tyr130 phosphorylation in cells; however, mTOR only phosphorylates Thr129 (ref. 43). Therefore, mTOR phosphorylation may precede WEE1 phosphorylation of CDH1 Tyr130. Together, these data indicate that APC/CCDH1 may function as a signal integrator in G1 to determine whether cells commit to cell proliferation.

Our experimental data are most consistent with the model in which WEE1 inhibition of APC/CCDH1 activity is independent of CDK2-mediated APC/CCDH1 inhibition. However, there remain two discrepancies. First, although both modelling and experimental data showed reduced CDC25A levels during G1 after WEE1 inhibition, the model predicts that CDC25A levels should increase after S phase entry, owing to inactivation of APC/CCDH1 at the G1/S transition. This post-G1/S increase in CDC25A does not occur in cells. This is likely to be because Cdc25A is degraded by CHK1-mediated phosphorylation in S phase cells49 (Extended Data Fig. 10a). Second, whereas the model is consistent with our data in that cells remain arrested in G0/G1 at very low levels of WEE1 activity, the model predicts high CDK2 activity and inhibition of Rb during the arrest, which we did not observe (Extended Data Fig. 10b). The reasons for this are unclear but could involve reduced CAK activity in arrested cells50.

WEE1i were designed to be synthetically lethal with TP53 mutations, which would cause DNA-damage-induced p53-mediated p21 expression to be inactive. However, WEE1i show efficacy in TP53-WT tumours; this demonstrates a need to understand the parameters that define WEE1i sensitivity. Here we identified expression of p21 and APC/CCDH1 complex components as potential indicators of sensitivity to WEE1i. Acute loss of p21 or CDH1 sensitizes cells to WEE1 inhibition, whereas reduced p53–p21 and APC/CCDH1 expression in resistant cells may reflect adaptation to chronic drug exposure. These findings indicate that CDKN1A status and APC/CCDH1 pathway activity may merit further investigation as biomarkers for WEE1i-based therapies, including in CDKN1A mutant bladder cancer. Our data also suggest that WEE1i toxicity may arise, at least in part, from increased APC/CCDH1 activity in quiescent stem cells, which impairs their ability to re-enter proliferation and maintain tissue function.

The code used for single-cell time-lapse image segmentation and tracking was implemented in Python (Jupyter notebook) and is available at GitHub (https://github.com/Alexis-Barr-Lab). This analysis pipeline combined Cellpose67 segmentation and tracking using Ultrack68 and was run using the Jex HPC cluster at the MRC LMS69. In brief, raw TIFF stacks (shape: T × Y × X) were background-subtracted using a rolling ball-style method with a Gaussian filter (σ = 15 px), followed by morphological reconstruction to separate foreground from low-frequency background. Frames were normalized (γ = 0.5) and stored in Zarr format for efficient chunked input–output. Normalized frames were processed with a Cellpose model (cpsam). Masks for each time point were generated, yielding per-frame two-dimensional label arrays written into a Zarr volume. A watershed was run on each frame to recover fine cell boundaries and split merged objects. Thresholding and binary morphology mask detections, followed by distance transform watershed with minima suppression, were performed to generate second label sets, which were also saved as Zarr. Graphics processing unit-accelerated boundary detection was implemented in CuPy; boundaries from both Cellpose and watershed masks were combined and smoothed (σ = 5) to create high-resolution contour maps for downstream linking. Centroids of watershed segments between frames were calculated. The Hungarian algorithm (linear_sum_assignment) was used to link cells, providing centroid-distance statistics (95th percentile, maximum, average and 25th percentile displacements) and informing Ultrack’s max_distance parameter. Ultrack was then configured and executed. For quality checks, tracks and labels were exported and visualized. Background subtraction was then applied to the channel of interest, and properties were exported.

Subsequently, we averaged the signal across cells at each time point and smoothed the data by calculating the three-point running mean. We then subtracted the background signal from the resulting value at each time point. The resulting series was a measure of degron concentration, corresponding to [APC degron], in arbitrary units. The derivative term was calculated by applying the finite difference method to the consecutive points of the [APC degron] time series. ksynth was estimated from the initial (net) rate of degron accumulation in S phase, when APC/C was assumed to be inactive.

Clustal Omega77 was used to align CDH1 protein sequences, and outputs (consensus frequency of residues, identity matrix and phylogenetic tree) were plotted in Python.

We built our cell cycle model using a system of coupled nonlinear differential equations describing the rates of change of biochemical species responsible for regulation of the G1 and S states. The regulation of subsequent cell cycle phases was omitted for simplicity, as our experimental approach was focused on quiescence release rather than cycling cells. #986. '90s art scene in the UK: An international, edgy trendsetter

📅 26 Oct 2026 | other | Source

The 1990s are remembered for Brit-pop and provocative fashion, but beyond the nostalgia, it was a decade of cultural experimentation and social change. Tate Britain's new exhibition looks past the familiar icons.

The 1990s in Britain are often remembered through a haze of nostalgia consisting of rave culture, Britpop and provocative art and fashion by the likes of Tracey Emin and Alexander McQueen.

Undeniably, the decade was a time of major change in British society — the long reign of Margaret Thatcher had come to an end and the West entered a period of relative post-Cold War stability.

Meanwhile, British cultural icons like Oasis, the Spice Girls and Vivienne Westwood garnered international fame.

But Tate Britain's new exhibition "The 90s: Art and Fashion," which opens on October 8 and runs through February 14, 2027, urges viewers to look beyond the familiar names and images.

Even though now prominent artists such as Damien Hirst and Juergen Teller are among the show's 70 contributors, the exhibition also aims to highlight the creative input of those in communities which were left out of the mainstream discourse.

After all, says chief curator Edward Enninful in the exhibition's program, "the energy of the 90s was not concentrated in a single movement, but distributed across communities, disciplines and geographies that have not always been equally represented."

For Enninful, the exhibition is personal: The Ghanaian-born British fashion editor, stylist and creative director became the youngest-ever fashion director of i-D Magazine in the 90s at age 18, helping shape the diverse and experimental London fashion scene of the 1990s. He later became editor-in-chief of British Vogue in 2017. Enninful writes that he and his co-curators aimed to present a body of work which "reflects a broader landscape of practices and voices that shaped the period, many of which operated beyond the structures of visibility at the time." The exhibition pays special attention to artists of color and women whose work was not necessarily showcased as prominently at the time.

Black masculinity became "a site of cultural negotiation" in Britain in the 90s, writes co-curator Helen Little in the program text: "Figures such as Chris Ofili, Steve McQueen, Keith Piper, Koto Bolofo and Ozwald Boateng reimagined the Black masculine, challenging the racist specter of Black men, who had been defined as part of a masculinity crisis linked to post-industrial decline and excluded from the narrative of 'Cool Britannia.'"

Just like "Swinging London" in the 1960s, "Cool Britannia" became a widespread phrase to refer to the 1990s as an era during which British youth culture, pop music, art and political optimism boomed.

The 90s also offered artists plenty of chances to create without waiting for support from traditional gatekeepers. Britain entered the decade in recession, but that economic uncertainty also left artists free to do what they wished.

Empty buildings and industrial spaces were turned into informal venues. A 23-year-old Damien Hirst orchestrated "Freeze," a self-organized student exhibition in 1988 in a disused London warehouse. It became a landmark early art exhibition for the generation of artists known as the "Young British Artists" or YBA.

Artists Sarah Lucas and Tracey Emin opened The Shop, a temporary art gallery and social space in 1992, as a place where exhibitions could develop outside established galleries. Lucas and Emin also paved the way for women artists thanks to their deeply personal works and their edgy expressions of female liberation. While the Spice Girls helped make feminism accessible to a younger generation of Brits in the 90s by popularizing the "girl power" slogan and shaping "identity through agency power and resistance," as Tate curator Helen Little argues, Emin and Lucas meanwhile "presented a new visual language of clothing as art to tackle societal taboos around female sexuality, vulnerability and desire" — from t-shirts with provocative slogans to a grunge look of scuffed Dr. Martens boots with razor blades. Their work "held a mirror up to gender, behavior and identity," Little writes.

At the same time, the British fashion industry came into the international spotlight.

In a feminine sensuality counter to the gender stereotypes dismissed by Emin and Lucas, waif-like Kate Moss came to represent the feminine ideal of the 1990s. Dubbed "heroin-chic," the style was an antithesis to the glamorous, athletic physiques of the supermodels who preceded her. Corinne Day's photographs of the model led to the raw aesthetic to be labelled "grunge" after the anguished style of rock music hailing from Seattle and led by Nirvana.

The trend has undergone significant cultural reexamination since then: "Along with the look also came criticism that the strung-out look of drug or alcohol addiction was glamorized while encouraging serious eating disorders in a generation of young women," points out Alex Farquharson, director of the Tate Gallery in his foreword to the exhibition.

The 1990s were also an exciting era for Britain in haute couture, as legendary designers John Galliano led Dior, while Alexander McQueen started directing Givenchy at the age of 27.

McQueen's runway presentations were more akin to performance art than commercial fashion shows — and they often included historical critiques. Among them was the famous finale of his Fall/Winter 1998 collection at a London warehouse, during which the model stood in a circle of flames. Titled "Joan," the show was a reference to the killing of Joan of Arc. McQueen designed several other runway shows that reflected on British history, with topics of conquest and punishment repeatedly coming to the forefront. His 1995 presentation "Highland Rape" referenced the history of Scotland's subjugation, with the designer describing it as "England's rape of Scotland."

As co-curator Jessica Vaughan points out in her exhibition text: "Art, fashion and photography were not simply transgressive reactions … but reflected a genuine public unease with Britain's global identity and its past." In a world that can now feel increasingly mediated by screens, algorithms and established platforms, the artwork of the 90s offers a reminder of the value of physical spaces and fresh DIY creativity of the last smartphone-free decade.

#985. Beer & Blackpool beach was how British players bonded - Pulis

📅 26 Oct 2026 | other | Source

Hearing about Tottenham's team-bonding trip to Marbella last week made me think about the similar methods I used as a manager to build team morale.

While some things have changed from my day, the idea behind taking your players away to a new environment where they will benefit from spending a lot of time together has not.

I wrote a few weeks ago about how Spurs boss Roberto de Zerbi needed time with his players on the training pitch after their poor start to the season, to bed them in and build a proper team.

He will have used their warm-weather camp in southern Spain to do more of that, getting to know what is almost a new squad of players.

With De Zerbi being cut from Italian cloth, I am sure the players will have been working mornings and afternoons to drill in the manager's principles in respect to team shape and direction.

But that's not the only purpose of taking them away. De Zerbi will also have been able to monitor the different personalities within the group away from the grass.

If the trip has worked then, as well as getting them ready to face Coventry on Monday night, I am sure he will now, for the first time, understand the strengths and weaknesses of each individual, on and off the field.

A few eyebrows were raised about Spurs going away so early in the season but it makes perfect sense to me when you consider so many new players arrived there this summer, with several signings late in the transfer window.

When I was Stoke manager, I often took my teams away to Austria in our pre-season for the same reasons De Zerbi and Spurs have gone to Spain.

Those trips were set up not only to test the players' fitness, but also to see how the group worked together.

I have always believed in building a family togetherness within the dressing room, with a commitment from every player to the benefit of the team.

The extreme tests that we asked our players to do over that 10-day period brought out an inner determination to fulfil the tasks given them and, in my opinion, helped us through some tough times early on in our Premier League campaigns.

Watching the lads slowly bond together on the training pitch and at meal times was great to see and I found out a lot about all of them, good and bad.

Yes, I have to admit one or two players were short of the character we wanted and needed, but they didn't last long and left with no bad feelings on my side.

A bucket and spade were not required when Pulis went to Blackpool for pre-season training with Bristol Rovers

Today, whether it's pre-season or a winter break, most Premier League clubs who take their players away go first-class and enjoy great hotels, great facilities and wonderful food.

Wherever the team bus goes, it is followed by an even bigger bus with all the non-playing staff on it.

It is very different to my experiences in my early years as a player, when we would be taken to Blackpool for three days' pre-season training.

Again, the idea was to bring the lads closer together, but that happened in a different way than today's professionals are used to.

We stayed in a guest house with three-quarter beds that we all shared - that certainly brought us closer together! - then trained on the beach each morning, doing our running sessions on the sand.

Then we played seven-a-side games in the afternoon on a local park pitch, after clearing the dog muck off first.

In the evenings, we were allowed out for a few beers, with no time stipulated for our return - apart from having to be downstairs and ready to hit the beach again at 8am the next morning.

That is where things have changed most of all, right through football, during the past few years. In the 1970s and 80s, there was a massive drinking culture right through the leagues - just listen to the stories that came out of clubs as big as Liverpool, Manchester United or Arsenal, when they had teams who not only dominated English football but the European game too.

In my early years of management, the bonding I've been talking about used to happen right through the season through the lads meeting in pubs after games and after training on week days as well.

Winter breaks were rare and, rather than for a training camp, the only time most players I knew went to Spain was at the end of the season when more alcohol would be consumed.

That was what British players classed as bonding sessions and, as I've written about before, even the ones at the top level were much more connected to normal society, rather than living in an eternal bubble.

Now, while they are certainly more aware of the dangers of drinking to excess, as a manager you have to find other ways for them to connect when there are other distractions to contend with.

One item I am very glad my generation was not brought up with is mobile phones. I used to go mad if I walked into a dining room at the training ground or a hotel to see a group of lads looking at their phone and not engaging with each other.

I sometimes think that, as a young player, I had nothing compared to the players of today but, really, we had everything.

The stories, the friendships and the memories of the togetherness I remember from every dressing room during that time still resonate with me and recreating them was always my aim as a manager.

You're in the army now, Harry... Pulis is not the only manager to take his players to a military boot camp - Gareth Southgate did the same in 2017 when he took his England squad to a Commando Training Centre in Devon

Some other things have changed too - I am not sure De Zerbi has ever considered taking his team away to an army camp like I did on many occasions early in my managerial career, where we worked on the players in different ways.

One year, I took Stoke down to Exmouth, where the lads were put through their paces doing cross-country circuits.

That probably doesn't sound too bad on the face of it, but it was freezing cold and chucking it down and the circuits incorporated climbing and swimming under water in a darkened tunnel - I did all of it too by the way - while trying to keep up with the Army PT Instructor.

That was just the morning, the afternoon was spent indoors, where the boys were set different kinds of tasks, where their minds were tested with more practical team-building exercises where they had, say, 20 minutes to use some drums and planks to cross an area without touching the ground.

At night, they slept in bunk beds with only plastic sheeting over the mattress, and one very thin blanket.

Like my own trips to Blackpool as a player, it was spit and sawdust but everyone bought into it and attacked every challenge with great gusto and spirit.

As I moved onwards and upwards as a manager I was allowed to take teams abroad, to locations that were a little more lavish.

Goals in each half from Raul and Roberto Soldado left Stoke a mountain to climb when they faced Real Madrid in a pre-season friendly in Austria in 2007

Irdning in Austria is my favourite destination, and somewhere I have taken many clubs over the years.

It is a small town in the Alps that is ideal in every aspect of what I wanted as a manager, and not just because of the mountains I made my players run or cycle up!

We have almost always stayed in the same place there, too - a 1,000-year-old building I call 'The Castle'. It has swimming pools, a large gym, saunas, steam rooms, a spa and a golf course - you get the picture.

Real Madrid stayed there all the time too - it led to us playing them in a friendly in 2007 because we had already booked it and they said they would play us if we swapped hotel - yet my first time there could have turned out to be a disaster.

It was in 2004, during my first spell as Stoke manager, when I was having a tough time under the Icelandic ownership who were at the club at that time - I liked them as people but we all tended to largely disagree when it came to football.

My first-team then was made up of some really strong characters, but who were also great lads and old-school to the core, in the ways I've been talking about in this column.

During our first three days in Irdning, they had worked their socks off. The fourth day was meant to be the rest day where, fishing, golf or shopping was on the agenda but the players had other ideas.

Gerry Taggart, who was a fantastic lad, came and asked if the boys could borrow the team bus and go somewhere to have a few beers away from our hotel.

#981. Cyberpunk: Edgerunners 2 ending, explained: Who dies, and who lives to tell their story?

📅 26 Oct 2026 | other | Source

The ending of Cyberpunk: Edgerunners 2 might be even more ambiguous than its predecessor. And according to writer and showrunner Bartosz Sztybor, that's exactly what the creative team intended.

"We had a lot of conversation. There were a lot of endings," Sztybor told Mashable about the nearly two years spent developing the season. Ultimately, they arrived at a conclusion he described as "very ambiguous, even more ambiguous than the ending in the first season. But also somehow hopeful."

That ambiguity is especially apparent in D's final moments. After spending years pursuing revenge for the destruction of his clan, the Nomad rides off with Capri, a chip containing digitized human consciousnesses, toward an orbital space station, seemingly sacrificing himself to keep her out of Arasaka's hands.

But does he actually die? And why does his ending feel so hopeful?

Major spoilers for the Cyberpunk: Edgerunners 2 finale follow.

D has spent years hunting the people responsible for killing his clan, only to discover that they're already dead. Worse, he learns that his clan was targeted because of him. His unusual tolerance for cyberware made him a potential host for Capri, part of an Arasaka experiment that also identified David Martinez and Talia as possible candidates.

Suddenly, the revenge that has defined D's entire existence is impossible. "I think we started with this idea that what would happen [if] your whole personality is based on that revenge thing," Sztybor explained. "And suddenly you see that, oh shit, I didn't fulfill my journey. So, what now?"

Omi, the only surviving member of D's clan, puts it more simply: "You should have followed your own path. You should have lived your own life, David."

D has spent so long living for revenge that he's forgotten how to live for himself. But his relationship with Capri changes something. Despite existing as digitized human consciousnesses stored on a chip, Capri is curious, playful, and eager to experience the world. She also has little patience for D's obsession.

"Why is everyone so obsessed with getting revenge anyway? Is getting revenge, like, fun?" she asks while controlling Talia's body.

The surprisingly simple question cuts to the heart of D's story. Revenge has given him a reason to keep going, but it hasn't made him happy.

Capri, on the other hand, does. For the first time in years, D seems to be having fun. His time with her offers a reprieve from the nightmares that have haunted him since his clan's destruction, and there's something almost intoxicating about that relief. Their relationship can even be read through the lens of addiction, with its initial rush of euphoria and the danger of becoming consumed by the thing that makes you feel good. In Capri, D seems to rediscover a part of himself that existed before his life became defined by trauma.

It's not hard to understand why he'd become so attached to her. His revenge has reached a dead end, and his actions have left him with few options in Night City. But with Capri, he gets to experience something he thought he'd lost. And in keeping her out of Arasaka's hands, he offers Capri a chance at freedom, too.

Their final rampage is violent, reckless, and, in its own twisted way, almost joyful. D isn't suddenly a hero, and his decision to take Capri toward the orbital space station may ultimately cost him his life. The series leaves their fate uncertain — and it's incredibly possible that D and Capri are somewhere in the Net — but there's something liberating about those final moments. For once, he's no longer acting out of anger toward the people who destroyed his past. He's doing something because he wants to.

And in his final recording for Roman, D smiles. It's the very expression Roman has spent the season trying to capture on camera. After everything D has endured, that small moment of happiness feels like its own kind of resolution.

Talia's story explores a different side of that same idea. Born with a terminal illness, the teenager has spent much of her life having her failing body parts replaced with cyberware, all in an effort to prolong her life. But those decisions were made by her parents, not Talia herself. And throughout the season, she tries to escape their control, adding even more chrome to her body in an attempt to reclaim the autonomy she's never really had.

It's what makes cyberpunk fiction feel so relevant. Beneath all the futuristic technology is a very real conversation about medical intervention, bodily autonomy, and the difference between keeping someone alive and allowing them to actually live.

When her mother seeks a treatment that would remove the cyberware from her body to extend the time she has left, Talia protests, "I wanted to die beautiful."

And that's what makes her friendship with Roman so meaningful. With him, Talia gets to experience something resembling an ordinary teenage life. She makes a friend closer to her own age. They go to the movies. It's such a mundane thing to do, especially in Night City, where so much of life is defined by violence and survival. But for Talia, those moments are precious because they're hers.

In the finale, Talia tells Roman that seeing herself through his camera made her feel beautiful. "You are beautiful," he replies.

It's a small exchange, but it brings her story full circle. After spending so much of her life having other people decide what her body should look like and how long she should live, Talia finally gets to see herself through the eyes of someone who appreciates her exactly as she is.

Her condition is irreversible. But when she leaves with Weak, she's finally making a choice about how she wants to spend whatever time she has left.

Weak has spent much of the season trying to protect Talia after her mother tells him that he's her biological father. But Talia's father later claims that story was a manipulation, leaving the truth uncertain.

By the finale, however, their biological relationship matters less than the one they've formed.

Once a legendary edgerunner known as King, Weak has spent years living in the shadow of his former glory. His relationship with Talia gives him something meaningful to care about beyond his own reputation.

And despite knowing that he can't save her, he chooses to stay. "Come on, let's live!" he tells Talia as they drive away. Weak can't promise her a cure or a long future. But he can give her the freedom to spend the rest of her life on her own terms. And in choosing to be there for her, he finds something worth living for, too.

Before leaving with Weak, Talia shares a tearful goodbye with Roman, the young aspiring filmmaker whose analog camera has documented much of the season's events. Along the way, he's captured something more personal, too: the lives of a group of misfits whose stories might otherwise have been lost to Night City.

"Roman, we'll see each other again. I know it," she tells him. "For now, you just keep that camera rolling, and you can come show me what you've made. Scroll something so great, it will make all this stuff feel small."

Her words echo D's own farewell to Roman: "This is the high point of my story, not yours. You still have a lot ahead of you."

Both characters, whose futures have become so uncertain, encourage Roman to imagine something bigger for himself.

Sztybor described Roman as "the only perfectly innocent character" in the violent world of Night City. While the first Edgerunners was, in his words, "a story about the end of innocence," Roman's presence helped the creative team arrive at a more hopeful conclusion for the second season. Roman's love of filmmaking became central to that decision. Roman has witnessed terrible things, but those experiences haven't extinguished his desire to create.

After Talia and Weak drive away, he looks at his camera, smiles, and takes off running with an exuberant yell.

The series then closes on a flickering space station in the sky, an image evoking D and Capri's final journey without confirming their fate.

That's the beauty of Edgerunners 2's ambiguous ending. We don't know whether D and Capri survive, how much time Talia has left, or what Roman will eventually create. But the finale isn't really about promising these characters a happy ending. It's about giving them the freedom to decide what to do with their lives. Because even in Night City, where so much of your life is decided for you, there's still something powerful about getting to choose how your story unfolds.
