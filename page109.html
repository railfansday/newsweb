📅 26 Oct 2026 | other | Source

Atlético Madrid have left Cristian Romero and Koke out of their squad for Tuesday’s Champions League match at VfB Stuttgart, with Romero’s adductor strain the immediate defensive concern. Johnny Cardoso returns after missing the win over Espanyol with gastroenteritis, according to Intothecalderon’s report on the squad announcement.

Romero is expected to miss the trip to Germany and next weekend’s home match against Deportivo A Coruña. Koke is also unavailable, leaving Diego Simeone without two established players as Atlético prepare for their first meeting with Stuttgart.

Romero asked to come off during Atlético’s 1-0 victory at Espanyol on Saturday, 17 October. Tests subsequently found a small adductor strain, ruling the Argentina international out of the Stuttgart game and the Deportivo fixture.

The defender has become an important part of Simeone’s side since joining Atlético. His recent contribution included a late winner against Alavés, while his place in the team has made this absence a notable change to the coach’s defensive options.

Koke is recovering from a left hamstring strain that kept him out against Espanyol. He was also grieving the death of his mother, who died on Tuesday, 13 October; his return timetable has not been set.

Cardoso’s return follows his absence from the Espanyol match because of gastroenteritis. The midfielder trained in the gym on Sunday, 18 October, and was named in the travelling group.

Atlético head to Germany on a six-match winning streak. Simeone’s side have lost eight of their 10 games in Germany, with their most recent victory there coming against Bayer Leverkusen in 2017.

Stuttgart have taken six points from their first two Champions League league-phase matches. They have not beaten a Spanish team in European competition, drawing three and losing five of their eight meetings.

Romero’s absence also comes after a strong run of form for the defender, whose late strike against Alavés was a recent highlight for Atlético. The squad will face Stuttgart on Tuesday, 20 October, before returning to Madrid for the Deportivo A Coruña match next weekend.

#1493. <i>Nature</i>’s job-satisfaction survey highlights the changing geography of science

📅 26 Oct 2026 | other | Source

Since 2010, Nature has periodically performed a survey on how researchers around the world perceive their careers. The survey asks whether scientists are satisfied in their jobs and optimistic about the future, and the reasons for their responses. The results of the latest survey of nearly 4,000 scientists — published on 13 October — include some surprises (Nature 658, 825–829; 2026), especially in light of the results obtained in the 2018 and 2021 surveys.

What’s the secret sauce for happy scientists at work? These eight countries offer some clues

What’s the secret sauce for happy scientists at work? These eight countries offer some clues

Respondents in several leading research-intensive nations, including the United States, Germany and the United Kingdom, provided less optimistic answers about the future of their careers when compared with those asked in previous years, according to the survey, which ran in May and June. And a greater share of this year’s respondents think that their career prospects are worse than those of previous generations were. In 2018 and 2021 — during the COVID-19 pandemic — higher proportions of scientists in these countries responded positively to these questions.

By contrast, respondents are less pessimistic in middle-income countries — notably in China and India, which are becoming attractive destinations for researchers. In this year’s survey, 70% of respondents in India feel optimistic about their prospects. Although the share of respondents in China who feel positive about the future (55%) is close to the global average (52%), it is noticeably higher than those of Germany (38%), the United Kingdom (33%) and the United States (34%). There is, however, one caveat: the responses from India’s scientists pre-date the nationwide student protests in July.

It’s hardly a secret that a fair number of high-income nations are going through upheavals that recent generations have not experienced. The US government is slashing funding for some grant programmes and directing money towards its priorities, creating anxiety and uncertainty about the future of some projects and research areas.

China’s AI research is booming: what it’s like to be on the front line

China’s AI research is booming: what it’s like to be on the front line

Scientists in Europe might also be looking at large changes to their grants. European nations are devoting a greater share of public funding to defence, which has led to cuts to other budgets, including science. Several universities are being urged to work on defence research.

When it comes to job satisfaction, however, the results are reversed. Survey respondents in India and China have relatively low levels of job satisfaction compared with those in several high-income countries. Only 51% of China’s scientists reported being satisfied and 59% of India’s. By contrast, 65% of respondents in the United States, 67% in Germany and 63% in United Kingdom are satisfied with their jobs. Scientists in Canada had the highest job satisfaction of the surveyed countries: 70% of respondents there were happy with their work.

Rising numbers of Asian and African PhD students choose to study in China

Rising numbers of Asian and African PhD students choose to study in China

Some low- and middle-income countries are struggling to improve working conditions for their researchers. For instance, scientists in China note that they are under pressure to publish in journals, as we reported in a News feature on artificial-intelligence research in the country (Nature 658, 598–600; 2026). By contrast, scientists in Europe and the United States generally work in environments in which they have higher levels of independence — a strong predictor of satisfaction — than do researchers in low- and middle-income countries.

So what of the future? With US midterm elections next month and various national elections due to follow in the next few years, global politics will probably have shifted again before Nature runs its next career-satisfaction survey — and so will the research landscape.

One thing can be said with high confidence: the next survey’s findings will be different from the 2026 results in ways that we cannot predict.

#1482. Real Sociedad equal their record European away win at Lillestrøm as Sergio Gómez becomes their top Europa League scorer

📅 26 Oct 2026 | other | Source

Real Sociedad won 6-0 in Norway to claim their first Europa League league-phase victory of the season, matching the biggest away win in their European history. Left-back Sergio Gómez scored twice and is now the club's top scorer in the competition ahead of Lyon's visit to Anoeta.

The scoreline at Åråsen Stadion equalled the biggest away win in the club’s European history, according to Noticias de Gipuzkoa. The mark dates from a 6-0 win at Vardar on matchday three in 2017. UEFA described that game at the time as the biggest away win in Europa League history.

Matarazzo’s side had struggled before Thursday. They lost 2-1 at home to Bournemouth on matchday one. They then drew 1-1 away to Union SG on matchday two.

Lillestrøm had the better of the opening minutes and pressed the visitors hard. Within eight minutes, Oyarzabal’s creativity then turned the match. Sergio Gómez struck in the 25th and 27th minutes. The first came with his left foot, the second with a delicate right-footed finish past Dahlberg. Luka Sučić added the third in the 33rd minute.

The hosts had a chance to respond after the break. Thomas Lehne Olsen missed a penalty in the 50th minute. Orri Steinn Óskarsson made it four in the 64th minute from a Gonçalo Guedes assist. Sučić scored again in the 72nd. Arsen Zakharyan completed the rout in the 80th minute from Benat Turrientes’s pass.

The figures point to a closer contest than the score. Expected goals finished 2.29 for Lillestrøm against 2.46 for Real Sociedad. The hosts had 14 shots to 16. The gap lay in accuracy: Real Sociedad put 8 shots on target, Lillestrøm 2. The visitors also had 56% of possession.

For Lillestrøm, the defeat compounds a difficult start. They had lost 2-1 at home to Torreense and drawn 1-1 at Sparta Praha in their first two league-phase games.

No Real Sociedad player has scored more in this season’s competition than Sergio Gómez, whose opening European goal came in the defeat to Bournemouth. The data provider rated him 9.04, just ahead of Sučić on 9.02.
