Arsenal transfer target Eli Junior Kroupi is on course to make his return from injury earlier than expected, following an update on a potential January exit from Bournemouth.

#1935. Villa Park... get ready for a Viking invasion! How spirited Champions League minnows plan to topple Aston Villa after stunning Bayern Munich - with TWO managers at the helm

📅 26 Oct 2026 | other | Source

By BEN WILLCOCKS, ASSISTANT SPORTS EDITOR

Published: 08:24 BST, 21 October 2026 | Updated: 09:10 BST, 21 October 2026

The Vikings are plotting another European raid and this time, it's Prince William who has reason to worry.

Tonight, Norwegian minnows Viking FK head to Villa Park with the wind in their sails, fresh from holding German giants Bayern Munich to a pulsating 3-3 draw in the Champions League last week.

Despite hosting Bayern on home soil, Viking were given little hope of upsetting the Bundesliga champions, whose £878.7million squad is worth more than 18 times that of the Stavanger-based outfit.

Viking did more than spring a surprise against Bayern Munich. The underdogs carved out a stunning 2-0 half-time lead and then showed real character to snatch a last-gasp equaliser after Harry Kane and Co had fought their way back into the game.

Aston Villa, meanwhile, have made an impressive start to their own European campaign. Unai Emery's side edged past Club Brugge 3-2 in their opening Champions League clash before thumping Fenerbahce by six goals last week, establishing themselves as a force to be reckoned with after lifting the Europa League trophy last season.

First up, Villa will have to contend with Viking's indomitable spirit, fuelled by a fervent travelling support.

Aston Villa host Norwegian minnows Viking in the Champions League on Wednesday night

Viking stunned Harry Kane and Bayern Munich last week, holding the giants to a 3-3 draw

Viking have two managers at the helm with shared responsibility - Morten Jensen (right) and Bjarte Lunde Aarsheim (left) - and it has proved hugely successful since their arrival in 2021

After waiting 34 years to see their side back among Europe's elite, the Norwegian faithful had barely settled before erupting in celebration as Viking took a shock lead after just 72 seconds.

Bayern's sluggish start may have been compounded by reports that Viking fans set off fireworks outside the German side's hotel at around 1.30am on the morning of the match, in an attempt to stop Vincent Kompany's side from sleeping.

With Gianni Stensness having fired them in front, the home fans kept up their deafening support throughout the match, roaring the team on through every twist and turn of a thrilling contest.

'That was the moment', Viking's chief executive Eirik Bjorno told Sky Sports, reflecting on the opening goal.

'You are preparing for wave after wave, maybe having a counter-attack or two, and then you score from the first corner.'

Erik Nevland, the club's sporting director, was left even more impressed by the second goal, which came in the 38th minute courtesy of Peter Christiansen.

'You can always score early,' he said. 'But that was something that we had never dreamed of. It was crazy. More than we could expect. It was just a night for the history books.'

In many ways, Viking's remarkable rise began with a simple dream – two great friends joining forces to lead the club as joint head coaches.

Viking uniquely have two managers steering their ship, with Bjarte Lunde Aarsheim and Morten Jensen sharing equal charge of the team since their appointment in January 2021.

When they first floated the idea, Aarsheim worked in banking and Jensen was employed in the oil and gas industry, both coaching separate local teams on the side.

The Norwegian minnows are competing in their first Champions League campaign since 1992

They will hope to wipe the smile off Aston Villa's most famous fan Prince William (pictured during his team's 6-0 thumping over Fenerbahce last week) when they head to Villa Park

Speaking of their partnership, Aarsheim told The Guardian earlier this month: 'We were talking on the phone one evening, it must have been around 2012 or 2013. I remember us saying: "Hey, one day we should coach a team together."

'What’s impressive with Morten is how big he thinks and that he dares to tell it out loud. He talks about winning the league, the Champions League, everything, while I’m thinking: "Let’s just get a good team first". But it transmits.

'We put dreams to the players and put that flame inside for them to dream bigger. We don’t want players simply to be satisfied to play here.'

The two-boss setup has been hugely successful since Aarsheim and Jensen's arrival, culminating in the club winning their first Eliteserien title in 34 years in 2025.

Despite missing out on the title last campaign, losing out to Bodo/Glimt on goal difference, Viking triumphed over Dinamo Zagreb to qualify for this season's Champions League through the playoffs, marking their first appearance in the elite competition since 1992.

Viking captain Zlatko Tripic acts as the crucial glue between the two managers during matches, tasked with executing the shared tactical vision on the pitch while Aarsheim and Jensen coach in tandem.

They also split matchday and media duties equally, resisting the temptation to divide responsibilities into separate areas of expertise.

Ranked as one of the leading youth academies in Norway, Viking have profited hugely from homegrown talent, but they keep their eyes out for a certain type of youngster.

The academy’s strapline, displayed on the club’s website, is simple: 'We are looking for potential, and looking for those who want it the most!'

Hard work, passion and dedication are at the heart of Viking's youth set-up, with those values expected to carry through to the first team when young players earn their chance at senior level.

Just as important is a strong connection to the club and the Stavanger region it represents. For sporting director Nevland, success is about more than recruitment – it is about having players who genuinely care about Viking, its identity and what it means to the local community.

Unai Emery pictured in training as Villa prepare to play host to the Vikings on Wednesday night

Homegrown midfielder Simen Kvia-Egeskog is a natural embodiment of that philosophy, following in the footsteps of his grandfather Svein Kvia, who made almost 300 appearances for Viking and remains a much-loved club legend.

As Turkish giants Fenerbahce will tell you, travelling to Villa Park is no easy feat. But Viking have already shown they are capable of unsettling a European giant, and their remarkable performance against Bayern Munich suggests they will not be overawed by the occasion.

#1922. Glass houses and job cuts

📅 26 Oct 2026 | other | Source

There were a lot of goals in the Champions League last night. Aston Villa and Liverpool both scored 6, which looks impressive obviously, but probably tells you something about the league stage of this competition. Maybe the quality of some of the teams is questionable, maybe it’s to do with the power of the Premier League, maybe it’s just one of those nights. I dunno.

Man City faced their French doppelgangers PSG and lost 4-2. How interesting it was to read Luis Enrique before this game give his backing to City, saying:

I hope everything works out well for Manchester City because I think what they have done on the pitch has been incredible over the last few years and they certainly deserved to win on the pitch all the trophies they have won. The trophies you win on the pitch remain on the pitch. They deserved to win that and I hope they can avoid the sanction.

For some reason, I have this image of Enrique sitting in a glass house with a large collection of stones in front of him, but there’s a sign in big letters that says ‘DO NOT THROW’.

Lance Armstrong still had to do the cycling. Ben Johnson still had to run the races. Nevertheless, isn’t it interesting that the man in charge of a club/team which has essentially been built in the very same way as Man City doesn’t view what happened there as worthy of sanction? Obviously what he thinks will have no bearing on what ultimately happens, nor should it. But perhaps there’s some cognitive dissonance at play here because applying careful consideration to the success you’ve had at a club like that might make it feel like less of an achievement, regardless of how well you’ve moulded a particular group of players into a very effective and, at times, entertaining team.

This year we had the first Enhanced Games, where athletes could compete and take Performance Enhancing Drugs. It’s the brainchild of some cunt who probably thinks generative Ai is great, but will inevitably be on the end of multiple lawsuits when juiced-up ‘roid heads take part and their enlarged hearts blow up mid-event while hardly anyone’s watching because this will be of zero interest to people who actually love sport. Maybe we can make an Enhanced League and just have Man City and PSG play each other every week, until it descends into a kind of dystopian last-man-standing thing. Erling Haaland is The Running Man. Watch out Erling, Khvicha is after you!

Elsewhere, it’s being reported that after hiring a team of consultants, Arsenal are set to cut up to 60 jobs in the name of ‘efficiency’. On the one hand, I guess every business will look at how they can streamline operations, to be more cost-effective, and to see where changes can be made to, effectively, save money. On the other, football clubs aren’t really normal businesses.

You have some people earning the average wage, you have others earning many multiples of that, and to be fair, that’s because they possess an extraordinary level of talent (mostly), in an industry where that is rewarded handsomely. Players and managers at this level will make that money at Arsenal, or if they can’t, they’ll go somewhere else to get it. That’s the job market, supply and demand.

However, in order to create the perfect environment for those people to operate at their very best level, there’s a supporting cast, so to speak, who are hugely important. These days football clubs like Arsenal require a large staff, not just on the football side of things (coaches, physios, medical), but behind the scenes too. The people we never see. The people who keep things running. New technologies might impact some people. Do you need someone on the end of a ticketing phone line when an automated chatbot can do the job? Maybe not as well, but well enough to save you a few salaries, and the net result is increased customer frustration because we’re fucking tired of being asked to talk to robots as if they’re sentient or know anything at all.

Grim, but this is the world we live in. We are owned by American billionaires with a particular corporate culture, so it should come as no surprise when that manifests itself in things like this. At the same time though, it’s hard to justify when the people who are going to lose their jobs and be made redundant are the ones whose salaries most likely contribute the least to our wage bill. A quick Google says the average wage in the UK is around £40,000 a year, and while I’m sure some of those cuts will also impact people with higher salaries than that, 60 job cuts at 40k = £2,400,000.

I realise this is very simplistic, but that’s about the salary of one decent player. It’s probably a good deal less than we’ve paid an agent to complete the transfer of a fairly average player. Mikel Arteta’s reported new salary is £20m a year. I don’t begrudge him that, as I said before we could pay twice as much and end up with a manager half as good, but when you see those wages, when you see a club spend tens of millions on players (and I realise the two things are connected, if not explicitly), and then you read about regular people losing their jobs, it just doesn’t feel right. Especially given the record revenues, massive uptick in commercial income, prize monies, and more of recent seasons.

I get it. It’s business, but a faceless consultation group coming in and the end result means 60 job cuts dressed up as efficiency when we all know it’s cost-cutting is the worst kind of faceless capitalism there is. I find it so hard to square the circle of how a club which exists in a community and does incredible work there, work which should be commended and appreciated by Arsenal fans far and wide, can just take a scythe to a significant section of its workforce and expect fans to just be ok with it. I know the footballing side of things is probably sacrosanct, but I’d wager it’d be easier to find the ‘efficiencies’ they want by being better there in terms of something as basic as recruitment than sacking 60 people. But what do I know?
