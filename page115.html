With nine trophies in their history, the Columbus Crew are among the most successful teams in Major League Soccer, but it has been two years since their last trophy, as much of the team has shifted and wasn't involved in their last trophy, a Leagues Cup win in 2024. Now, as they gear up to host the Lamar Hunt U.S. Open Cup final on Wednesday against St. Louis City SC (live on Paramount+ and CBS Sports Network, 8 p.m. ET), it's a chance to salvage the season, and there are still players there who have experience winning, even in this competition.

During the summer, forward Josef Martinez joined the club after what he described as a tough time with Club Tijuana in Liga MX. While at the moment, it was a move that felt like it should be made leaving CF Montreal, sometimes those moves don't work out, but Martinez is also grateful to be with the Crew currently, where he has been able to get back to full fitness and have a chance to contribute now with the chance to win a trophy.

"We have to recognize that this is an opportunity to turn on the little bit of a struggle season; this is the opportunity to win something," Martinez said. "I have had the opportunity to win one time; I lost one in Miami, and this is my third one. So, it's special when you have a final. I don't remember how many players have the opportunity to play that."

Martinez won the Open Cup with Atlanta United in 2018 while also capturing the Golden Boot, MLS Cup, and MVP awards all in that same season. Things haven't gone as expected recently with the Crew being his fifth team in the past three years but that doesn't mean that the 33-year-old Venezuelan doesn't have something to offer the squad both on and off the pitch as they look to secure their first trophy post-Wilfried Nancy. And no matter what, that's a moment worth looking forward to.

"A final is special, man. It's an opportunity to win something with the club. That is the most important right now," Martinez said, summing up the occasion.

This isn't something that the Crew will need extra motivation for, as when you're playing a sport that you love, the goal is to get to a moment like this. Martinez may have already etched his name in MLS history, but for many on the team, they'll be looking to him and Mendez to set an example on Wednesday. It's something that Martinez is aware of, and it's a role that he relishes as a veteran of the league. He came to Atlanta to prove that he could make it and became "El Rey" (The king) for a reason, and even at his age, he's still a forward who strikes fear into the opposition, even if they're unbeaten in 22 straight games like St. Louis are.

"We're professional," Martinez said. "We know what the game is; we know what the situation is right now. And we know that is important. We have to go there and play soccer and fight. That's most important. We play soccer. We don't play any other sport. We have pressure; but they have pressure too. So, I think [who] makes fewer mistakes, they can have the opportunity to win."

Being able to deal with that pressure is something that Martinez brings to this squad, which has undergone plenty of change. The Crew may not make the playoffs as they're 11th in the Eastern Conference, so they'll need to look at this as a moment that will make or break their season. But when looking across the pitch, St. Louis City SC have never been in a final in their history so the pressure will be felt both ways but Martinez's goals can make the difference.

"Yeah, we're happy [to be in the final], man. When we score, we strikers want to score every game," Martinez said. "And we get frustrated when we can't do, we can't do not that. But it's a game. We have to be ready for all of this. And that will be a tough game because they are a really good team."

Martinez will have the squad ready for the task at hand as it's a moment where both he and the Crew can get back to their best while being able to host a final in front of their home fans. You can't ask for a better chance than this in what will be an excellent final.

#1376. Victories like this show Bournemouth's top stars that the club is no stepping stone as they enjoy another night to cherish in 3-0 humbling of Ruben Amorim's AC Milan, writes KIERAN GILL

📅 26 Oct 2026 | other | Source

Published: 22:20 BST, 22 October 2026 | Updated: 23:57 BST, 22 October 2026

At £15 a pop, the half-and-half scarf sellers outside of the Vitality Stadium were cleaning up, and while those souvenirs are often derided as cringe fodder for tourists, there was no blaming those Bournemouth fans queueing to buy one this time.

On the one half, Bournemouth, a club beaten 2-1 at home by Bury in League One 15 years ago on this night. On the other, AC Milan, the Italian behemoths and seven-time conquerors of Europe.

It was only once inside the Vitality that those supporters found a free scarf awaiting them courtesy of their club, reading: ‘We are all going on a European tour.’ Not to worry. They would happily take multiple mementoes from this – the night AFC Bournemouth famously flattened AC Milan.

There was a special feel to it all, from the fireworks exploding over the Vitality to the playing of Andy Williams's The Impossible Dream to the enormous tifo reading: ‘How did we end up here?’

A few in the Milan end may have been asking themselves the same, especially when their once-great team was trailing 1-0 after eight minutes and 2-0 after 32 in this Europa League fixture.

Both of those goals were penalties – Justin Kluivert scoring one and Marcus Tavernier the other – while Milan then spent the remainder of this match playing with 10 men as their goalkeeper Mike Maignan was red carded for shoving Tyler Adams in the face.

Bournemouth rolled over visitors AC Milan 3-0 in a historic outing at the Vitality Stadium

15 years on from facing liquidation, fans will savour this impressive showing on the continent

By the end, when Amine Adli delivered the coup de grâce with a 25-yard free-kick, Ruben Amorim could only laugh. Literally, the former Manchester United manager now in charge of Milan chuckled as Bournemouth’s supporters serenaded him with: ‘Ruben Amorim, it’s happened again.’

Considering one Italian journalist in the press box angrily ripped his accreditation to smithereens as the visitors trailed 3-0, Amorim’s smiling response may not go down too well back home.

Yet our newspaper counterparts from Italy saw this coming. They were wearing a told-you-so look after it all, having warned us this would happen. ‘You don’t understand,’ one said when asked before kick-off. ‘This isn’t the AC Milan of old.’ Indeed it is not, and Amorim tried to hide behind Maignan’s dismissal afterwards: ‘We need 11 players on the pitch at all times. We killed our game.’

Bournemouth boss Marco Rose added: ‘Our club, we stay humble. We know we are good. If we have the whole squad, we know we can compete with anyone, anywhere. But the season is long. We have so many games. Sunday, Wednesday, Sunday. It’s so demanding.’

Bournemouth might not have even existed were it not for their former chairman Jeff Mostyn, who was here as a fan, writing another £100,000 cheque to save them from liquidation in 2008.

This represented a major milestone for them in their first-ever season in Europe, and while Bournemouth were happy to embrace this as extraordinary on this occasion, they want these nights to become the norm. Their owner, Bill Foley, is striving for that and victories like this cannot hurt show their better players such as Chelsea target Alex Scott why this club is no stepping stone.

Bournemouth won here even without the injured Scott. Rose’s side took the lead when Evanilson headed Tavernier’s cross, but Koni De Winter handled the ball on its way wide. Nobody in the stadium noticed it, but VAR Dennis Higler of the Netherlands did, and then referee Vassilis Fotias of Greece did, too, via his monitor. Kluivert rolled the ball into the bottom-right corner for 1-0.

Amorim’s anger with his players was already rising in the technical area when Maignan pushed Adams in the face at a Bournemouth corner. The whistle was blown. Again, hardly a soul in the crowd knew what was happening until Maignan was shown a straight red card.

AC Milan are a far cry from their status in the game as the seven-time European champions

Milan were further reduced when Mike Maignan was handed a straight red card for pushing Tyler Adams in the face

Needing to bring on replacement goalkeeper Lorenzo Torriani, the unfortunate one sacrificed was Omari Hutchinson, the 22-year-old whose shin pads carry a picture of himself representing England’s Under 21s. A new taker in Tavernier made it 2-0.

Adli’s free-kick in the 84th minute capped this victory with Milan ending the match without a single shot on target. Even with 10 men, that was an embarrassing statistic.

This was a night to cherish for Bournemouth, now winners in all three of their Europa League outings. Indeed, a fair few will hang those scarves in their homes as a reminder. But as a club aiming to go places, they hope this is just the first of many matches with European heavyweights.

#1373. Political psychology: Why extremist parties are thriving and what democratic politicians can do against it

📅 26 Oct 2026 | other | Source

Far-right and far-left parties are celebrating success after success, leaving moderate politicians in the dust and worrying many Germans. Political psychology helps us understand what's behind growing polarization.

On September 6, Germany's eastern state of Saxony-Anhalt held elections for its state parliament. The big winner: The right-wing AfD (Alternative for Germany) party, which garnered 43.8% of the votes; more than ever before.

It's still unclear who will govern the state because the AfD does not have an absolute majority.

But it was a landslide win for the AfD, whose Saxony-Anhalt branch is classified as right-wing extremist by Germany's domestic intelligence agency, and that worries many Germans.

The day after the election, Chancellor Friedrich Merz from the conservative CDU said he was "deeply shocked" by the election results. "Not only did something change in Saxony-Anhalt yesterday but throughout all of Germany."

Merz's CDU lost more than half its share of the ballot compared to the last state election in Saxony-Anhalt. The CDU dropped from 37.1% in 2021 to 17.2% in 2026.

Two weeks later, at state elections in Mecklenburg-Western Pomerania on September 20, the CDU didn't even make the 5% threshold to enter parliament.

It was the first time ever for the CDU; they got only 4.9% of the vote in the northeastern state. The strongest party was once again the AfD with 38.2%.

That same day, in the city state of Berlin, the socialist Left Party won the election and will likely govern Germany's capital. In Berlin, the CDU was the second-strongest party and the AfD came in third.

These state elections show that parties on the extreme ends of the political spectrum are growing stronger in Germany.

The country has experience with both extreme right- and left-wing governments: the Nazis that led Germany into World War II and the Socialist Unity Party (SED) that ruled then-East Germany from 1949 until 1989.

A return to extreme politics is a scary proposition for some people.

In a representative monthly survey called "What worries the world?", the polling institute Ipsos found that almost one-fifth of Germans (18%) said they were worried by growing extremism in the country when they asked about 1,000 people between the ages of 16 and 74 years in March and April 2026.

Generally, people in Germany are concerned about where their country is going. In the Ipsos survey, 82% said they saw Germany as moving in the wrong direction.

Asking people about their fears or worries is just one way to take the nation's temperature. There are other options to measure politically relevant emotions in the population as well.

Kerstin Pahl, a researcher at the Max Planck Institute for Human Development in Berlin, told DW that possibilities include quantitative and qualitative surveys, as well as analyzing online discourse and the content people share on social media. It's also possible to read a nation's sentiment by observing real-life displays of emotions, like public protests and demonstrations.

For researchers like Pahl, it's also interesting to see how political issues can come to symbolize much more than their material or factual meaning.

"People may see their region's ailing infrastructure not just as proof of a lack of investment, but also as a sign that they have been forgotten [by their leaders]," said Pahl.

Extremist parties winning a higher percentage of the votes is what we see on the surface. Below that lie two kinds of societal polarization: Affective and ideological polarization.

"Affective polarization is a growing hatred for the political opponent. Most studies investigated this in the context of the US between supporters of the Democrat and the Republican Party," Eva Walther, head of the social psychology department at the University of Trier in western Germany, told DW. "When this happens on the party level, it's a problem."

Politicians of different parties often have to work together and reach compromises. They don't need to like each other, but they should treat each other with respect, Walther said.

"When this respectful attitude starts crumbling, it's a problem for democracy," she added.

The other phenomenon, ideological polarization, is when society is split into distinct groups based on what issues people view as the most pressing problem for them.

For example, many people to the right of the political center in Germany see migration as the biggest problem, while many people to the left of the political center say it's the climate crisis.

When different crisis situations occur at the same time, such as migration and climate change, researchers refer to a "polycrisis."

Polycrises intensify ideological polarization, making it even harder for societies to find a common way forward.

"At the moment, we have the climate crisis overlapping with an affordable energy crisis, more of an economic problem," Walther said.

In the eyes of those for whom the climate crisis is the most urgent issue, fossil fuels should be abandoned as soon as possible. But for those most concerned with affordable energy, relying only on renewable energy sources would needlessly raise prices.

The belief that a politician is competent and willing to tackle major challenges, to act, is a big factor in how much people trust democracy.

But that's also what makes overlapping crises so hard to manage: No matter which problems politicians attempt to solve first, one part of the population will be disappointed.

"Polycrises make it difficult for politicians to act, which means that people have less trust that politicians can solve the problems important to them," Walther said.

Right-wing extremist parties, however, aim to "satisfy people by giving them simplified answers to complex problems," added Walther.

This leads people to believe that extremist politicians can solve their problems ― which in turn leads to growing polarization.

But politicians from democratic parties can learn from clinical research to tackle this dilemma, according to Walther.

"When a person is in pain and goes to see a doctor, it's important that the doctor recognizes the patient's pain," she said.

Even if the doctor can't instantly solve the issue and make the pain go away, showing that they take the issue and the patient seriously is crucial. And the same goes for politicians.

"They need to make people's needs the first priority in their daily work," Walther said. "People want to be seen, so it's important for politicians to make it clear that they see and understand their problems."

Whether it's a doctor failing a patient or politicians failing the electorate, patient and electorate are likely to scream — and that's a signal we can all read.

#1368. Why Paris Saint-Germain's clash with Barcelona could be peak Champions League drama

📅 26 Oct 2026 | other | Source

If not listed, please contact your TV provider.

Paris Saint-Germain midfielder Vitinha did not beat around the bush before Tuesday's clash with Barcelona. "I think," he said, "all the ingredients are in place for this to be one of the great games."

The UEFA Champions League's format switch came with the promise that top-tier matchups would come as early as possible every season and on Tuesday, the competition seems poised to live up to its word. Two favorites to win the whole thing, PSG and Barcelona, will meet in the French capital in the first barometer of their abilities to go the distance but that is merely scratching the surface on the excitement around this particular game.

PSG and Barcelona are not only amongst Europe's elites but they each boast a collection of world-renowned talent, all of whom are poised to showcase moments of excellence no matter which way the game swings. Players like Ousmane Dembele and Lamine Yamal are headliners for teams that prefer to entertain when they take to the pitch, making them must-watches on a regular basis. History proves that these two sides rarely ever cancel each other out, either. If anything, their similarities only bolster the sheer entertainment every time PSG and Barcelona match up, the pair responsible for several Champions League classics over the years.

Tuesday's match is likely to be no exception. It is also on course to become the greatest league phase match ever staged if history even comes close to repeating itself.

Here's why Barcelona's trip to PSG is worth the watch.

Date: Tuesday, Oct. 20 | Time: 3 p.m. ETLocation: Parc des Princes -- ParisLive stream: Paramount+Odds: Paris Saint-Germain +145; Draw +350; Barcelona +140

True to form for two teams contending for Europe's top club prize, PSG and Barcelona have each assembled an impressive collection of attacking talent who are worth watching on a regular basis. Their particular skillsets vary but one thing they all seem to have in common – none of them shy away from the limelight, and so several are poised to create a highlight-reel worthy moment on Tuesday at the Parc des Princes.

The most notable among them, of course, is Barcelona's Lamine Yamal. The 19-year-old has nine goals and 10 assists across all competitions to start the season, each goal contribution a reminder that he is already a generational talent even with much of his career ahead of him. His goal in Saturday's 5-2 win over Real Betis is no exception, his strike from the edge of the penalty area worth watching over and over. Yamal will be a central figure in Tuesday's game, the teenager essentially a make-or-break player for an aspirational Barcelona.

THAT IS ABSOLUTELY OUTRAGEOUS FROM LAMINE YAMAL 😱 pic.twitter.com/vJb1FyQoEa

"The best way to stop Lamine Yamal is to keep the ball away from Barcelona," PSG manager Luis Enrique said Monday. "If he cannot get the ball, everything is calm. That would be ideal, but we know it won't be the case because it is difficult to play against Barcelona and Lamine Yamal. The way to defend a team with their quality is to be wary and approach it as a team. But it's not just Lamine. There's Raphinha, Pedri … there are a lot of top players."

Enrique is right that Yamal is not Barcelona's only attacking threat – Raphinha has been a staple since Hansi Flick took over as head coach two seasons ago, while Anthony Gordon's addition this summer has helped. Flick's side's attacking output has increased year-over-year. Barcelona average 4.3 goals from 3.7 expected goals per game this season in LaLiga, up from 2.5 goals from 2.3 expected goals in the previous campaign.

PSG have plenty of attacking might to call upon on Tuesday, too. Enrique's side splits the goalscoring responsibilities between a few players, including Ballon d'Or winner Ousmane Dembele and France international Desire Doue. Both scored in last week's 4-2 win at Manchester City, while Spain World Cup winner Ferran Torres leads their scoring charts in Ligue 1. Then there's Khvicha Kvaratskhelia, who might just be PSG's most dynamic player on his best days. It is entirely unpredictable which players will rise above the rest on Tuesday but one glance at the list of options tells enough of a story – one of them is bound to showcase the range of their abilities on Tuesday, likely to leave many in awe along the way.

As PSG cruised to a win at City on Matchday 2, the back-to-back Champions League winners only bolstered their case as the team to beat in Europe for another year running. It is hard not to describe them as the favorites on Tuesday as a result but this time around, Barcelona might actually be able to go toe-to-toe with Enrique's dominant side.

Since Flick took charge two years ago, Barcelona have lived and died by the sword. The manager's attack-minded style was supposed to compensate for a leaky back line and for the most part, the plan has worked, so much so that they have two domestic titles to show for it. Down the stretch in the Champions League where margins define games, though, their defense had proved costly, most notably in the 2024-25 semifinals in which Inter beat them 7-6 on aggregate. Things have taken a turn in the right direction in recent weeks, though, because Barcelona signed the one player who is essentially a magical solution – World Cup winner Rodri.

The midfielder is well-known for his jack of all trades, master of all-like tendencies, a skillset that earned him the Ballon d'Or two years ago and the World Cup Golden Ball award this summer. He fills in the gaps that led Barcelona to concede some unimpressive goals over the last couple of years and should be up for the task of doing so against PSG on Tuesday. Like Yamal, Rodri will also be instrumental in Barcelona's ability to come out of Paris with three points.

"I imagine a beautiful battle [in midfield]," Pedri said. "Both midfields suffer more when they do not have the ball so whoever has the ball tomorrow will have an advantage. We arrive with a good dynamic and with confidence."

Rodri's arrival, alongside Barcelona's bolstered attack, ultimately allows the visitors to test this season's big hypothesis at an early juncture of the campaign.

"It will be a good test for us to show how far we are [from PSG]," Flick said. "I would like my team to play with confidence, play brave and, of course, play together as a team. We did it in the last 11 games; now we play against, if you see the last two years, the best team in the world. For us, it's a good opportunity to show our level, show what we can do and [see] what we have to improve. After this match, we will also know a lot of things."

There is sometimes a dissonance between what makes for an important match and what makes for an entertaining match but if history is anything to go by with PSG and Barcelona, it is one and the same for these teams. There will be something for everyone, no matter what exactly your preferred brand of fun happens to be.

Enrique and Flick will do what they can to cancel out the other, a tactical chess match of sorts set to take place in Paris on Tuesday. Each coach is determined to embrace their own versions of an attack-minded style of play but in their pre-match remarks, both were willing to admit that shutting the other team out entirely is perhaps unrealistic. They seem prepared for the ebbs and flows of the game, underscored by a respect for the opponent without compromising their own ambitions.

"I love watching Barça, the way they play," Enrique said. "It's great to see the ambition and identity, which is similar to what we are looking for. But of course I would love to win tomorrow, as a Parisian, and we want to become the first team to beat Barça this season."

That balance of respecting the opponent while sticking true to each team's identity will be the theme of Tuesday's match, and is also the very reason why entertainment feels like a near guarantee. Enrique's task of neutralizing Yamal will be just as exciting for the tactics-obsessed observer as it will be for the more casual fan, in part because the only way Enrique's PSG know how to win is through an attack-minded approach. Similarly, Flick's attempts to overpower PSG's indomitable press will be entertaining regardless of what compelled you to turn the game on. The entertainment value is just as notable as each team's ambitions of winning the Champions League, a defining feature of an event that has the feeling of a big game even if the knockouts are months away.

#1362. Bundesliga, other top European leagues call for major FIFA reforms

📅 26 Oct 2026 | other | Source

The Bundesliga and three other leading European football leagues have said major reforms to world-governing body FIFA are "essential." However, they stopped short of calling for the resignation of boss Gianni Infantino.

The letter released this Friday and signed by the heads of the Bundesliga, the Premier League, La Liga and Serie A called for major changes to how FIFA operates — and for a greater say in decision-making.

"Football's ​longstanding foundations have, in recent years, been threatened by a fundamental governance problem, whereby FIFA's president has actively promoted exploitative ​ideas rather than safeguarding against them," it said.

FIFA President Gianni Infantino has been facing a wave of criticism since late July, when his plan for private investment through a commercial vehicle, FIFA Forward Enterprise (FFE), emerged. The idea was for FFE to organize FIFA's tournaments while maximizing profits.

Vigorous opposition from UEFA, European football's governing body, as well as CONCACAF (North and Central America plus the Caribbean) and the Asian Football Confederation led Infantino to quickly drop the plan.

However, the league bosses noted that this had not solved a deeper problem and appeared to dismiss a recent announcement by Infantino of an independent external review of FIFA governance as well as consultations on strengthening decision-making processes.

"It is not enough for surface-level, process changes to be explored in the aftermath of FFE — fundamental governance reform is essential to restore clear constitutional boundaries within FIFA, including confidence that individual disciplinary matters remain demonstrably insulated from political interference," Friday's letter from the leagues said.

The last line was in reference to the widespread discontent around FIFA's handling of the Folarin Balogun case, with the United States striker's one-match ban being suspended to allow him to play a World Cup last-16 match against Belgium in July. While the ban was imposed by FIFA's disciplinary committee, it came after US President Donald Trump had personally called Infantino asking for a "review" of the ban — something Trump publicly admitted.

The leagues on Friday called for greater checks and balances to be implemented at FIFA, while at the same time demanding that they and others be given a say in decisions such as increases in the number of international matches.

The letter went on to say that "… when FIFA takes major unilateral decisions directly affecting us — for example, by expanding the International Match Calendar (IMC) and extending the periods in which domestic competitions must pause — the stakeholders most affected are not meaningfully involved in the decision-making process.

"A FIFA governance that concentrates unchecked regulatory, commercial and political power in a single office, while relegating affected stakeholders to mere advisory status, is not fit for purpose."

The letter, while implying much criticism of Infantino, did not call for him to step down 3 it didn't even mention him by name.

The letter comes as UEFA has said it is looking for a suitable candidate to challenge Infantino in the next presidential election in March, in which Infantino has said he will run for reelection.

UEFA has also called for an independent external review of how the FFE plan was developed, and has filed legal applications through the US courts for the disclosure of documents related to the plan. This was with a view to a possible criminal complaint being lodged in Switzerland if it is established that Infantino undervalued FFE at $20 billion (€17.5 billion).

FIFA has described UEFA's legal moves as a "smear campaign" against it but has not yet commented on Friday's letter from the league bosses.

Infantino aims to discuss his proposals at a FIFA Council meeting in Zurich on October 15, a week after UEFA is to hold a meeting of all of its 55 member associations on October 8 in Berlin.

Any person aiming to challenge Infantino in the March 18, 2027 FIFA presidential election must register as a candidate by November 18.

#1361. Why Nottingham Forest are standing by Oliver Glasner despite Austrian sidelining influential players in his dressing room - and the numbers that back up George Syrianos

📅 26 Oct 2026 | other | Source

Published: 02:00 BST, 23 October 2026 | Updated: 02:00 BST, 23 October 2026

It is awfully quiet at Nottingham Forest these days.

At any other time in the last four years, five points from seven games and 19th in the Premier League would have had alarm bells ringing at the City Ground and possibly cost the manager his job. By this time last year, Forest had already sacked Nuno Espirito Santo and Ange Postecoglou.

If things feel different now, that is probably because they are. Oliver Glasner was hired at huge expense to bring his trophy-winning magic to Forest and the Austrian is usually a slow starter.

Besides, despite only one win from his first eight games in all competitions, the underlying numbers say Forest have been unfortunate and are due a run of better luck.

None of that changes the importance of Friday’s trip to Ipswich.

Oliver Glasner has had a disappointing start at Nottingham Forest, with his side sitting 19th in the Premier League after one win in seven

Some doubt whether Glasner can harness a free attacking spirit like Morgan Gibbs-White in his rigid tactical framework

Forest’s chief football officer George Syrianos drove Glasner’s appointment and will likely stand or fall by how it proceeds.

In recent meetings with supporters’ groups, Syrianos emphasised that this was a long-term project. According to Forest's modelling, Glasner is the third-best coach in the Premier League at coaxing his squad to play above their potential. Success with him requires patience.

Good, rational explanations for a good, rational appointment. Yet few things are as difficult as sticking to principles when a team keeps losing.

Syrianos has had various roles at Forest but now arguably holds more power than ever before. He pushed Glasner to Evangelos Marinakis when Steve Cooper was sacked in December 2023, only for the Forest owner to choose Nuno Espirito Santo instead. Glasner would eventually land at Palace and we all know what happened next – three major honours following decade upon trophyless decade.

Forest have hired Glasner to win things. Delivering the FA Cup, Community Shield and Conference League at Palace was a stunning effort and at a club of similar dimension, the hierarchy believe he can do the same.

Forest are already out of the Carabao Cup so depending on their league position in January, we can expect them to attack the FA Cup.

Marinakis wants to be back in Europe as quickly as possible and given the strength of some of England’s wealthiest clubs, a good cup run might be more realistic than a top-six finish. Right now, just moving away from the relegation zone would be a relief.

Forest’s chief football officer George Syrianos drove Glasner’s appointment and will likely stand or fall by how it proceeds

No doubt about it – Glasner deserves time. Few Forest bosses since Brian Clough arrived with a better track record than Glasner’s. Despite never managing among Europe’s elite, he still has four trophies, including two European titles. To rip up those plans after a handful of league games would be impulsive even by Forest’s standards.

Miltiadis Marinakis, the son of Evangelos, is a regular visitor to the club’s training base and was there again this week. He will be aware of the statistics that indicate Forest deserve to be higher in the table than they are.

You can take your pick of the Opta data that suggests Forest are hard done by. According to the Expected Points table, Forest’s performances should have them in sixth. By the Expected Goals metric, Forest should have 11 goals rather than five. No other Premier League club is underperforming those standards so dramatically.

Forest’s defensive numbers are good, but they must sharpen their finishing. They have the fourth-worst big chance conversion rate and those five goals have come from 120 shots, with only Manchester United and Brighton taking more.

Two seasons ago, Forest finished seventh by outperforming their underlying numbers to an improbable degree. Maybe the current sequence is simply payback time from the football gods – not that this will be of any comfort to Glasner.

For much of Forest’s top-flight journey, Callum Hudson-Odoi and Ola Aina have been key figures. No longer.

While Aina’s exclusion is logical as Glasner wanted Daniel Munoz from Crystal Palace to play at right wing-back, the decision to sideline Hudson-Odoi is strange. Glasner seems similarly unconvinced by midfielder Ibrahim Sangare, who signed a new deal only three months ago.

With Morgan Gibbs-White injured, Glasner picked the energetic Nico Dominguez against Arsenal over the silkier Hudson-Odoi and even brought on forgotten man Arnaud Kalimuendo ahead of the former Chelsea winger in the second half.

If Glasner stays in charge, the days of both are likely to be numbered. No huge drama for the finances, either – Hudson-Odoi cost £3million in 2023 and Aina joined for free in the same summer. Both would bring Forest a profit if sold.

Yet in the short term, the pair need careful management. Along with Anthony Elanga, Aina and Hudson-Odoi embodied the unbreakable spirit that nearly carried Forest into the Champions League under Nuno in 2025. They remain strong voices in the squad – just like Gibbs-White.

Gibbs-White wanted to leave Forest for at least part of last summer and duly changed his agents. The 26-year-old is believed to be on the radar of Bayern Munich, should Michael Olise join Real Madrid at the end of the season. Few expect Gibbs-White to be at the City Ground by next September.

Between now and May, though, Glasner must extract the best from him.

(From left) Callum Hudson-Odoi, Anthony Elanga and Ola Aina embodied Forest's spirit under Nuno Espirito Santo. But Elanga has left and Hudson-Odoi and Aina are now sidelined

Glasner is convinced he can turn Liam Delap into an elite centre forward - but the £50million man has just one goal in six games

Some doubt whether Glasner can harness a free attacking spirit like Gibbs-White in such a rigid tactical framework. Glasner would counter that by pointing out that Olise and Eberechi Eze flourished under him in a similar system at Palace.

Ultimately, Glasner needs his big bet to pay off. He is convinced he can turn Liam Delap into an elite centre forward and was instrumental in the decision to sign the 23-year-old from Chelsea.

Delap did well against Arsenal but one goal in six is not what you hope for from your £50m No9.

Delap had his breakthrough season at Ipswich and he will hope that returning there will light the fire – and put a few out at the same time.
