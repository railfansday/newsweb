#1020. Michael Carrick's five-minute monologue: Under-fire boss admits Man United have 'lost our way', demands 'humility' from Bruno Fernandes and Co and refuses to say if he'll keep the captaincy

📅 26 Oct 2026 | other | Source

By HARRY BAMFORTH, LIVE SPORTS COVERAGE REPORTER

Published: 13:53 BST, 23 October 2026 | Updated: 20:03 BST, 23 October 2026

Under-pressure Manchester United boss Michael Carrick claimed that the club have 'lost our way' in a five-minute opening speech to the media ahead of their crunch clash with Bournemouth.

There is increasing uncertainty surrounding Carrick's future at Old Trafford following United's humiliating 4-1 defeat away at Como in the Champions League on Wednesday evening.

The defeat by the beautiful lakes of Italy extended United's winless run to seven games in all competitions, with the club not having tasted victory since early September.

The 45-year-old is set to take charge of the Red Devils as they take on the Cherries on Sunday and in his pre-match press conference ahead of the match, Carrick spoke from the heart.

'I'll start today. I just want to say a few things, really,' said Carrick. 'Recently, we haven't had a good time. I think that's pretty obvious. So I totally accept what we're going through at the moment.

'We had five months of relative success in a spell where I was happy with a lot of things I was seeing and that we were doing as a team. We haven't done well enough lately. We've had a spell that we're really not happy with.

Michael Carrick gave a passionate near-six minute address to the media on Friday afternoon

'Wednesday was a bad night. It was a bad night for a lot of reasons. It hurt a lot.

'It obviously hurt me to see us play that way, to see how the game panned out. As I said after the game, it was really unacceptable.

'The supporters are a huge part of this football club. They have to make the effort and the time and money they spend, to feel how we all felt on Wednesday was a tough moment.'

During the impassioned speech, Carrick seemingly issued a call to arms directly to his current squad by saying that Manchester United is 'bigger than any single individual', even listing names of legends such as Sir Alex Ferguson and Sir Bobby Charlton when making his point.

And while Carrick is happy to 'hold the umbrella to keep everyone else dry', he also wants his players to step up and reach the levels demanded by the 20-time champions.

Carrick continued: 'We need to respect this football club and respect what it means to be here, respect what it takes to win, what you have to do to win.

'I'm not entitled to be here. Entitlement cannot be at this football club. We're not entitled to win. We've won in the past, we've got an incredible history, but that doesn't mean that you're entitled to just win.

'I'm fully aware of that. It takes hard work, takes a lot of sacrifice. You've got to invest yourself in the football club and you've got to do a lot of things that maybe aren't so nice, but you've got to do it for each other, and you've got to win.

United are winless in seven games after being humiliated by Como in the Champions League on Wednesday

Carrick refused to say whether had had spoken to Bruno Fernandes since his captain questioned the head coach's tactics

'I think it's important we live up to that, and it's important for me to push those values, and it's important for our supporters to feel that moving forward.

'We've had it, we've kind of lost our way a little bit if I'm totally honest, and we will get it back. I'm totally convinced we'll get it back. Respect this football club, respect what it takes, because it's very, very important to me.'

Following the speech, Carrick was asked to address whether he is considering stripping Bruno Fernandes of the club captaincy after the midfielder questioned his tactics following the defeat to Como. He refused to answer directly.

However, Carrick claimed that the dressing room is 'not divided' despite the outspoken nature of Fernandes and Lisandro Martinez in recent weeks.

Martinez was first to break ranks, taking aim at his Old Trafford bosses – and director of football Jason Wilcox in particular – following a 2-0 defeat to Atletico Madrid in the Champions League last week. Fernandes then followed suit to question why United were so passive again.

He said: 'It's a decision from the staff to not press man to man and sometimes leave space behind and try to get the ball, wait for them, but sometimes when you give space to these teams they get confidence.'

Asked if he meant that Carrick got his tactics wrong, Fernandes added: 'That's on the manager and staff to say, not me.

‘Sometimes you have to be more brave under pressure, take the risk of pressing high and making them uncomfortable. As you saw with Como, the centre back was coming to mark me in my own half. We need to do that as a team but we were unable to.

‘A team that plays through the middle with small connections, if you give them too much time and space then they can hurt you out wide.’

#1018. Lego and Daft Punk just announced an electric collab: Everything you need to know

📅 26 Oct 2026 | other | Source

Electronic music purists and brick builders, this one is for you: Daft Punk is officially entering the Lego game.

Following a cryptic teaser that interrupted the duo's WDPK 83.7 stream on Oct. 21, Lego has now confirmed a multi-year partnership with the legendary artists. The shining star of the announcement? Lego has officially greenlit a retail set based on Daft Punk’s 2007 Alive pyramid stage, complete with custom minifigures of Thomas Bangalter and Guy-Manuel de Homem-Christo behind a mixing console made out of bricks.

The approved concept recreates the glowing pyramid from the 2007 world tour — arguably the most influential live stage production in EDM history. Alongside this exciting stage build, the official launch film features the helmeted robots and a giant holographic red brick, confirming that official Daft Punk minifigures are finally hitting shelves.

Here's the catch: the pyramid stage build from Daft Punk’s 2007 Alive tour will not be available to buy for some time. Lego confirmed that while the partnership is official today, final product launches and retail availability are slated for 2027.

The original fan submission relied on intricate translucent bricks and custom lighting rigs. Whether Lego retains the full LED light-brick integration remains to be seen. If Lego does somehow pull off the illuminated stage lighting and chrome-finished helmets, this will easily be one of the fastest sellouts in Lego Ideas history.

Julia Goldin, Chief Product and Marketing Officer at the Lego Group said, "What makes this partnership so special is the enduring appeal both brands share. Like the LEGO brand, Daft Punk inspires generations through creativity, innovation, and a deep connection with fans. We’re excited to build on these foundations to reimagine the Daft Punk legacy in a fresh and unexpected way, creating something unique that resonates with fans across all ages. This is just the beginning of a bigger journey — a creative collab made for fans ready to lose themselves to play.”

#1017. Ronaldo stays on under cloud as Messi exits internationals

📅 26 Oct 2026 | other | Source

Just hours before Lionel Messi played his last match for Argentina, Cristiano Ronaldo posted his latest salvo in a row with Portugal. Ronaldo wants to play on despite the troubles, while Messi bathed in adulation.

They are the two players who have defined the last two decades. As football became ever more popular, commercial and dominant in the cultural and sporting landscape, Cristiano Ronaldo and Lionel Messi remained its twin peaks.

Along with that has come endless social media posts, stories and discussions about who is better. Does Messi's World Cup and two Copa America titles with Argentina trump Ronaldo's Euro 2016 win and two Nations League titles? Or does the Portuguese's greater number of goals mean more? How about their club honors?

It is, essentially, a subjective argument and not one that can ever be resolved. It remains to be seen whether that's also true of the public spat between Ronaldo and his Portugal coach Jorge Jesus.

On Tuesday, Messi scored a 126th goal in his 208th game for Argentina. Shortly before he did, Ronaldo posted a 2,000-word statement in which he stopped short of quitting the national team but said there was an "absolute breakdown of trust" between him and the coach.

Ronaldo's comments refer to Portugal's recent match against Norway in which he said he was initially to be rested before being asked to warm up as a substitute without ever being brought on. The striker, 41, walked away from the team's camp for the remainder of the international break, but said the story is not over.

"I can promise all my fans, and naturally especially all Portuguese people, that we will have one or more farewell matches. There will be time for that," said Ronaldo, who plays for Al-Nassr in Saudi Arabia.

"Despite the absolute breach of trust in the coach's word, as anyone who witnessed these conversations can attest, I should not have done it," Ronaldo said. "The responsibility is entirely mine. I apologize to the Portuguese people once again, but the breaking of one's word has, for me, a defining impact.

"I felt that, from that point on, my staying would only harm the group, and I did what I have always said I would do if I didn't feel useful: I picked up my bags and left," he continued before suggesting he accepted there may be a need for the coach or federation to punish him.

It was a long way from the emotional scenes in Rosario, Argentina. Instead of anger and recrimination, the overriding emotions there were gratitude, nostalgia and sadness.

"Not coming anymore is going to be deeply painful. I swear I'd like to play for the rest of my life, because playing for the Argentina national team is the most beautiful thing that can happen to you," Messi, who now plays his club football for Inter Miami in the US, told the 80,000 fans who had packed in to see him score a penalty in a 3-0 win over Benin.

For some, the contrasting evenings offered a neat synopsis of the pair. Ronaldo, less naturally gifted than his counterpart, has made the most of his talents with a singular focus which many believe often makes him prioritize himself over the team. He has had similar fallouts with managers and head coaches before, including Jose Mourinho at Real Madrid, Erik ten Hag at Manchester United and Portugal's 2022 World Cup coach, Fernando Santos.

Ronaldo's much-discussed aim to reach 1,000 career goals is held up by some as further evidence of where his motivations lie.

"You know what my goal is. I want to win trophies, and I want to reach that number (1,000 goals) that you all know. I will reach the number for sure, if no injuries," he said last December. He is currently on 979.

For others, that is simply what is required to be the best. While Messi's quiet character and general refusal to play out his life in public suggest a more humble character focused on helping the team, his genius has undoubtedly changed the teams around him. In the 2026 World Cup, he barely broke into a run but still led an otherwise fairly limited Argentina to the final with eight goals.

There will be no more Messi goals for Argentina and, despite his promises, no absolute assurances that the world will see Ronaldo in a Portugal shirt again. For now, both will head back to their club sides to play out their twilight years away from the intensity of Europe's top leagues. But wherever they go from here, the spotlight will keep following.

#1010. This Flock Camera map shows more than 300,000 devices

📅 26 Oct 2026 | other | Source

You probably know where the coffee shops and gas stations are in your neighborhood. But the cameras logging your license plate as you drive past? Those can be a little harder to keep track of.

Proton, the Swiss privacy company behind Proton Mail and Proton VPN, is helping make them easier to find by hosting a free map of Flock surveillance devices on its servers (you can explore the map on Proton's website). Flock tried to get the researcher behind the original project to take it down, according to The Intercept.

If you need a refresher on Flock, the company makes automated license plate readers used by police departments, businesses, schools, and homeowners associations. The cameras photograph passing vehicles whether or not their drivers are suspected of anything. Flock devices can record license plates, times, and locations, plus identifying details such as a vehicle’s make, model, color, and bumper stickers. According to Flock, its cameras log more than 20 billion vehicle sightings each month across 49 states.

Those records then go into Flock's cloud, where police can search them and share them with other agencies across city and state lines. You don’t have to be pulled over for your car to become part of a searchable record of where you’ve been — and that access has been abused. A Washington Post investigation found at least 50 officers accused or charged with misusing license plate readers, often to track people in their personal lives. (Flock's system was involved in 46 of those cases.)

On Proton's map, you can enter your ZIP code to see devices in your area, or zoom and pan around the country. Note that the data is a snapshot from December 2025, though. Cameras installed or removed since then won’t be reflected.

This isn't the first effort to put surveillance cameras on the map (literally). As Mashable previously reported, DeFlock Maps lets people look up license plate readers in their neighborhoods, including devices from Flock, Motorola Solutions, and other manufacturers. Volunteers report cameras they spot and add their locations to OpenStreetMap, the open-source mapping project.

MapQuest also added a "Surveillance Layer" to its app this month, letting users see known license plate reader locations, including Flock cameras. Turn it on under Map Options, and camera markers appear alongside the roads you’re traveling.

The difference here from other crowdsourced projects is that the locations come from Flock's own systems, according to the map's creator.

Security researcher Joshua Michael discovered in November 2025 that Flock's servers were exposing an access token anyone could obtain without logging in. He used it to retrieve an inventory of device locations, reported the issue to Flock, and published his findings and a map in December. That inventory included more than 170,000 cameras and over 130,000 other Flock devices.

Proton has questioned Flock's leadership directly about how its technology is used. In an interview published in August, CEO Garrett Langley said, "Any level of abuse greater than zero is unacceptable and makes me sick," pointing to audit logs and an optional tool designed to detect potentially abusive activity. But he declined to say how many police department contracts Flock had suspended or terminated over abuse. "I don’t think you should hold the entire city accountable for just the bad decisions of a few," he said.

When it came to Michael's research, Flock's response included legal threats, The Intercept reported. The company fixed the exposed-token flaw, while also publicly maintaining that it had never been hacked and that no Flock information had leaked. On his website, Michael says Flock also sent him a cease-and-desist letter (which he published) threatening litigation and told customers it had notified law enforcement, describing him as a "malicious actor."

Michael says that customer email acknowledged Flock had found no indication of unauthorized access when it closed the issue in December. He argues that this illustrates the very concern behind his research: that information could be taken without the company detecting it.

"I’m not backing down," he recently wrote on Instagram.

In September, brand-protection company Doppel also filed a trademark complaint on Flock's behalf. It argued that Michael's website could confuse customers or harm the company, even citing the disclaimer saying it wasn't affiliated with or endorsed by Flock — though Flock had not disputed the map's accuracy, as CyberNews reported.

Proton rejected the trademark argument and published a copy of the map on its Swiss servers in order to keep it available.

"This is an attempt to suppress information, not to prevent consumer confusion," Proton head of legal Marc Løebekken said.

For residents, that means another way to find out what's watching their streets — and a starting point for questions to local officials. As Proton puts it on the map's website: "You have a right to know who's recording you when you leave home."

Anaerobic degradation of complex organic compounds to methane and carbon dioxide is common in the deep subsurface biosphere and has long been considered to require syntrophic cooperation, with methanogens confined to the terminal use of simple C1 and C2 substrates. Thermodynamic considerations, multi-omics analyses and microcosm experiments suggest that archaeal lineages are capable of methanogenic degradation of complex substrates1,2,3,4,5,6. However, experimental validation in pure culture was lacking. Here we report the isolation of a thermophilic archaeon, Methanoglobus dolicholipophagus AA-75, affiliated with Archaeoglobi, from a Chinese petroleum reservoir. Strain AA-75 not only converts methanol into methane, carbon dioxide and acetate, but also uses saturated fatty acids varying from C7:0 to C18:0 and unsaturated fatty acids including C16:1, C18:1 and C18:2 to produce methane. We term the latter lipotrophic methanogenesis, which integrates β-oxidation of fatty acids with the Wood–Ljungdahl pathway and the canonical methyl-tetrahydromethanopterin:coenzyme M methyltransferase and methyl-coenzyme M reductase complexes, together with an intracellular electron transfer system. A formaldehyde-methanol branch probably functions as an auxiliary route for C1 metabolism during methanogenesis. Methane produced from stearate is moderately 13C-enriched (δ13C–CH4 = −40.0‰ to −36.3‰), distinct from the strong 13C depletion characteristic of canonical methanogenic pathways. These findings demonstrate that methanogens can function as primary degraders of complex organic matter, challenging the classical syntrophic paradigm and calling for refinement of current models of carbon cycling in the deep subsurface biosphere.

#1007. Suspected pneumonic plague in Siberia: What we know about the disease

Even so, distrust of how Russia handles information persists. On one of DW's Russian-language social media channels, a user wrote: "It wouldn't surprise me if this turns into a disaster now, both in terms of the spread and the quarantine measures."

All that is known for certain, at the time of writing and first publication (October 5, 2026), is that pneumonic plague is highly contagious.

Pneumonic plague is a form of plague that infects the lungs. Like every form of the disease, it is caused by Yersinia pestis, a bacterium usually found in small mammals, such as rodents and in the fleas that feed on them.

The World Health Organization describes the pneumonic form as the most virulent type of plague.

Its incubation period can be as short as 24 hours, and it is the only form that passes easily from person to person through respiratory particles released when a sick patient coughs. This is why, according to the WHO, it can trigger severe epidemics.

Symptoms appear quickly: high fever, headache and weakness, followed by a rapidly worsening pneumonia with shortness of breath, chest pain and a cough that often brings up blood.

A person can catch pneumonic plague by breathing in the bacteria directly, or by developing it when bubonic and/or septicemic plague go untreated and spread to the lungs.

Pneumonic plague is not a separate disease. It is one of three forms of plague: bubonic, septicemic and pneumonic.

What sets them apart is where the infection settles in the body, and that shapes how the disease spreads and how deadly it is.

Bubonic plague is the most common form and the one most people think of when they hear the word "plague." It usually follows a flea bite. The bacteria travel to the nearest lymph node, which swells into a painful lump known as a bubo. According to the WHO, it rarely spreads between people.

If bubonic plague goes untreated, the bacteria can move further through the body. If they spread into the bloodstream, the disease can become septicemic plague, which causes bleeding under the skin, tissue death in the fingers and toes, and organ failure. If the bacteria reach the lungs, the illness becomes pneumonic plague, the form that can pass from person to person through the air. Both septicemic and pneumonic plague can also develop directly, without a bubo appearing first.

Left untreated, bubonic plague kills between 30-60% of patients. The septicemic and pneumonic forms are always fatal without treatment.

Pneumonic plague is curable, but only if treatment starts quickly. The WHO warns that without treatment it can kill within 18 to 24 hours of the first symptoms.

Common antibiotics can cure the infection, and recovery rates are high when patients are treated within about a day of falling ill.

Speed is what decides the outcome. Doctors are advised not to wait for laboratory confirmation and to start antibiotics as soon as plague is suspected, according to the US Centers for Disease Control and Prevention (CDC).

The drugs themselves are not rare or specialized. They are standard, widely used antibiotics, and patients usually need a course of up to two weeks. To stop the infection spreading, patients are kept in isolation, and wearing masks helps protect those around them, the WHO says.

People close to a patient are treated as well, even if they have no symptoms. Both the WHO and the CDC recommend a seven-day course of preventive antibiotics for close contacts of anyone with pneumonic plague.

The WHO does not recommend a plague vaccine for the public. It reserves vaccination for high-risk groups, such as laboratory staff who work with the bacterium and health workers.

Paris is also hosting its first NFL game on Sunday afternoon, with the match between the Pittsburgh Steelers and New Orleans Saints at the Stade de France expected to go ahead as planned but requiring further police deployment.

PSG have 10 players nominated for the men’s award: Dembele, Achraf Hakimi, Khvicha Kvaratskhelia, Marquinhos, Nuno Mendes, Joao Neves, Willian Pacho, Fabian Ruiz, Ferran Torres and Vitinha.

Warren Zaire-Emery is also nominated for the men’s young talent of the year award, Matvey Safonov is shortlisted for the goalkeeper award, and Luis Enrique is up for men’s coach of the year.

Dembele, Kvaratskhelia and PSG captain Marquinhos may still be able to attend the London ceremony, however, as they are currently sidelined with injuries. PSG confirmed Kvaratskhelia ruptured his ACL in the win over Barcelona in midweek.
