#2555. Alverca vs Arouca - prediction, team news, lineups

📅 26 Oct 2026 | other | Source

By Adeyeye Oluwapelumi | 22 Oct 2026 23:09

Alverca will look to extend their winning run in the Primeira Liga to four matches when they welcome Arouca to the Complexo Desportivo FC Alverca on Saturday.

The hosts sit ninth in the Primeira Liga table after collecting 11 points from eight games, three fewer than the fifth-placed visitors, who have enjoyed a great start to life under new head coach Vitor Severino.

Having marked their long-awaited return to the Portuguese top flight with an 11th-place finish last season, Alverca parted ways with Custodio Castro over the summer, but they appear to be on course for an improved campaign under Sergio Ferreira, whose side have climbed into the top half following an impressive run of results.

The Ribatejanos have collected all nine points available from their last three league outings, beating Nacional 3-1 away and Rio Ave 1-0 at home before delivering their most convincing performance of the campaign in a 4-1 triumph over Famalicao on matchday eight.

Although Chiquinho’s early opener was cancelled out before half time, Alverca raised their game after the interval, with second-half strikes from Figueiredo, Vivaldo Semedo and Andre Vidigal securing an emphatic victory.

The Ribatejanos’ recent resurgence represents a considerable improvement on the opening weeks of the campaign, when Ferreira’s men failed to win any of their first five Primeira Liga fixtures, suffering three defeats and picking up just two points.

However, Alverca were brought back down to earth at the weekend when they suffered a 2-0 extra-time defeat away to Tondela, bringing their Taca de Portugal campaign to an end.

Eager to put that disappointment behind them, the hosts can take encouragement from their home record, having lost just once in their last seven Primeira Liga matches on their own turf, alongside three victories and three draws.

The hosts’ recent progress will face a stern examination against an Arouca side enjoying a promising start under Severino, who has won both of his opening two matches since replacing Vasco Seabra.

Severino began his tenure with a 1-0 league victory over Estrela Amadora before guiding the Wolves into the fourth round of the Taca de Portugal courtesy of a thrilling 4-3 extra-time win against second-tier Belenenses.

The visitors now return to league action seeking consecutive victories for the first time since opening the campaign with back-to-back successes against Vitoria de Guimaraes and Moreirense.

The Wolves’ triumph over Estrela also ended a three-match winless spell in the Primeira Liga, during which a 2-1 home defeat to Santa Clara was sandwiched between draws against Estoril Praia and Sporting Lisbon.

Arouca have scored 11 goals and conceded seven in the league this season, underlining their attacking threat, although their away form remains a concern after three consecutive top-flight matches on the road without a victory (D2, L1).

Alverca are expected to be without Bastien Meupiyou, Julian Martinez and Sabit Abdulai, who are all continuing their respective recoveries from injury.

Chiquinho will be one to watch after registering a goal or an assist in each of his last four league appearances since returning from suspension.

Meanwhile, Arouca remain without left winger Miguel Puche, who is recovering from a serious knee injury sustained last season.

Ivan Barbero is expected to lead the line for the visitors after scoring in consecutive matches across all competitions, with the forward looking to add to his tally of five league goals this term.

#2549. West Ham Set Record Never Before Seen In English Football

📅 26 Oct 2026 | other | Source

West Ham have established an extraordinary attendance record that has never previously been seen anywhere in English league football. Even relegation to the Championship has failed to dent the Hammers’ massive support.

According to statistics published by The Times, the Irons are averaging 60,882 supporters for Championship matches at the London Stadium. That is an astonishing 30,647 more than nearest divisional rivals Wolverhampton Wanderers, whose average is 30,235.

No English league club had previously averaged even 20,000 more than every other team in its division. West Ham have therefore smashed the old record by more than 10,000, which is quite remarkable considering we’re playing in the second tier.

I have never been anything but amazed that West Ham could go from a 34,000-capacity Upton Park to regularly attracting around 60,000 at the London Stadium.

Clubs move grounds, of course, but adding almost 30,000 to the matchday attendance and then maintaining those extraordinary numbers following relegation feels pretty unique. It is difficult to think of another English club whose recorded gates have grown so dramatically following a stadium move.

We previously highlighted how the move has almost doubled West Ham’s average attendance, but this latest statistic places that growth into a remarkable historical context.

West Ham have already recorded the highest Championship attendance of the season, with 62,264 watching the home game against Charlton. The Carabao Cup victory over Portsmouth also attracted a competition-record first-round crowd of 53,369.

West Ham fans were under the impression David Sullivan was on his way out

I believe the excellent season-ticket renewal figures can be explained by two significant factors.

Firstly, interim chief executive Karim Virani made the shrewd decision to reduce Championship season-ticket prices by up to 30 per cent. At a time when supporters could easily have walked away following relegation, the club recognised the need to offer better value.

Secondly, many fans renewed while believing a major ownership change was approaching. David Sullivan appeared close to departing at the time, with West Ham entering a period of considerable boardroom upheaval.

Unfortunately, whilst Sullivan did relinquish executive control, he’s since increased his stakeholding, something which wasn’t exactly in the brochure.

Anyway, irrespective of Sully hanging on for dear life, West Ham supporters have responded to relegation by setting a record that no other English club has ever managed.

That is a quite remarkable endorsement of the size and loyalty of this fanbase.
