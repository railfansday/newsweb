#1539. Michael Carrick and Bruno Fernandes drive home at 12.30am as Man United's captain is called an 'absolute disgrace' - but boss faces the sack, says Jamie Carragher

📅 26 Oct 2026 | other | Source

By LUKE AUGUSTUS, ASSISTANT SPORTS EDITOR

Published: 07:52 BST, 22 October 2026 | Updated: 15:23 BST, 22 October 2026

Manchester United's forlorn players and staff returned back to Manchester and drove home in the rain in the early hours of Thursday morning as the latest storm builds momentum at the club.

The Red Devils were thrashed 4-1 at Como in the Champions League on Wednesday evening - in what will be the latter's greatest-ever European display.

It proved a night to forget for United too as their winless run in all competitions now stretches to seven games - with their last win a 4-0 victory over Sabah on September 10.

Michael Carrick and Bruno Fernandes were both pictured driving away from Manchester airport at around 12.30am with the head coach and captain at the heart of the latest difference of public opinion.

Goalkeeper Senne Lammens and midfielder Mason Mount, who both started in Italy too, were also pictured driving to their homes in the damp conditions.

A week after Lisandro Martinez's damning comments about the club's hierarchy, Fernandes' remarks have sharpened the focus on Carrick's tenure after he questioned his tactics in Como post-match, in particular their lack of a man-to-man press.

Under-fire Michael Carrick was pictured driving home from Manchester Airport in the early hours of Thursday following their humiliating 4-1 Champions League defeat at Como

Bruno Fernandes' comments about Carrick's tactics post-match have heaped pressure on the head coach and also seen the United captain come under-fire for making his views so public

Reacting to Fernandes' comments, CBS Sports pundit Jamie Carragher was in shock at what he heard - brandiing 'awful attitude' Fernandes a 'disgrace' and declaring his actions as 'not what a Manchester United captain should be'.

'We said Lisandro Martínez was bang out of order last week for calling out the hierarchy above him. For Bruno Fernandes to do that is a disgrace really,' Carragher said.

'When you think about last season, he got player of the year. That was on the back of Michael Carrick becoming the manager. He wouldn't have got that award without Michael Carrick, because Michael Carrick put him back in his rightful position and he then went on to have a fantastic season because he's a talented footballer. But when you think of him as a footballer, he's a talent, but in terms of attitude and the way he goes about things, it's not right at all.

'And he's reacted like that because he's been brought off and he's been embarrassed tonight by the manager as he brought him off. And when he talks about pressing, whether it's man to man or zonal, for years, Bruno Fernandes has meant Manchester United can't press because he does things for the crowd.

'I commentate on games watching Man United and he runs all over the place like a kid in the schoolyard for the supporters. They all think he's working hard - he's not. He's killing the rest of the team doing it.

'Years ago, there was a captain called Bryan Robson, one of the greatest players to ever play for Man United because he played for the club, he didn't throw the manager or the players under the bus.

'He kept fighting for Manchester United and watching him throw his arms up every week in a game is an absolute disgrace, calling the manager out considering how he's performed this season on the pitch.

'Awful attitude and not what a Manchester United captain should be.'

"For Bruno Fernandes, it's a disgrace, really." 😳@Carra23 doesn't hold back on the Manchester United captain after Bruno questioned Michael Carrick's tactics following their defeat to Como 🗣️ pic.twitter.com/7KpJR3RT9O

CBS Sports pundit Jamie Carragher labelled Fernandes as a 'disgrace' for his comments

United captain Fernandes bemoaned their lack of pressing tactics during their defeat at Como

Cesc Fabregas revealed that he told his players to ease off Manchester United during their Champions League humiliation at the hands of Como in Italy on Wednesday night.

Como were four goals up against Michael Carrick's side when Fabregas ordered his players to conserve energy ahead of Saturday's Serie A clash with Sassuolo.

United scored a late consolation goal through substitute Joshua Zirkzee, and Fabregas admitted that he was angry at himself for not heaping further embarrassment on his opponents.

'I regret the last 10 minutes because I was the one who told the team to slow down the tempo because there's another match and at 4-0 we could manage things a little,' said the former Arsenal and Chelsea midfielder.

'I told the players to stop running and stay in the middle and that is something I regret because I'm the first to always push them to fight until the end. Their goal hurt me because we dropped our intensity and dropped our defensive block, but it was my fault.

'I'm very happy with how the players applied everything we tried to do, not only in terms of quality, which was high, but also the way we were pressing relentlessly until the 85th minute.

'We are trying to create a culture at the club, a style of play and an identity. Tonight was another convincing moment for us.'

Como celebrated the greatest result in the club's history, seven years after they were playing in the fourth tier of Italian football.

'I can't lie to you that it (beating United) wasn't pride,' added Fabregas. 'We know the history of our club, how much they have suffered over the last many years. Obviously, I hope that tomorrow all the boys and girls go to school wearing the Como shirt and feel proud.

'I always tell the lads, enjoy yourselves, fight, do everything to make the best career possible for each one of you, because it goes by quickly.

'They made me enjoy myself tonight, but not so much for the win, for the result, because they played like I like my team to play, with conviction, with quality, with a clear head, with aggressiveness, with a fierce pressing.'

Como's star man was Nico Paz who tormented United with two goals and an assist to hand the Premier League club their heaviest defeat in Europe since losing 4-0 to Barcelona in 1994.

'It has been one of the most beautiful nights of my career,' said Paz. 'I'm still young, but I will never forget it. I used to dream of playing in a match like this, and tonight we've gone even further. It's a historic night for Como fans.'

Carragher's disdain for Fernandes' actions didn't stop there as he continued, before conceding that Carrick's days the United hotseat are very much numbered.

'Isn't it funny? How many managers has he had, Bruno Fernandes? So every single manager at Manchester United doesn't have an identity? How many managers are we on now for Bruno Fernandes? Five? They change the manager every season... Manchester United is rotten from top to bottom.

'I said earlier on, it's not about the manager. It's everything about the club. And who has got the answers? We comment on it all the time, "they might need to change the manager or do this, do that". Are we ever right really? Because no one ever seems to get it right. But listen, there's no doubt the manager needs to be changed now. Right now this can't go on for a club of the stature of Manchester United.

'But I'm glad Michael Carrick come out and just said that about Bruno Fernandes, that he don't run, because they don't run, this team.

'And what he did last season, no one's proclaiming Michael Carrick as one of the greatest managers in European football, but what he did was he come and helped his own football club last year when he was out of work himself. He's come in and he did amazing.

'And the reason Manchester United are in the Champions League is because of Carrick - he got them in there. The reason they got another 100, 150 million pounds into the club is because he got them in. No one though he was going to get into the Champions League last season. So I feel for him a little bit. He loves the club, he's a legendary figure as a player, but obviously it's not working out for him as a manager and that needs to be changed.

'But I'm delighted he brought him off, Fernandes, and I'm delighted that he had a little pop back there in the press because that's what they need some of those players.'

Speaking after losing to Como, Fernandes broke ranks to question why United were so passive again.

He said: 'It's a decision from the staff to not press man to man and sometimes leave space behind and try to get the ball, wait for them, but sometimes when you give space to these teams they get confidence.'

Asked if he meant that Carrick got his tactics wrong, Fernandes added: 'That's on the manager and staff to say, not me.

'Sometimes you have to be more brave under pressure, take the risk of pressing high and making them uncomfortable. As you saw with Como, the centre back was coming to mark me in my own half. We need to do that as a team but we were unable to.

'A team that plays through the middle with small connections, if you give them too much time and space then they can hurt you out wide.'

Having conceded to Atletico after 50 seconds last week, United were behind within two minutes as Como star Nico Paz struck the first of his two goals.

'It's not the first time, we started on the wrong foot, and they scored very early,' said Fernandes. 'Not much pressure on the ball, not many good movements with the ball, not many things going our way.

'Conceding early makes us go into game with a mood of defeat, it's difficult to get out of that, trying to be brave, to have the ball and make something happen. If we are not able to do that, it is going to be very difficult to get out of this situation.'

Fernandes was substituted in the second half and looked frustrated as he left the pitch

Fernandes was surprisingly substituted by Carrick midway through the second half, and the United skipper shook his head in frustration as he made his way back to the dugout.

When Carrick was told about Fernandes' comments, the United boss gave a pointed response by suggesting that his players weren't willing to run enough.

'We actually were half-pressed man-to-man in the second half and then conceded a goal from that,' he said. 'There's a little bit on whatever system you play, whatever tactics you play, but if you don't run - you don't run forward and you don't run back - you aren't going to win.'

United will look to end their winless run on Sunday afternoon when they host Bournemouth in the Premier League.

A 99-million-year-old fossilized lizard tail seems to be the oldest-known evidence of the creatures’ ability to intentionally detach their appendages. Many modern-day lizards escape from predators by shedding and leaving behind their tails as a distraction, an ability called caudal autotomy. A 3D reconstruction of the fossilized tail revealed rosette-like wedges of muscle around a clean-cut bone — characteristics similar to those of modern shed tails. Researchers had long suspected that this behaviour was ancient in origin, but had found little evidence of it in known fossils.

Reference: Palaeontologica Electronica paper

The US government has launched an investigation into nine universities’ employment of international researchers. The probe’s purpose is to reduce “visa fraud”, according to vice-president JD Vance, who said that these universities are using too many J-1 visas — the main type used by international postdoctoral researchers in the United States. Critics say that the rationale for the investigation is flawed, that international postdocs play a crucial part in US laboratories and that the announcement is deepening the sense of unease felt by international scientists.

The National Natural Science Foundation of China (NSFC) has selected 44 young researchers as its first group to receive funding for up to 15 years, through one of the longest research grants available to scientists globally. Each will receive initial funding for five years, then be considered for a further five years after that time has elapsed, and so on. The grants come with no short-term targets, says Dou Xiankang, president of the NSFC. “We will assess only one thing: whether you are there in the laboratory.”

Climate scientists in the United States are facing a surge in public-records requests about their work linking emissions from fossil fuels to environmental harms. “Climate attribution is under attack,” says climate scientist Christopher Callahan. Requests of this kind are legal. But some researchers say the field has become a target because it plays a growing role in lawsuits against major industrial emitters. They say that the volume of requests seems intended to dissuade work on climate attribution, wastes time and public money, and could suppress healthy scientific debate.

The Japanese space agency, JAXA, yesterday launched a pioneering mission to grab and return a sample from one of Mars’s moons, Phobos. The Martian Moons eXploration (MMX) probe will drop a rover developed by the French space agency CNES and dubbed Idefix, after the little dog from the Asterix comics (Anglophones might know him as Dogmatix). Idefix weighs 25 kilograms on Earth, but will weigh only 12 grams — about the weight of one Earth pencil — when it lands on Phobos in August 2027.

Tiny doses of the psychedelic drug LSD seem to boost some elements of cognition — but only in women. In a small trial, women who took 15 micrograms of the drug focused for longer periods and responded faster to a stimulus than did those who were given a placebo. This trial alone can’t confirm that LSD was behind these benefits, but the signal “warrants further investigation”, says psychopharmacologist Mikkel Højlund. (Nature | 4 min read)

Reference: European College of Neuropsychopharmacology Congress Abstract (Source: Iva Totomanova et al.)

In August, a type of drug called an orexin agonist was approved by US regulators to treat narcolepsy — the first of what researchers hope will be many uses for this class of drug. Several pharma companies are already exploring the effectiveness of orexin agonists to treat conditions beyond sleep disorders, such as attention deficit hyperactivity disorder (ADHD). “Diabetes was the tip of the iceberg for GLP-1s, and I’m convinced the same story will play out for orexin,” says Andrew Plump, president of research and development at pharmaceutical company Takeda.

Large language models (LLMs) can mimic thought and feeling with uncanny skill — but they are not conscious, and deeming them so could hinder attempts to regulate them, argue neuroscientists Anil Seth and Giulio Tononi and philosopher Ned Block. LLMs beguile us by aping human language, the authors argue, noting that other impressive systems — such as AlphaFold, the protein-structure-prediction tool — aren’t considered to possess consciousness. And the mind isn’t merely a carbon-based computer — the living tissue of the human brain is essential, according to the leading theories of consciousness. Silicon-based computers are very unlikely to be sufficient for consciousness, and don’t satisfy certain crucial properties that conscious brains do, the authors write.

Researchers and start-up companies have plenty of ideas for ways to enhance the ocean’s natural ability to absorb carbon dioxide from the atmosphere, from adding alkaline slurry to growing kelp forests. One of the biggest challenges of marine carbon-dioxide removal (mCDR) is measuring how much CO2 is being sequestered — crucial for nabbing the carbon credits that could pay for these projects on large scales.

Education researcher Thomas Nygren is one of those grappling with the question of whether AI tools are undermining critical thinking. (Listen to the audio version of our September feature, read by Nature’s Benjamin Thompson | 16 min)

#1533. Barcelona’s defensive shortage opens door for teenage debut

📅 26 Oct 2026 | other | Source

Hansi Flick has identified Rodri Hernández and Frenkie de Jong as possible emergency options in Barcelona’s defence for their Champions League meeting with Paris Saint-Germain on Tuesday, October 20. Reuters report that injuries to Jules Kounde and Eric Garcia, with Andreas Christensen also unavailable, have left the coach short of senior defenders.

The shortage adds to the defensive concerns Barcelona carried into the PSG fixture, after a further pair of injuries left them with five fit senior defenders. Kounde and Garcia were injured on Sunday, October 18, while Christensen is also set to miss the match.

Barcelona’s centre-back concerns have now brought two midfielders into consideration for the back line. Flick has named Rodri and De Jong as alternatives, while 18-year-old centre-back Alex Campos is in the squad for the PSG game despite not yet making his Barcelona debut.

Campos’ inclusion gives Flick another centre-back option, though the teenager would be making his first appearance for the senior side if selected. The coach could also use Rodri or De Jong in defence, with the choice coming against a PSG side Barcelona face in a significant European test.

Raphinha is another potential returnee for the starting XI. The Brazilian could start for the first time since September 19 after a hamstring problem, providing Flick with an additional selection decision as he prepares the team.

The three unavailable defenders leave Barcelona managing a shortage at the back rather than simply replacing one injured player. Christensen’s absence has formed part of the broader defensive issue, after his injury kept him out of the picture ahead of the PSG match.

Flick has presented Rodri and De Jong as possible solutions in central defence and said he expects Kounde to return soon. The Barcelona coach indicated that the France international could be available for Sunday’s match against Real Madrid, on October 25.

Flick also assessed the PSG fixture as an important test for his side. The immediate task is to decide whether to use a midfielder in the back line, select Campos for a senior debut, or combine those options.

Barcelona face PSG on Tuesday, with Campos in the squad and Raphinha potentially in line to start. Kounde’s possible return against Real Madrid would follow five days later, giving Barcelona a second high-profile fixture in the space of a week.

#1532. Serie A | Torino 3-2 Monza: Belghali stunner spoils Juric’s return

📅 26 Oct 2026 | other | Source

Torino made it back-to-back Serie A wins as Kian Fitz-Jim, Rafik Belghali and Nikola Vlašić struck to spoil Ivan Juric’s return to the Olimpico Grande Torino.

Ignazio Abate’s side arrived on 10 points after Monday’s 3-0 win at Parma, and made one change, with Fitz-Jim replacing Daniel Bragança in midfield. Ricardo Rodríguez, Emirhan Ilkhan, Niccolò Fortini and goalkeeper Franco Israel were all unavailable. Monza were on seven points after thrashing Cagliari 4-0, with Lorenzo Lucchesi back from suspension in place of goalscorer Eddy Kouadio. Matteo Pessina, Jan Ziółkowski and Mayé were injured.

Dany Mota headed wide from an early corner. Ardian Ismajli nodded just past the post from a set piece at the other end, while Rolando Mandragora fired over from distance.

Torino broke the deadlock six minutes before the break. Fitz-Jim collected Giovanni Simeone’s pass, drove into the area and fired into the roof of the net.

Monza had appeals for a penalty waved away when Ricardo Mangas went down in the box in stoppage time. They were level within two minutes of the restart, when Gustavo Varela pounced on a defensive mistake and rolled a low finish into the bottom corner from the right side of the box.

Torino lost Mandragora to injury shortly after, but responded strongly. Noel Törnqvist made good saves to keep out Belghali, Simeone and Pietro Comuzzo, but could do nothing about Belghali’s effort on 61 minutes. The wing-back skipped past two defenders and curled a superb strike from outside the area into the bottom corner.

Lucas Perri saved from Jay Robinson and a Mangas header at the other end, while Che Adams forced another stop from Törnqvist with a long-range drive.

Vlašić settled it in the 72nd minute, firing a loose ball high into the net from the edge of the area. Substitute Michael Folorunsho went close with a late effort from distance, but Torino held on, despite a late own goal from Scottish defender Nathan Patterson.

#1531. El Salvador coach threatens to remove players from squad, strips perks a month before facing USWNT

📅 26 Oct 2026 | other | Source

If not listed, please contact your TV provider.

After the October international window concluded, the Concacaf region now looks ahead to the upcoming Concacaf W Championship in November. The tournament serves as a qualifier for the 2027 FIFA Women's World Cup and for spots at the 2028 Summer Olympics in Los Angeles. One quarterfinal game has already sparked a head-coaching controversy a month before the official kickoff.

The Concacaf W Championship is now an eight-team, direct knockout format. The United States and Canada earned direct byes into the tournament, while six other teams from the region competed for the remaining spots. The teams were then matched by official ranking, with the highest-ranked team facing the lowest, and the USWNT drew El Salvador in the upcoming quarterfinal. Canada will face Panama, Mexico will play Haiti, and Jamaica and Costa Rica will round out the quarterfinal round.

As the U.S. women's national team look ahead to the November competition after mixed results against Spain, where they lost 2-0 and drew 2-2, they'll face a national team dealing with a complex head-coaching controversy. El Salvador and the USWNT will meet in the quarterfinals, just a month after head coach Eric Acuna made disparaging comments about his own players on La Selecta following a two-game series against Paraguay.

El Salvador faced Paraguay in a pair of friendlies and lost both. La Selecta opened the series with a discouraging 5-1 defeat, prompting a controversial set of comments from coach Acuna, then closed out the window with a 3-2 loss.

The finale on Monday was an eventful match, with El Salvador up two goals in the first half by Brenda Ceren and Danya Gutierrez, but things spiraled after halftime. The group conceded a goal early in the second half, then Nicolle Amaya received a second yellow and was sent off, giving Paraguay a player advantage. Paraguay capitalized and scored an equalizer, then Gutierrez received a second yellow -- prompting another player exit for El Salvador.

With a two-player advantage, the game-winning goal for Paraguay came in the 80th minute, when Monserrat Ayala scored, and La Selecta couldn't rally for a result. The second loss led to Acuna doubling down on his comments from the previous match.

After the first loss in the October window, coach Acuna told the press that the effort was poor and that the team didn't have a clear enough focus on the games ahead. He also expressed that his immediate adjustment was going to be one of retaliation towards his players and revoke off-camp privileges immediately leading up to game two.

"I just took away all the privileges from the team because, unfortunately, there can't be any free coffees, any of these perks, or anything like that if they haven't earned them. I'm sorry," Acuna told the media.

He then went on to say that the players didn't come to training camps to have coffee, visit their mothers or their fathers, and that they were in camps solely to work for El Salvador.

"And that is what they are going to do, and I'm saying it on air so they know. They no longer have privileges. They can't leave the training center; they can't leave to have coffee, nothing. We have to focus on the match and clean up our image," he said.

Unfortunately, El Salvador players were still the target of Acuna's comments after a second consecutive loss against Paraguay. Not only did the manager double down on his comments; he specifically named players for their form, compared them to one another, and implied his roster overall isn't good enough to compete.

"We continue to fail. Our goalkeepers keep giving away loose balls, bouncing around; it's complicated, but the truth is they have more experience than us ...  What this basically leaves me with is a list of players that, if I press a button, can they perform?" he said.

He also compared players to one another in terms of overall level and commitment. Judging Juana Plata against Victoria Meza, he said Meza was "better on one leg." He also said looking at the bench makes him want to cry because he has no faith that the available players could change the trajectory of the game.

"I have nothing on the bench to save the team ... No matter how much you ask for changes, you cannot compare [Sam] Fisher with Brenda [Ceren], [she] is three times the player than she is," he said.

Fisher suffered an ACL tear in August 2025, which required surgery, and she was recently called back into national team camps, and Acuna implied that players from the October might not be called in for November matches.

Currently, a select number of players from the October camps have released their own personal statements on social media. Youth players have also spoken out and called for Acuna's removal and the El Salvador Football Federation issued a statement.

Ceren, who was one of the players Acuna referenced as a better player than Fisher, wrote the following:

"Wearing the jersey of El Salvador's National Team has been, is, and will always be the greatest pride of my sporting life. Every time I step onto the pitch, I do so with the conviction to give one hundred percent for the crest and for the millions of Salvadorans who dream alongside us. In recent days, various opinions have been circulating about the team. As one of the leaders of this group, I want to make it clear that this team is not made by a single player," she said.

"La Selecta Femenina is a collective of brave, professional, and committed women, where each of my teammates, whether those who start, those who come on as substitutes, or those who wait for their moment on the bench, contributes immeasurable value through their daily effort and sacrifice ... No name is above the group, and no comparison defines the commitment that all of us feel for our country."

Isabella Recinos, who has previous experience with the U20 side, also posted on social media directly calling for a change in leadership.

"Stop blaming the players. Same results. Same excuses. Same coach. Players give everything on the pitch, then get called out in public when it doesn't work. That's not leadership or accountability. Real leaders protect their squad and take responsibility first," she said on an Instagram story.

"When a team keeps losing to opponents it should beat, the tactics, selections, and culture have to be questioned too, not just the people playing. Respect isn't optional. How players are treated says everything about the person in charge. This is not the first time. When are people going to wake up? For him to release an apology statement speaks for itself. Wake up and protect these women/players," she wrote.
