Bournemouth boss Marco Rose could not hide his pride after his side beat AC Milan comfortably in the Europa League last night.

Milan suffered a 3-0 defeat against Bournemouth in their third Europa League game. Reduced to ten men around the half-hour mark following Mike Maignan’s straight red card, the Rossoneri were undone by first-half penalties from Kluivert (8’) and Tavernier (32’) and a late Amine Adli free-kick.

In the torrential rain, the evening proved difficult from the outset, as the hosts’ aggressive start quickly put them in control. Playing a man down made the task harder still, and although the Rossoneri tried to respond after the break, they could not find a way back into the game.

Rose spoke in the press conference room at the Vitality Stadium after the full-time whistle to give his thoughts on what was a memorable night for his side, with MilanNews relaying his words.

#1879. FIFA Slaps Real Madrid, Atletico Madrid With 1-Year Transfer Bans

📅 26 Oct 2026 | other | Source

Home » Home 2 » FIFA Slaps Real Madrid, Atletico Madrid With 1-Year Transfer Bans

The ban will start from the July transfer window this year and finish with the January transfer market in 2017.

Atletico were also fined 900,000 Swiss francs (820,000 euros) and Real 360,000 (330,000 euros).

“The two clubs were found to have violated several provisions concerning the international transfer and first registration of minor players as well as other relevant provisions with regard to the registration and participation of certain players in competitions,” said a FIFA statement.

Barcelona has just ended a one year ban for similar reasons.

Real and Atletico were “issued with a reprimand and given 90 days in which to regularise the situation of all minor players concerned,” FIFA said. They can still sell players during the transfer ban.

FIFA said the alarm was raised over the two clubs by FIFA’s Transfer Matching System and its disciplinary committee completed the inquiry.

Under international football rules, players under 18 cannot be transferred to another country, except in limited circumstances. All transfers need the approval of a special FIFA committee.

“The investigations concerned minor players who were involved and participated in competitions with the clubs over various periods between 2007 and 2014 (Atletico Madrid) and between 2005 and 2014 (Real Madrid),” FIFA said.

The ban follows a similar sanction handed to European champions Barcelona, who were unable to register new players during 2015.

Barca were initially banned in April 2014, but managed to delay the sanction taking affect until the transfer windows in January and the summer 2015 by launching an ultimately unsuccessful appeal to the Court of Arbitration for Sport (CAS).

By delaying the ban, Barca managed to sign the likes of Luis Suarez and Ivan Rakitic in the summer of 2014 to propel them to a treble of La Liga, Champions League and Copa del Rey.

The news is just the latest institutional embarassment for Real Madrid just over a week after they appointed French legend Zinedine Zidane.

Madrid have sacked two Champions League winning coaches in Carlo Ancelotti and Rafael Benitez in the past seven months, as well as being thrown out of the Copa del Rey for fielding an ineligible player.

The sanction could also be bad news for the likes of Paris Saint-Germain and Manchester United, who had targeted Madrid stars Cristiano Ronaldo and Gareth Bale with Real highly unlikely to dispose of their best players without the chance to sign suitable replacements.

La Liga leaders Atletico Madrid’s usually hectic transfer policy will also be put on hold. However, Diego Simeone’s men look better placed to cope with the ban having invested heavily in young talent over the past year.

Neither Real no Atletico made an immediate comment on whether they would appeal.

#1878. Man United head coach Michael Carrick addresses claims Bruno Fernandes could lose Red Devils captaincy

📅 26 Oct 2026 | other | Source

By Matt Law | 23 Oct 2026 19:13, Last updated: 23 Oct 2026 19:15

Manchester United head coach Michael Carrick has refused to rule out stripping Bruno Fernandes of the captaincy at Old Trafford.

Carrick started Friday's press conference with a statement with lasted five-and-a-half-minutes, as he called on the players at the club to step up during a period of adversity.

Fernandes appeared to criticise Carrick's tactics following Wednesday's 4-1 defeat to Como.

"Conceding early makes us go into game with a mood of defeat, it's difficult to get out of that, trying to be brave, to have the ball and make something happen. If we are not able to do that, it is going to be very difficult to get out of this situation," Fernandes told TNT Sports.

"It's a decision from the staff to not press man to man and sometimes leave space behind and try to get the ball, wait for them, but sometimes when you give space to these teams they get confidence."

Carrick was asked during his press conference on Friday whether he would consider taking the armband away from Fernandes due to the Portugal international's comments.

"The most important thing for me is the team, first and foremost, is the squad, is the dynamics in the squad and again, it's about the football club. I always do what's best for the football club," Carrick told reporters.

“I'm just saying, whatever the situation is, I'll always do what's best for the football club.”

Carrick also insisted that the dressing room at the club is not divided despite the difficulties.

Lisandro Martinez criticised Man United's board, including director of football Jason Wilcox, following the team's recent defeat to Atletico Madrid in the Champions League.

An apology was latter issued by Martinez, and Carrick is confident that the players are still all pulling in the same direction.

“It's not divided, no, it's not divided. We just need to get back to doing what we do best and we will," said the Man United head coach.

Carrick is under major pressure to deliver a positive result in Sunday's Premier League clash against Bournemouth at Old Trafford.

#1877. Juventus’ Forward Numbers Make a January Case

📅 26 Oct 2026 | other | Source

Juventus’ 1-1 draw at Cagliari sharpened a practical question for Luciano Spalletti: should attacking depth be the club’s first priority in January? Get Italian Football News identifies a blunt attack and a thin squad among the coach’s main problems, while Tuttosport reports that Juventus have begun considering ideas for the winter market. Neither report confirms a specific target or a completed transfer plan.

The case for reinforcement is clear from the numbers and the available options. Juventus sit sixth with 11 points, five behind joint leaders Inter and Lazio, while Spalletti’s side has scored nine goals and conceded five in its first six league matches. A run of fitness concerns affecting Juventus has further narrowed the choices available to him.

The league position is an early warning, not a verdict on the season. Still, the comparison with Igor Tudor’s opening run is uncomfortable: across their first six matches, Tudor took 12 points and Spalletti has taken 11, with both teams scoring nine and conceding five. Get Italian Football News notes that Tudor was later dismissed, although the shared figures do not mean the two situations are identical.

For Juventus, the more immediate concern is the conversion of attacking opportunities. The team has managed only one win in its last four Serie A fixtures, according to the report, and the current returns from its central forwards give the club a concrete reason to assess its options. A January addition would be an editorial conclusion from those figures, not confirmation that a signing has been authorised.

Tuttosport also reports that Juventus have started outlining ideas for the January window, with a forward identified as a priority in its coverage. The paper says Spalletti is increasingly persuaded that Kolo Muani needs an attacking partner. That points to a question of fit as well as headcount: whether the club can add a dependable scorer who complements the current forward line.

Randal Kolo Muani returned to Juventus in a summer transfer reported at €41 million, but he has scored once in the first six matches covered by Get Italian Football News. His earlier spell in Italy offered a far more productive sample: eight goals and one assist in 16 appearances during a six-month loan from Paris Saint-Germain in 2024-25.

Nick Woltemade, who joined on loan from Newcastle on deadline day, had not scored in four Serie A appearances at the point described in the report. Spalletti has given Kolo Muani freedom to move across the frontline, with Nico Gonzalez making runs as a second striker, but the approach has not yet produced enough finishing. The numbers do not settle what either player can offer over a full season; they do explain why another reliable option belongs in the January discussion.

The Cagliari match also fed into Spalletti’s thinking about a partnership. Tuttosport reports that he sees scope for Kolo Muani to operate alongside a second forward, with the pair able to exchange roles. That is a reported tactical preference, not evidence that Juventus have selected a player to fill the role.

The shortage is not limited to the strikers. Get Italian Football News reports that Kenan Yildiz, Manuel Locatelli, Khephren Thuram, Jeff Ekhator and Jeremie Boga have been unavailable or restricted by injuries. Those absences reduce Spalletti’s options across the squad, making a forward signing helpful but insufficient on its own.

The report also identifies difficulty managing key moments and a lack of personality as separate concerns. Juventus had gone 14 consecutive matches under Spalletti without winning after conceding first, while Nico Gonzalez and Gleison Bremer were emerging as leaders in a squad the source describes as still needing to mature. A new striker cannot by himself change that record or restore every absent player.

Get Italian Football News argues that Juventus are not yet keeping pace with Inter and Roma or showing the consistency required to challenge for the Scudetto. Its account places those concerns alongside the team’s thin squad; it does not name a January target. The immediate test comes against Lazio at the Allianz Stadium, a match the report frames as important to Juventus’ Champions League qualification race.

On the evidence available, a dependable forward should be at the top of Juventus’ January priorities: Kolo Muani has one goal in six matches and Woltemade none in four league appearances. That is a recommendation drawn from the scoring record, not a confirmed club decision. The next meaningful indicators will be whether the forwards improve before the winter window and whether Juventus can recover players currently missing through injury.

Born and raised in Turin, he developed an early obsession with the tactical patterns that made Juventus so difficult to beat, and that curiosity never left him. He watches matches with a notepad nearby and a tendency to rewind passages of play more times than anyone around him considers reasonable.

#1862. Football regulator insists it will have 'absolute power' to force Man City owners to sell if they are proven to have run the club 'in a way that is neither fit nor fair'

📅 26 Oct 2026 | other | Source

Published: 10:43 BST, 21 October 2026 | Updated: 12:45 BST, 21 October 2026

The chair of the Independent Football Regulator (IFR) has confirmed it has the ‘absolute power’ to force Manchester City’s owners to sell the club if they are found to have run the club improperly.

Man City were last month found guilty of the vast majority of charges brought against them by the Premier League for financial wrongdoing. The club have since appealed.

An independent commission ruled Man City had disguised payments worth £900million in a nine-year period peppered with ‘sham’ deals.

Man City were found to have artificially inflated sponsorship agreements and pumped money into the club from its Abu Dhabi owners via disguised payments in a bid to swerve Premier League spending rules.

The IFR, which was founded last year, has permission to remove club owners and suspend or revoke licences if they deem the wrongdoing by said club to be sufficiently serious under the 2025 Football Governance Act.

David Kogan, the chair of the IFR, confirmed to the Rest is Football podcast that the body would have the power to force Man City owner Sheikh Mansour to sell the club if it were deemed appropriate.

The chair of the Independent Football Regulator says it has the ‘absolute power’ to force Manchester City’s owners to sell the club if they are found to have run the club improperly

'We have the power to ask an owner to step away from a club if we believe that they run a club in a way that is neither fit, fair or done with propriety,' Kogan said.

When asked if an owner can refuse, Kogan responded: 'No, we’ve got absolute power to do so.'

Kogan said that the IFR's role would be to judge individuals against the Premier League's owners and directors' test.

The IFR chair stressed it would be premature to discuss what action the body might take, with Man City having appealed the commission's verdict and with several findings having been redacted in the 40-page document.

‘There are two further elements that might come up before the appeal that we don’t know,' Kogan said.

‘One is: what are the sanctions? Nobody knows this yet. The other is that we have only seen a redacted version of the findings, we have not seen an unredacted version. The point about the unredacted is the evidence behind the unredacted, which may throw up any number of things.

'I am not going to get pushed into making any judgement on this until we’ve seen all of those questions being answered and then we will have to decide whether we’re going to mount our own investigation and come to a conclusion on that and what we’re going to do.

‘And pre-empting that would be a colossal mistake.’

IFR chair David Kogan stressed the body will not pre-empt the ultimate outcome of Man City's case, but could later launch its own investigation into individuals

Kogan last week reiterated that he would immediately resign from his role if there were any political interference.

'I made it very clear last year that any form of political interference in the way we operate is going to result in my resigning from this role, and I stand by that completely,' Kogan said.

Prime Minister Andy Burnham previously said that he would be 'concerned to lose' Man City's Abu Dhabi owners from the Premier League, and the United Arab Emirates government has warned the PM that they could yet withdraw billions of pounds worth of funding over the ruling.

Kogan has said the regulator had come under 'absolutely no political pressure whatsoever on this or any other topic'.
