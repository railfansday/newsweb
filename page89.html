#1898. Good stuff from the other Mikel

📅 26 Oct 2026 | other | Source

I’ve written here before about how the first thing many people do every day is pick up their phone, and subject themselves to an early morning blast of ‘news’, which is often some of the worst stuff you’ve ever seen. Until tomorrow, when somehow it gets worse.

Many of you, and I appreciate this a great deal, use Arseblog to kick-start your day. Whether it’s via the website or the app, the blog each day is a starting point, so in the spirit of getting this Friday going in a positive way, I recommend reading Mikel Merino’s piece in The Player’s Tribune.

He outlines the period between the injury he picked up against Manchester United in January and the World Cup, and how touch and go it was for him to make it into the Spain squad. There was a lot going on at the time, his wife was pregnant, he was on crutches and barely mobile after surgery, and the idea of missing our season and as well as the World Cup could easily have seen him spiral in a negative way. Regardless of your stature in the game, whether it’s Premier League or Sunday League, not being able to play football because of injury is the worst.

I do think that sometimes the ‘power of positive thinking’ is a bit woo-y, but in this case it really worked for him. He refused to accept the verdict that it was impossible for him to recover in time, and was proven right.

I especially liked the detail about his frustration at not being able to walk his dog:

“One day I said, “Enough.” I had to do something. I bought a red mobility scooter. The next morning, I was cruising down the street with the dog on a leash. She had her tongue out, ears flapping, looking at me all the time, maybe to see if I would increase the speed.

“Bzzzzzzzzzzzzzzzzzzzzzzzzz. I was so slow!! You could walk past me. It was actually hilarious. Sometimes I hid behind sunglasses and a cap, but other days I couldn’t be bothered, and my neighbours spotted me.

“I’m just happy they didn’t film it and put it online, hahahaha. I can’t even imagine the memes.”

In this day and age, how a Premier League footballer was spotted out and about on a mobility scooter, with his dog, and didn’t end up online is beyond me. But it’s also great that he didn’t. Every bit of our lives is subject to surveillance, and the way people default to getting their phone out and filming – even when what they should be doing is helping, for example – is a societal scourge. So, I love that he buzzed down the streets of London or Hertfordshire or wherever he lives and didn’t end up on some Footy Bantz page Photoshopped as Stephen Hawking. We’ve had so much of our privacy stolen by the ubiquity of technology, I think we sometimes forget it’s something we should have an absolute right to.

There’s also a really interesting bit about the reaction to the Wolves draw, and a team meeting the players had with Mikel Arteta absent.

The guy with the bottle on TV? We just laughed.

Between this, and the video the Arsenal website produced about his recuperation from the injury, you can easily see why Merino is such a valuable member of this squad. There’s an obvious intelligence coupled with his experience that feels reliable and important. Anyway, I won’t labour the point anymore, have a read of the article, and maybe watch the video too.

He also revealed in an interview with Cadena Ser in Spain that it was his challenge on Martin Zubimendi that caused the injury in the warm-down after the Leeds game, but sounded a positive note on his fellow Spaniard:

“He’s fine. He had a slightly sore ankle precisely because of a challenge with me, and if there’s anyone who doesn’t want to hurt ‘Zubi’, it’s me. But he’s fine, he’s a bull and he’ll be back in no time.”

We might get an update from the manager on Zubimendi when he meets the press a bit later ahead of our trip to Nottingham Forest on Sunday. His absence probably meant a 90 minutes for Declan Rice that we were trying to avoid, especially with a trip to Munich coming up next week, but let’s see what the state of the squad is ahead of this game.

I’m going to guess that he will be asked at his presser about the job cuts story, and while I understand why he was reticent to comment expansively on the Man City stuff, I hope we get more from him on this. Obviously, and it should go without saying, this isn’t really anything to do with him. The decision to bring in consultants whose modus operandi is always to recommend a cull happened above him, but he is still the manager of the club, a figurehead, and somebody who has taken on the responsibility of changing the culture on and off the pitch.

In that sense, he has done an incredible job, taking us from the vitriolic doldrums to Premier League champions again, and it has been a team effort. The footballing side has been supported by the countless people who work in other areas and who have made some small contribution to the success the club has had. In the Arsecast yesterday, we spoke about this, and how the announcement has left people who work at Arsenal worried, scared for their futures, and when you’re left wondering if you’re one of the 60 or so who could go, it’s clearly going to impact the mood and morale. So, I’m curious to see how he addresses this today – hopefully with a bit more than an injury update, for example.

Later on we’ll have a preview podcast for you on Patreon, for some extra reading today Tim’s column touches on our left hand side, and the Arsecast is below if you haven’t had a chance to listen yet.

With Michael Carrick's managerial position on the line, Manchester United welcome Bournemouth to Old Trafford for a Premier League clash on Sunday afternoon.

The Red Devils are seven matches without a win for the first time since 2015 following a 4-1 defeat by Como in the Champions League on Wednesday night.

A day later and it was the turn of Bournemouth to grace the European stage, with the Cherries winning 3-0 against Italian giants AC Milan at the Vitality Stadium.

As a result, the visitors will certainly have no fear ahead of their trip to the Theatre of Dreams, which could be the scene of Utd's latest head coach departure this weekend.

Carrick stated in his pre-match press conference that Wednesday's performance in Como was unacceptable, calling on his squad to produce a positive response on a return to Premier League duties.

Meanwhile, Bournemouth are on a three-game winning run, last enjoying four victories on the spin in December 2023.

Ahead of Sunday's match, Sports Mole looks at the head-to-head record and some notable previous meetings between Man Utd and Bournemouth.

Man United have dominated the previous meetings between the two teams, posting 14 wins to Bournemouth's five, while there have also been seven draws in this particular fixture.

The Red Devils were victorious in the first three matches between the two sides, which occurred in the FA Cup and League Cup, while Bournemouth's first-ever victory over the 20-time English champions proved to be a 2-0 success in the third round of the FA Cup back in January 1984.

The two teams did not meet between February 1989 and December 2015 due to Bournemouth's presence in the lower divisions, with their first league fixture taking place in the first half of the 2015-16 campaign.

The Cherries recorded a famous 2-1 success over Man United on that occasion, but they have only won three against them since - a 1-0 victory at the Vitality Stadium in November 2019, and 3-0 wins at Old Trafford in December 2023 and December 2024.

The Red Devils have actually been victorious in seven of their last 14 Premier League encounters with the Cherries, demonstrating their dominance in this fixture, but they have now been beaten 3-0 by Bournemouth in two of their last three Premier League encounters at Old Trafford.

The latest of those three Old Trafford matchups produced undeniably the best game between these two in history, with Manchester United and Bournemouth drawing 4-4 in a rollercoaster Premier League fixture.

Man United academy product Marcus Rashford is the leading goalscorer in this particular fixture, finding the back of the net on five occasions.

#1882. Arsenal's meeting with Bayern Munich and Harry Kane is a litmus test in Europe for a team playing ugly football, writes IAN LADYMAN - as David Raya fires back at 'boring' critics

📅 26 Oct 2026 | other | Source

Published: 08:14 BST, 21 October 2026 | Updated: 12:18 BST, 21 October 2026

It is 144 days since Arsenal came within a couple of bad penalties of winning the Champions League Final in Budapest.

Manager Mikel Arteta still hasn’t got over it.

‘I have tried but when I see the ball and the music I realise how much I still want it so badly,’ he said here last night.

In terms of his players, perhaps the biggest test since that early summer defeat to PSG comes on Wednesday in Bavaria. Bayern Munich have lost just once at home since last April and not anywhere so far this season.

A meeting with Bayern and Harry Kane rightly stands as some kind of litmus test of progress. Arteta said after last season’s disappointment that his team had to evolve yet here we are in mid-autumn with similar themes at play.

Sunday’s win at Nottingham Forest in the Premier League came courtesy of one shot on target and that in itself a deflection. It was a dreary and rather ugly way to win. Arsenal are our English champions but don’t always seem to play like them.

Mikel Arteta will look to impress against Bayern Munich after Arsenal's stodgier start to the domestic season

On Tuesday he admitted that he is still haunted by losing the Champions League final in May

Arteta made light of it all as he sat and talked at the Allianz Arena on Tuesday evening but his goalkeeper David Raya did not.

Does – he was asked by Daily Mail Sport – the criticism of Arsenal’s style irritate the dressing room or not?

‘People have their own opinions. We have our own opinion.

‘At the end of the day, we have lost one game in eleven this season and if you call that boring or irritating, I don’t know what to say.

‘We enjoy playing football. We enjoy going out there every single day to win football matches and that’s the most important thing.’

That game in Budapest last May ended with Arsenal having seen only 25 per cent of the ball. That’s the kind of number we expect to see when, say, Manchester City face Burnley at home.

Again Raya was happy to engage on the subject and the argument he presented was not without its merits.

Asked whether Arsenal are confident of imposing themselves more successfully on the best European teams this season, he said: ‘I think so. But you have to see that in perspective as well.

‘It was our first Champions League final in so many years and we score in the fifth minute and sit back a little bit knowing how good they are offensively. We didn’t want to give anything away.

‘It is different now. I think you can see the way we play, you can see the different ways of playing that we have.

‘I think we are more prepared this year than last year so hopefully we can keep that going.’

David Raya brushed off criticism of Arsenal's playing style this season, stressing that the players had their own opinions

On Wednesday in Bavaria, Arsenal will face an opponent that at least wishes to play in their opponents’ half of the field. In the Premier League it is not always the way.

Equally, Arteta’s players have a European memory bank containing more than images of just one game against just one team. Arsenal beat Bayern at home last season. The season before they ran all over Real Madrid at the Emirates and then beat them in Spain, too.

When the mood takes them, they can run hot. The puzzle is – given the attacking talent at their disposal – why it doesn’t happen more often.

Wednesday may well be informative from that point of view while exactly what Arteta really feels about it is hard to know. The Spaniard is as inscrutable as they come in public but if he wasn’t motivated by the noise that tends to follow his team around then he wouldn’t be human.

On this occasion, he chose to approach the issue with a hint of deadpan and a little sarcasm.

‘I heard our style was incredible,’ he said.

‘Because we scored so many goals, we have had more possession, more shots, different players participating, no goals from set-pieces.

‘So the story is completely different to last year.

‘I think we are talking about a very small minority [who criticise] and the other ones they know they have enjoyed a lot the way we have played and dominated games this year.

‘I think we have proven that so that’s my opinion.

‘If you analyse all the top teams in England they all have a list [of criticism]. We all have a list because none of us are perfect.

‘Thank God that we are not perfect. Thank God that we can still improve even though we have won the amount of games that we have done because otherwise the next day, what do you do?

‘You just expect to be better than the opponent and that’s it. Especially in the Premier League we know that is very far from the reality.

‘I think on Sunday [at Forest], we could have done things better. And that's in our hands. We need to recognise that openly as well.

‘But we did a lot of great things as well against a really tough opponent. Very, very well coached as well. And we did something that we didn't manage to do two years in a row. So that's another step. A really positive one. And then we're done from it and we're okay.’

Arsenal will face a Bayern team on Wednesday expected to be without coach Vincent Kompany on the sidelines. The former City defender has been struck by a virus that is impacting on his ability to walk and has been ordered by his doctors to rest.

Raya opened his part of the media proceedings by offering Kompany his best wishes and that was a nice and clearly pre-meditated touch.

In terms of the game, the nature of the competition’s league format makes these match-ups feel marginally less significant than they once did. But there is a bigger picture at play for this Arsenal team.

Opportunities for statement performances don’t come round very often in a Premier League where the low block is king. In Europe – at places like this – it is different. A win on Wednesday for Arsenal would mean more than Arteta is perhaps willing to admit.
