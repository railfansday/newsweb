#3547. ISRAEL UP NEXT: TUESDAY VOTE...

IDF officials fear Israeli Prime Minister Benjamin Netanyahu may seek to strike Iran before the Knesset election scheduled for Tuesday, leading to tensions with the military's leadership.

The IDF's brass disagree with the prime minister over the urgent necessity of an offensive and fear it may delay the election.

U.S. President Donald Trump was forced to issue a statement on Sunday night saying there would be no attack on Iran overnight, and the administration expressed surprise at Netanyahu's recent remarks.

On Sunday night, Israel's Channel 12 News reported on another spat between Netanyahu and IDF Chief of Staff Eyal Zamir, which occurred during Netanyahu's visit to Palmachim Air Force base Sunday. Netanyahu visited the base and was photographed against the backdrop of an Arrow interception missile battery, threatening Israel's enemies: "You will suffer disproportionate blows, stronger than you can imagine."

Polling station committee members and observers representing parties and candidate lists may not relay information about the number of voters who have actually cast ballots at a particular polling station to their parties. The ban applies to all means of transmitting such information, the Supreme Court clarified in a supplemental ruling on the matter.

The clarification establishes an absolute prohibition on transferring polling station turnout data to political entities.

In response, the Israeli Democracy Guard, a nonprofit organization, welcomed the ruling and called on the Central Elections Committee to immediately update the guidelines for all polling station workers across the country to prevent disruptions on Election Day and violations of the law.

Amcha Yisrael chairman Ofer Winter reiterated Sunday that he has no intention of dropping out of the election race. At the party's closing rally, Winter sharply criticized a campaign spearheaded by Netanyahu to keep Amcha Yisrael out of the Knesset.

"Tens of millions of shekels have been invested to crush Amcha Yisrael. Over the past two months, people have come to people's homes, synagogues and children's bus stops, all for one reason: They are afraid," Winter said.

"Established forces in the political system don't want new people on the scene. We have not surrendered, and we will not surrender to this ugly and violent smear campaign," he added.

Prime Minister Benjamin Netanyahu said he ordered the Israeli military and Mossad intelligence agency to overthrow the Iranian regime.

"I instructed the Mossad and the IDF to bring down this regime, because that is the only way to put an end to this threat," he said, speaking at the pro-government Channel 14. Responding to a report of tension between him and the head of the IDF, Netanyahu said "someone is trying to stir up a conflict," citing warnings that "Iran is trying to interfere in the elections to bring me down."

Earlier on Sunday, Channel 12 reported a conflict between the military and the prime minister over fears that Netanyahu may use the Iran war for campaign purposes. IDF Chief of Staff Eyal Zamir reportedly warned that a recent order might spark a confrontation with Iran, demanding that Netanyahu sign off on it in writing.

Speaking on Channel 14, Netanyahu said, "This supposed rivalry between us is all nonsense," attacking Channel 12 and saying, "I keep looking for the times when they report the truth. It almost never happens."

Yashar party chairman Gadi Eisenkot warned that Prime Minister Benjamin Netanyahu would do whatever it takes to remain in power, urging voters not to take political change for granted at the party's final campaign rally Sunday.

"I see the polls, I know the data, and I know Netanyahu – he will stop at nothing, no lie and no extreme measure, to remain in power," Eisenkot said.

"Anyone who thinks change is guaranteed is mistaken and misleading," he said. "Until the polling stations close on Tuesday, each and every one of us must act knowing that change is in danger. The responsibility for making it happen rests with each and every one of us."

Prime Minister Benjamin Netanyahu warned his voters that if the opposition wins the upcoming election on Tuesday, Israel will "give citizenship to hundreds of thousands of Palestinians."

Speaking on the pro-government Channel 14, Netanyahu said Israel will make the move if the opposition bloc led by election frontrunner Gadi Eisenkot (Yashar) wins the election, adding that it would be "the end of the Jewish state."

Netanyahu said the move will be the result of the new government, which he warned would include Arab parties, allowing the reunification of Palestinian relatives. The prime minister added that "this is exactly what happened" in the aftermath of the Oslo Accords.

The High Court of Justice rejected a petition seeking to prohibit election officials and party representatives from carrying firearms at polling stations, which argued that the presence of armed individuals could intimidate voters, heighten tensions and undermine free and fair elections.

The petition, filed by the Adalah Legal Center for Arab Minority Rights in Israel and the Joint List, followed announcements by Prime Minister Benjamin Netanyahu's Likud party and far-right Finance Minister Bezalel Smotrich's Religious Zionism party that they would deploy some 2,000 election observers in Arab communities, as well as a police directive allowing licensed gun owners to carry their weapons at polling stations.

In its response to the petition, the government said that the decision is a police matter, since police "have the authority and responsibility to determine the security arrangements required on that day." The government said it received no information of "disruptions, disturbances or planned threats against voters."

Joint List chairman Yousef Jabareen said the court "has once again demonstrated that it is neither willing nor able to protect the rights of the Arab public from persecution by the racist right-wing government," adding that Netanyahu "is panicking over the rising voter turnout among Arab citizens."

The son of former Prime Minister Menachem Begin – who founded Prime Minister Benjamin Netanyahu's Likud party and signed Israel's peace treaty with Egypt – urged Likud party members to vote Netanyahu out of office.

"Ten or fifteen years ago, I served in a government headed by Benjamin Netanyahu," former lawmaker Avinadav Begin said. "It was a good government that benefited all Israeli citizens. We held good, constructive, productive discussions – they were closed to the public but completely open to disagreements among colleagues," he said.

"But then bad things happened to Netanyahu, to the point that five years ago, I said about him from the Knesset podium, 'How much further can a person sink? How much lower can he drag himself?' ... Since I said those words, Netanyahu's tragedy has unfolded before our astonished eyes," the retired politician added.

"Yesterday, he threatened us with the destruction of Israel's economy, and today, he threatened us with an atomic bomb being dropped on Tel Aviv, accompanied by a frightening AI-generated image. A prime minister like this is a threat to us all."

Retired IDF general Ofer Winter, who leads the new far-right Amcha Yisrael party, said Prime Minister Benjamin Netanyahu's post showing an AI image of a nuclear strike on Tel Aviv is "utter madness."

Winter, who endorsed Netanyahu in August, said Israel's leaders "need to stop scaring the public." Speaking to Army Radio, Winter added that his party has "the ability to get Israel back on track," adding that the country must "take more initiative" in defense matters.

#3712. American Express cuts 5% of staff and won’t let AI agents ‘run rogue’

📅 26 Oct 2026 | technology | Source

The Amex job cuts come with a $336M restructuring charge, and the finance chief says the aim is to prepare for AI. The CEO says AI agents will only get in through approved channels.

American Express is cutting about 5% of its global workforce and taking a $336M restructuring charge. Chief executive Stephen Squeri told analysts about the cuts on Friday, CNBC’s Hugh Son reported.

The company had almost 77,000 employees at the end of 2025, according to CNBC. Chief financial officer Christophe Le Caillec told CNBC the restructuring was to prepare for AI. It would also make Amex “leaner, more agile”, he said.

“You don’t want to automate processes that are not optimally structured. So what we’re doing here is to reduce layers and increase spans of controls.” Christophe Le Caillec, American Express, to CNBC

Squeri told analysts the charge was about efficiency.

“We’ve got good momentum, but to accelerate innovation and win over the next few years, we needed to become [a] more agile and efficient organization.” Stephen Squeri, American Express, on the earnings call

The Amex job cuts came a day after Barclays confirmed plans to cut 5,000 jobs, also about 5% of its staff. In May, Wall Street’s six largest banks reported 15,000 job cuts between them.

Squeri spent about 10 minutes of his prepared remarks on AI, CNBC reported. Most of it was about using AI to personalise experiences and offers for card members.

He said Amex wants to serve customers who use AI agents. But it will not let assistants “run rogue” through its systems.

“We need to protect our customers [and] merchants, and we’re only going to allow agents to access our systems that are either approved through API or MCP channels.” Stephen Squeri, American Express

APIs and MCP (Model Context Protocol) are two ways for outside software to connect to a company’s systems. On 13 October, Amex published its Intent Intelligence framework. It sets out how to confirm what a customer wants to buy before an AI agent completes the purchase. Google announced its own agent-led payments plans in May.

Revenue rose 9% to a record $20.1bn, Amex said in its earnings release. That was slightly below the $20.12bn analysts expected, according to LSEG data cited by CNBC. Adjusted earnings per share were $4.68, above the $4.48 average estimate.

Net income rose 28% to $3.7bn. It includes a $982M pre-tax gain from selling Amex’s stake in Global Business Travel Group. Part of that gain paid for the restructuring. Amex raised its full-year guidance to $18.30 to $18.60 a share.

The shares fell roughly 4% in Friday afternoon trading, CNBC reported.

#3407. Has Pakistan reopened its border with Iran for trade?

📅 26 Oct 2026 | technology | Source

Traders say some exports are crossing border after a weeklong halt, but Tehran says its cargo is not getting through.

Islamabad, Pakistan – On a normal day, hundreds of freight trucks and vehicles pass through Pakistan-Iran border crossings, carrying goods and people. On October 17, Islamabad suspended cargo traffic through the Gabd-Rimdan and Taftan crossings in Balochistan province, citing security risks.

On Monday, some goods started to move again.

Mir Naveed Jan Kalmati, president of the Gwadar Chamber of Commerce and Industry, told Al Jazeera that trade “partially resumed this weekend”.

Border officials also cleared consignments that were dated from October 16 or earlier, goods truck association officials said.

Haji Noor Muhammad Shahwani, central president of the Balochistan Goods Truck Owners Association, told Al Jazeera from Quetta that Pakistani exports such as rice and bananas were crossing the border.

Trucks loaded in Iran before the closure, including those carrying liquefied petroleum gas (LPG), were also entering Pakistan, he confirmed to Al Jazeera. “Those that were already loaded are clearing and coming out now. They are coming slowly.”

The halt of Iranian LPG to Pakistan created severe shortages and price spikes in Pakistan, which imports the bulk of its energy from the Middle East. Pakistan’s economy has already been impacted by shipping disruptions in the Strait of Hormuz caused by Iran and its response to the United States-Israel war.

Rizwan Ahmed, a customs clearing agent based near the Gabd-Rimdan crossing said customs had told agents to file declarations for the stranded trucks, which would then be released.

“Customs has given us the go-ahead, saying, ‘Bring in as much cargo as you can,’” Ahmed told Al Jazeera. He did not say whether this applied to goods loaded after October 16.

Ahmed said his own consignment of exports had just left. “We have rice, retail goods, potatoes, bananas,” he told Al Jazeera.

But Iran said changes to import paperwork introduced by Islamabad are holding up its cargo at the crossings.

On Sunday, Iran’s ambassador to Pakistan, Reza Amiri Moghadam, said on X that Iran had placed no restrictions on exports to Pakistan, including LPG, and its borders remained open.

“Changes in the implementation of Pakistan’s E-Form procedure for imports from Iran have created a situation in which traders are effectively and literally unable to dispatch cargo from Iran to Pakistan,” he wrote.

The halt in trade between the two neighbours triggered speculation that the Pakistani step might have been taken under US pressure as part of Washington’s efforts to suffocate Iran economically. Pakistan insisted it was done amid a deteriorating security situation in Balochistan province on the border with Iran.

Gunmen attacked trucks waiting near the Gabd crossing on the Pakistani side of the border on Thursday. The military said 10 attackers were killed in operations around Gabd and Gwadar city, and one civilian died.

The attack came in a province that has faced a separatist rebellion for decades.

Balochistan accounted for 131 of the 225 armed attacks recorded nationwide in September, the highest monthly national total in 12 years, according to the Pakistan Institute for Conflict and Security Studies.

Pakistan’s Ministry of Foreign Affairs was asked about the border at its weekly news briefing on Friday.

“Pakistan, as a responsible member of the international community, adheres to and shall continue to adhere to all applicable sanctions regimes in accordance with international and domestic laws,” spokesman Sajjad Haider Khan said.

He said he did not have direct knowledge of the situation on the ground. He did not say whether the border was open or whether the US had pressured Pakistan over the closure.

The Ministry of Commerce did not respond to Al Jazeera’s request for comment.

A Commerce Ministry memorandum from October 16, seen by Al Jazeera, withdrew its October 3 memorandum extending a waiver on the E-Form, also called the EIF, for imports from Iran. It took effect immediately.

The purpose of the E-Form is to record imports and their payment through banks.

Moghadam, the Iranian envoy, said consultations with Pakistan were under way. Citing reports, he added that several hundred LPG trucks had crossed into Gabd but were yet to be unloaded or cleared.

Shahwani, from the Balochistan Goods Truck Owners Association, said the unloading problem had not been resolved.

“It is probably some problem with the banks, something to do with international trade,” he said.

Bakhtiar Ismail, the deputy commissioner of Chagai in Balochistan, wrote on X on October 18 that the closure was a temporary measure to improve border management and support counterterrorism efforts.

Iranian Ministry of Foreign Affairs spokesman Esmaeil Baghaei cited technical issues, according to the IRNA news agency.

Pakistan has mediated between Washington and Tehran since the US-Israel war on Iran began on February 28. The US has since imposed a naval blockade on Iranian ports, impacting Iranian exports. The US has also imposed wide-ranging economic sanctions to “asphyxiate” the Iranian government and cut off its revenue streams.

Imtiaz Baloch, a security analyst who focuses on Balochistan, told Al Jazeera that the US policy of maximum pressure on Iran had a direct or indirect influence.

That could be done directly, by forcing Pakistan’s government to close the border, or indirectly, through companies in the LPG trade or international banking channels, he said. But he added that no concrete or visible statement had linked the closure to US economic policy.

“My assessment is that security is part of it, procedures or technical glitches are part of it and the banking system is also involved,” Baloch said.

#4359. UK looking to give carmakers incentives to turn EVs into ‘power plants on wheels’

📅 26 Oct 2026 | technology | Source

V2G tech enables drivers to charge automatically when electricity is cheaper and then sell some back to the grid at times of high demand

Electric cars that can be used as “power plants on wheels” could be given government backing as part of plans under consideration to help UK manufacturers.

Ministers are considering giving manufacturers incentives to add “vehicle to grid” (V2G) technology to cars as part of its review of the zero-emission vehicle (ZEV) mandate.

The technology allows drivers to charge automatically when electricity is cheap overnight, and then sell some of it back to the grid at times of high demand.

If millions of cars have the ability to send power back – or power the driver’s home (V2H) – it could help to solve one of the big problems of the transition away from fossil fuels to renewables: how to balance the grid during still, cloudy or dark periods when wind and solar generation is lower than electricity demand.

The move could give incentives to help to spread a technology that is not yet widely used, but which could lessen the load on the grid and the need to fire up expensive gas power plants to fill gaps in supply. Home batteries can do the same job.

Smart charging tariffs, which do not need V2G tech, already help with the opposite problem by encouraging electric vehicle (EV) owners to charge up when electricity is overabundant and cheap – reducing the need to pay renewable generators to switch off.

However, campaign groups said the ZEV incentives could also have the perverse effect of allowing carmakers to sell more polluting petrol models in the next few years.

Carmakers could earn a “credit” for selling cars installed with V2G technology, also known as bidirectional charging, according to plans detailed in a government consultation that closed on Friday. That credit would allow the carmakers to sell another petrol car without facing the risk of fines if they miss the ZEV targets.

The government is preparing to cut electric vehicle sales targets under the ZEV mandate. Environmental groups are braced for changes that could mean an additional 147m tonnes of carbon emitted into the atmosphere by 2050.

Ben Nelmes, the chief executive of New AutoMotive, a thinktank tracking the transition to electric cars, said: “Vehicle to grid is exciting technology that could help balance the grid and cut bills, but carmakers should only be rewarded if it’s actually used, not just for fitting it.

“Under these proposals, one or even two extra credits worth around £4,000 each would hand manufacturers thousands per car for kit that costs a few hundred pounds, even though few drivers own the expensive charger needed to use it. Ministers should make sure the people who benefit from V2G are drivers and billpayers, not carmakers’ shareholders.”

Some people already sell their electricity back to the grid through companies such as Octopus Energy in the UK, Renault in France, and BMW in Germany. Octopus Energy has claimed that drivers could save £620 a year with the service.

Vicky Edmonds, the chief executive of the Electric Vehicle Association England, which represents drivers, said: “We have millions of EV drivers in the UK, but only a fraction are using vehicle to grid or storage technologies. These technologies have enormous potential but consumers are still getting to grips with more immediate questions around the cost of their car, the cost of charging, and whether an EV meets their day to day needs.

“It is far too early to be giving manufacturers a credit that effectively allows them to sell fewer EVs, for a technology that has not even been tried and tested, at scale, by drivers in the real world.”

Once the technology takes off, electricity supplied by V2G cars could add up to the equivalent of a power station by 2030, able to provide 1.2 gigawatts of power on demand, according to the National Energy System Operator, which balances Great Britain’s grid. That could grow to as much as 41 GW by 2050, Neso forecasts, enough to meet more than a quarter of peak energy demand.

#3643. Some iPhone Duo Models Are Breaking. iFixit’s Teardown Might Explain Why

📅 26 Oct 2026 | technology | Source

The first batch of Apple’s first folding phone may already have some screen issues.

The iPhone Duo is a strong first foldable from the company, but multiple Reddit reports say the phone's inner screen cracks when customers open the device. Some apparently happen very soon, like during device setup. (Others might be from influencers trying to break the thing, like YouTuber JerryRigEverything.) People are already calling this “crackgate.”

It's unclear how widespread the problem is or whether it only affects a small number of units. (WIRED's review unit hasn't had any issues after more than two weeks of daily use.) Apple did not respond to WIRED's request for comment.

This is familiar territory for foldable phones. A hinge means strain on the screen and the components inside. It's also a place for dust to seep in and break the phone, as with Samsung’s original Galaxy Fold in 2019, forcing the company to pause sales and fix the issue. Some devices handle it better than others, and as the product category has grown, those issues have largely diminished. But fewer problems don't mean easier fixes.

On Sunday, repair company iFixit released its teardown of the iPhone Duo, including a video detailing the specific parts inside and what to look out for if people need to fix their $1,999 phone one day—maybe sooner than they think.

The phone got a provisional repairability rating of 5/10, though that score may be updated to factor in how Apple supplies replacement parts. A 5/10 doesn’t sound great, but it’s actually the highest repair rating iFixit has given any foldable phone.

“The state of foldables today is one of the hardest engineering challenges across a number of disciplines out there in the market today,” iFixit CEO Kyle Wiens said at an event the day before Apple announced its folding phone in September. “It is the pinnacle of material engineering and materials science. It is ludicrously difficult to accomplish this.”

The iPhone Duo earned good marks on some repair fronts. The battery and back panels are straightforward to remove and replace. The outer trim comes off in one piece. The 5.4-inch front screen comes off easily enough. Disassembling the pieces and putting everything back together is doable too, since almost everything is secured with brackets and screws you can remove and reuse.

The tricky part, according to iFixit, is the inner display. A thin matte nano-texture finish sits on top of the OLED screen, and if you’re not careful, you could easily peel the layer off. (Apple strictly tells users not to remove this finish on a support page.)

Removing the full inner screen requires melting the adhesive with a heat gun and some real elbow grease to pull the screen away. Users have reported breaking this part of the screen, and it seems it is the most fragile component and the hardest to replace. (Apple has an inner screen repair service, but it costs $999.) The Duo also collects dust in typical places like the USB-C port, outer edges, and speaker, but it accumulates along the inner hinge, causing it to sound "crunchy" like other folding devices when dust gets inside.

Apple long resisted letting users repair their own devices. When it finally started offering real repair options in 2021, its programs got off to a rough start and were less helpful than they could have been. Slowly but surely, thanks to various legislation in the US and the European Union, Apple has been introducing efforts to let people fix their own things. The iPhone Duo had a widely accessible repair manual on launch day, which hasn't always been the case for how the company shares that kind of information.

The iPhone Duo may not be perfectly repairable—folding phones might never get there. But when a device starts having problems like cracked screens, it’ll be nice to know how to fix the thing.

Car rental giant Turo will start advertising which Teslas on its marketplace are outfitted with the automaker’s advanced driver assistance system, Full Self-Driving (Supervised) — or FSD, a collaboration that could boost further adoption of the driving software.

Eligible Turo “hosts” who rent out their cars on the platform will also receive a free one-month trial of FSD. Turo said it is working with Tesla to directly access the automaker’s API to verify which cars have FSD enabled.

The announcement comes just a few days after Tesla offered updated figures that show the carmaker is finally getting more customers to pay for the software, which can handle most driving tasks but still requires human supervision.

Last week, Tesla revealed that around 1.7 million customers have paid for FSD globally, out of more than 10 million cars built and sold to date. The company said more than 75% of customers who took delivery of a car in North America during the third-quarter paid for an FSD subscription. That was a roughly 20% increase from the second quarter, though the company’s U.S. sales have been in decline.

Tesla has spent much of the last year re-working some of its showrooms to focus more on FSD as a selling point. In a post last week, the company’s chief executive Elon Musk said the effort is working.

“People are buying FSD with a car attached, not the other way around,” wrote Musk.

“Customers are now coming in and saying: ‘Can we get into an FSD drive?’ They’re not asking for [Model] 3, [Model] Y, or Cybertruck,” Tesla’s finance chief Vaibhav Taneja said on last week’s earnings call.

For Turo, the addition of FSD rentals gives the company a chance to boost what are already some of its most heavily-rented vehicles. Turo said Tesla’s Model 3 and Model Y are the third and fourth-most popular vehicles on its platform this year. “[T]his integration further increases host earning opportunities as interest in self-driving technology grows,” said Turo.

“This industry-first integration with Tesla is another example of how Turo is redefining what car rental and access can look like,” Turo chief executive Andre Haddad said in a statement. “Most travelers have had limited opportunity to experience driver’s assistance technology, but the demand is clear. Our own research shows that 55% of Americans are interested in driverless technology for travel, growing to 71% for Gen Z. Turo’s marketplace model is uniquely positioned to close the gap between consumer curiosity and hands-on access.”

Customers who rent an FSD-enabled Tesla through Turo will be served cautionary language that requires drivers to monitor the software, and be ready to take over at any moment. Drivers will also have the option to view a Tesla-created tutorial.

“When enabled, your vehicle will drive you almost anywhere with your active supervision, requiring minimal intervention,” a screenshot of the Turo app reads.

The listings themselves, meanwhile, allow owners to promote their cars as “Self-Driving,” according to another screenshot provided by Turo.

Explore eight remarkable pyramids beyond Egypt.

Think of a pyramid, and chances are you’re picturing Egypt. But here’s a surprising fact: Sudan actually has more surviving ancient pyramids than Egypt. And that’s just the beginning.

In Mexico, one of the largest pyramids ever built is hiding beneath what looks like an ordinary hill, with a church sitting on top. In Guatemala, towering Maya temples emerge from the rainforest. And in Iraq, an ancient stepped monument was designed to bring worshippers closer to the realm of the gods.

Across Africa, the Americas, and Asia, ancient societies built various pyramid-shaped structures. Some architectural traditions developed independently, while others, like the pyramids of Nubia, were shaped by cultural exchange with Egypt.

Here are eight extraordinary pyramids outside Egypt, and the fascinating stories behind them.

The Pyramid of the Sun is the largest structure at Teotihuacan, an ancient city northeast of modern Mexico City. Its massive base measures about 225 by 222 meters, and the pyramid rises roughly 65 meters above the surrounding landscape.

But it’s not just an enormous pile of stone. Its builders placed it along the city’s central axis and constructed it as part of a large ceremonial complex surrounded by monumental temples, plazas and avenues. Archaeologists have also found ritual offerings associated with its construction, including the remains of sacrificed children, suggesting that building the monument was itself a religious act.

But perhaps the most intriguing thing about the Pyramid of the Sun is that we don’t know whether its builders actually worshipped the Sun there.

The people who built Teotihuacan left no known record calling it the ‘Pyramid of the Sun.’ They called the structure Tonatiuh Itzacual, which means “enclosure of the Sun,” giving the monument the name by which it is known today. Centuries after the city was abandoned, the Mexica arrived at the ruins and believed Teotihuacan was the place where the sun and moon had been created.

Modern researchers have found that the pyramid’s orientation corresponds to significant dates in the solar year. However, the identity of the deity originally associated with it remains debated. Possibilities include a solar deity, a fertility deity, or a supernatural figure connected to the underworld.

In other words, we know quite a lot about how this enormous monument was built. But its original religious meaning is still partly a mystery.

Egypt may have the world’s most famous pyramids, but its southern neighbour has more surviving examples. Sudan is home to at least 230 ancient pyramids, according to Guinness World Records.

In the Nubian desert of modern Sudan, the pyramids of Meroë are a reminder that the ancient Nile world extended far beyond Egypt.

The pyramid-building tradition of the Kingdom of Kush flourished from approximately the eighth century BCE into the fourth century CE. Royal cemeteries appeared at sites including El-Kurru, Nuri, and Meroë.

Meroë was once a major centre of the ancient Kingdom of Kush, a powerful Nubian kingdom centered in what is now Sudan. The Kushite pyramids are noticeably narrower and steeper than their Egyptian counterparts. Some rise to about 30 meters and were built over underground burial chambers for kings, queens, and members of the royal family.

Their steep sides, compact dimensions, and adjoining funerary chapels gave them a distinctive architectural identity.

The connection with Egypt was real, but Kush was not simply an Egyptian copy. Kushite rulers even controlled Egypt for nearly a century, making the pyramids part of a much larger story of cultural exchange along the Nile.

These monuments remind us that ancient Nubia wasn’t merely a peripheral part of Egyptian history. It was home to a powerful civilization with its own rulers, religious traditions, and architectural achievements.

Cholula’s pyramid has one of the strangest disguises in archaeology. It looks like a hill; and what a hill it is.

Beneath its grassy slopes lies an enormous ancient monument with an estimated volume of 4.45 million cubic meters. That makes Cholula the world’s largest known pyramid by volume, even bigger than the Great Pyramid of Giza.

Unlike many familiar pyramids, Cholula wasn’t constructed in a single monumental building campaign. Successive generations expanded the structure, building new architectural layers over older ones. Over many centuries, the monument grew into the giant artificial mountain we know today.

Centuries of abandonment allowed vegetation and soil to cover the monument. When Spanish colonists arrived in the early 16th century, the buried structure reportedly looked like a natural hill, and a church was eventually built on top.

The church, the Sanctuary of Our Lady of Remedies, remains one of Cholula’s most recognizable landmarks.

Archaeologists later excavated an extensive network of tunnels through the pyramid, revealing its successive construction phases. These passages helped researchers study the monument without dismantling the massive structure above them.

But what makes Cholula extraordinary isn’t just its size. It’s the way different periods of history occupy the same space: an ancient religious monument, centuries of changing cultures, and a Christian sanctuary overlooking the modern city.

At Tikal, pyramids rise from one of the world’s most dramatic archaeological settings—the rainforest of northern Guatemala.

The ancient Maya city is home to numerous monumental buildings, including six conventionally numbered main temples. Some of these structures still rise above the surrounding forest canopy, offering a glimpse of a once-powerful urban civilization.

The Maya built five major pyramid temples at the site between roughly 400 and 900 CE. After Tikal was abandoned, the jungle gradually reclaimed the city, leaving enormous structures emerging from the canopy.

Temple IV is the tallest, reaching about 65 meters. Built around 740 CE during the reign of the Maya ruler Yik’in Chan K’awiil, it is among the tallest surviving temples of the ancient Maya world. Its elevated position offers an extraordinary view across the forest, while the surrounding complex functioned as a major ceremonial and political center.

For the Maya, mountains were important spiritually. They could represent sacred places associated with creation, ancestors, and the supernatural world. Building towering temples allowed rulers to connect political authority with that sacred landscape.

Today, the experience is almost the opposite of what ancient builders intended. Instead of dominating a bustling city, Tikal’s temples rise above a tranquil sea of trees, reminders of a civilization that once transformed the rainforest around them.

Few pyramids in the world look as stunning as El Castillo in Mexico’s Chichen Itza.

Also known as the Temple of Kukulcán, this roughly 30-meter-high Maya pyramid is famous for its architecture and its apparent connections to astronomy and the calendar.

The pyramid has four stairways, each with 91 steps. Add the upper platform and the total becomes 365—matching the number of days in the Maya solar year.

But the pyramid’s most spectacular feature isn’t something carved into the stone. It’s something created by sunlight.

Around the equinoxes, sunlight creates a series of triangular shadows along the northern staircase that appear to form the body of a descending serpent representing Kukulcán, the feathered Serpent deity that the Maya people worshipped.

There are important caveats, however. The 365-step interpretation is widely cited, but the monument’s present form has undergone restoration, so the calendar connection should not be treated as definitive proof that every numerical feature was intentionally designed that way.

Similarly, the serpent-light phenomenon was traditionally associated with the equinoxes, but some studies indicate that the pattern of light and shadow follows a solar cycle throughout the year rather than appearing only on those exact dates.

Mexico isn’t the only place with a pyramid called El Castillo. Across the border in Belize, another monument of the same name rises above the ancient Maya city of Xunantunich.

At roughly 40 meters high, it is one of Belize’s most prominent ancient structures and once served as a ceremonial and civic center.

One of its most remarkable features is a series of elaborate stucco friezes. These decorated surfaces include symbolic figures associated with the supernatural world and offer clues to Maya religious and political beliefs. Parts of the original decoration have been protected, with replicas allowing visitors to appreciate the designs.

But Xunantunich also has a rather unusual claim to fame: a ghost story.

The site is wrapped in a famous local legend about a mysterious woman known as the “Stone Lady.” She is reportedly seen ascending the structure before disappearing into its walls.

In some versions of the tale, the woman wears white and has glowing red eyes. The story is folklore, not archaeological evidence, but it has become so closely associated with the site that the modern name Xunantunich itself means “Stone Woman” or “Maiden of the Rock.”

Between its carved monuments and its enduring local legends, Xunantunich shows how ancient places can acquire entirely new meanings long after the civilizations that built them have disappeared.

Borobudur is not a pyramid in the Egyptian sense. It is a Buddhist monument, and one of the most extraordinary examples of religious architecture ever built. Yet its unusual stepped form has led UNESCO to describe it as a “stepped, unroofed pyramid.” We thought that’s good enough for our list.

Built in Central Java during the 8th and 9th centuries, this temple rises through three major tiers with five square terraces, three circular platforms, and a huge central stupa. The upper platforms contain 72 openwork stupas, each enclosing a Buddha statue.

Its entire architectural arrangement represents a Buddhist understanding of the universe.

According to UNESCO, its successive levels symbolize three realms of existence: the world of desire, the world of forms, and the formless world. Moving upward through the monument becomes a symbolic journey toward enlightenment.

Where an Egyptian pyramid was generally associated with the burial and afterlife of a ruler, Borobudur was designed around a different spiritual experience. Its ascending terraces transformed an architectural journey into a representation of Buddhist cosmology.

Long before the Maya constructed their great stone temples, ancient Mesopotamians were building monumental stepped towers of their own. One of the most remarkable survivors is the Great Ziggurat of Ur, in present-day Iraq.

Built around 2100 BCE during the reign of the Sumerian king Ur-Nammu, the ziggurat originally measured approximately 64 by 46 meters at its base. Its estimated original height was between 21 and 30 meters.

Its massive mudbrick core was covered with fired bricks, while monumental staircases led upward through a series of terraces toward a temple dedicated to Nanna, the Sumerian moon god.

Unlike a conventional pyramid, the ziggurat was built as a series of terraces rather than a single sloping structure. It wasn’t designed as a royal tomb. Instead, it formed part of a larger religious and administrative complex at the heart of the ancient city.

The idea was that the raised temple brought the divine realm symbolically closer to the human world. It also made the building visible from far beyond the city, reinforcing the importance of Ur and its patron deity.

The ziggurat has undergone several major restorations, including work during the reign of the Neo-Babylonian king Nabonidus and a substantial reconstruction of its lower façade under Saddam Hussein in the 1980s. The original summit temple no longer survives.

The eight monuments in this list are only a glimpse of a much larger world of ancient pyramid-shaped architecture. From the deserts of Sudan to the rainforests of Central America, these structures reveal how different civilizations used monumental buildings to connect the earthly world with ideas about gods, kings, and the afterlife.

And archaeologists are still uncovering new details. In 2024, researchers announced the discovery of an approximately 1,700-year-old royal Maya tomb at Chochkitam in Guatemala, excavated in 2022. Inside the burial were a jade mosaic mask and carved human bones bearing imagery and glyphs that may help researchers reconstruct the history of a little-known ruling dynasty.

Ultimately, these pyramids tell us something more interesting than the fact that ancient people in different parts of the world liked building tall, triangular monuments. Similar shapes could serve profoundly different purposes. One civilization built a staircase toward the gods; another built a royal tomb; another created an architectural representation of spiritual enlightenment.

The pyramid may be one of humanity’s most recognizable architectural forms. But there has never been just one pyramid story.

#3717. Verizon raises 2026 forecast and signs $2bn AI deal with AWS

📅 26 Oct 2026 | technology | Source

Verizon raised its forecasts for 2026 on Monday after a strong quarter for new phone customers. It also announced a deal worth more than $2bn with Amazon Web Services (AWS) to support AI workloads.

Under the AWS agreement, Verizon will provide high-capacity network connections and help add computing power spread across many sites. The deal is aimed at generative and agentic AI, which can carry out tasks on its own.

The deal follows a Verizon agreement in July worth more than $1bn to connect Google’s data centres over unused fibre.

As a result, Verizon now expects 1.1 million to 1.2 million new postpaid phone customers this year. Before, it had aimed for the top half of a range from 750,000 to 1 million.

The company also lifted its adjusted earnings forecast to $5.04 to $5.06 a share. It now expects free cash flow to grow by almost 12%.

Revenue rose 1.5% to $34.3bn, short of the $34.74bn analysts expected, according to LSEG data cited by Reuters. Fewer customers upgraded their phones after Verizon cut device discounts, Reuters reported.

Profit was stronger. Net income rose 5.2% to $5.3bn. Adjusted earnings per share grew 8.3% to $1.31, and free cash flow rose 12.1% to $7.8bn.

Verizon kept its plan to spend $16bn to $16.5bn on its network and other assets this year.

“We are continuing to raise the bar, and we are only one year into a multi-year growth story,” said chief executive Dan Schulman.

Schulman has restructured the company since taking over about a year ago. In June, Verizon also launched simpler phone plans with clearer pricing.

Verizon shares rose 2.4% before the market opened. Elsewhere, demand for computing capacity has driven other large AI data-centre deals this year.
