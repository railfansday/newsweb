#1659. Lisandro Martinez set to leave Man United in defensive overhaul, how abusive Leeds fans got to Sir Alex Ferguson despite Elland Road police guard, Harry Amass heads for exit and Kai Rooney's rapid rise: MAN UNITED CONFIDENTIAL

📅 26 Oct 2026 | other | Source

By CHRIS WHEELER, NORTHERN SPORTS WRITER and NATHAN SALT - FOOTBALL REPORTER

Published: 17:00 BST, 20 October 2026 | Updated: 15:24 BST, 22 October 2026

Manchester United’s defensive rebuild next summer is set to signal the end of Lisandro Martinez’s Old Trafford career.

The Argentine landed himself in hot water after last week’s 2-0 Champions League defeat at Atletico Madrid when he criticised his employers over United’s transfer policy in the summer window, saying he expected more of director of football Jason Wilcox, but sources insist that would not be the reason for the defender's departure.

Martinez was fined by the club and apologised for the timing of his comments in a statement that added: ‘Everyone at the club is doing everything they can to make the team successful.'

The 28-year-old started Sunday’s 1-1 draw with Leeds at Elland Road, but came off at half-time with a thigh injury having already spent 20 months out of action during his four-year United career. When he is fit, Martinez has been a regular on the left side of central defence since moving to United from Ajax for £57million in 2022, making 115 appearances and scoring four goals.

But he will be out of contract at the end of June and is unlikely to sign a new one. United would be very reluctant to let the 2022 World Cup winner leave as a free agent, so it’s more probable that the club would trigger a 12-month extension in his contract and try to sell him in the summer.

United retain an interest in Jarrad Branthwaite having had two offers worth up to £50m rejected by Everton in 2024, and could try again in the summer.

Lisandro Martinez is set to leave Manchester United next summer to begin the club's defensive overhaul

United retain an interest in Jarrad Branthwaite (centre) having had two offers worth up to £50m rejected by Everton in 2024, and could try again in the summer

Uncertainty also hangs over the futures of Luke Shaw and Harry Maguire. Shaw is out of contract after 12 years at Old Trafford and doesn’t have an extension clause in his current deal, unlike Maguire who has a 12-month option to extend the one-year contract that he signed in April.

United have a decision to make on both players, but it's understood there are no doubts over Matthijs de Ligt's future at the club even though the Dutchman has been out of action for almost a year with a back injury that required surgery. De Ligt, who has been an unused substitute for the last two games against Leeds and Atletico Madrid, is under contract until 2029 with the option of another year.

It’s a measure of the bad blood between Manchester United and Leeds that Sir Alex Ferguson’s chauffeur-driven car arrived at Elland Road on Sunday protected by four police outriders.

The two clubs were taking no chances with Ferguson’s safety at one of the most incendiary fixtures in the English football calendar, and made sure that the legendary United boss got into the ground without any incident.

The 84-year-old is using a walking stick at the moment as he recovers from an achilles problem that has also required him to wear a protective boot.

Despite the efforts of the police and Leeds security staff, however, Ferguson was verbally abused by one fan in a video circulated on social media as he made his way from his seat in the stands – where he was sat with his former kit man Albert Morgan – to the directors’ lounge at half-time.

Ferguson dominated the rivalry with Leeds during his time as United boss, winning 20 of his 36 matches against them and losing only five.

If further evidence of the animosity between the two clubs was needed, ex-Leeds boss and current Canada head coach Jesse Marsch referred to United as ‘the scum’ when he congratulated Daniel Farke’s side on their performance in the 1-1 draw in a post on Monday.

Ferguson dominated the rivalry with Leeds when he was United manager, winning 20 of his 36 matches against them - the last of which being this 3-0 League Cup win in 2011

Contrary to speculation over summit talks between Michael Carrick and Wilcox at Carrington on Monday, Confidential understands the two men did not meet at the training ground.

Even though the players were off after Sunday's draw at Leeds before training and travelling to Italy on Tuesday for the Champions League clash with Como, Carrick and Wilcox didn't have an opportunity to get together after a tough week for both men.

Wilcox came under scrutiny in the wake of Lisandro Martinez’s bombshell TV interview in Madrid, while United’s 2-0 defeat by Atletico and a pair of 1-1 draws with Tottenham and Leeds in the Premier League left Carrick without a win in six games.

However, insiders say that Wilcox still retains the trust of minority owner Sir Jim Ratcliffe, and there is no appetite to change head coach despite Carrick overseeing United’s worst start to a league season in 40 years.

There is a growing expectation that Harry Amass will leave United in January after playing just 18 minutes of first-team football so far this season.

Even with a raft of injuries at left back, 19-year-old Amass has found himself on the outside looking in with right backs Diogo Dalot and Noussair Mazraoui and centre back Ayden Heaven all chosen ahead of him while Luke Shaw and Patrick Dorgu are sidelined through injury. His only appearance this season was a cameo off the bench in the 4-0 Champions League win over Sabah last month.

United were open to both a loan or permanent move for Amass in the summer, but their inability to recruit a new left back meant the club’s hands were tied. Burnley were among the teams who held talks before choosing not to proceed, and Confidential understands around a dozen Championship clubs are ready to come back to the negotiating table with the January window fast approaching.

Amass, who joined from Watford's academy three years ago, thrived on loan at Sheffield Wednesday last season, and with United out of the Carabao Cup and seemingly reluctant to use Amass even when stretched to the max in his position, expect movement for the youngster in the next eight to ten weeks.

Harry Amass is expected to leave United in January after only making one appearance this season, off the bench in the Champions League against Sabah

United are one of the Premier League clubs being linked with a move for Bournemouth winger Marcus Tavernier in January.

However, Old Trafford sources say they are not proactively tracking the 27-year-old, who is also said to be attracting interest from Liverpool and Chelsea four years after he moved to the Vitality Stadium from Middlesbrough in a £10m deal.

Tavernier has been in fine form to start the season, scoring four times in seven league games including in Bournemouth's 2-0 win over Sunderland on Sunday that secured the Cherries their first three points of the campaign.

It’s understood rival clubs have been encouraged by Bournemouth’s willingness to consider selling players at the right price in January. England midfielder Alex Scott – who was on United’s radar before they signed Carlos Baleba, Youri Tielemans and Andrey Santos as part of a midfield rebuild in the summer – may be one of them if clubs offer between £80-100m.

While Wayne Rooney generates plenty of headlines behind a microphone these days, it was son Kai who made a statement on the pitch over the past week

While Wayne Rooney generates plenty of headlines behind a microphone these days, it was son Kai who made a statement on the pitch over the past week.

Injuries have been a source of real frustration for the 16-year-old, but he is fit again and back in the fold. Rooney made his UEFA Youth League debut for the Under 19s away to Atletico Madrid, and followed that up three days later with his Under 21s debut against Nottingham Forest.

He dropped back down to the Under 18s, also against Forest, to get himself back on the scoresheet but things are heating up nicely for Rooney who remains one of the only true centre-forward options in the academy.

Chido Obi is out on loan while Louie Bradbury has joined Stoke City. It means that Rooney takes on huge significance across the age groups, not least with United having used a false-nine system to this point in the Under 19s and Under 21s.

#1655. Kayode: Mancini ‘showed enormous trust’, I have no favourite Serie A club

📅 26 Oct 2026 | other | Source

Italy talent Michael Kayode says Roberto Mancini showed ‘enormous trust’ in him and admits he doesn’t have a favourite club in Serie A, although watching ‘Juve, Milan, Inter and Roma was spectacular.’

Kayode made his senior Italy debut during the latest international break, scoring in his first appearance against Turkiye.

“Honestly, I would have been happy just to come on in the final minute to make my international debut,” he told Sky Sport Italia.

“Instead, the coach showed enormous trust in me by giving me a place in the starting line-up against Turkiye. Then things went better than even I could have imagined. It was a match I’ll never forget. Representing your country is the most wonderful thing of all.”

Kayode has been playing for Premier League side Brentford since January 2025, when he completed an initial loan move from Fiorentina.

Brentford made his move permanent for €18m the following summer.

Kayode has scored one goal in eight appearances across all competitions with Brentford this season, helping his club climb to 6th place in the Premier League table, just three points behind Chelsea in fourth.

“I think it’s a fantastic start,” the Italian defender admitted.

“We’ve worked incredibly hard since the beginning of the season to reach this level, and we’re really happy with what we’re doing. We’re not setting any expectations: we’re taking it one game at a time.

“I was lucky enough to work with Thomas Frank for six months when I first arrived, and [Keith] Andrews was part of his staff as the coach responsible for set pieces and routines. So we all knew him really well,” Kayode continued.

“When he was promoted to manager, we were all delighted, and it made the transition very straightforward for everyone at the club. Obviously, we weren’t expecting to have such a fantastic league campaign last year. It’s a shame we missed out on Europe by one point. But we’ve begun this season brilliantly; we’re establishing ourselves, and I think we can achieve great things.”

Kayode spent part of his childhood in the Juventus academy but was ultimately released by the Bianconeri. As he explained at a press conference attended by Football Italia in September, the club’s decision did little to dampen his determination to pursue a career in football.

“It was tough, of course, really tough. But quitting? No, never,” he added in his latest interview.

“After all, I was 14 when I left: it would have been crazy to quit. But obviously there were sad, difficult moments.

“My parents have always taught me that, whatever happens, whether it’s something terrible or something wonderful, you should always smile and stay positive in life,” he added.

Kayode went on to name the three football legends he would have loved to play with: “Ronaldo, the phenomenon, Maradona and Maldini,” and said Erling Haaland is probably the best player in the Premier League currently.

Kayode also played for Fiorentina, with which he made his Serie A debut in 2024, but does he have a favourite club in Italy?

“I never supported one particular team; I never focused on just one,” he replied.

“But Serie A was at an incredible level, especially when I was little. Watching Juve, Milan, Inter and Roma was spectacular.”

#1651. Newcastle Confidential Q&A with CRAIG HOPE: Tino Livramento's mystery injury revealed, what sources are telling me about Matthias Jaissle's future, Anthony Elanga fitness boost and January transfer plans

📅 26 Oct 2026 | other | Source

Published: 16:59 BST, 21 October 2026 | Updated: 19:06 BST, 22 October 2026

Newcastle United are a club with more questions than answers right now, after a run of three defeats in four matches that leaves them 11th in the Premier League.

Two painful defeats last week, away at Coventry and at home to Aston Villa, have punctured some of the confidence that had built up from the club's strong start to the season.

From Matthias Jaissle's future and tactics to injuries and January transfer plans, there is no shortage of issues to address. And for this week's Newcastle Confidential, we've handed the floor to you.

Here, CRAIG HOPE tackles your biggest questions with insight and information from inside St James' Park…

Q. How much time will Jaissle be given if results don't improve? Is he already under serious pressure? (Kazuyoshi Funaki, Sibierski’s Baldy Heed & Clare Bremner)

As a journalist, it’s our job to stay across the above questions every day, especially when results aren’t good. That does not mean we think the manager should be changed (and I don’t, in this instance) but exploring the notion is valid.

Everything I’ve had back in the past week or so has been in support of Jaissle. So much so, the idea of a ‘vote of confidence’ type message was dismissed even as a suggestion. Ultimately, it is PIF who will decide – and in five years of ownership they have only sacked one manager, Steve Bruce a couple of weeks after the takeover. The message from their side is one of calm and patience.

Everything I’ve had back in the past week or so from sources in and around the club has been in support of head coach Matthias Jaissle

A manager cannot lose every game, of course, and the team entering the bottom three and staying there for a prolonged period would require a serious reconsideration of said patience – which sources have acknowledged this week – but while Newcastle are in a relatively comfortable league position, all of the hierarchy’s energy will go into supporting the head coach.

Q. Will the club reconsider its Vision 2030 ambitions, accept that this is a season of transition and give Jaissle their full backing? (Doddsy)

In terms of Vision 2030, it stands, and no one internally is rowing back on that. Chief executive David Hopkinson sees such ambition as imperative to progress and exuding a message of confidence and bullish intent.

I would not draw direct correlation when it comes to Jaissle’s future and Vision 2030, and we should not take the 2030 date as a literal deadline of ‘best club in the world or heads will roll’. It is more Hopkinson’s way of focusing minds from an off-field perspective.

However, Vision 2030 will be a stick with which to beat the hierarchy if results do not improve – that is the upshot of being so bold. I admire it in many ways, and believe it to be necessary from an internal standpoint, but I’m not sure it will do them too many favours in the short term.

Q. Why did the owners choose Jaissle as manager? (Poolie Mags and MuckeFe111)

Well, it was not, as some have speculated, because they wanted a manager from a PIF-owned club. It took £9million to buy out Jaissle from his Al Ahli contract and talks were far from straightforward. When I suggested Newcastle might return to Al Ahli in an effort to sign young French midfielder Valentin Atangana, said to be a Jaissle favourite, it was dismissed on account of the somewhat frosty relations.

Newcastle’s hierarchy believed – and still do – they were getting a coach who could be an elite-level manager. This summer, I detected an absolute conviction among those who appointed Jaissle that he would succeed.

Sporting director Ross Wilson called Hopkinson after his first meeting with Jaissle and the latter knew immediately that Wilson was sold on the German. So much so, there was one meeting where Newcastle chiefs wanted to walk straight back into the restaurant and offer him the job on the spot.

Other options, such as Kieran McKenna, were explored (and Hopkinson has said 130 candidates came up in their data analysis) but all roads led to Jaissle once they knew it was possible.

Sporting director Ross Wilson (left) called David Hopkinson (right) after his first meeting with Jaissle and the latter knew immediately that Wilson was sold on the German

Former Ipswich manager Kieran McKenna (left) was one of the options Newcastle looked at but they were set on Jaissle

Q. Why do you think a section of our fanbase and media have turned against Jaissle after a handful of weeks and games with barely any pre-season? Teams below us have got less grief in the last week! (G Star)

It’s a fair question, but online reaction is framed through results. Team wins, manager good. Team loses, manager bad. So the extreme reaction we’re seeing to Jaissle among some fans, primarily online, is governed by that, I suspect. If Newcastle beat Crystal Palace, all will be well (for a few days until Everton).

In reality, the truth I feel is somewhere in the middle. There is concern at obvious issues when it comes to game plan and shape, but also understanding that Jaissle inherited a squad with missing parts. The boos after Aston Villa were for a combination of the two: a poor performance coupled with frustration at a diminished playing staff and the likelihood of a difficult season. But yes, online especially, I think a more balanced reaction to results would reflect better on all fanbases, including Newcastle.

As for the media, I don’t think we have turned on Jaissle. We have to report on what have been two alarming performances (and even before the recent defeats, there was reason to urge caution despite decent results) but, on the whole, I believe we accept he needs time. Saying that, there also has to be evidence of progress.

All of the above is also the price on the ticket at a club like Newcastle – the scrutiny and interest will always be there. It will be a worrying day when it is not.

Q. Why are you calling out the current manager’s tactics and results when, in my opinion, you said nothing last season? (Micky B and Magpie Lee)

The criticism of performances and tactics was there (YouTube headlines on my channel from March and April included: ‘Broken: This team is not a team anymore', 'Too Soft', 'One Word: Weak' and 'This Didn’t Work!'). But I believed Eddie Howe was still the right manager to turn things around and that he deserved one below-par season. I stand by that and always will.

Yes, at the time I was fully aware of a want (mainly online) to go after the manager, but I believed from my conversations around the club that Howe retained the support of his players and the hierarchy. I also thought that was right.

I’m not afraid to go after managers (see Steve McClaren and Steve Bruce...) but I’ll do so when I believe it’s fair, not just because a team is going through a difficult spell. My belief remains that Howe was the best manager in the club’s recent history, while also falling short towards the end of last season.

As for Jaissle, I’m analysing his tactics and results, of course, but on the whole I’d say I’ve been supportive of him. I don’t think his team is playing well at all, but there is mitigation.

I don’t think Jaissle's team is playing well at all, but there is mitigation

Q. What would the owners consider a successful season? (Adrian Bromfield and Ben Levey)

There is a hope, and perhaps even an expectation, of European football. Financial forecasts have been drawn for all eventualities, and Newcastle can absorb a finish similar to last season (12th), but returning to continental competition is openly spoken about as an ambition in conversations I’ve had.

How does that tally with patience for the new manager? Well, it doesn’t, does it? And that is the fascination in seeing how this season plays out. Because, for me, qualifying for Europe is probably unlikely.

Q. Is there any update on Tino Livramento? (various)

Yes, Confidential can exclusively reveal that Livramento has suffered a knee injury. Given his history of significant knee problems, the club are understandably cautious.

I'm told that the issue, first felt in training last month, was initially thought to be innocuous. However, after tests, it was revealed that Livramento needed a period of rest and recovery. He has avoided serious injury, I understand, and has been doing some light work on the grass the past week or so.

It is too early for a definite return date, but work has now begun in terms of easing him back to full fitness. The England defender was forced to pull out of last month’s international fixtures and he is unlikely to make next month’s internationals either.

Christian Huggel is a professor in the Department of Geography, University of Zurich, Zurich, Switzerland.

Simon K. Allen is a senior scientist in the Department of Geography, University of Zurich, Zurich, Switzerland, and at the Institute for Environmental Sciences, University of Geneva, Geneva, Switzerland.

Many houses were submerged or washed away by the August flash floods in Nepal. Credit: Prabin Ranabhat/AFP via Getty

On 26 August, an avalanche and massive flood swept through Nepal’s Rasuwa district. Thousands of lives were lost, homes were destroyed, roads and energy infrastructure were washed away, and trade and tourism were disrupted.

Disaster early-warning systems can succeed — but collective action is needed

This catastrophe should not be treated as just another disaster. It is the latest — and worst — in a series of exceptionally massive deluges of rock, ice and debris from mountains. The unprecedented scale of the damage shows that current approaches of assessing and managing these risks have failed. Moreover, with global warming accelerating, further disasters of this magnitude are expected.

How scientists analyse and officials manage environmental risks in mountainous regions must urgently be restructured. Here we outline how such hazards are changing and propose how researchers can assess coupled, cascading risks to protect communities and save lives.

The Rasuwa disaster is not a freak event. Over the past few years, the world has seen several similar massive rock and ice collapses with ensuing floods (see ‘Cascading risks’).

Sources: M. C. Matthew et al. Landslides 21, 2307–2326 (2024); D. Schneider et al. Earth Surf. Process. Landf. 36, 1948–1966 (2011); Y. Zhong et al. Landslides 22, 989–1001 (2025); J. A. Coe et al. Landslides 15, 393–407 (2018); A. Kääb et al. Cryosphere 15, 1751–1785 (2021); Alaska Volcano Observatory yearly reports (https://avo.alaska.edu)

In February 2021 in India, an avalanche from Ronti peak led to a huge flood in the Chamoli district of Uttarakhand. The flow of water and debris devastated the Ronti Gad valley, killing more than 200 people and destroying hydropower structures1.

Just a few months later, in June 2021 in Nepal, a small glacial lake burst its banks, sending flood waters surging into the Melamchi River. This triggered a cascade of hazards that washed away more than 50 million cubic metres of sediment and altered river-bed levels by roughly 20 metres in some places2. Around 600 people were displaced and at least 20 died. The region’s freshly established drinking-water infrastructure was severely damaged and vast areas of agricultural land were lost.

Losing your way of life to climate change matters — and we need a way to measure it

Losing your way of life to climate change matters — and we need a way to measure it

Similarly, in October 2023 in Sikkim, India, a massive slide of glacial sediment into South Lhonak glacier lake initiated another outburst and flood. The waters swept an incredible 270 million m3 of sediment down the Teesta River. More than 100 people died, around 26,000 buildings were damaged and hydropower structures were destroyed. The impacts, including infrastructure damage, affected Bangladesh, too3.

Furthermore, in May 2025, the village of Blatten in the Swiss Alps was buried under an avalanche that released nearly 10 million m3 of rock and ice after a glacier collapsed. The glacier was weakened by a series of rock slides that deposited material onto it4. The village was destroyed and there was one fatality, but the other residents were evacuated before it was inundated.

And, in Alaska, less than three months later, an extraordinary landslide (roughly 64 million m3) crashed onto South Sawyer Glacier and into Tracy Arm Fjord5, generating a tsunami with heights of close to 500 m. Fortunately, this happened a few hours before cruise ships would have been travelling through the fjord, avoiding what might have been a maritime disaster.

Roads, bridges and other infrastructure were swept away by the water after the August avalanche in Nepal.Credit: Ambir Tolang/NurPhoto via Getty

The Rasuwa disaster exceeds these events. On the northern slopes of Langtang Lirung mountain, a failure in the bedrock caused a massive rock avalanche that took the overlying glacier with it. The resulting avalanche (around 125 million m3 of rock and ice) is one of the largest on record worldwide.

As the ice, debris and water rushed down the Lhende Khola, Bhotekoshi and Trishuli river valleys, the ice melted and extra sediment and water were incorporated into the flow. Damage extended over 100 kilometres from the source. More than 1,400 people have died, more than 5,000 are missing and tens of thousands have been displaced. Settlements, roads and bridges were washed away and around 10% of Nepal’s hydropower production was knocked out. Reconstruction costs have been put at around US$5 billion.

And on 11 October, the Himalaya region of central Nepal was hit by floods in the upper Budhi Gandaki river valley that were triggered by another large rock and ice avalanche from a steep mountain slope. The avalanche was around half the size of the August collapse. The floods damaged settlements, roads, bridges and hydropower structures.

These events resulted from a combination of interacting and cascading processes: collapses of steep rock or glacier slopes, failures of banks of sediment, outburst floods from lakes and further entrainment of water, debris and sediment by the flood as it raced downstream.

The scientific community knows this and has repeatedly signalled the need for an improved understanding of these cascading hazards and for them to be incorporated into risk analyses6,7. But, so far, little has changed in how such hazards and risks are considered in practice.

The likelihood of one-off events is typically gauged on the basis of the historical record. But events at the scale that we are now seeing are rare or missing from those archives. That makes it hard to predict how frequently massive avalanche-triggered floods might occur in future — the number seen over the past few years is too small to determine long-term trends. But one thing is certain: the world is warming, meaning further rock-slope failures and glacier collapses are likely to occur in coming decades. What has happened historically will not be a good model for what is to come.

Expanding development in mountainous areas is also increasing the exposure of people, settlements and infrastructure to hazards. For example, hydropower development in the Trishuli basin in Rasuwa has expanded since 2010, as has development along the road to the Rasuwagadhi–Kerung border between China and Nepal.

Scientists and decision makers must rethink how they analyse hazards and risks in mountainous regions. Rather than a single method, we propose a complementary set of approaches that can be combined as needed depending on the context.

First, define the purpose and scope of the assessment. For instance, a risk analysis can inform land-use planning, guide funding allocations, identify protection gaps, calculate potential economic losses or establish priorities for risk management at scales ranging from a single hydropower plant to large populated areas. Together, governments and local communities need to decide what level of risk they are willing to tolerate and consider how to protect the most-vulnerable groups and infrastructure.

Second, we suggest applying a combination of several risk-evaluation methodologies. Each approach covers a specific risk aspect and is complemented by the others. Disasters such as the one in Rasuwa underline the importance of using several tools to capture the multifaceted impacts comprehensively.

Probabilistic risk analysis can be used to compute the distribution of asset losses for events of various sizes and frequencies8. For example, for a hydropower plant, such an analysis might provide a statistical representation of the extent of damage in relation to different event probabilities.

However, performing a probabilistic analysis is challenging when assessing future events that have few or no precedents. In such cases, we suggest that analysts consider using models of credible, physically plausible scenarios. These can be built from likelihoods and judgements based on existing knowledge9 and by extending the analysis to a wide range of possible events.

For this, the geological record can sometimes provide clues. For example, in Rasuwa, large boulders lying well beyond the current river bed are testimonies of similar floods some 5,000 years ago10. A plausibility analysis would then check whether such an event is plausible under current or near-future conditions.

Storyline approaches that consider a range of far-reaching possibilities in both qualitative and quantitative ways should be applied to explore cascading effects beyond current populations and structures11. For instance, the Rasuwa disaster destroyed important trade and transport routes between Nepal and China with consequences for Nepal’s economy. The destruction of hydropower sites also affects neighbouring countries that depend on energy imports, including Bangladesh.

Risk-network analysis can be used to address gaps in risk assessments, such as the lack of inclusion of the consequences for disadvantaged populations. Such methods evaluate, for example, connections between flooded houses and their inhabitants, lost local businesses and livelihoods, and disruptions to social networks and education and health-care services.

Arsenal are reportedly exploring a move for Bayern Munich goalkeeper Leonard Prescott as Mikel Arteta looks to strengthen his long-term options between the posts.

According to an exclusive report from TEAMtalk, the Gunners have entered the race for the highly rated teenager, whose uncertain contract situation in Germany could open the door to a move to the Premier League.

The 17-year-old is regarded as one of German football’s most promising young goalkeepers, having progressed through Bayern’s youth system since joining from Union Berlin in 2023.

Prescott has already caught the attention of the German champions’ senior setup, earning a place on the Bundesliga bench at just 16. His impressive development has drawn interest from several European clubs, and Arsenal is now considering whether they can persuade him to continue his career in north London.

The teenager’s future remains uncertain, with his contract nearing expiry and extension talks reportedly proving difficult. His representatives are understood to want greater clarity on his path to regular first-team football.

Arsenal could offer an appealing long-term project, particularly as the club continues planning for the future of its goalkeeping department. David Raya remains the established first choice, but the Gunners must consider options beyond the Spaniard’s current spell at the Emirates.

However, convincing Bayern to let go of one of their brightest prospects will not be straightforward. The Bundesliga giants want to keep the goalkeeper but could still agree to new terms.

Chelsea, Tottenham Hotspur and Juventus are also monitoring the situation, meaning Arsenal will face significant competition if they decide to formalise their interest.

Bayern’s goalkeeping succession plans could influence Prescott’s decision. Manuel Neuer is approaching the latter stages of his career, but Jonas Urbig is also viewed as a potential long-term successor.

Arsenal must therefore show they can offer a clearer route to senior football. With Prescott’s contract situation unresolved, the Gunners could secure a promising young talent before his value increases further.

#1642. England star Marc Guehi suggests taking the knee may have 'lost its meaning' and questions its significance as he leads Premier League stars in rejecting the anti-racism gesture

📅 26 Oct 2026 | other | Source

By JACK GAUGHAN and BEN WILLCOCKS, ASSISTANT SPORTS EDITOR

Published: 13:18 BST, 19 October 2026 | Updated: 09:00 BST, 23 October 2026

Marc Guehi claimed that taking the knee could have lost its meaning as an anti-racism message after not taking part in the gesture on Saturday.

The Manchester City and England defender stood instead of kneeling before their win over Ipswich Town, whose defender Issa Diop also took the same stance on a ‘No Room For Racism’ weekend.

Dominic Calvert-Lewin, Ainsley Maitland-Niles, Rayan and Tyler Adams all chose to remain standing at their matches, with fans at Elland Road jeering Leeds United and Manchester United stars who chose to kneel.

Guehi admitted that he is unsure on the racism campaign as a whole, adding that ‘maybe’ taking the knee has a diminishing impact.

‘I’ve not been taking the knee for a while now,’ Guehi said. ‘I’ll always clap just because of what it signifies. But in terms of if something can be done? Hopefully. Hopefully we can do a bit more but we will see.’

Guehi added: ‘More can be done. More can always be done. We’ll have to see how we move the campaign along.

‘I don’t think it’s necessarily a football issue, I think it’s a wider societal issue and football is that beacon of hope to try to get the message out. We’ll try, hopefully with the PFA and the league, to see what can be done.’

Meanwhile, Guehi is the latest City player to back the club in their appeal of guilty verdicts for serious breaches of financial rules.

‘The club have been nothing but fantastic with me,’ he said. ‘I just want to be someone that is part of the future success. But you just get the sense from everyone at the football club that it's a family football club.

‘People love one another and just want to do the best for each other. We have a lot of trust and belief in the football club and what's been going on here.’

Marc Guehi fears that taking the knee as an anti-racism gesture may have lost its meaning

Guehi did not perform the gesture while his Man City team-mates took the knee on Saturday

Leeds United manager Daniel Farke has also explained why his striker Calvert-Lewin chose not to take the knee ahead of Sunday's 1-1 draw with Manchester United.

'Dom is more or less like, "No, I don't go down. I show my respect by standing up." Every individual is different and shows respect for this campaign in a different way,' he said, defending the forward after his goal earned Leeds a 1-1 draw at Elland Road.

'It's definitely a good campaign and a good topic that football and sports, in general, support situations like this because a dressing room is a perfect example of how we should all live together.

'Different passports, different languages, different religions, but they are all together as human beings and friends who work together toward one target. This is how the world should be.'

Parts of Elland Road loudly booed as players took the knee ahead of Leeds' clash with Manchester United, amid fears the gesture has lost its impact since its introduction during the Covid-19 pandemic.

It is not the first time players have refused to take the knee. Last year Calvert-Lewin did so in Burnley's fixture with Leeds, while Crysencio Summerville has been known to take the same action.

Former Premier League star Wilfried Zaha previously explained why he objected to the gesture in 2021, saying: 'I feel like taking the knee is degrading, because growing up my parents just let me know that I should be proud to be black no matter what and I feel like we should just stand tall.'

When Marcos Alonso was at Chelsea, he opted to point to the Premier League's 'No Room For Racism' badge on his arm rather than join his fellow players.

Two Bournemouth players stood while their team-mates took the knee against Sunderland

Ahead of the 2021-22 season, Luton indicated that they would not be taking the knee and instead all stood in the centre circle.

At that stage, players were still taking the knee ahead of every game – but that policy was stopped in August 2022. Instead, a decision was made to limit the gesture to match rounds falling during the No Room For Racism initiative.

It was agreed ahead of the season among Premier League captains that they would continue to perform the gesture twice per season.

Players and staff have the opportunity to perform the pre-match gesture for the next two gameweeks in October, with the Premier League reiterating 'the commitment to tackling discrimination and highlight the importance of diversity in football'.

The move started in the UK in 2020 following the death of George Floyd in US police custody, which sparked the Black Lives Matter movement.

#1641. Ravenna confirm date for Ronaldinho’s return to the pitch: ‘It’s real’

📅 26 Oct 2026 | other | Source

Ravenna President Ignazio Cipriani confirms Brazilian legend Ronaldinho will take part in a Serie C match against Forlì on November 15: ‘The return is real.’

Former Milan and Barcelona star Ronaldinho will return to the pitch for a competitive match on November 15, 2026.

The former Brazil international will feature in a derby between Ravenna and Forlì in Italy’s Serie C, the country’s third division.

“The Magic never left; now it’s time to bring him back onto the pitch. The return is real,” Ravenna President Ignazio Cipriani said in a statement via Ansa.

“They told us it was impossible. On November 15, Ronaldinho will take the pitch at Stadio Benelli in our shirt, and in a match as important as the derby against Forlì.

“We believed in it from day one and, thanks to the passion and work of our whole team, we turned an idea that seemed impossible into an occasion the entire world now awaits.”

Ravenna confirmed Ronaldinho’s signing at an event in Miami this past June.

The former Brazilian star hasn’t yet trained with the team, but, as Corriere della Sera reported, he has been watching their games, and his brother and agent, Roberto De Assis, was spotted at Ravenna’s stadium at the end of September.

It’s still unclear how many minutes Ronaldinho will play, but his return to action has now been confirmed.

The 46-year-old last played a competitive match over 10 years ago, with Fluminense in 2015.
