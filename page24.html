#3200. Aaron Rodgers sounds annoyed with Steelers' travel plans for Paris: Why the QB is unhappy

📅 26 Oct 2026 | other | Source

If not listed, please contact your TV provider.

For the first 17 years of his career, Aaron Rodgers didn't play in a single international game, but over the past three seasons, he's become one of the most traveled players in the NFL.

Rodgers and the Steelers are in France to face the Saints in what will be the NFL's first game in Paris. This week's matchup will mark the third straight year that Rodgers has played an international game, and since this one is in Paris, Rodgers will become the first QB in NFL history to play an international game in three straight seasons, with all three games being played in a different country.

In 2024, Rodgers and the Jets traveled to London to face the Vikings, a game they lost 23-17. In 2025, Rodgers and the Steelers traveled to Ireland to play in the NFL's first game in Dublin. In that game, they also faced the Vikings and the Steelers won, 24-21.

Rodgers' three international trips all have had one thing in common and that's the fact that each team decided to travel late in the week. The Steelers left on Thursday night and arrived on Friday in Paris and Rodgers seemed slightly annoyed by that travel plan when he was asked about it this week.

"I've played in three countries now," Rodgers said. "In each of them, we've left on Thursday early evening. It would have been nice one time to come over on a Monday."

The Saints decided to fly out on Monday night and land in Paris on Tuesday, and Rodgers sounds like he would have been on board with that plan.

"I think getting acclimated has its advantages," Rodgers said of adjusting to the six-hour time difference between Pittsburgh and Paris. "Obviously, there's 'science' behind coming in later in the week."

Rodgers used air quotes when he said the word "science," so it's pretty clear that he's not buying the fact that leaving later in the week gives you an advantage. Although the science is debatable, teams leaving later in the week for a game in Europe have had a lot of success this year, going 3-0.

NFL teams that have left on Thursday night and arrived on Friday for a game in Europe this year are 3-0. Texans, Colts and Jaguars (for their first London game) all went this route. #Steelers #Saintshttps://t.co/jtVBSpYjSv

There are riots in Paris right now, but that wasn't taken into consideration when the Steelers made their travel plans. Both teams locked down their Week 7 travel itinerary long before the season started and they stuck with those plans. The Saints could have delayed their arrival due to the civil unrest in Paris, but they decided not to make any changes. They showed up on Tuesday morning and they haven't had any issues.

The Steelers cancelled a fan event out of an abundance of caution this week, but otherwise, it's been business as usual for both teams.

The NFL started playing international games back in 2007, but Rodgers didn't get to play in one until 2022, which was his final season with the Packers. Rodgers played in zero international games from 2007 through 2021, but after this week, he'll have played in four international games over the past five seasons.

Rodgers was asked what he thinks the NFL will do next regarding the international schedule, and he gave an interesting answer.

"I think there could be 16 sites around the world at some point," Rodgers said. "Canada, Mexico, probably another South American country, multiple European countries. There was a game in Australia this year."

Rodgers is right, the NFL will likely play 16 international games at some point. That seems to be the league's endgame, and according to Rodgers, the NFL is only doing it for the money.

"The league has a pretty far reach and there's more coin to be made, they're going to look into it," Rodgers said.

With Rodgers set to retire, he won't have to worry about playing in any more international games, and if he wants to travel after his career is over, he'll be able to make his own plans instead of being stuck doing what his team wants.

No later than Halloween night, baseball will have a new first-time World Series champion. By way of preview, let's go through this year's two World Series participants and not just compare the Brewers and Rays position-by-position, but pick our favorites and build an All-World Series roster. Liam Hicks does not have the Contreras name recognition, but he's a very good player. He's a better defender and he's thoroughly outhit Hicks during the postseason, plus he's the undisputed starter. Hicks will occasionally sit against lefties in favor of Nick Fortes. When in doubt, pick the guy who doesn't get platooned. Contreras is my All-World Series team catcher.

He had a 12-pitch walk against Yoshinobu Yamamoto in Game 2 of the NLCS, and an 11-pitch go-ahead homer against Blake Snell in Game 3. Jonathan Aranda is a very good player himself, though he's mostly been a singles guy in the postseason. Bauers is simply the better player at this point in time.

The numbers say Brewers rookie Cooper Pratt is the pick at shortstop. And heck, Pratt even went 5 for 14 during the NLCS. He's had some jittery moments in the field though, with three errors in eight postseason games and a few other misplays that weren't scored errors. Walls is a terrific defender who can make spectacular plays and also make the routine ones routine, the latter of which is an underappreciated defensive skill. We're picking between glove-first shortstops, so give me the more reliable Walls.

Second base was an easy call for Turang, and third base is an easy call for Caminero, who this year joined Hall of Famer Eddie Mathews as the only players in history with two 40-homer seasons through their age-22 seasons. The Brewers have leaned on gloveman Joey Ortiz and speedster David Hamilton at the hot corner and hey, those two went a combined 5 for 14 in the NLCS. They're not Caminero, though. Caminero is the pick here and he'll anchor our All-World Series Team lineup.

He's the master of the "put the ball in play and run like crazy" approach. Chourio can do that too, though, plus he has power and is better defensively.

It was one of those unexpected things that teams need to win the World Series. The Rays have leaned on Jonny DeLuca, who is more of a platoon corner outfielder than an everyday center fielder, in center because Cedric Mullins had a poor season. Jax can throw an inning or two out of the bullpen in Game 1 and still be available to start Game 4.

Bottom line, Brewers manager Pat Murphy has almost no bad options available to him in the bullpen. That doesn't guarantee the players will perform, but Milwaukee has a very deep and very good bullpen. Cash does not have that luxury. He's had to white-knuckle his way through the late innings to get the ball from his starter to Baker this postseason.
