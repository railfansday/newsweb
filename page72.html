'The status doesn't give players preferential treatment. The status is about respect for their careers. However, a lot has changed in a few months. Amorim is unbeaten in seven league games so far with Milan, while in the Europa League his side bounced back from a 2-0 defeat to Benfica by routing RB Salzburg 4-0 last week.

The 3-2 win over Atalanta at San Siro certainly helped the momentum, though the way the Rossoneri nearly threw away a three-goal lead caused some concerns too. A further blow comes in the form of injuries to Samuel Chukwueze and Goncalo Ramos on the eve of the trip.

Amorim spoke to the reporters who gathered at the Vitality Stadium for his press conference the day before the game on the south coast. MilanNews relayed the questions and the responses from the coach, who sat alongside Ruben Loftus-Cheek.

Can you give us a report on the injuries? Will Cisse be replacing Ramos?

“We’ll see the starting eleven tomorrow. Gonçalo isn’t terribly injured, but Chukwueze’s situation is different, and we’ll evaluate it. Generally, all European teams have injury problems, which is why we rotate. Cisse will play, but you’ll find out his position tomorrow.”

You face a key opponent tomorrow. Will it be a decisive match?

“I don’t know. Every game will be decisive. We’re in the third round and there’s still a lot to play for. We’ll be facing a team with a different quality. They have a certain pace, they’re known for their speed and quality in transitions.

“It will be a different game; we’ll have to be ready to follow their rhythm. It won’t be decisive, but it will be important to understand the level and moment of our team.”

What potential does Cisse have? How far can he go?

“The first thing is not to exaggerate the situation and put pressure on the younger players. I’m talking about us as a club: we need to help the younger players understand that football is a rollercoaster. He’s at the beginning of something, we’re at 50% of his potential.

“There are things he’s not doing at a high level yet, but let’s give him time: we need to understand that football changes rapidly. We need to work hard during the week and then there will be more and more space for him in the team. But I repeat, he’s only at 50% of his potential.”

“Overall, I think the important thing is that I’m here as AC Milan’s manager. I have nothing to say about United. I can say that I loved the club and the atmosphere: what happened doesn’t change that.

“Then I moved on, now I’m at Milan and I’m focused on this: at every big club, problems are always around the corner. I focus on ourselves and I don’t think about the teams. My past at United doesn’t matter. What I said when I signed there remains the same.”

On Bournemouth: how have they changed since you were in England?

“There are some new details with Rose. But I see the same pace, the same pressing: it’s Rose’s identity. They have quality in one-on-one situations and have signed many strong new players on the market.

“There are obviously different things, but their identity lies in quality, pace, and speed. It’s a test for us, I’m happy to see the players on the pitch, even if they rotate.”

Now that you’re back in England, do you feel different?

“First of all, I want to say that Loftus is a talented guy; the problem is in his mind. He needs to make this step forward, and he’s still young; he’s got a very long career ahead of him. He can play for the national team.

“About me: I have more experience as a coach, but I’m not a different person. I understand football better, and that will help me deal with problems in the future. I think I’m the same person, just a little more experienced in some things.”

What makes you choose Estupinan over Bartesaghi?

“These are technical things, which I see in training. But not how they train. Bartesaghi is a runner, Estupinan is a player who combines and creates balance.

“They are alternating. Tomorrow you will see one and in Udine the other. It depends on the characteristics we want on the pitch, in any case both must be ready to play.”

Did you take Rabiot’s words as a starting point to speak to the team these days?

“Every match is different. We can’t say we’ll improve on anything from the previous match in the next game. After the 3-1, we had possession: we need to improve some technical and tactical aspects. It will help us control the game.

“Tomorrow we have nothing to prove compared to the previous match: we have to try to win in this important competition for us. And if we don’t have any more injuries, it will be even better.”

#2228. Frustrated Hearts skipper Stephen Kingsley admits Parkhead horror show was a 'calamity of errors'

📅 26 Oct 2026 | other | Source

By CALUM CROWE, SCOTTISH DEPUTY CHIEF SPORTS WRITER

Published: 19:02 BST, 19 October 2026 | Updated: 19:59 BST, 19 October 2026

At the end of what proved to be a humiliating afternoon for Hearts, there was no hiding place for Wouter Vrancken and his players.

A complete and utter abomination of a performance from start to finish, there were no positives to be taken from being thrashed 7-1 against Celtic. Plainly, it was seven going on nine or ten.

Nor was there any real mitigation that could be offered in the wake of the club’s heaviest ever defeat to Celtic in a competitive fixture.

It was a sobering experience which surely dynamited any notion of Vrancken’s side challenging for the league title once again this season.

Teams of genuine substance do not capitulate in such fashion. Having won a lot of plaudits for their attacking style of play in recent weeks, it was a huge reality check.

In the aftermath, Vrancken spoke of his players making too many individual errors. That was undeniably true.

Hearts defender Jamie McCart puts through his own net in the 7-1 defeat to Celtic

Celtic's Camilo Duran gets in on the act just after the interval in the thumping of Hearts

Hearts conceded twice from set-plays inside the opening ten minutes and were guilty of some brainless football at times in terms of how cheaply they lost possession.

But the manager should also come under scrutiny for showing such tactical naivety, a refusal to adapt and an inability to stop the bleeding.

There may be some who view it as premature to rule Hearts out of the title race at this relatively early juncture of the campaign. They are only eight points behind Celtic with still almost 30 games to play. Some might view it as harsh to be so critical on Vrancken so soon into his tenure.

But if Hearts are to be taken seriously as title contenders, they will be judged to a higher standard than before. That’s just how it works.

For Stephen Kingsley, who captained the side against Celtic, he certainly didn’t attempt to dress it up afterwards.

He admitted the performance and result was an embarrassment, and that Hearts were simply too naïve in how they went about things.

But with a UEFA Conference League clash to come against Mo Salah’s Trabzonspor in Turkey on Thursday night, Kingsley insists there is simply no time to dwell on it or feel sorry for themselves.

‘It was very raw after the Celtic game on Sunday,’ said Kingsley. ‘I think it was just embarrassment with how we lost the goals in the first half.

‘We shot ourselves in the foot in terms of how we defended. Basically every single goal Celtic scored came from a mistake from us. It was a calamity of errors.

‘It’s hard to sum up the emotions after a game like that. I think it was naive, eh? I think after the second one goes in, there’s a point where you need to build yourself back into the game. But I just don’t think we did that. I don’t think we gave ourselves a chance to because of the mistakes that we made that were punished.

‘Fair play to Celtic, they were clinical. But very, very frustrating from our point of view. You can’t defend like that and expect to get a result.

Hearts' Stephen Kingsley (left) and full-time with Alex Oxlade-Chamberlain and Mika Baur

‘We’ve got major plaudits from a lot of people for how we’ve played recently. But when you take those plaudits, you need to take the criticism when it comes as well.

‘It’s one of those results that, if you linger on it, then you can let it fester and seep into future performances. But we can’t have that. It’s up to myself as one of the more experienced players, and the other boys in the changing room that are a bit older.

‘There’s no one going to be pointing any fingers at one specific person or one specific mistake. I think we all need to take accountability for our own performances, our own mistakes. Have a look at ourselves and what we can do better and learn from it.

‘We’ve got another really difficult game in Europe this week. We need to bounce back quickly and give the fans a performance to be proud of, because Sunday was far from that.’

Kingsley was in the Hearts team that lost the league title at Celtic Park back on the final day of last season. Whilst conceding that Sunday’s performance and result was nowhere near good enough, he insisted that nothing will ever top the pain of what happened last season.

‘No, nothing will ever be worse than that,’ he added. ‘I just feel that it’s very difficult to analyse in terms of what happened on Sunday.

‘I think the number of mistakes that we made leading to goals is the biggest frustration now. But, yeah, as I’ve said, we’ve got another massive game on Thursday. We will dust ourselves down and hopefully this is just a blip in an otherwise successful season.’

Milan have won five out of nine games this season so far and two of three since the break. The 4-0 rout of RB Salzburg last week got the European campaign going after the round one loss to Benfica, while beating Atalanta 3-2 at San Siro on Sunday saw the Rossoneri climb into the top four.

The Diavolo face a Bournemouth side who underwent big changes in the summer and recorded their first league win at the weekend two, but they have taken six points from six (vs. Real Sociedad and Sturm Graz) in their maiden European campaign.

Loftus-Cheek spoke to the reporters who gathered at the Vitality Stadium for his press conference. MilanNews relayed the questions and the responses from the Englishman, who sat alongside head coach Ruben Amorim.

Are you a dressing room leader given your experience?

“I have a lot of experience now, thanks for mentioning my age (laughs). I feel a responsibility on the pitch, to bring my experience. These are the games when you have to bring it onto the pitch, especially because it will be a big match, with a very passionate stadium. I want to help the team as much as possible.”

You left Chelsea three years ago: how much have you changed on and off the pitch?

“I think I’ve improved as a person and as a player. The league is different. I’m enjoying my experience in Italy, learning the language, the different culture. So I’ve grown a lot as a person.”

Do you have ambitions of returning to the national team?

“It’s not the first thought on my mind. Milan deserves my full focus. We have a lot of matches in this period, we’re a great team, we have the pressure to win games. If I play well, the opportunity to return to the national team will come.”

“Throughout my career, I’ve had problems with injuries. It’s been my big challenge to feel good season after season. I think I have to accept the way my body is, but I have to feel better mentally.

“I’m playing with confidence in my abilities. I feel good today, I feel good when I’m on the pitch. I give 100% for the team, for my team.”

“I think we have a lot of confidence this year – in the coach, in ourselves, and in achieving something. The whole team is united, and I feel it in the dressing room at Milanello. I feel like we all want to achieve something and improve. There are obviously things we need to improve on.”
