While the hosts are struggling in Serie A and have just lost in Europe, their opponents have surpassed all expectations so far.

After a mixed start to Maurizio Sarri's reign, Atalanta beat Venezia earlier this month, then kicked off their Conference League campaign with a comfortable 3-1 victory over Pafos.

However, the Bergamaschi have since suffered back-to-back defeats, leaving Sarri under fire and hastily searching for solutions.

First, his side fell three goals behind against local rivals AC Milan before mounting a late fightback and almost snatching a point, then they were beaten again four days later.

La Dea wasted several chances and failed to register a single shot on target, suffering a 2-0 Conference League loss to Ajax in Amsterdam.

Allied to three straight defeats before the last international break, that makes five from seven across all competitions - a dire run by Atalanta's high standards.

The only real positive so far has been their home form, with four wins from six in Bergamo, where they have also won all three previous Serie A games against Frosinone.

The Canarini's overall record against Atalanta is truly terrible, having failed to score in seven of eight meetings across Italy's top two tiers.

In Serie A contests, they are trailing La Dea by an aggregate scoreline of 17-2, so history is certainly not on their side.

Yet, recent results indicate that Massimiliano Alvini's men could defy such grim precedent this weekend.

Having stunned Como before the international break, Frosinone were well beaten by Napoli on their return, but they bounced back with a 4-2 victory over Sassuolo last time out.

After a chaotic five-goal first half, red-hot striker Antonio Raimondo then completed his hat-trick, taking him onto seven goals from as many league games.

Following promotion from Serie B, the Canarini have made an unexpectedly strong start to their comeback campaign, and they can look down on Atalanta with a tally of 13 points so far.

After Thursday's reverse against Ajax, Sarri complained that Atalanta were missing four central defenders, and that could be the case again this weekend.

Sead Kolasinac recently joined Giorgio Scalvini, Isak Hien and Odilon Kossounou on the sidelines, adding to La Dea's long absence list.

Kamaldeen Sulemana and Giacomo Raspadori are also unavailable, while key midfielder Franck Kessie is struggling to recover from a calf strain and remains a major doubt.

After Gianluca Scamacca failed to fire in Amsterdam, Nikola Krstovic could return to the hosts' starting XI, as Sarri continues to rotate his two strikers.

Without doubt, Raimondo will lead Frosinone's front line, as he currently tops the Capocannoniere chart: the Bologna loanee claimed Serie A's 'Rising Star' award for September, and he is surely in contention for the main prize this month.

Though Florian Grillitsch has yet to return following hernia surgery, Alvini should have everyone else available, so he may even name an unchanged XI.

With Atalanta lacking rhythm, hit by injuries and fatigued by their Conference League duties, the Canarini can pick up another point this weekend.

#2345. Ex-Everton scout reveals they're only 'one' way Moyes' team can beat Arsenal

📅 26 Oct 2026 | other | Source

Everton will need to keep a clean sheet at Arsenal if they are to emerge with anything to show from the game on Saturday.

That is according to former senior Everton scout, Bryan King, speaking exclusively to Football Insider.

The Toffees will visit North London off the back of their first blip of the season, having drawn with Hull City and lost to Chelsea since returning from the international break.

Arsenal travelled to Bayern Munich in midweek and were held to an end-to-end 2-2 draw, which Everton will hope has taken plenty out of the team.

The champions have struggled against deep defences this season, securing their 1-0 win over Nottingham Forest last week with their only shot on target in the game.

Yet they remain a formidable force at the Emirates Stadium, winning all four of their fixtures hosted there this season so far.

The Emirates hasn't been a happy place for the Toffees in recent years. 🔵Our last five visits have seen 4 defeats, 1 draw and a -11 goal difference.Can Everton change that trend this weekend? 👀#Everton #EFC #Arsenal pic.twitter.com/1mH2o7V7RB

Everton came close to earning something at the Emirates Stadium when they went there in March.

The Toffees had great chances to take the lead and were solid in defence until the final minutes, when Viktor Gyokeres and Max Dowman finally unlocked the pick and secured a crucial three points in the title race.

Saturday's clash will be less pressurised, and Everton will likely arrive with a similar plan of action.

AC Milan have announced the official club stance regarding away ticket prices, showing solidarity with the Curva Sud.

Milanisti travel not just around Italy but around Europe to follow their team, showing lifelong devotion. Despite this, the fan groups are sometimes forced to take a principled stance on certain matters, for the greater good and the health of football.

In a statement on their social media page, the Curva Sud confirmed that they will not travel to the match against Udinese on Sunday. The reason was due to the ticking price being over €60, something they feel is disproportionate. It is not the first time the group have taken such action.

Now, an official statement on the club’s website has made their thoughts on the matter crystal clear, as they have chosen to back the stance of those who are boycotting the game in Udine at the weekend.

“AC Milan stands firmly behind its supporters in their fight against excessive away ticket prices. Our fans have announced they will boycott the upcoming match against Udinese after being charged higher prices for away tickets than supporters of other clubs. We understand their frustration and fully support their position.

“Supporting a team away from home already comes at a significant financial cost. Beyond match tickets, fans must pay for fuel, motorway tolls, transport and, in many cases, accommodation. These commitments make it all the more important that supporters are treated fairly regardless of which club they follow.

“The global appeal of Italian football depends heavily on the loyalty and passion of supporters of clubs like AC Milan. Charging those same fans excessive prices for away games is not only unfair but undermines the unity of the league at a time when Italian football needs to come together to compete against the Premier League and other European leagues.

“Practices like these only deepen divisions between clubs and creates an unhealthy antagonism between big and small teams in Serie A.”

The statement from the club continues, addressing how to possibly fix things: “Under the leadership of Gerry Cardinale, AC Milan wants to help resolve this issue for the benefit of all fans across Serie A.

“We will push for a league-wide cap on excessive away ticket prices. We are also prepared to introduce a policy of reciprocity: clubs charging our supporters above average ticket pricing should expect their own fans to face higher prices when visiting San Siro, subject to applicable regulations.

“Any additional proceeds generated would be donated to Fondazione Milan to support charitable initiatives benefiting local communities.

“It is time for Italian football to come together as a league and as a collective fan base, especially at a time when this important cultural institution has never been more challenged.

“Italian football cannot grow stronger by treating supporters differently based on the size of their club. We are all in this together, and we call on every Serie A club to embrace a fairer, more sustainable future for our game.”

Sky Sports reporter Vinny O’Connor has provided Liverpool fans with a wide-ranging series of injury updates on Thursday.

Andoni Iraola is currently without several first-team regulars due to various fitness issues, with a mixture of recent body blows and longer-term casualties who’ve already been sidelined for numerous months.

Bradley Barcola was a notable absentee from team training today, although the aforementioned journalist indicated that the French winger was undergoing an individual recovery session after a heavy workload in recent days and weeks.

After a hectic run of four matches in 10 days, there was always a strong chance that a few Liverpool players would pick up injuries, and unfortunately that has been the case in this past fortnight.

At least the Reds have until Sunday to get ready for a tough home fixture against Brighton, which commences another intense period of five games in 15 days, including the visit of Arsenal to Anfield.

Of the half-dozen names that O’Connor mentioned, Munoz is the only one who might potentially be available for the weekend, but hopefully Szoboszlai will recover in time to face the Gunners on Sunday week.

While Iraola will ideally have a couple of players back in the next fortnight, the hope is that others don’t join the injury list, which is already much longer than we’d like it to be and won’t be cleared for at least another couple of months while Ekitike, Leoni and Bradley continue to recover from long-term issues.

Fingers crossed that Liverpool can through the next run of games to the November international break without any further casualties decimating squad depth.
