Newcastle United head coach Matthias Jaissle has provided a fresh update on the fitness of his squad ahead of Sunday’s Premier League clash with Crystal Palace at Selhurst Park.

The Magpies enter this contest seeking to avoid a third successive defeat after following up a heavy 4-1 humbling at Coventry with a narrow 1-0 defeat at home to Aston Villa last weekend.

Newcastle have struggled to pose the same attacking threat since Anthony Elanga picked up a hamstring injury, forcing him to miss the last four league games.

The Swedish winger made a positive start to the new season, scoring two goals in his opening two top-flight appearances for the Magpies against both Liverpool and Tottenham Hotspur.

Meanwhile, midfielder Jacob Ramsey has also been sidelined for the last four matches since sustaining a thigh problem in the pre-match warm-up before a 4-1 defeat at Leeds United on September 14.

Speaking at a press conference on Friday, Jaissle confirmed that both players have rejoined training this week and could be involved on Sunday, but the duo will continue to be monitored closely.

"Anthony [Elanga] has trained this week and worked really hard,” Jaissle told reporters “He won’t be able to play 90 minutes but he is hopefully ready, at least, to be able to join the squad.

'I am happy Jacob Ramsey is back on the pitch. We need to take it step-by-step with him, manage his load management, we don't want to take too many risks.

"I am pleased that slowly we are getting the players back and the next question is the physical level.”

Elsewhere, summer signing Matias Fernandez-Pardo is a doubt for the trip to Palace as his week of training has been affected by illness.

“Matias has still some issues health-wise, so he can only train partially and hopefully he can make it at the weekend,” said Jaissle.

Defender Tino Livramento and goalkeeper Ewen Jaouen are two Newcastle players who remained sidelined, though, as they continue to recover from respective knee and thigh injuries.

Jaissle was also questioned about Sean Steur’s first-team status; the 18-year-old summer arrival has been an unused substitute for the last two matches after featuring in the previous five league matches (three starts, two substitute outings).

"We are all sure, the whole club, the coaching staff and the players what great potential Sean has,” the Magpies boss insisted. “He has shown that already and he showed, like many other lads, there are ups and downs and this is normal.

"Now it is time he is getting that confidence, that maturity throughout training sessions, training weeks and we see it as a long-term project.

"Nico [Gonzalez] is doing good in that position but that is the competition. We are happy with his progress."

On facing Palace, Jaissle continued: "It was a week we were fully committed to what we want to develop. We found things we can do better clearly in the post-match analysis and we have been working on themes.

"At Crystal Palace, we want to show another phase to get three points… There is always times where you would and wouldn’t like to play against an opposition. They have had a heavy defeat and then drew [2-2 with Besiktas in the Europa League on Thursday]. They will want a reaction.

"We can only focus on ourselves, what we can control. We are fully focused on Crystal Palace, to deliver the match plan."

Newcastle, who have failed to win their last five visits to Selhurst Park (D3 L2), currently sit 11th in the Premier League table, two points behind the top six and three points above the relegation zone.

#1977. Why defiant Man City fans are going bananas: former forward Imre Varadi on his trademarked giant inflatable fruit based on his famous terrace nickname... with club set to order another 30,000!

📅 26 Oct 2026 | other | Source

Published: 15:56 BST, 20 October 2026 | Updated: 23:40 BST, 20 October 2026

Bananas are back in fashion, and Imre Varadi is a man in demand. 'The phone is ringing off the hook,' he explains and right on cue his device pings and lights up with another message.

This time it is a request for him to appear on a radio show coming his way via former team-mate Lee Chapman.

They, like the rest of us, want to know how at the age of 67, and 40 years after he first played up front for Manchester City, his cult status has been revitalised by giant inflatable bananas.

It is a twist of fate not only good for Varadi’s ego but good for his business as two years ago he acted upon the random fact that City supporters came to affectionately refer to him as Imre Banana back in the day by trademarking the name.

Call it a striker’s instinct. Or business acumen after 25 years working as an agent. He saw the opening and made his move.

Now he produces branded inflatable bananas, T-shirts and pin badges.

He sells them around the world and has a partnership with Manchester City, who are understood to be planning to order another 30,000 bananas.

City fans are embracing bananas all over again as a symbol of solidarity in the wake of criticism after the club was found guilty of breaking the Premier League’s financial rules.

Imre Varadi produces inflatable bananas with his nickname as well as T-shirts and pin badges

The bananas have become a symbol of City fans' defiance after the guilty verdict handed down by the independent commission

'I think it’s their way of saying that whatever happens we’ll be behind the team and the club, and we’ll still be having fun,' says Varadi. 'We’ve had bad times. We’ve been in the second and third divisions and never forget the history, but we love the club and we’ll always stick with them and support them.

'It is great fun and it has helped to galvanise everybody. The sea of bananas in the away end at Anfield was great to see and to be at the Etihad on Saturday [against Ipswich] and see all the Mums and Dads and kids carrying them to the game was incredible.

'I’m so honoured and proud to be such a part of it. I don’t think many of them know I was ever a footballer.'

Varadi was born in London with an Italian mother and a Hungarian father and came through the youth ranks at Luton Town.

'They released me and Kerry Dixon, and we both cried our eyes out,' he says. 'But you dust yourself down and get on with it. Kerry ended up playing at Dunstable and I went to Letchworth.

'After a year, Harry Haslam rang me and told me to come to Sheffield United, so I went up there played in a reserve game and scored a hat trick, played in another reserve game and scored again.

'He gave me a two-and-a-half-year professional contract and I never looked back. Kerry as we all know went to Reading, Chelsea and played for England and had a fantastic career.'

Imre Varadi scores City's second in a 3-1 win over Aston Villa in 1986

Varadi in action during a 1-1 draw against Manchester United at Maine Road in 1986

From left to right; Martin Hodge, Varadi, Mick Lyons, Gary Megson and Pat Heard at Sheffield Wednesday in 1984

Varadi did not stay anywhere for too long. He left Bramall Lane for Everton and scoring the winner in an FA Cup tie against Liverpool at Goodison Park remains one of his fondest memories.

'Everton hadn’t beaten Liverpool in years, and I lost it when I scored, running round the back of the goal to celebrate with the supporters. Within three or four seconds I realised these were the wrong supporters.

'It was the Liverpool supporters, and someone threw a meat and potato pie and hit me right in the middle of my face. I can still taste it today. I’ll never forget it.'

At Newcastle he wore the famous No9 shirt, scored goals at a rate close to one-in-two and spent a season playing alongside Kevin Keegan.

In the first of his two spells at Sheffield Wednesday, he won promotion to the top-flight before a season at West Bromwich Albion and the move to City in 1986.

He finished the season as City’s top scorer but could not do enough to stop them sliding back into the second tier. As the next season got under way at home against Plymouth Argyle, there was a supporter called Frank Newton who took an inflatable banana with him to Maine Road, basically for a laugh.

'I remember jogging out on the game and seeing a banana,' says Varadi. 'That was strange enough and then one banana became two or three and then eight or nine and then it went whoosh.' Fans started singing Imre Banana instead of Imre Varadi and the craze spread across the country.

Varadi with Kevin Keegan during his time at Newcastle between 1981 and 1983

'West Ham had inflatable hammers, Norwich had canaries, Grimsby had the fish, but we started it,' says Varadi. 'People have called me Imre Banana from there.'

He scored 31 in 81 games for City before returning to Wednesday in September 1988. There were then three years at Leeds, again with Chapman, but mainly as back up. He did not make enough appearances for a title winners’ medal in 1992.

There was a brief return to Luton, six games on loan, and two successful years at Rotherham among other ports of call before ending his career in non-league, coaching at Matlock Town and Stalybridge Celtic before taking his qualifications to become an agent.

'I just sort of fell into it,' he says. 'I always thought I’d go into coaching but because I had a lot of clubs, I had a lot of contacts and knew a hell of a lot of people. The opportunity came to go into an agency, so I took my exam and I’ve been a FIFA licensed agent ever since.'

For 16 years he worked at the Stellar Group agency, then struck out alone with a small group of clients. 'I’ll always do that,' he says. 'I love working, I work every day. I love interacting with players, speaking to clubs and watching games.'

Varadi was at home on his sofa recovering from a toe operation when he finally did something about an idea about the inflatable bananas.

'I’d thought about doing it years ago but never got round to it,' he says. 'And there I was, watching City winning the fourth title in a row and the bananas were coming out again to celebrate.

'I decided I’d take a closer look into the business and found inflatables were a multimillion-pound industry. I was sure there was a gap I could slide into. Some people think I’m jumping on the bandwagon but I’m the original story. I’m the original Imre Banana. That’s me.'

By Oliver Thomas | 23 Oct 2026 18:04

Crystal Palace will be looking to end a 54-year run when they welcome Newcastle United to Selhurst Park for a Premier League clash on Sunday.

The Eagles enter this contest having won just one of their last five matches across all competitions, most recently drawing 2-2 with 10-man Besiktas in the Europa League on Thursday.

Pressure is beginning to mount on manager Pierre Sage, who saw his Palace side suffer a heavy 5-0 away defeat to M23 rivals Brighton & Hove Albion in the Premier League last weekend – their fourth loss in seven league games this term.

Palace have now conceded a division-high 17 goals and four defeats from seven top-flight matches leaves them down in 14th place in the table, just two points above the relegation zone.

The South Londoners will welcome this weekend’s home encounter with Newcastle, though, as they boast a five-game unbeaten run against the Magpies at Selhurst Park (W2 D3).

Two late goals from Eagles striker Jean-Philippe Mateta secured a 2-1 comeback victory at home to Newcastle in April of last season.

Should Palace beat Newcastle again on Sunday, they will claim consecutive league wins over the Magpies for the first time since a run of three between August 1971 and September 1972.

Palace are also looking to win back-to-back Premier League home wins for the first time since April 2025 (three straight) after beating Oliver Glasner’s Nottingham Forest 3-1 a fortnight ago.

In need of a positive result to ease the pressure on his shoulders, Sage was reassured by Palace’s performance against Besiktas after a disappointing showing at Brighton.

"I am satisfied with the performance," Sage told TNT Sports. "A little bit frustrated for the lads, because they gave a lot this evening and I think they deserve better.

"We lacked concentration in the final minute of the first-half, at the end. It was the same thing when we were 2-1 up. In this kind of situation, you pay quickly if you make a little mistake.

"But we have to remind ourselves the situation was worse before the game. It's a good step to go forward and now we need to win against Newcastle."

Sage was also pleased to see Mateta mark his return to the Eagles side with a stunning strike to put Palace in front in Turkey.

Mateta, who was sidelined for over a month at the beginning of this season, heads into Sunday’s contest having scored four goals in his last five appearances against Newcastle.

"I'm really happy for [Mateta]," said Sage. "He was in a good area, it was a good moment for him just before coming off, because he was not really ready to play [a full 90 minutes].

"You see him tired, but when you have this mindset, when you want to play every ball and you give your life on the pitch, sometimes it's good for you.

"We have a lot of satisfactions with the individual performances. But I have to underline that, first of all, it's about the collective performance.

"They're willing to play, they're willing to go forward, they're willing to press. And I think at the end, they made a good game, without the deserved result."

Victory for Crystal Palace could see them climb into the top half of the Premier League table if other results go their way, before they travel to Fulham in the EFL Cup fourth round next Wednesday.

#1961. Who cares?

📅 26 Oct 2026 | other | Source

Morning all, welcome to a brand new week.

So Man City remain top of the Premier League after a 3-0 win over Liverpool at Anfield yesterday. The home side were on the end of a really terrible decision which saw them go down to 10 men, after which City scored three goals. And I could not have cared less. It felt utterly pointless.

Liverpool fans sang songs about the opposition being ‘cheating bastards’, which they are, while the visiting fans whooped it up in their costumes, backing their owners, being described in some quarters as ‘defiant’. We’re all tribal, I get it, but this was grim stuff all round. The tiptoeing around the verdict on commentary only added to the futility of the experience.

Men employed by a company who pay billions for the rights to show the Premier League, and who are intelligent enough to know the damage City have perpetrated over the years, also have to kind of protect the product. But here is the real danger for Sky and the Premier League. It’s not about the microphones picking up some salty language about Gary Neville being a wanker and then being quickly muted. It’s not about how Man City are working hard to dismiss all this as ‘noise’ and present themselves as strong characters because they won a game of football with all this going on.

It’s that their continued participation in a competition they have won and then dominated through fraud is going to make your average football fan question why they are even bothering to watch. What’s the point? We keep hearing about a process, and while I understand an appeal is underway, they have already been found guilty. Not just a little bit guilty either. 114 of 115 charges. Read the findings. The evidence is laid out in black and white. There is too much pussyfooting around that cast-iron fact.

So, like many of you I sat down to watch this game yesterday, because of everything that had gone on since the guilty verdict came down. The added spice of it being at Anfield because of the rivalry of these two clubs over the decade in particular felt like it could spark something hugely entertaining. But then you realise that even that is false. It only happened because Man City cheated, and there’s a hollowness to it. It’s fake. A facade of sporting rivalry that Liverpool were ultimately victims of because when you win 90+ points in a season (twice?) and don’t win the league you’ve been swindled.

Maybe I’d feel different this morning if Liverpool had won, but I don’t really think so. By full time I was almost chiding myself for committing time to something so trivial and inconsequential. They won, they scored some nice goals, but if the sanctions are what they ought to be, they don’t matter a single bit, except to City fans, and I really couldn’t care less about them.

They’ll beat Ipswich in their next Premier League and I won’t care. They’ll go to Aston Villa after that and probably win there too and I won’t care. It’s all a charade at this point, and it’s why we need a resolution to this as quickly as possible. Otherwise apathy takes hold. People understand they’re being asked to pay for something which is fundamentally bogus. In the lexicon of the Premier League itself, a sham. And why should people commit time and money to something like that?

There have been decent voices speaking up on this over the weekend.
