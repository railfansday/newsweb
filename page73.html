After Harry Mills’ 25th-minute penalty put Lewes ahead at The Dripping Pan, a scrappy 37th-minute equaliser from Charlee Hughes ultimately gave the Jammers their first point since mid-September. The Rooks are still third in the league despite the dropped points.

Nelson said: “Not good enough, in my opinion, because I feel we had enough chances to put it to bed.

“It's unfortunate in terms of the way we conceded, but for me, [we were] too selective in our tempo and intensity to implement ourselves into the game.

“It feels like a loss because I think it was a good opportunity to not just stay in the hunt, but to continue our winning streak and that momentum.

“It's another game we felt like we could win, and looking at the balance of it, I feel we should have got three points. But we don't deserve it if we don't put the ball in the opposition's net.”

While the Rooks struggled to kick on after Mills’ goal, with Maldon enjoying strong spells either side of half-time, the game’s decisive phase came in the final 30 minutes.

Lewes launched countless waves of attack and, rejuvenated by several substitutions, threw bodies forward to find a winner.

However, everything denied them, from Maldon’s solid defending and debutant Bradley Robinson’s goalkeeping to the post and crossbar.

“For me, the energy came a little bit too late, where it becomes a part of the game where you're like, ‘We have to score now’, and in those moments, sometimes you don't keep cool heads to fashion out chances,” said Nelson.

“It becomes a little bit erratic. I felt we did well to sustain attacks, but we need to try to put the icing on the cake to get over the line.”

On a positive note, Lewes’ week off will help their injury situation as captain Andrew Briggs and experienced defender Tom Bender recover from lay-offs.

Nelson admitted after the Maldon game that both players were set to miss another four to six weeks, potentially placing their returns in mid-December.

Ten games await the Rooks between 31st October and 12th December, with new signing Kurtis Cumberbatch set to slot into midfield for that run in Briggs’ absence.

Lewes return to action next Saturday (Oct 31) at home to Wingate & Finchley, currently ninth, kicking off at 3pm.

#2218. Liverpool transfer news: Reds 'inform' La Liga club over £25m release clause plan to sign attacker

📅 26 Oct 2026 | other | Source

By Saikat Mandal | 23 Oct 2026 13:38

Liverpool have reportedly informed Valencia of their desire to sign David Otorbi, but prising the youngster away from Mestalla is unlikely to be straightforward.

The Reds signed Victor Munoz from Osasuna during the summer, and with Andoni Iraola at the helm, they are likely to be linked with several of Spain's most promising talents.

Having progressed through the youth ranks at Los Che, Otorbi made his first-team debut in 2024 and has since made 13 appearances for the club.

Despite signing Bradley Barcola from Paris Saint-Germain, Liverpool remain on the lookout for further attacking reinforcements and have reportedly registered a strong interest in Otorbi.

Otorbi's agent, Nacho Gallart, confirmed earlier this month that his client was attracting considerable interest from Premier League clubs, with Newcastle United among the sides linked with the youngster.

Liverpool scouts were recently in attendance at Mestalla to watch Harvey Elliott, but they also took the opportunity to assess Otorbi.

According to Tribuna Deportiva, Liverpool officials met Valencia manager Javier Aguirre and sporting director Braulio Vazquez to discuss a potential move for the 19-year-old attacker.

The Spaniard is under contract with Valencia until 2028, with the club holding an option to extend his deal by a further three years.

The report claims that Liverpool have informed Valencia and the player's agent of their desire to activate his £25m (€30m) release clause.

The La Liga club wasted little time in activating the youngster's three-year extension, doing so before he had reached the number of appearances required to trigger an automatic extension.

As a result, Otorbi is now set to have a new release clause worth around £50m (€60m), alongside an increase in his salary.

Paying an inflated fee for a relatively unproven player would make little sense for Liverpool, who should consider alternative targets if Valencia refuse to negotiate a more reasonable deal for their prized asset.

#2216. Celtic winger Jota determined to be better than ever after making long-awaited comeback

📅 26 Oct 2026 | other | Source

By CALUM CROWE, SCOTTISH DEPUTY CHIEF SPORTS WRITER

Published: 20:01 BST, 19 October 2026 | Updated: 20:01 BST, 19 October 2026

Celtic winger Jota is confident he can recapture his best form after finally making his long-awaited comeback from a serious knee injury.

The Portuguese star had been out of action for 18 months after suffering an anterior cruciate ligament injury in a match against Dundee United at Tannadice in April 2025.

After missing all of last season, Jota has returned to action over the past week in the victories over Motherwell and Hearts.

Coming off the bench in the 7-1 thrashing of Hearts yesterday, he entertained the fans with a few moments of sublime skill in the second half as Celtic ran riot.

Opening up on his injury hell for the first time, the 27-year-old admitted he had to overcome personal ‘demons’ during such a long lay-off.

But Jota feels the best may yet be to come in a Celtic jersey – and is confident he will eventually be better and stronger than ever before once he regains full fitness.

Jota was thrilled to be playing at Celtic Park for the first time since his injury 18 months ago

The winger entertained fans with some sublime moments of skill in the 7-1 thrashing of Hearts

Jota has had demons to overcome since suffering an ACL injury at Tannadice in April 2025

Asked about getting back to his top level, he said: ‘Yeah, definitely. Otherwise, if I didn’t have that aim, I wouldn’t be helpful for Celtic if I was happy with the things I did before. I want to improve and be better.

‘I’m very positive that good things will come. I’m accepting the present and how things are going and I’m trusting the process.

‘There’s no lost time because, in the end, I grew a lot. I didn’t lose. I actually gained because, inside, I’m way stronger now. So it will help me definitely.

‘The happiest thing in life for me is to play football. I couldn’t do that in the last year. So right now, every time I step on the pitch, it means so much.

‘Just the passion for football, I think that’s my compass. I could clearly see that during the months I was away. My love for football is immense.

‘I could prove that because maybe someone else wouldn’t want to come back because of the struggle or like some guys just don’t have that.

‘I think that was my fuel to keep on working every day, to be healthy and fit, even if things weren’t going that way.

‘There’s a solution for everything. You just need to accept the present and keep on believing that good things will come.’

Jota was given a standing ovation against Hearts as Celtic delivered their best performance of the season in an emphatic win.

He is not registered in the Europa League squad so will be unable to play in Thursday’s clash with Celta Vigo at Parkhead. But he thanked the fans for their support during his period out of action and is confident it is only a matter of time until he is fit enough to play from the start.

‘It was incredible,’ he added. ‘Playing at Celtic Park again was a moment I was expecting for months.

‘I think no better day to do it than with a good result, a good performance and just in front of our fans. It was a happy day.

‘My love for the Celtic fans is like a reciprocal relationship. We’ve built on this for a long time and the love I received from them is also because of the things we achieved.

‘I just want to guarantee that I will keep on trying to do everything that I can to help the team and to give joy and happiness to the fans whenever they come.

‘In terms of me starting games, there’s obviously metrics involved. We need to be clever obviously because I didn’t play for a long time and we don’t want to make any mistakes.

‘So I think we’re in a comfortable situation when everyone realises what needs to be done for me to grow up and to feel better and healthier. Yeah, just trusting the process.’

There has been a host of speculation surrounding Carrick's future following the Champions League defeat to Cesc Fabregas' side.

However, Man United have not pressed the panic button, with Carrick set to remain in charge for Sunday's Premier League clash against Bournemouth.

The Englishman spoke to the media on Friday, and he opened his own press conference with a statement.

"I'll start today. I just want to say a few things really. I think recently we haven't had a good time, that's pretty obvious. I totally accept what we're going through at the moment. Carrick, though, has to ignore the noise and prepare his team for Sunday's Premier League clash with Bournemouth at Old Trafford.

The slightest setback seems to unsettle them, and suddenly the belief disappears.

The La Liga outfit looked short of ideas for much of the game, yet the moment they found an equaliser, Celtic fell apart. The lack of composure was alarming. One goal was all it took for Celtic to lose their shape, their confidence and ultimately the match. Both goals were entirely avoidable, and a night that promised much quickly descended into another miserable European collapse.

Celta’s second goal, in particular, was a dreadful one to concede. Celtic were exposed down the right, but had more than enough opportunities to get the ball clear. Callum McGregor was too weak in the challenge. The finish from Rodriguez was exquisite, but the home side were inviting pressure and paid the price.

Their instinct is to retreat, keep possession for possession’s sake and play safe passes across the back rather than take the game to the opposition. In doing so, Celtic surrender territory and make life increasingly difficult for themselves. It’s a worrying habit that keeps resurfacing.

Celtic’s European struggles have reached a new low. They have now lost four consecutive matches on the continent, shipping 14 goals across those defeats. A damning reflection of their ongoing struggles.

According to Opta, they had endured such a dismal run only once before in the same season, during their 2017-18 Champions League campaign. This latest defeat means they have now equalled that unwanted club record, underlining the scale of the problems facing O’Neill’s side in Europe.

The disparity between Celtic’s domestic success and their continental struggles is becoming ever more pronounced. Chris Sutton’s description of them as “flat-track bullies” carries added weight after another disappointing night in which they failed to stand up to a stern European examination.

For a club of Celtic’s history, stature and ambitions, their diminishing influence in European football is a troubling reality. They are falling further behind, and displays like this only reinforce the sense that their continental standing is in decline.

In contrast, this was a night to savour for those of Celta persuasion. Not only did the 2-1 victory get them off the mark in the Europa League at the third attempt, having also lost their opening two matches, it was witnessed by their largest-ever travelling support for a European fixture.

Around 3,000 supporters made the journey to Scotland, surpassing their previous record of 2,900 for last season’s Europa League last-16 tie against Lyon. Their vocal backing provided a raucous soundtrack to a memorable comeback, ensuring those who travelled to Glasgow had plenty to celebrate on the long journey home.

The noise from the away end reached another level when Rodríguez struck twice in quick succession to turn the game on its head, with wild celebrations reverberating around the stadium. It was a magnificent show of support that brought an unmistakably Spanish flavour to the occasion, particularly given the notable absence of the Green Brigade, who continued their boycott of all home European fixtures amid an ongoing feud with the hierarchy over its treatment of supporters and the direction of the club.

The only side now languishing below Celtic in the 36-team Europa League standings is Ararat-Armenia, whose goal difference is three worse than the Scottish champions. A sobering measure of just how badly their campaign has unravelled.

However, Celtic have little time to dwell on this latest setback. They face an uphill battle to climb the table, and anything less than maximum points at home to Besiktas next time out would leave their hopes of reaching the knockout stages hanging by a thread.

With five league-phase games remaining, there is still time for Celtic to salvage their increasingly precarious position, but their margin for error is shrinking fast. They need to respond, and they need to do so immediately.
