#219. What’s the Best iPhone to Buy or Avoid Right Now? (2026)

📅 26 Oct 2026 | other | Source

Apple sells seven iPhone models, from the $699 iPhone 17e to the $1,999 iPhone Duo, but which iPhone is best for you? This is a good time of the year to buy an iPhone if you need to upgrade, since Apple just released its flagship models. (We don't expect the more affordable iPhone 18 models until spring 2027.) We've tested nearly every model and outlined their strengths and weaknesses so you can get the right one for you.

Check out our other Apple buying guides, including Which iPhone 18 Models to Buy, Which iPhone 17 Model to Buy, Best iPhone 17 Cases, Best MagSafe Accessories, Best MagSafe Chargers, Best MagSafe Wallets, and Best Apple 3-in-1 Chargers. Wondering what's new in iOS 27? We have details on that, too.

Updated October 2026: We’ve added more details on the iPhone Duo and links to our reviews and other related coverage now that it's available.

If you’re upgrading from an iPhone, you can trade it in to Apple for a small discount. Before you do that, check whether you can sell it elsewhere for more money. iPhones hold their value incredibly well, particularly if they’re in mint or good condition. We have a guide that runs through the process of selling your phone, and we generally recommend you use websites like Swappa, Gazelle, or Back Market.

We also have a handful of setup guides you should follow:

Most people in the US purchase iPhones from carriers, which means the devices are locked to that network. If you bought an iPhone from Verizon, you can't easily switch to another carrier without breaking a contract and paying extra fees. It also poses headaches for travelers who want to use international SIM cards or eSIMs.

The easier approach is to buy directly from Apple, though there are some things you should know:

Connect to any carrier later: Choose this option for the most hassle-free experience. You get an unlocked iPhone, then all you need to do is transfer the SIM card or eSIM from your old iPhone to the new one to get started. This might be painless, though you may need to contact your carrier if the iPhone can't complete the eSIM transfer. In the US, Apple hasn't included a SIM tray since 2022's iPhone 14.

Choose a carrier: If you choose a carrier through Apple.com, you will get an unlocked iPhone, no matter which carrier you choose (except if you purchase with AT&T on an installment plan). The downside is that Apple doesn't have the same offers as the carriers, so you may lose out on a deal. However, carrier offers often require new lines, trade-ins, or an upgraded plan, and the savings come in the form of bill credits, so these deals aren't as amazing as they may seem.

Finance: You can finance your iPhone either with Apple's credit card via monthly installments or as Apple iPhone Payments via a Citizens One loan. You need to choose a carrier here, but Apple confirmed these devices are all unlocked. You have to fill out a credit application to see if you qualify. If you opt to finance through a carrier on Apple.com, only the T-Mobile and Verizon models will be unlocked. The AT&T and Boost Mobile options are locked iPhones, so it's no different from buying it on an installment plan directly from those carriers.

Apple Upgrade: Buying directly from Apple also nets you access to the Apple Upgrade leasing option. Is it worth it? Broadly speaking, no. Most people do not need to upgrade to a new iPhone every year or two. It's expensive, and you must return your leased iPhone in good condition to avoid additional charges. AppleCare is not included, as it was with the discontinued iPhone Upgrade Program.

The most hyped-up feature from the iPhone 16 range was Apple Intelligence, which rolled out in iOS 18.1. This is Apple's suite of AI features, following the wave started by the likes of ChatGPT and Google's Gemini. Apple Intelligence is available on the iPhone 15 Pro/Pro Max and newer, including the iPhone 16e (not to mention select iPads and MacBooks).

What exactly is Apple Intelligence in the first place? It's a collection of features that draw on the power of large language models to introduce “smart” capabilities. For example, in the Voice Memo and Notes app, you can now start a recording and get a real-time transcription. You can even record a call and get the transcription saved in the Notes app. Siri has been redesigned to understand when you misspeak and can even plug into ChatGPT for more powerful responses to open-ended questions. There are writing tools available throughout the operating system that let you change the tone of your emails or proofread text so you don't make an unnecessary typo on Threads.

This is just a small sampling. We've boiled down many of the Apple Intelligence features here, and additional features keep rolling out in iOS updates. For example, Genmoji—which lets you create your own emojis—debuted alongside the AI image-generation app, Image Playground, in iOS 18.2. In iOS 27, Apple added the revamped, conversational Siri AI assistant. Siri now understands personal context and can retrieve information from your messages, emails, and photos. It can also act on your behalf to draft a message with a particular tone or find a good restaurant for your partner's birthday dinner. Here's everything you can do with Siri AI.

If you don't care for Apple Intelligence and want to turn it off, you can! We have a helpful guide detailing the exact steps.

Magnetic MagSafe wireless charging is available on the iPhone 12, iPhone 13, iPhone 14, iPhone 15, iPhone 16, iPhone 17, and iPhone 18 models (except the iPhone 16e), and it allows you to magnetically attach various accessories to the back of the iPhone. (iPhones support Qi2, which is the open standard, whereas MagSafe is proprietary; you can use Qi2 accessories just like MagSafe.)

You can get first-party accessories like Apple's MagSafe Wireless Charger, which sticks to the back for faster charging. Or snag the MagSafe Wallet so you can keep your credit card attached to your phone. There are plenty of third-party accessories (you'll find quite a few in our guide), and MagSafe doesn't just have to be charging accessories like power banks or wireless chargers. There is a wealth of useful tools, from wallets, tripods, and camera cages to notepads and ring lights that can attach to the back of the iPhone and add extra versatility.

If you plan to use a case, get one that has MagSafe embedded inside to ensure a strong magnetic connection with Apple's accessories (non-MagSafe cases can interrupt MagSafe and make the magnetic connection weaker). All of the cases in our case guides are MagSafe-tested. And yes, a case is a smart investment because iPhones are made of glass, and glass breaks, even if Apple uses ceramic to strengthen it. The iPhone 17 is nearly the perfect phone, and it's what most people looking for a new iPhone should buy right now. With this model, Apple finally upgraded the screen's refresh rate to 120 Hz. This might not sound like the snazziest feature, but you'll immediately notice everything feeling smoother and more responsive as you use the phone, especially if you're coming from an iPhone with a 60-Hz display. The slightly larger display can also hit up to 3,000 nits of peak brightness, which means you shouldn't have any trouble reading it in sunny conditions, and the new anti-reflective coating should also help cut glare. It's smart and helpful because you can keep holding your iPhone firmly in portrait orientation, but digitally switch to landscape or portrait orientation when taking a selfie without having to rotate the phone in your hand. It features the Action Button, which replaced the mute switch on older iPhones, but you can bring that functionality back or customize this button to do something else. There's also Camera Control, which launches the camera and doubles as a shutter button. The iPhone Duo may have dominated the headlines recently, but Apple's true flagships are the iPhone 18 Pro and iPhone 18 Pro Max. Breaking the trend of incremental annual updates, the iPhone Duo is Apple's first foray into foldables, and it's astonishingly good for a debut. On the upside, it has the biggest iPhone battery ever, promising 24 hours of mixed use. You can expect to have around 40 percent in the tank after an average day's use.

Apple's take on the software is what we’re most excited about, as it finds unique ways to take advantage of the new form factor and encourages third-party apps, like Slack, to optimize for the folding format. However, it may take a while for all your favorite apps to be properly optimized. Controls are stacked vertically, there's plenty of split-screen support, and transitions are typically smooth. Features like Kid Cue, which helps you catch your kid's attention for photos, and enhanced StandBy support, which lets you use the outer screen as an alarm clock while the inner display gently pulses with light to wake you, will set this iPhone apart. The iPhone Air is the first model of its kind. Borrowing from other Air models—the original MacBook Air and iPad Air—it's the thinnest iPhone ever made at a mere 5.6 mm and one of the lightest at 165 grams. I was pleasantly surprised by the iPhone Air's runtime, which often hit more than five hours of screen-on time. That's better than I expected. With average use, it lasted me a full day, and I didn't feel too anxious about it dying on me. However, on busier days, I needed to top it up in the afternoon. The iPhone's Charging Port Is USB-C, Not Lightning

The iPhone 15 range ditched the long-standing Lightning port for USB-C, and it's in every iPhone since. This is the same charging port on iPads, MacBooks, Android phones, Windows laptops, and many more personal tech gadgets. USB-C is often called the “universal port” because it can technically handle various responsibilities. For example, you can plug in external displays and extend the iPhone's screen or connect the same USB-C accessories you might be currently using on your laptop, like SD card readers. Sadly, it does mean your Lightning accessories won't work unless you get dongles.

You can read more about what the port does here, but there are two important things to note:

The iPhone 15, iPhone 15 Plus, iPhone 16, iPhone 16 Plus, iPhone 16e, iPhone 17, iPhone 17e, and iPhone Air support the USB 2.0 standard just like the iPhones before, so you'll max out at 480 megabits per second of data transfer speeds.

The iPhone 15 Pro, iPhone 15 Pro Max, iPhone 16 Pro, iPhone 16 Pro Max, iPhone 17 Pro, iPhone 17 Pro Max, iPhone 18 Pro, iPhone 18 Pro Max, and iPhone Duo support the USB 3.0 standard and are capable of up to 10 gigabits per second of data transfer speeds.

All iPhone 14 and newer devices completely ditch the physical SIM tray in the US, meaning you can no longer just pop your physical SIM inside to turn on cellular connectivity. Instead, you'll have to rely on an eSIM. You can read more about them here. This tech has been available in iPhones since the iPhone XS and XR, but now you have to use it. Apple has also started switching more global models to eSIM only, like the iPhone Air.) When you're setting up your new iPhone, you'll automatically be asked to transfer your number from your previous iPhone, and the whole process should take a few minutes without any hassle. (eSIMs are more secure than physical SIMs.) Once you transfer your number, your physical SIM card won't work anymore.

There's a chance you may still run into some issues, especially if you're coming from an Android phone (or want to switch back to an Android phone). You'll have to check with your carrier to get your cellular connection working again. It shouldn't take long, but it's not as simple as popping in a physical card.

The Action Button debuted on the iPhone 15 Pro models, and it's now standard on every iPhone in the lineup. It sits above the volume rocker on the left edge of the phone. It effectively replaces the old Mute Switch, but think of it as just a configurable button. You can have it still control the mute function of the phone, or customize it (Settings > Action Button) to trigger the flashlight, a Voice Memo, or even launch an app or Shortcut.

Press and hold this button, and you'll trigger Apple's Visual Intelligence. It'll use the camera to identify subjects in front of you, very much like Google Lens, so you can ask questions via Google or ChatGPT about what you're looking at.

#217. In the biotech world, you can’t see everything

📅 26 Oct 2026 | other | Source

A reporter’s unsettling takeaway from You Can See Everything.

Earlier this week, I attended a screening of You Can See Everything, the buzzy new documentary about the disgraced former biotech CEO Elizabeth Holmes.

I went in not really knowing what to expect. I’d avoided spoilers (and won’t be giving anything away here, either!). I was wondering what new information this film could reveal, given how familiar many of us are with the Theranos story thanks to years of media coverage, the best-selling book Bad Blood, and the Hulu series The Dropout, starring Amanda Seyfried.

Like many others who have seen the film, I came away feeling unsettled. But not just about Holmes’s fraud and the somewhat disturbing interactions between the subjects. The film reignited a concern I’ve had about covering science for most of my career: Despite our best efforts, we never can actually see everything.

In case you’re rusty on the details, Elizabeth Holmes dropped out of Stanford University in 2003 to run Theranos, a diagnostics company she founded, when she was just 19. She went on to claim that her company had invented a way to run over 100 diagnostic tests on just a few drops of blood.

Holmes raised hundreds of millions of dollars for Theranos, which was valued at $9 billion in 2014. Forbes named her the youngest self-made female billionaire—she was worth $4.5 billion when she was just 30 years old.

Things started to unravel in 2015, when an investigation by the Wall Street Journal raised concerns by former Theranos employees that the tests didn’t work. Some also said that the company’s device had not been tested properly, and that blood samples had been analyzed on machines from other companies instead. MIT Technology Review named it one of the biggest technology failures of 2015.

Forbes had revised Holmes’s worth to $0 by 2016. That year, a damning government report said that the company’s lab was struggling with basic procedures and gave patients results even when they knew they could be wrong.

In 2018, Theranos, Holmes, and former company president Ramesh Balwani were charged with “massive fraud.” In 2022, she was sentenced to a little over 11 years in prison for defrauding investors. She began serving her sentence in May 2023.

It was around a month before this that filmmakers Nathan Fielder, Lance Oppenheim, and their colleagues began documenting Holmes and her partner, Billy Evans, at home with their two young children. I’m not giving anything away when I say it’s an extremely intimate portrayal—at least of how the two adults interact with Fielder, who is also a central character in the film.

As you might know if you’ve seen the trailer, there are plenty of super close-ups. I felt as if I spent most of the film staring straight into the faces of those people. And almost the whole way through, they all seemed to be trying to figure each other out. Holmes is known for her dishonesty, but she invited cameras into her home in an apparent bid to reframe the narrative and position herself as an honest person with an unusual mind. Will anyone believe her?

I couldn’t help feeling some empathy for Holmes, a mother we see preparing to leave her two kids for a lengthy prison sentence. And even though I have long known the facts, Holmes is clearly an effective manipulator. You can see her plant the seeds of doubt in Fielder’s mind. At times, I felt she was convincing me, sitting in my cinema seat. Maybe she really didn’t mean to lie?

But then I remembered how other journalists felt after being taken in by her claims. “I blew it,” Fortune’s Roger Parloff reflected in 2015, a year after profiling Holmes and her company. “I’m pretty sure she lied to me,” STAT’s Matthew Herper wrote recently, having interviewed Holmes in 2015. Even the former employee turned whistleblower Tyler Shultz, who knew the product didn’t work, said that he could “have a five-minute conversation with her and walk away feeling like my work was changing the world,” he said in 2022.

I didn’t cover Theranos in the 2010s. If I’d interviewed Holmes, would I have fallen for it all?

One of the early red flags in the Theranos case was the lack of published, peer-reviewed data. Much of the company’s work was classified as a “trade secret.” Scientists tend to publish their work in journals—a slow process that involves having the work evaluated by often anonymous experts in the field, who provide feedback, highlight questionable conclusions, and often ask for clarification. Peer review is meant to add rigor to the publication process. You should be able to trust published scientific papers.

Scientists aren’t perfect. Some will exaggerate their results or inflate their importance. Some manipulate data to serve a hypothesis. Sometimes these infractions are caught during peer review, but not always. There are plenty of examples of scientific findings that could never be replicated by other labs and ended up collapsing under the weight of real evidence. Many papers ultimately end up with corrections. Some are withdrawn—10,000 research papers were retracted in 2023.

At any rate, there’s been a shift to sharing research in other ways. Today, many scientists opt to publish their work at preprint servers like arXiv, medRxiv, and bioRxiv before they submit them to established journals. Those outlets bypass peer review. So do company press releases, social media posts, and many conference presentations. And I get a lot of my stories from conversations and interviews.

I’ll admit I’ve looked at a scientific finding and thought: A scientist has just said they’ve done that. How can I be sure they really have?

I like to think that I hold myself to high journalistic standards here, as does everyone at MIT Technology Review. I would never publish a story about some new scientific development without doing my own form of peer review, and asking scientists—ideally people I know and trust—what they think of a new claim.

But the film is a stark reminder of how easy it can be to fall for an appealing narrative. While most scientists are, I hope, pretty honest, and Holmes is certainly an outlier, we should all continue to interrogate the claims being fed to us. If you’ve seen the film, let me know what you think!

Sellers must also show a real example made using those instructions. An AI-generated image of what your project could theoretically look like won’t cut it.

"AI should support our sellers' creativity and craftsmanship, not replace them," James Ossman, Etsy’s vice president of customer operations, wrote in the announcement.

For most listings, sellers will need to include at least one completely unedited photo showing the actual product against a real background. Some categories, including digital, customized, and made-to-order items, have different requirements. Sellers can still edit additional photos using software or AI tools, but those images must accurately show what buyers will receive.

There are potential consequences for misleading listings. If a seller's images don't meet the requirements and a buyer opens a case because the product looks materially different, the seller could be responsible for a refund. Bot-generated listings without human involvement will be removed and could also result in the seller’s shop being removed from Etsy.

For shoppers, the frustration goes beyond disliking AI art. According to online testimonies, generated images can make it harder to judge the actual product. In a Reddit discussion about the announcement, one user said they stopped buying from a shop selling molds used to make bath bombs after it replaced real product photos with AI imagery. One X user said: "Half the listings there looked nothing like what showed up in the mail, so this is overdue."

The company, to their credit, had been listening to complaints. "Today, many active buyers say they can’t tell which items involve AI-generated content. It’s become a top-three barrier to shopping more often on Etsy," Ossman wrote in its notice to sellers.

Yes. Etsy will continue allowing AI-generated art when sellers have a clear creative role and disclose their use of AI. They cannot present it as handmade or entirely their own creation.

That approach dates back to Etsy's July 2024 policy update, which allowed artwork generated using sellers’ own original prompts, provided they disclosed the AI use in the listing description. But Etsy drew a line at selling AI prompt bundles — collections of instructions for generating content — separately from finished artwork. The new restrictions expand those limits to AI-generated recipes, patterns, and other functional guides.

Enforcement is the next test. Etsy says it’s investing in technology to identify misleading images and fraudulent listings, with plans to expand removals over the coming months. It's also introducing prompts that alert sellers when an uploaded image appears AI-generated and offer guidance on meeting photo requirements. Shoppers will also have a new option to report misleading AI imagery.

#204. Germany sit out FIFA Under-15 World Cup as Russia return

📅 26 Oct 2026 | other | Source

Germany is one a handful of FIFA's 211 nations to refuse an invitation to play at the inaugural Under-15 World Cup. The tournament will also mark Russia's return to the FIFA fold.

With nearly 1,000 matches and 189 teams, the Under-15 World Cup in Azerbaijan is bloated even by FIFA's usual standards. It marks a change in its attitude to children's international tournaments, which have not existed on anywhere near this scale before.

For Germany, it is too much too soon. The German FA (DFB) told DW that it had "consciously decided not to participate." The statement from the organization suggested the DFB — whose development of young players is under scrutiny after a run of poor performances by the men's national team — felt such a tournament was not appropriate for players so young.

"At this age level, our identification and development process is still underway, and we therefore do not believe it is appropriate to nominate a fixed squad for an international tournament at this stage," it read.

"More broadly, the DFB's approach to talent development is based on the principle of supporting young players in a long-term and age-appropriate manner. We carefully assess at which stage international competitions and development measures are beneficial for each age group.

"In our view, young talents should not be exposed too early to the accompanying aspects of professional football. These include increased expectations and pressure, public evaluation of their performances, and early interest from agents and the football market."

The refusal to play in the tournament, which began this week, is consistent with Germany's more cautious approach to youth development. While in some leagues, like the Premier League in England, there is no regulation about youths playing professionally, in Germany's top flight, the Bundesliga, players must be 16. In fact, before the 2020-21 season, players could not be granted a pro license before they were 17 years and six months old.

That change enabled Youssoufa Moukoko to make his Bundesliga debut for Borussia Dortmund a day after his 16th birthday in 2020, becoming the league's youngest player in the process.

Jean-Noël Barrot, the French foreign minister, said another war between Iran and the US would be bad for both sides, and only worsen rising oil prices, which have hit their highest level since May. Ed Miliband, the UK foreign secretary, also urged both sides to embrace diplomacy.

The Istanbul meeting, an unprecedented gathering of regional and European leaders initiated by Turkey and Germany, represented a rare chance for European ministers to have any input into the crises afflicting the Middle East, including in Yemen, Palestine and Iran. They have largely been left as bystanders, apart from sending air force protection to Saudi Arabia as it tries to fend off Houthi drone and missile assaults on its airports and energy installations.

The Middle Eastern states were represented by Fidan, Egypt’s Badr Abdelatty, Pakistan’s Ishaq Dar and Saudi Arabia’s Faisal bin Farhan. Pakistan, Turkey and Saudi Arabia formed the Mecca defensive security pact in August, a grouping Egypt may join.

The European side was represented by Barrot, Miliband, Germany’s Johann Wadephul and Italy’s Antonio Tajani. The agenda covered the Palestinian issue, including settlement expansion, settler violence and the two-state solution, as well as security in the strait of Hormuz, Yemen and efforts to end the Russia-Ukraine war and prevent spillover into the Black Sea.

Wadephul said: “We are united by the impact these deadlocked conflicts are having on the economy and security of our countries and by the will to seek new ways to bring about solutions.”

He made no criticism of the US but accused “the Houthis and Iran of blackmailing world trade and continuing to stranglehold global supplies of energy and fertiliser”.

“We are feeling the effects of the attacks on shipping routes and whether at the petrol pump or the supermarket,” he said. “Elsewhere there is threat of famine.”

Tajani said the aim of the meeting was to work for “de-escalation to guarantee freedom of navigation”.

“I do not believe the problem can be solved from a purely military standpoint,” he added. “If we want to reopen Hormuz, which is vital for our economies, we must promote dialogue and make Iran understand that this attitude cannot continue.”

#202. The four astronauts NASA picked for the first crewed moon mission in 50 years

📅 26 Oct 2026 | other | Source

Sign up for CNN’s Wonder Theory science newsletter. Explore the universe with news on fascinating discoveries, scientific advancements and more.

Astronauts who will helm the first crewed moon mission in five decades were revealed on Monday, queuing up the quartet to begin training for the historic Artemis II lunar flyby that is set to take off in November 2024.

The astronauts are NASA’s Reid Wiseman, Victor Glover and Christina Koch, and Jeremy Hansen of the Canadian Space Agency.

Wiseman is a 47-year-old decorated naval aviator and test pilot who was first selected to be a NASA astronaut in 2009. A native of Baltimore, Maryland, he’s completed one prior spaceflight, a 165-day trip to the International Space Station that had launched aboard a Russian Soyuz rocket in 2014. Most recently, Wiseman served as chief of the astronaut office before stepping down in November 2022, making him eligible for a flight assignment.

Wiseman will serve as commander of the Artemis II mission.

Hansen, 47, is a fighter pilot who was selected by the Canadian Space Agency for astronaut training in 2009. From London, Ontario, Hansen is one of only four active Canadian astronauts, and he recently became the first Canadian to be put in charge of training for a new class of NASA astronauts.

He will be the first Canadian ever to travel to deep space.

Glover is a 46-year-old naval aviator who returned to Earth from his first spaceflight in 2021 after piloting the second crewed flight of SpaceX’s Crew Dragon spacecraft and spending nearly six months aboard the International Space Station.

“It’s so much more than the four names that have been announced,” Glover said during the Monday announcement at NASA Johnson Space Center in Houston. “We need to celebrate this moment in human history. … It is the next step in the journey that will get humanity to Mars.”

Glover, born in Pomona, California, served in several military squadrons in the United States and Japan in the 2000s, and he completed test pilot training with the US Air Force. When he was selected for the NASA astronaut corps in 2013, he was working in the US Senate as a legislative fellow. All told, Glover logged 3,000 flight hours in more than 40 aircraft, over 400 carrier arrested landings and 24 combat missions.

Glover’s first mission to space was as part of the SpaceX Crew-1 team, which launched to the International Space Station in November 2020 for a six-month stay on the orbiting laboratory.

Koch, 44, is a veteran of six spacewalks — including the first all-female spacewalk in 2019. She holds the record for the longest single spaceflight by a woman, with a total of 328 days in space. Koch is also an an electrical engineer who helped develop scientific instruments for multiple NASA mission. Koch, a native of Grand Rapids, Michigan, also spent a year at the South Pole, an arduous stay that could well prepare her for the intensity of a moon mission.

The Artemis II mission will build on Artemis I, an uncrewed test mission that sent NASA’s Orion capsule on a 1.4 million-mile voyage to lap the moon that concluded in December. The space agency deemed that mission a success and is still working to review all the data collected.

If all goes to plan, Artemis II will take off around November 2024. The crew members, strapped inside the Orion spacecraft, will launch atop a NASA-developed Space Launch System rocket from NASA’s Kennedy Space Center in Florida.

The journey is expected to last about 10 days and will send the crew out beyond the moon, potentially further than any human has traveled in history, though the exact distance is yet to be determined.

The “exact distance beyond the Moon will depend on the day of liftoff and the relative distance of the Moon from the Earth at the time of the mission,” NASA spokesperson Kathryn Hambleton said via email.

After circling the moon, the spacecraft will return to Earth for a splashdown landing in the Pacific Ocean.

Artemis II is expected to pave the way for the Artemis III mission later this decade, which NASA has vowed will put the first woman and person of color on the lunar surface. It will also mark the first time humans have touched down on the moon since the Apollo program ended in 1972.

The Artemis III mission is expected to take off later this decade. But much of the technology the mission will require, including spacesuits for walking on the moon and a lunar lander to ferry the astronauts to the moon’s surface, is still in development.

NASA is targeting a 2025 launch date for Artemis III, though the space agency’s inspector general has already said delays will likely push the mission to 2026 or later.

The space agency has been seeking to return people to the moon for more than a decade. The Artemis program was designed to pave the way to establishing a permanent lunar outpost, allowing astronauts to live and work deeper into space long term as NASA and its partners map a path to sending the first humans to Mars.

Vanessa Wyche, the director of NASA’s Johnson Space Center, declined to provide details to CNN about the selection process. But she emphasized the diversity of the Artemis II crew, which includes men and women rather than only a staff of White male test pilots as has been the case for historic missions of the past.

“I can tell you, they still all have the right stuff,” Wyche said. “We have requirements different than we did (when we) just had test pilots” on inaugural missions.

Koch said in an interview with CNN’s Ed Lavandera that the group found out they were selected a few weeks ago.

“We were all sent to a meeting that was on our calendars under a different pretext that didn’t sound as lofty as the one it was going to be,” Koch said. “And accidentally two of us were very late to that meeting.”

She said the offer rendered her “speechless.”

“It truly is an honor,” she added. “It’s an honor — not to get myself in the space — but because it’s amazing to be a part of this team that’s going back to the moon and on to Mars.”

An interview with the four astronauts will air on “CNN This Morning” on Tuesday, which starts at 6 am ET.

#201. What is TikTok’s Im so sorry trend? The AI photo reveals, explained

📅 26 Oct 2026 | other | Source

There's always a new AI photo trend on TikTok, whether people are turning themselves into caricatures or posing for an '80s yearbook portrait. If you've tried those and are looking for another excuse to play around with AI image generation, the "I'm so sorry" trend has people digging through their childhood photos — and apologizing for what they find.

People are sharing edited versions of their baby pictures, childhood snapshots, and old Halloween costumes, then revealing the changes one slide at a time. With every swipe, another detail gets stripped away until they unveil the real photo — and the result is usually pretty horrifying. Horrifying enough that users feel compelled to caption their posts with "I'm so sorry." The carousels are usually set to Harry Styles' "Coming Up Roses" or Prince's "Purple Rain."

The appeal of the trend is in the anticipation. Once you understand the format, even an innocent opening photo becomes suspicious. You start inspecting the hair, the outfit, the background. Something is going to go. Something is going to appear. You just don’t know what!

In the Halloween versions, that can mean revealing a costume was much more homemade or culturally offensive than the opening image suggested. Other versions start with a picture-perfect baby portrait before revealing a newborn photo that is not as cute as expected (I'll leave it at that). Some depict yearbook photos or women after giving birth.

According to Know Your Meme, one of the earliest known examples came from TikTokker @theywanthunter on Aug. 22, when he posted a carousel featuring his supposed girlfriend saying, "Happy 2 years bae," before revealing that both the woman and the man in the image had been edited. The video had accumulated more than 25 million views by October.

Viewers online are eating it up, for the most part. "The 'I'm sorry I lied' trend on TikTok has to be the funniest trend ever," one user wrote. Another said it was "actually making me feel better about my childhood photos."

But others are less amused by exactly how those slideshows are made. "Why do we keep normalizing uploading pictures of children into a public AI?" another user asked, raising a real concern that in order to participate in the trend, people are handing real childhood photos over to AI tools.

Those very real concerns center on what happens after the upload. Depending on the service and its settings, images may be retained or used to help train AI models. The public can also easily copy and manipulate those without permission. UNICEF has warned that real photographs of children are being altered into sexually explicit deepfakes. Of course, that doesn’t mean participating in this trend will lead to abuse, but it helps explain why some viewers are questioning the privacy trade-off behind the joke.

Start with the photo you want to reveal at the end, then use an AI image editor (any will do) to create a more polished-looking version. You might change the hairstyle, upgrade the outfit, or give the background a makeover.

Then, for a gradual reveal, add in one edit at a time and save each version. Then arrange the images in reverse order. Upload the images as a TikTok photo carousel or create a video slideshow using an editing app, and add captions over each photo, admitting to each change as you go.

Finish with the original photo and the obligatory "I’m so sorry," then add your chosen audio from either the Harry Styles or Prince track. They're both pretty great songs.
