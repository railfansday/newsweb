Arsenal head to the City Ground with something to prove. With a tricky set of results and a major European night looming, Mikel Arteta is expected to make changes.

The Gunners visit Nottingham Forest on Sunday (4.30pm). And they’re looking to record a third consecutive victory following Tuesday’s 3-0 Champions League win at Lille.

Arteta has just one league win in five games against Forest, which came in January 2024. Both of his last two visits to the City Ground ended in goalless draws.

However, the manager is confident after his side’s five-goal haul in the victories over Leeds and Lille. He will be looking to continue their fine form.

The Gunners host Bayern Munich in their next game, but Arteta was happy to focus on Sunday’s opponents. The Spaniard claimed the visit to the City Ground was a ‘beautiful challenge’ and insisted his men must be ready.

David Raya should start in goal after keeping a clean sheet against Lille.

Ezri Konsa, alongside the new signing Gabriel Magalhaes, makes up the back three, with Riccardo Calafiori switching back to his preferred position of left-back.

Up front, it is a simple decision. Kai Havertz is set to come in for Viktor Gyokeres, who had a nightmare evening against Lille, touching the ball just 13 times.

The bench will be crucial for Arsenal, with Lewis-Skelly, Eze, Gyokeres and Max Dowman all likely to play a part, particularly with Bayern Munich looming.

#2498. Manchester United defence: Why Carrick must trust Yoro and Heaven

📅 26 Oct 2026 | other | Source

Manchester United endured another embarrassing draw against 10-man Tottenham after failing to register their second win despite bringing in a plethora of players.

Manchester United invested heavily last season in their attacking options, bringing in Bryan Mbeumo and Matheus Cunha.

This season, they overhauled their midfield. United spent massively on Carlos Baleba, Youri Tielemans and Andrey Santos. Despite spending massively, they have failed to produce the control they would have expected in the midfield. The lack of control was evident in the recent Tottenham game, where Mainoo-Tielemans partnership failed to control the game collectively.

The midfield question was covered in our recent report as well, highlighting a lack of control and carried comments from Gary Neville and Jamie Redknapp.

The bigger question is when United will strengthen their defence. As per our previous report as well, Manchester United are planning to overhaul their defence in the upcoming transfer windows.

United were heavily linked with several left backs but eventually failed to bring any one. The situation left Luke Shaw as the only senior option in the position, while Patrick Dorgu is used upfront by Michael Carrick.

Harry Amass is another option for Michael Carrick, but he is only here because of a failed transfer move. He has been rarely used at left back as Dalot and Mazraoui get the nod in the position ahead of him.

The situation at right-back isn’t all good either. Diogo Dalot is the first option there, but he rarely provides the assurance that puts Michael Carrick at ease. Noussair Mazraoui is the second choice at right-back, but his attacking numbers aren’t great either.

Harry Maguire and Lisandro Martinez form up United’s centre-back pairing while Leny Yoro and Ayden Heaven are used all over the defensive line in rotation when needed. Yoro was used at right-back against Tottenham while Heaven arrived as left-back towards the end of the game.

Manchester United’s season hasn’t been defensively strong or anywhere near being good. They have conceded goals in all Premier League games and only kept one clean sheet against Sabah in the Champions League.

Lisandro Martinez has come under scrutiny after errors on four occasions. Against Ipswich Town, Manchester United won 5-2, but his error resulted in Ipswich’s second goal.

His own goal against Fulham gave away two points at Craven Cottage in the 1-1 draw. Another error led to Solanke’s goal against Tottenham, when he took his eyes off the ball.

His injury concerns are another aspect, and maybe Michael Carrick needs to make a decision.

Manchester United’s success depended heavily on youth over the years. When pundits questioned them, Sir Alex Ferguson gave them the chance to prove them wrong. Alan Hansen made the famous quote “You can’t win anything with kids” on the BBC’s Match of the Day on August 19, 1995. United did exactly that, played youth and went on to win everything.

Michael Carrick needs to do exactly that. He needs to trust youth and allow them to make mistakes. Leny Yoro and Ayden Heaven arrived as massive potentials, but they haven’t been given the license to express themselves, without fearing for their places. Both the youngsters have rarely started together or in a stretch of games. It is maybe time for them to take their chances.

Michael Carrick needs to keep Harry Maguire as a senior experienced figure around them. There is no doubt over Martinez’s ability, but he is not the answer to United’s long-term problem. United need to blend youth with experience as their identity moving forward. Lisandro Martinez perhaps isn’t the answer to United’s problem.

A defensive revival expected in the upcoming transfer windows needs to happen now. It must happen with chances to the Youth. Michael Carrick needs to be brave enough and throw the youngsters in the mix. What it will bring is a long-term solution. They need to experience the errors and pressures that will shape them in football.

An important game at both ends of the League One table will take place at the Accu Stadium on Saturday, as Huddersfield Town welcome Wigan Athletic.

The hosts sit at the foot of the division after a midweek defeat while the visitors find themselves second in a five-game unbeaten streak in all competitions.

Huddersfield Town will set out to continue an unbeaten run on Saturday with the chance to move to the top of League One.

After handing Martin Drury the permanent reins over the summer, following consecutive disappointing mid-table finishes, they have enjoyed a strong start, currently sitting second in the third tier on 22 points from their first 11 games.

The Terriers have won six and only lost one of those, including winning three and drawing one of their last four outings, having firstly beaten Burton Albion and been pegged back late by Sheffield Wednesday in early October.

Drury's men then picked up an impressive 2-0 victory away at then-leaders Plymouth Argyle, before hosting Peterborough United on Tuesday and dishing out a 4-1 beating as Ilias Bronkhorst opened the scoring inside three minutes, Joe Taylor scored a brace and Bali Mumba added a fourth 10 minutes from time.

With confidence and momentum growing at the Accu Stadium, and only trailing leaders Stockport County on goal difference, Huddersfield Town will aim to continue their hot streak and put three more points on the board on Saturday.

Their visitors, meanwhile, head into the weekend in search of three important points in their battle at the other end of the table after a poor start.

Heading into their first full season back under the management of Gary Caldwell with the hopes of building on a 16th-placed finish last time around, Wigan Athletic have struggled to get going thus far.

The Latics currently sit bottom of the table having picked up just seven points from their first 10 outings, winning two and losing seven of those with no team scoring fewer than their seven goals.

One of those victories did come last weekend against Reading after back-to-back defeats, but they failed to build on that result in midweek, visiting Oxford United and losing 2-1, having levelled 20 minutes from time through Christian Saydee only for Kai Andrews to score the decisive goal seven minutes later.

Wigan now face arguably their toughest test of the season thus far with the hopes of stopping the rot and taking a major scalp to begin turning their season around.

Huddersfield Town form (all competitions):

Huddersfield Town remain without defenders Radinio Balker, Murray Wallace and Jack Whatmough and midfielders Marcus McGuane, Herbie Kane, David Kasumu and Ryan Ledson at the weekend due to ongoing injuries.

Given those absences, Joe Low and Ibane Bowat should again partner up in the middle of the defence, while Archie Collins and Cameron Humphreys will continue in the engine room.

Joe Taylor will lead the line with confidence, having moved onto eight league goals for the season with his midweek brace, while Derensili Sanches Fernandes has been another star man since his summer arrival, recording five goals and five assists in 11 League One appearances.

Wigan Athletic are unable to call on Fraser Murray, who was sent off last time out, while Luke Harris, Ryan Trevitt, Dara Costelloe and Max Power may all remain sidelined.

Christian Saydee may come back in from the outset after his goal off the bench in midweek, while Scott Hogan will hope to keep his place up top having scored the decisive goal in last weekend's win over Reading.

Reggie Walsh and Jon Mellish are also among those pushing for starting opportunities, while Jensen Weir is a mainstay in the engine room.

#2487. West Ham’s Championship Adventure Comes With A Financial Warning

📅 26 Oct 2026 | other | Source

For years, football’s “Big Five” leagues have been considered untouchable: the Premier League, La Liga, Bundesliga, Serie A and Ligue 1.

But could the EFL Championship now have a stronger claim to being among Europe’s biggest leagues?

So far this season, Championship clubs have attracted a combined crowd of 3,044,652 supporters, averaging 23,066 spectators per game across the division.

Leading the way are West Ham United, whose relegation to the Championship has seen the London Stadium continue to fill up week after week. The Hammers are currently averaging an impressive 60,882 supporters per match, a figure many of Europe’s top-flight clubs can only dream of.

And it is not just West Ham drawing huge crowds.

Wolves are averaging 30,737, while Middlesbrough have 30,133 and Southampton are attracting 29,176 supporters per game. Derby County are close behind on 27,751, with Sheffield United averaging 26,639, Cardiff City 26,229 and Birmingham City 26,146.

Those are attendance figures that compare favourably with many clubs competing in Europe’s leading divisions.

What makes the argument even more compelling is that this is not simply a one-season phenomenon.

The Championship welcomed a record-breaking 12.7 million supporters during the 2023/24 campaign, with the English Football League confirming it was the second most-attended league competition in Europe, behind only the Premier League and ahead of divisions such as the Bundesliga, La Liga and Serie A.

The London Stadium is breaking attendance records

Yet beneath the packed stadiums and vibrant atmospheres lies a financial reality that paints a very different picture.

For all its popularity, the Championship remains one of the most financially challenging leagues in world football. Despite attracting crowds that many top-flight divisions would envy, only four clubs reported a profit during the 2023/24 season, and those profits were achieved primarily through player sales rather than sustainable day-to-day football operations.

The reason is simple: the prize for promotion is so lucrative that clubs continue to spend heavily in pursuit of a place in the Premier League. The financial rewards of reaching the top flight, including broadcast revenue, commercial growth and increased sponsorship opportunities, have created an environment where many owners are willing to accept significant short-term losses in exchange for the chance of long-term gain.

The scale of those losses is remarkable. A BBC report published earlier this year claimed Championship clubs had collectively lost more than £3 billion over the previous decade. More recently, football finance expert Kieran Maguire revealed that seven Championship clubs alone reported combined losses of £169 million during the 2024/25 financial year, despite one of those clubs ultimately securing promotion to the Premier League the following season.

It highlights one of football’s greatest contradictions. The Championship is a competition capable of filling stadiums, generating television audiences and creating atmospheres that rival many of Europe’s leading divisions, yet financially it remains heavily dependent on owner investment and the dream of promotion, with the former option now limited due to Squad Cost Ratio (SCR).

Nobody is suggesting the Championship can yet match Europe’s elite leagues for playing quality, global television reach or international star power. However, when judged on attendances, atmosphere, historic clubs and overall scale, the Championship has grown into something far bigger than a traditional second division.

I know many West Ham supporters are understandably enjoying the season so far, myself included. The club’s position in the automatic promotion places, combined with a renewed sense of expectation rather than underdog status, has created a positive atmosphere around the London Stadium. The absence of VAR has also been widely welcomed.

However, beyond the impressive attendances and feel-good factor lies a stark reality. Promotion remains the overriding objective. While life in the Championship may be proving enjoyable, the financial rewards of the Premier League are simply too significant to ignore. For a club of West Ham’s size and ambition, a return to England’s top flight is not just desirable; it is essential.

The Championship may offer a more authentic football experience, but the promised land of the Premier League remains where clubs generate the revenues needed to compete and thrive in the modern game.
