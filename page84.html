#1998. Sheffield Wednesday's Barry Bannan happy to stay at Hillsborough for another '20 years'

📅 26 Oct 2026 | other | Source

Bannan first joined the Owls in the summer of 2015, becoming a firm fans’ favourite and ultimately club captain.

A decade later, he found himself foregoing time in the swimming pool with his children on a family holiday in Turkey to join a Zoom call with then owner Dejphon Chansiri to discuss when players and staff could expect to be paid again.

By October 24 of the same year, Chansiri was out, and the club had entered administration.

“Sometimes you have to reach rock bottom to come back again, and (administration) was that point,” said Bannan, 12 months on.

Bannan says he “learned a lot” from those last months under Chansiri where players and staff increasingly became reliant on him for answers – and often he had no answers to give.

“It made me a better person, a better captain, a better leader, because you have to listen to individuals, whether it’s a staff member, whether it’s a player, their problems, and normally the problems that you have as a captain would be football-based and easy to deal with, because you’ve been there and done it,” he said.

“But when it’s something that’s out of your hands, you’ve still got to listen and find a way. As hard as it is to take positives from that time, I think I’ve come out of it as a better person.”

The uncertainty around Wednesday’s ownership and direction affected Bannan personally.

Conversations with individuals connected to the initial preferred bid convinced the 36-year-old that, difficult though it was to accept, his future lay elsewhere, and so he joined Millwall in January.

Bannan likens being away from Wednesday for the first time in almost 11 years to cheating on a partner. He lived alone in London, spending time at his family’s home in Nottingham where he could.

His two-year-old son Wilf drew comfort from sharing the spare room with his dad on the nights when he was back at home, and Bannan can remember the heartache of having to tear himself away early on Thursday mornings to get to training.

“I’d literally be 15 minutes out the door, and my wife would text me saying, ‘he’s at the top of the stairs crying and asking for his daddy,'” Bannan said.

Bannan has nothing but positive things to say about the “amazing” people at Millwall, but jumped at the chance to return to Hillsborough this summer after the David Storch-led takeover was complete.

With the club stabilised and going well in League One, the fan favourite hopes he can stick around for another 10 or even 20 years.

“In January I left, but I always knew that I would be back here one day. I’ll finish my football career here, whether it’s the end of this season or three, four more seasons if given the chance.

“I’d like to go into coaching, management, whatever it may be, I feel as if with my relationship with the owner that hopefully I get a chance to work here in the future as well when my career does finish.

“If I could be here 10, 20 years further down the line, it would be amazing for the whole family.

“The sky’s the limit for the club. If it keeps going the way it’s going with David in charge, I think it’ll go from strength to strength.”

#1997. Michael Carrick dismisses Erik ten Hag's damning suggestions of 'toxic' culture and broken promises at Man United - and insists he has the backing of his Old Trafford bosses despite six games without a win

📅 26 Oct 2026 | other | Source

Published: 21:21 BST, 20 October 2026 | Updated: 21:23 BST, 20 October 2026

Michael Carrick is adamant there is no ‘toxic culture’ at Old Trafford despite explosive allegations by former Manchester United manager Erik ten Hag.

United landed in Italy for their Champions League clash with Como without a win in six games and missing nine senior players as they were met with a storm warning ahead of Wednesday's game at the Stadio Giuseppe Sinigaglia.

Asked about Ten Hag’s comments, where he complained of broken promises and a toxic dressing room culture during his time at the club, Carrick insisted he has the support of the owners, chief executive Omar Berrada and director of football Jason Wilcox.

He also knows that will count for nothing if he cannot deliver results after the club’s worst start to a league season in 40 years.

‘That’s Erik’s opinion,’ said Carrick. ‘Why wouldn’t I (feel backed)? I’m in this position. I have a really good relationship with everybody at the football club.

‘There are really no issues and no problems with any of that side. There are certain conversations we have that we’re really clear — with Jason, with Omar, with the owners, and down to the players. It’s really important we’re all pulling in the same direction.'

Michael Carrick has stressed that he has full backing of Manchester United's hierarchy

The current head coach was forced to answer to an inflammatory interview from one of his predecessors Erik ten Hag

Carrick also dismissed suggestions of a toxic culture at United after Ten Hag claimed that the club fed it by giving power to the players during his time in charge.

‘Since I came back, the players and the staff have given us everything that they could give,’ Carrick added.

‘We’ve had some good performances. We’ve had some performances that we want to do better and we can improve on. We’ve made some mistakes.

‘But I have to say, the type of performance we put into the weekend and for large parts against Atletico, there’s a lot of togetherness there. There’s a lot of will and determination to do better and wanting to do it for each other, which is important.

‘So in that regard for the group, I couldn’t be happier with the togetherness, for sure.’

With United struggling for form, Carrick accepted his future is going to be a source of debate, adding: ‘I don’t need any reassurance. It’s pretty clear. I know the role, I know my responsibilities. I don’t need anyone else to tell me when it’s going well or when it needs to be better.

‘I know we’ve got to win football games for everything to flow and everything to fall into place.’

Ten Hag saw things differently, saying of the ownership: ‘They promised me loyalty, they promised me backing, they promised communication, that we get aligned. We spoke about the issues. But the promises, they didn’t keep.’

Ten Hag also hit out at Ronaldo during his second spell at the club, telling the Athletic: ‘He let the team down, and he let me down, because we planned with him.

‘I flew out to Portugal to speak with him about what I was aiming for before I came to United. Obviously, he’s one of the best in football history. I respected him a lot. But at that time, as a manager, I had to make a rational decision and keep the players I admire in.’

As for the present crop, Carrick must do without the suspended Leny Yoro, while Lisandro Martinez, Marcus Rashford, Kobbie Mainoo, Benjamin Sesko, Luke Shaw, Patrick Dorgu, Ayden Heaven and Manuel Ugarte are unavailable due to injury or illness.

It leaves Carrick with a major headache in defence with Matthijs De Ligt set to make his comeback from a back injury after nearly a year out of action.

‘It’s great to have him back,’ said Carrick. ‘He’s had such a long journey of maybe 11 months or so of being out, being patient, worked hard.

‘So we’re delighted to have him back. He’s fit, he’s ready and he’s been training for a number of weeks now, so he’s in a really good place.’

The weather forecast made equally grim reading for United after they landed at the end of a day of downpours here. Up to 64 millimetres of rain is due to fall before the game, and lightning is expected close to the early kick-off time.

‘There are no immediate concerns for me,’ smiled Carrick. ‘We’re kind of used to the rain, so we’ll make the most of it.’

Sat next to him at the Stadio Giuseppe Sinigaglia on Tuesday night, Andrey Santos was asked about Martinez’s bombshell interview after last week’s defeat in Madrid in which his teammate criticised the Old Trafford hierarchy — and by association the club’s summer signings – over United’s transfer strategy in the summer.

‘We know the comments about Licha,’ said Santos, a £50million buy from Chelsea. ‘But I think now it’s done, so we have to move on to tomorrow. We are on the same page (as a group) and we believe in ourselves and our teammates.’

But 21-year-old centre-back Johnson is more demanding still, and unhappy the last three matches have all ended 1-1.

Lindsey was frustrated by what he felt was a negative line of questioning in his press conference to preview the visit of the Valiants, but the high expectations do not just come from some on the terraces, but the dressing room too.

“We're unbeaten at home (in the league) at the minute but actually, I would be quite disappointed that we've had a few draws,” said Johnson, who made his loan from Sunderland permanent in the summer.

And Lindsey revealed Johnson made his frustrations known after Tuesday's game at home to Accrington Stanley, where it took a 90th-minute equaliser from the in-form Ollie Pearce to rescue a point.

Lindsey made what went on behind closed doors public because he took it as a sign of a healthy dressing room, rather than a negative.

“They're disappointed when we don't win,” said the coach, who quite rightly went out of his way to emphasise how good a start to the season it has been for his newly-promoted side.

“They're disappointed when we drop points.

“Zach Johnson's a real example of that. He came in after the game on Tuesday night and he had a five-minute rant, a young lad like that showing a real desire to be disappointed from dropping points.

“I kind of let him float, let him go. Let him have a rant.

“I don't mind it. He's a great pro, a good lad. He speaks well, understands the game, trains well and plays well. He's exactly what any manager would want in their team.”

The pressure Rochdale put on York last season, winning 106 points yet missing out on the only automatic promotion spot, means Johnson and his team-mates from last season are well used to must-win games, and he sees Saturday's as another.

“Getting a point against Newport, they're unbeaten at home as well and we had 10 men for 70 minutes of the game (after a red card for Jovan Malcolm), it just shows a different side to York,” he said.

“It's great character, great determination.

“But ultimately, when we draw at Newport, even though we were 1-0 up, I feel like to take a massive positive step in the league, we've got to get three points against Accrington Stanley. The fact that we got a point is very frustrating, especially due to it being a set-piece (that Stanley scored from).

“So now it has to be three points against Port Vale. There's no other way about it.

“There's three points between us and 17th so if you can make that gap go bigger and then push on, you can look like a threat in this league, and that's what we want.”

Johnson says his mindset as a York player changed late last season.

“Coming as a loan player last season, I never really looked at promotion,” he admitted. “I looked at it from a selfish point of view. I wanted to get my games and develop individually.

“It was only until sort of the last few games where I really clicked with the lads. Then because of York and the fans and the way that they took to me, I really wanted to get them into the Football League.

“When I had the opportunity to come back here, it was just a no-brainer for me, and I've loved it since coming back here as well.

“Now I've got to look at myself as a League Two player and you want to try and climb up the ranks as quickly as possible.

“The more I think that I'm a League Two player in a League Two team trying to get us higher, I feel like that can keep me grounded and hopefully keep improving my performances.”

#1987. The Phil Foden show in front of 20,000 empty seats at the Etihad: Man City's hat-trick hero has found the fizz and purpose that deserted him last season as he stakes his claim in style, writes JACK GAUGHAN

More competitive adverts for the Champions League’s group phase are available – a night when thousands of blue seats remained strikingly vacant - but Phil Foden won’t mind that.

While all eyes are on how Manchester City replace Erling Haaland in the eventuality of a more pressing injury than the knock from the weekend, Foden made his point around who fills in for Antoine Semenyo.

Semenyo will miss the next two months with a knee problem and it will be a fight between Foden and Iliman Ndiaye as to who makes that spot their own. Foden’s hat-trick with AEK Athens in town after notching against Ipswich Town four days earlier do his chances no harm. His first two goals were products of super link up with Rico Lewis, the pair in sync in one-touch blurs of movement, and Foden slamming high into the net with a purpose not always seen recently.

And after appearing generally more buoyant at the weekend, Foden is playing with a little more fizz – the sort he found early on last season before his form nosedived – and just looks more confident.

It is only a start at the moment and Semenyo’s absence should provide a greater chance to build upon that.

‘I consider Phil one of the best English players,’ Enzo Maresca said. ‘For different reasons in the last two or three years, he was not good enough. But I know how good he is. So we are just trying to help him; we are sure that he's going to help us this season.’
