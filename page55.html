#2566. 21-year-old’s success away from Portsmouth continues – a starting XI role could await under John Mousinho

📅 26 Oct 2026 | other | Source

Portsmouth are hoping to progress up the Championship table this term after back-to-back relegation scraps. Pompey have recruited to fuel these ambitions, with Marezi making his mark up top while decent fees were spent on Rocco Shein and Abu Kamara too.

The goalkeeper department saw a new arrival too, as Polish shot-stopper Daniel Bielica joined to provide more stern competition for Nicolas Schmid. The latter has kept his place for the most part, but it will be intriguing to see how the battle pans out.

In the future, the Pompey duo may well have another competitor for the no.1 shirt. Once again, 21-year-old academy graduate Toby Steward is impressing out on loan as he builds towards a future in the first-team at Fratton Park.

Steward has been highly regarded at Portsmouth for some time. The ‘keeper trained with the England setup in his youth and has been gaining senior experience from a young age, spending spells on loan with nearby non-league teams like Gosport Borough and Tonbridge Angels before stepping up over the past two years.

The 2025/26 campaign saw Steward enjoy a strong year with Scottish Championship winners St. Johnstone, and he returned for their Scottish Premiership campaign in the summer. The Saints have started well, sitting 5th after nine games with three wins, three draws and three defeats to their name.

Pompey man Steward has been their mainstay in between the sticks. One clean sheet in nine league appearances might not be the most flattering tally, but the youngster’s impressive performances are highlighted by a 77.6% save percentage and an xG prevented total of 4.05.

He’s commanded his box well, with eight sweeper actions and 11 high claims – the latter being the most by any Scottish Premiership goalkeeper this campaign (Fotmob).

Schmid and Bielica are aged 29 and 27 respectively. Both could feasibly be Portsmouth’s starting goalkeeper for a while to come, but Steward’s case for a future in the first-team fold is strengthening.

The young shot-stopper’s stats in Scotland show he’s a very well-rounded goalkeeper for his age. His time out on loan has helped develop him, and that is something Pompey can be proud of given that their track record of progressing academy prospects has not been great in recent years.

At 21, if Steward continues to impress, he could soon be battling Schmid and Bielica at Fratton Park. His higher ceiling and room to develop may well see him favoured as a higher value asset too,

For now though, Steward will watch the battle from afar as he looks to keep impressing in Scotland.

By Adeyeye Oluwapelumi | 22 Oct 2026 23:02, Last updated: 22 Oct 2026 23:11

Gil Vicente will look to end Porto’s perfect start to the Primeira Liga season when the sides meet at the Estadio Cidade de Barcelos on Saturday, with the visitors aiming to bounce back from their latest Champions League setback.

The Dragons have collected all 24 points available from their opening eight league matches, leaving them five points clear at the top of the Portuguese top flight table, while the Roosters sit sixth after picking up 14 points from the same number of games.

Having ended a three-year wait for the Portuguese top-flight title last term in Francesco Farioli’s first season in charge, Porto have maintained that momentum with a flawless start to their 2026-27 league campaign.

The Dragons surpassed their seven-match winning start to the 2025-26 league season two weeks ago, when they came from behind to secure a 2-1 victory away to newly promoted Maritimo courtesy of goals from Victor Froholdt and Oskar Pietuszewski.

That success followed a 3-1 Clasico victory over Benfica on matchday seven, though the only concern on an otherwise perfect title defence is that Farioli’s side have conceded in each of their last four top-flight matches after keeping clean sheets in their opening four fixtures.

More concerning, however, is their European form, with Porto losing each of their opening three Champions League matches, including defeats to Manchester City and Real Betis before Wednesday’s 1-0 home loss to PSV Eindhoven.

Back on domestic duty, the Dragons will be keen to maintain their dominance in the Primeira Liga, and a trip to face opponents they have beaten in three of their last four top-flight meetings should offer encouragement, particularly as the reigning champions won 2-0 on their most recent competitive visit to Barcelos.

Having finished sixth last season before former boss Cesar Peixoto’s departure in the summer, European hopefuls Gil Vicente currently occupy the same position under Luis Pinto, whose side have won four of their opening eight league matches and sit just four points behind the top four.

The Barcelos outfit have also found form at a crucial stage of the campaign, going unbeaten in their last four outings across all competitions and winning the most recent three, with an extra-time victory over Uniao de Santarem sandwiched between Primeira Liga successes against Moreirense (1-0) and Braga (2-1).

Pinto will be pleased with his side’s recent momentum, particularly after they endured a four-match winless run in the league (D2, L2) between late August and mid-September, and Gil Vicente are now targeting three consecutive top-flight victories for the first time since February.

Achieving that feat will require the Roosters to rediscover their home form, having failed to win either of their last two matches in Barcelos after opening the season with consecutive victories on their own turf.

Gil Vicente will be without Mohamed Bamba and Arthur Tchaptchet, who remain sidelined through injury, while Hector Hernandez is a doubt after withdrawing from the squad shortly before Monday’s victory over Braga.

It remains to be seen whether Mohamed Kaba, who replaced Hernandez in that game, will earn his first league start for the club, although Walace Franca could instead lead the line after coming off the bench to score last time out.

Porto are expected to remain without William Gomes, who has missed their last two outings after sustaining an ankle injury during the Dragons’ previous league match against Maritimo.

Meanwhile, Vasco Sousa remains in the final stages of his recovery from a fractured fibula, with Saturday’s encounter likely to come too soon for the midfielder.

In the defeat to PSV, Samu Aghehowa made his first competitive start since returning from a lengthy injury lay-off, but the Spanish forward was withdrawn before the hour mark, leaving the door open for Andre Silva to return to the starting lineup.

#2562. Italian Press Raises Questions Over Nuno’s West Ham Striker Decision

📅 26 Oct 2026 | other | Source

Italian media have begun questioning what has happened to Taty Castellanos after the striker was named among the substitutes for West Ham’s victories over Lincoln City and Swansea City. The Argentine has found himself watching from the bench in recent matches, prompting suggestions in Italy that he may have fallen out of favour with Nuno Espírito Santo.

Lazio Press reports, that the former Lazio forward has lost his starting place because his performances — and particularly his finishing — have failed to meet expectations. Given the number of chances West Ham’s forwards have missed recently, it is perhaps understandable that questions are being asked about the striker’s contribution.

However, I am not convinced Castellanos has been dropped at all. In fact, I suspect Nuno’s recent selections have far more to do with the opposition and the tactical requirements of individual matches than any particular dissatisfaction with Taty.

Taty has been underwhelming for West Ham in the Championship.

It is difficult to conclude that poor finishing has cost Taty his place when none of West Ham’s strikers are currently proving particularly clinical. One only has to look at Saturday’s 1-0 victory over Swansea City.

Castellanos, Pablo and Joël Piroe all failed to convert good opportunities, so I am not sure the Argentine is performing any worse in front of goal than his competition.

Let’s be honest, all of West Ham’s strikers are currently being outscored by centre-back Konstantinos Mavropanos. That would ordinarily represent a considerable concern, but I am not convinced it bothers Nuno Espírito Santo as much as some might imagine.

Nuno demands that his forwards defend from the front. As long as they press, compete and put in a shift for the team, he appears reasonably satisfied with their contribution.

I believe Nuno selected Pablo against Swansea because his industry and energy, combined with that of Mohamadou Kanté, gave West Ham the best chance of disrupting the Championship leaders’ passing game.

It was a tactical selection rather than definitive proof that Castellanos has fallen out of favour. Pablo carried out his instructions extremely well, helping West Ham press Swansea into the mistake which produced Divine Mukasa’s winning goal.

Castellanos is an unusual player in that he appeared perfectly capable of operating in the Premier League without necessarily looking more dominant in the Championship. He remains a useful forward, although striker would be the first position I would look to improve if West Ham secure promotion.

Even so, it is far too early for the Italian press to write him off or start raising questions about his future. There has been no indication that West Ham intend to sell him, while two games on the substitutes’ bench hardly amount to permanent exile.

I would not be remotely surprised to see Taty restored to the starting line-up against Southampton.
