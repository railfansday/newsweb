#1693. Bayern Munich 2-2 Arsenal: Gunners take huge step in the right direction, as Declan Rice earns point that means so much more in Mikel Arteta's bid to build dynasty

📅 26 Oct 2026 | other | Source

Published: 22:38 BST, 21 October 2026 | Updated: 23:06 BST, 21 October 2026

A draw in Bavaria will not stand out in the list of achievements carved out stubbornly by Mikel Arteta’s Arsenal as he has taken them from nowhere to somewhere over the last six years or so. But the Arsenal manager will know what this night means, nevertheless.

This is a season that must see Arsenal break fresh ground if they are to become the team we all feel they can be. They know they have questions to answer and they know what they are. They are about style and the ability to bring about repeat success. They are about building a domestic dynasty and winning things in Europe.

At times Arsenal look to be quite close to that but at other times – such as in winning so drearily with one shot on target at Nottingham Forest on Sunday – they look to be rather too far away.

So, yes, this felt like a big step in the right direction. An enterprising attacking performance – Arsenal could have scored four or five goals – against a team that hardly ever loses in its own stadium and then, a come from behind draw just when it seemed everything may be lost.

The feeling the Arsenal team will have had when celebrating Declan Rice’s late equaliser down beneath their own fans here will have been one that should propel them forwards.

Bayern are not used to teams doing that to them here and when the ball flew across Manuel Neuer and into the far corner you could feel the air being sucked out of this cavernous bowl and up into the night sky.

Declan Rice smashed a powerful low effort to make it 2-2 late on against Bayern Munich

Rice latched onto a through ball and his shot had too much for Manuel Neuer

Harry Kane had put Bayern Munich in front from the penalty spot in the second half

For Bayern had thrown their best at Arsenal here. Harry Kane had done so too. Arsenal had led midway through the first half through Eberechi Eze but as time wore on Bayern’s rapid counters threatened to become too much. Defeat beckoned.

Kane – as irrepressibly as inspirational as ever – had supplied a world class assist for Alphonso Davies in the first half and then, on the hour, a penalty of his own.

A loss here would have not been a disaster for Arsenal but it would have felt telling. It would have given their many critics further ammunition with which to do them down. Not good enough in Europe. Flat track bullies. Stuck in the mud. Not moving forwards.

So it was a good night for Arsenal if not a perfect one. An injury to Bukayo Saka, for example, was not on Arteta’s wish list. He was clipped from behind in the tenth minute and went down holding the back of his knee.

That was the end of his evening and the injury will be a worry. The only consolation to Arteta will have been that his replacement took his opportunity.

This has not been a stellar season for Eze but you would not have known it watching him drive a pass from Martin Odegaard across Manuel Neuer and in to the far corner with his left foot in the 25th minute. The supply from Odegaard was exemplary but it needed a finish to match.

At that point, Arsenal were worth the lead, even if Bayern had attacked on the counter to good effect.

Arsenal goalkeeper David Raya had to save sharply from Serge Gnabry after one such break while a lofted Kane pass a couple of minutes before Eze’s goal could have led to something had Ismael Saibari not applied such a clumsy touch.

Arsenal, meanwhile, had their own moments to ponder. Twice from corners they had opportunities at the far post but Riccardo Calafiori and Bruno Guimaraes couldn’t take them.

Alphonso Davies equalised for the German side with a tidy finish past David Raya

Eberechi Eze fired a composed effort past Manuel Neuer to break the deadlock in Munich

After a slow start, it had become a good game. Arsenal were purposeful and ambitious if a little too square across the back at times. Eventually, they were caught out.

Kane’s pass inside Jurrien Timber from just inside the Arsenal half could not have been more geometrically perfect had he used a set square and Davies ran on to open his body and beat Raya quite comfortably.

Kane’s goal – his 58th in the competition – was eventually to arrive on the hour and arrived after a spell of Bayern pressure. Before that, though, Arsenal had another couple of huge moments.

Almost immediately after half-time, Neuer produced a stunning save to push a Gabriel header up on to the crossbar from another corner. At the other end Raya denied Dayot Upamecano in similarly brilliant fashion.

It was an increasingly engaging game but one that Bayern were beginning to enjoy rather more. Their forays into the attacking third had become more regular and brought with them a greater menace.

Kane, for his part, was showcasing his full repertoire, spending as much time in deep position as he was through the middle.

There were concerning scenes for the Gunners when Saka limped off early on in the game

This was a draw worth far more than the solitary point for Mikel Arteta's side

But he was in the right place on the hour to meet a cross from Konrad Laimer and when his shot struck the sprawling Jurrien Timber on the arm a penalty was the only decision available. Kane took it and, after a little stutter, slotted it to Raya’s right with the keeper committed in the other direction.

Bayern almost sealed the game soon after only for Laimer’s right foot shot to miss Raya’s right hand post.

So Arsenal remained in it and – with Viktor Gyokeres on to change the shape of their attacks – they eventually took what they deserved. The move down the right side was slick and Rice’s finish – after a one-two with the big striker – was perfect, across Neuer with power.

The celebrations that followed were about much more than a single Champions League point.

#1681. Away Fan Guide: Arsenal

📅 26 Oct 2026 | other | Source

Website: Updates will be posted on Arsenal.com and on X accounts @Arsenal and @ArsenalMatchday

Everton Entrances: Away turnstiles are situated at the south east corner of the ground.

The turnstiles are fully accessible and step free access to the podium level is available via the South Bridge and two ramps on the West side of the stadium.

Once you are on podium level, there is level access to all turnstiles. Away supporters can access the stadium via turnstiles K, L, and M, with turnstile K and L having an accessible entrance.

To enter, you will need to scan your ticket on the reader next to your entrance. Stewards are on hand to assist.

Turnstiles will open 90 minutes before the kick off and Arsenal advise supporters to arrive as early as possible.

For any ticketing enquiries on matchday, the Box Office is situated next to the Armoury Store on the West side of the Stadium.

Everton Travel Stewards will be on hand to help supporters with any issues.  For updates from Merseyside Police, follow @MerPolEFC on X.

This fixture is sold out and supporters should not travel unless they have a valid match ticket.

The Match Day Box Office is located on Hornsey Road at the southern end of the stadium next to The Armoury Store. There is level access to the Box Office and there are also audio frequency induction hearing loops fitted for hearing impaired supporters.

The ticket collection point is located on podium level opposite turnstile block S. There is level access to the ticket collection point. Please note that the ticket collection point is for prearranged ticket and duplicate ticket collection only

Any persons under the age of 14 must be accompanied by a responsible adult.

Supporters in the visitors’ section will receive a digital ticket.

A summary of your travel options is set out below, or you can plan your best route by public transport using the TFL  Journey Planner.

A full guide on travelling to The Emirates Stadium can be found HERE.

Arsenal Tube Station, on the Piccadilly Line, is the nearest to the stadium. Alternatives are Highbury & Islington on the Victoria Line, which is around a 15-minute walk and Finsbury Park which is a 15/20-minute walk to the ground

King's Cross is the main connecting station for National Rail and many London Underground lines. From here you can travel to the ground via the Piccadilly line (to Arsenal) or the Victoria line (to Highbury & Islington). King’s Cross station is wheelchair accessible.

Overground: alternatively, a short-overground rail journey of one stop will take you to Finsbury Park station.

Please note: Holloway Road station will be exit-only before matches and closed for up to 2 hours after the match. Pre-match eastbound trains are non-stopping. Drayton Park is also closed on matchdays.

The main bus stops close to Emirates Stadium are located on Holloway Road, Nags Head, Seven Sisters Road, Blackstock Road and Highbury Corner. High-frequency services will take you within a five-to-10-minute walk of the ground and direct routes cover much of north and central London.

Several bus routes pass close to the stadium including the 271 which goes from Highgate Village to Liverpool Street Station, the 43 which goes from Friern Barnet to London Bridge Station, and the 19 which goes from Finsbury Park to Battersea Bridge.

London buses are low floor vehicles (this means they can be lowered to pavement level when the bus stops and the doors open). On most buses the wheelchair ramp is positioned at the exit doors. There is enough room for one wheelchair user on each bus; supporters should be aware that buses can get very congested on matchdays.

Further information on buses is available through www.tfl.gov.uk or consult the maps of local bus routes below:

For information on taxis and minicabs in London click here.

In London, all black cabs are wheelchair accessible. Some of the newer ‘black cabs’ are also fitted with induction loops and intercoms for hearing aid users.

There is no designated drop off/pick up point outside Emirates Stadium. We suggest Drayton Park by the Danny Fiszmann Bridge, which gives step-free access to the south-east side of the Stadium.

Please note that due to matchday road closures there may be wait times up to an hour after a game to gain access for pick up.

Supporters are strongly advised not to drive to Emirates Stadium. The ground is situated in a mainly residential area with an extensive Event Day Parking Scheme in operation. Only car owners with resident's permits will be allowed to park on-street in the designated areas and any cars parked illegally will be towed away.

Arsenal has six accessible parking spaces available for away supporters on a match-by-match basis. Parking can be booked via your own clubs Disability Liaison Officer.

If you are travelling by coach, you will be parked on Queensland Road. Once leaving the coach, you will need to head towards Benwell Road and take a right towards the stadium. There is a ramp, with handrails, which leads directly onto podium level and towards the away end.

Please see below the current price guide for catering in the stadium

CASHLESS STADIUM: Emirates is a cashless stadium. All bars, restaurants, kiosks, and retail outlets accept card payment only.

Security at Emirates Stadium is at a high level. Outside the turnstiles stewards will search by way of a full pat down body search.

We encourage supporters not to bring a bag and only essential items. Maximum bag size is A4. All bags are searched and tagged prior to entry.

There are no ‘left luggage’ facilities at Emirates Stadium. The nearest left luggage facilities are at Kings Cross station.

Emirates Stadium is a no smoking stadium. This includes e-cigarettes. Anyone found smoking will be ejected from the ground. For anyone considering leaving the stadium, note that no re-admission to the ground is permitted.

A comprehensive and downloadable Access Guide is available HERE.

The Disability Liaison Team are identifiable in purple bibs and are on hand to assist our disabled visitors.

For accessibility advice ahead of the fixture, contact the Disability Access Officer, Aaron Heskins, at aheskins@arsenal.co.uk

Wheelchair viewing: There are three accessible viewing areas located at the back of Lower Tier for wheelchair users, in blocks 20, 21, and 22. The areas are all accessed via ramps (stewards are on hand to assist, if required)

Easy Access and Ambulant Disabled Seating: Seating for Easy Access and Ambulant Disabled supporters is generally located in the rows immediately in front of the Lower Tier wheelchair accessible viewing areas, as this means that supporters have level access from the concourse and one or two steps down to their seats.

Catering & Low counters: Available across the stadium at all larger counters.

Audio Descriptive commentary: Alan March Sport provide Audio Descriptive Commentary for blind and partially sighted supporters. If you require this, please notify your clubs Disability Liaison Officer with your seat details, and one of our team will deliver your receiver to your seat around 30 minutes before kick-off.

Audio Matchday Programme: Arsenal provide blind and partially sighted supporters with an audio version of all home match day programmes in the form of a podcast, which supporters can be downloaded HERE a few hours before kick-off.

Accessible toilets: There are two unisex accessible toilets located inside the away end of the stadium. All are fitted with RADAR locks and all supervising stewards hold RADAR keys in case a supporter with an access requirement is not in possession of a key.

Changing Places: The Changing Places Facility is located in Block 48 on Club Level. It is available to any supporter with access requirements in the stadium on a match day or a non-match day and it is fitted with a RADAR lock. If you would like to use this facility on a matchday, please contact your club’s Disability Liaison or Access Officer, prior to attending the stadium, so that we can ensure you are provided with the full information before attending.

Assistance Animals: If you require an assistance animal with you at Emirates Stadium, please contact us on 0207 619 5000 (Option 2)

Subject to space, flags and banners are allowed in the away end. These will need to be fire certificated. For questions relating to this contact Mark Brindle on mbrindle@arsenal.co.uk

Cash payments will be accepted for programmes, which are available, priced at £4 each, from the many sellers around the ground.

Supporters who need to bring prohibited items into the stadium due to medical conditions should contact leanne.jensen@wearehullcity.co.uk.

Everton has a zero-tolerance approach to discriminatory behaviour or language at matches and while travelling to and from fixtures.

The Club’s All Together Now campaign promotes equality, diversity and a football family for everyone.

Discriminatory language, including chanting, and anti-social behaviour is not tolerated. Report incidents to a steward or text "Foul" to 67777 with a description. Include all relevant information such as seat details.

Everton supporters can also report discrimination confidentially at: reportit@evertonfc.com

Everton does not condone pyrotechnics under any circumstances.

Possession or use may result in Club sanctions, arrest, criminal conviction and a football banning order.

Entering the playing surface without permission is a criminal offence.

Those responsible risk Club sanctions, football bans and criminal consequences which can affect employment, education and international travel.

Please consider fellow Evertonians, particularly on concourses.

Behaviour such as throwing beer may result in ejection. More serious incidents may result in arrest and stadium bans.

Everton works with the relevant authorities to identify those responsible for unacceptable behaviour. CCTV can be used as evidence after an incident, even where an individual was not detained on matchday.

Please enjoy your matchday and stay safe.
