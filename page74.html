Samir Nasri has spoken out against the appointment of one of the favourites to succeed Michael Carrick, as the pressure builds on the Manchester United manager.

Manchester United are in a dreadful run of seven consecutive games without a win. Already knocked out of the EFL Cup, 13th in the Premier League and 20th in the Champions League league phase, the Red Devils are having a difficult start to the season. Carrick is therefore under more pressure than ever.

Three managers have been mentioned as possible successors to the Englishman: Antonio Conte, Julian Nagelsmann and Eddie Howe.

These managers have the advantage of being available. It should be noted that the names of Unai Emery and Fabian Hurzeler, who are currently in charge of Aston Villa and Brighton respectively, have also been mentioned.

Speaking on Canal Champions Club, Nasri gave his view on the Red Devils’ shortlist and expressed doubts about one option. ‘Nagelsmann or Conte, given how the squad is built, ouch... It is not the right marriage. It is nonsense, you appointed Michael Carrick, and if it is to sack him at the start of the season...’ - the exasperated pundit said.

The former France international would not understand the choice of Antonio Conte at all.

‘They already took a long time to recover from the sacking of Ruben Amorim, who built a squad in a 3-4-3. You are going to take over, for example, a Conte who plays with three at the back: you have to rebuild, reshape the squad. I think they should stick with Carrick or someone else who knows the league well, an Englishman’ - he said.

On this shortlist, one name stands out with those characteristics: Eddie Howe. The former Newcastle United manager (2021-2026) knows the Premier League perfectly and prefers a 4-3-3 system. He is also the reported favourite of Jason Wilcox, United’s technical director, should Carrick be sacked.

Liverpool head coach Andoni Iraola has delivered injury updates on Bradley Barcola and Victor Munoz ahead of Sunday's Premier League showdown with Brighton & Hove Albion.

The attackers were among seven Liverpool players missing from team training earlier this week, when Barcola was restricted to individual practice as a precautionary measure.

The Frenchman had completed the full 90 minutes of the 2-1 win over Brentford last weekend, before coming on for the final 16 minutes of Tuesday's 4-3 Champions League win over Villarreal.

Meanwhile, Munoz was missing from the squad entirely in midweek owing to a reported knee injury, albeit not one that has caused too much alarm at Kirkby.

Nevertheless, Iraola admitted to reporters that it would be "difficult" for Munoz to be involved against Brighton, although he delivered the good news that Barcola is fit and available.

"Bradley has trained normally, he's fine, no problem," Iraola said, explaining the winger's absence from training in midweek. "Sometimes you have to deal with the loads and stay inside.

"Victor, he has a knock he received in the first half against Brentford. He's dealing with some pain since then. Villarreal was too early and I don't know. I think it's going to be difficult, but I really don't know."

Should Munoz sit out the visit of the Seagulls, the Spaniard will be one of six Liverpool players to take up a spot in the treatment room, a list that includes Dominik Szoboszlai and Jeremie Frimpong.

The latter two were both forced off at the Gtech Community Stadium last weekend, Szoboszlai after exacerbating an existing hamstring problem and Frimpong with a new thigh injury.

Szoboszlai's issue may heal in time for him to face Arsenal on November 1, but Frimpong is expected to remain sidelined until after the November international break.

Liverpool are also without long-term absentees Hugo Ekitike (Achilles), Conor Bradley (knee) and Giovanni Leoni (knee), the latter of whom recently suffered a setback that could keep him absent until 2027.

Munoz made the most of his fifth Premier League start of the season last weekend, driving home a fine equaliser against Brentford despite already carrying the knock that ruled him out of the Villarreal contest.

Without the Spaniard in the squad in midweek, Iraola deployed Rio Ngumoha and Cody Gakpo out wide, and both repaid Iraola's faith by setting up three of Liverpool's four goals between them.

While Gakpo laid on one assist, Ngumoha stole the show with two helpers and a sensational nine dribbles from 10 attempts, joining an elite crowd in the process.

The 18-year-old became the third-youngest player to set up more than one goal in a Champions League game after Warren Zaire-Emery and Theo Walcott, at a time when Liverpool are trying to tie him down to a new contract.

Despite Gakpo's seniority and own positive performance against Villarreal, Ngumoha has surely done enough to join Florian Wirtz, Barcola and Alexander Isak in the Liverpool attack to face Brighton.

#2182. Michael Carrick Manchester United future: Why Bruno Fernandes role must change for Englishman to save Old Trafford job

📅 26 Oct 2026 | other | Source

By Darren Plant | 23 Oct 2026 14:01

Bruno Fernandes's criticism of Manchester United manager Michael Carrick earlier this week was always going to attract scrutiny. Having been part of a team that was comprehensively outplayed by Como in the Champions League, trying to "out" the Englishman was never going to sit well.

That all felt fair game. With Fernandes being deployed as part of what was described as a "half press", Como ran rings around the Portuguese who, on more than one occasion, unsuccessfully chased down several passes before displaying the body language which suggested that Como were too fast and too sharp for him.

Carrick was quick to fire back at Fernandes offering a public critique of his strategy against Cesc Fabregas's exceptional outfit, and it was the response that you would expect from someone who spent much of his career playing under Sir Alex Ferguson.

He also started Friday's press conference, ahead of the Premier League game with Bournemouth, with a statement on Man United's recent struggles.

However, the question has barely been raised over whether Carrick should take the brunt of the blame for Fernandes becoming increasingly less influential in a Man United shirt.

There could be reasons for that. Fernandes's never-ending gesticulating towards officials is a permanent eye sore in Man United matches, plus why would Carrick overly-tinker with the role of a player who delivered 30 goals/assists in 2025-26 and won the Premier League Player of the Season award?

But while Fernandes is the easy target, Carrick may have lost sight of the fact that the club captain is reaching a crossroads in his career where his role in this Man United team must change.

The Premier League has evolved in recent seasons. This has become the era where physicality and pressing is as important as resilience and skill.

As per Stats Muse, Fernandes - who is now 32 years of age - is third in the most minutes (16,256) accumulated by outfield players in the Premier League since 2021-22, only behind central defenders James Tarkowski and Virgil van Vijk.

Arsenal's Declan Rice is marginally behind him, but Fulham's Alex Iwobi is the next midfielder in the list with 13,729 minutes. That is a difference of 2,527 minutes, the equivalent of 28 matches.

Furthermore, Fernandes has accumulated over 3,000 minutes in each of the last six Premier League campaigns. He also completed the 90 minutes in all 17 of Man United's Premier League games under Carrick in 2025-26.

As per Fotmob, Fernandes made 366 sprints in last season's Premier League, the sixth-most by an attacking midfielder. In terms of distance covered, he was third with 383.2km, only behind the much younger Morgan Rogers and Enzo Fernandez.

Meanwhile, Fernandes was 14th for distance covered by any player in the Premier League in 2025-26. Bernardo Silva, who was playing in a more possession-based side, is the only player of the 13 above him who was older.

Whether it is concluding that Fernandes's comments stem from an attitude problem, or describing it as an "absolute disgrace", or Wayne Rooney calling Fernandes a "baby". They all make headlines without identifying that Fernandes may simply be absolutely exhausted from the overwhelming resonsibility that is placed in his shoulders at Old Trafford, both physically and mentally.

How Carrick uses Fernandes has become a major problem. An argument could be made for taking him out of the team against Bournemouth on Sunday. Partly for a rest, but also due to having contributed no goals or assists in five successive Premier League games.

Realistically, that will not happen. Carrick, fighting to keep his job, cannot afford to drop Fernandes, nor should he be using him on the left, like he did against Leeds United last weekend.

A strength of United is the pace of their attackers. For all the talk of Matheus Cunha's questionable work-rate, Marcus Rashford's ineffective pressing and the absence of Benjamin Sesko, Carrick still has enough options in attack where defences can be exploited through the speed of the aforementioned players, Amad Diallo and Bryan Mbeumo.

The only suitable place for Fernandes in this Man United team is a deeper midfield position in a three, alongside Carlos Baleba and one of Kobbie Mainoo, Andrey Santos and Youri Tielemans.

For all the qualities that Tielemans brings, and showed with his assist against Leeds, you can argue that his lack of speed is detrimental to a Man United team with Fernandes in it. Tielemans is making as many sprints per game (7.9 to 8.1) as Fernandes, emphasising that it is not a lack of effort. They just cannot be used effectively in the same team.

Carrick is in a position, not helped by the club's recruitment, where it makes sense for the younger, more combative Mainoo or Santos to take over Fernandes's pressing responsibilities and allow him to sit deep, or roam. Neither player are number 10s, but they can act as the key to unlock a return to form for Fernandes.

That comes with its own restraints, but Santos, for example, was in the top five for tackles and interceptions during his time at Strasbourg in 2024-25. Carrick is yet to make use of those qualities, or the Brazilian simply has not had a chance to show it because of the failings of the press in front of him.

While Carrick is essentially in a position where he has nothing to lose, he also has to accept his own weaknesses. When he was Middlesbrough manager, there was a running joke, or prediction, among sections of the fanbase where they were confident that they could predict his substitutions and felt that it contributed to an initial quick start descending into a Riverside Stadium stint that did not fulfil its potential.

If he is not prepared to change his approach after Man United's worst start since 1986, when is he ever going to?

Man United have had an over-reliance on Fernandes for several years. Carrick reaped the benefits from it last season and he still can.  But gone are the days where a 32-year-old can play 90 minutes every Premier League game and be expected to press an increasing amount of teams who play out from the back.

Not only is it a waste of Fernandes's qualities, it is a waste of everyone's time.
