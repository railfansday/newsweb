#1178. Klopp wins first game as Germany coach, without 'top dogs'

📅 26 Oct 2026 | other | Source

It may have taken a little longer than he would have liked, but Jürgen Klopp has finally won a game as Germany coach. Despite a number of debutants, it was a familiar face who stood out in the victory over Serbia.

After 47 days, three matches and 44 squad players, Jürgen Klopp has a first win as coach of Germany's men's fotball team. A straightforward 2-0 victory over Serbia in the Nations League on Thursday meant the former Liverpool and Borussia Dortmund coach avoided becoming the first Germany boss to win any of his first three games.

"I'm really happy today," Klopp told German public broadcaster ZDF. "We were outstanding when defending and when we had the ball. We simply played wonderful football."

Klopp had raised a few eyebrows by naming two squads for the four fixtures in this extended international window. But after a somewhat fortunate draw against the Netherlands and a shock loss to Greece with the more senior of the squads, it was left to those lower down the pecking order to get things going Thursday in Munich.

That meant a completely new starting XI and debuts to ‌goalkeeper Jonas Urbig ⁠and ⁠winger Derry Scherhant. Such players should "develop without the top dogs" Klopp said before the game. And while Urbig was untroubled by Serbia, his Bayern Munich teammate Tom Bischof made his mark by turning in the opener after Florian Wirtz, one of the sprinkling of senior players, hit the post. It was Bischof's first goal for Germany in only his second national team game.

"It's indescribable," said Bischof after the game at Bayern's Allianz Arena. "The stadium was on fire. I let it all out and was overjoyed." Unfortunately, he said, "my knees hurt a little from sliding on my knees. But that doesn't matter."

After a string of tournament failures, the latest new dawn for Germany comes with a little more patience. As well as Klopp's track record, there is a general acceptance from inside the country and abroad that Germany are no longer a top tier side.

"We're not the Bayern Munich of European football," Klopp said prematch. "We're a team that needs to find its footing."

While one win against a limited Serbia side will not change that, the emphatic nature of the performance impressed the new boss.

"I liked everything — the opening phase, the middle section, and the end. Our counter-pressing was off the charts," said the man whose Gegenpressing (Counter-pressing) tactic became his trademark at Dortmund. "We can afford to take all the liberties and risks we want when we win the ball back together. Everyone was there during our pressing moments."

The second half brought more debuts for players who may not have made a traditional 26-man squad. Cologne winger Said El Mala and Brentford's Vitaly Janelt made their Germany bows while Celtic's Mika Baur became the country's 1,000th men's international.

It was "surreal," Baur said. "Today was a highlight for everyone, not just for me with my debut."

While Klopp may have seen enough from a few of the second string to afford them opportunities in the future, the performance of Liverpool midfielder Wirtz, who scored Germany's second goal, may be the most critical of the night.

Wirtz, 23, has struggled to find his best form in the Premier League and has blown hot and cold in his 47 caps for Germany. A cool and composed finish suggested that the player who was so highly regarded at Bayer Leverkusen can be coaxed to life by Klopp.

"It was loads of fun. Every single one of us gave it our all on the field. We stuck to the game plan and did almost everything right," said Wirtz, who took the captain's armband later in the game. "The players around me made it easy for me. We had a lot of movement in the middle. We were all fired up."

The challenge now for Klopp is to work out which of his wide pool of players are the ones he can rely on to take Germany to Euro 2028 and beyond. The next step is a chance for revenge against Greece in Thessaloniki on Sunday.

Bayern Munich coach Vincent Kompany was hospitalized after collapsing on Friday. The Belgian coach collapsed following a training session and reportedly required emergency transport. "Vincent Kompany had to seek medical treatment on Friday after collapsing during training for FC Bayern's second team," the statement said. Kompany also missed sessions on Monday and Tuesday ahead of the club's Champions League fixture against Arsenal, which were overseen by assistant coach Aaron Danks. The 40-year-old contracted a viral infection last week and skipped post-match interviews after Bayern's 6-1 victory over RB Leipzig, though he still managed the match from the sidelines. Danks confirmed in a pre-game news conference that the virus had affected Kompany's legs, with the manager feeling pain in them. It's not known if that same virus has any relation to Friday's collapse.

It's not the first medical emergency that Bayern have dealt with this season as midfielder Jamal Musiala had also collapsed due to microseizures related to a neurological dysfunction. During preseason, he went down off the ball after playing five minutes before needing to receive medical treatment.

Next up, Bayern Munich will face Freiburg in the Bundesliga on Sunday. Currently undefeated to start the season, they are level atop the league table with Borussia Dortmund while sitting 14th in the Champions League standings.

#1168. Turkey: Stock market scandal hits half a million investors

📅 26 Oct 2026 | other | Source

Asset managers are alleged to have artificially inflated the value of their funds. Turkish authorities have made multiple arrests, and a leading AKP lawmaker has resigned.

Investors in Turkey want one thing above all else: for their money to retain its value. Given inflation of around 30%, that is not exactly easy. As a result, many people are buying gold, foreign currency or stocks and increasingly, mutual fund shares that promise high returns.

But the stock market has become a trap for around half a million Turkish investors. In mid-September, the benchmark BIST 100 index plummeted by 6% in a single day and lost about 12% within just a few trading days.

This sharp drop in stock prices was triggered by a major market manipulation scandal. Approximately 455,000 investors have been affected, more than 45 people have been arrested and a high-ranking politician from the ruling Justice and Development Party (AKP) has resigned.

Everything started when some investment fund providers had trouble making timely repayments to investors. Among those affected were funds managed by the companies Pusula Portfoy and Tera. Two Tera funds alone held assets totaling around €6.5 billion ($6.37 billion).

In response, the Capital Markets Board (SPK) intervened and shut down the funds of seven asset managers, including Tera, Pusula and Atlas. According to the state-run news agency Anadolu, a total of 131 funds are set to be liquidated.

The Istanbul Public Prosecutor's Office is now investigating charges of fraud, violations of the capital markets law and the formation of a criminal organization, Justice Minister Akin Gurlek announced.

The suspicion: The fund companies are alleged to have artificially inflated the value of their funds, while in reality their funds contained worthless "junk stocks."

Among the 45 people arrested so far are executives from investment firms. In addition, the assets of 42 individuals and 46 legal entities have been frozen, according to Gurlek on X.

The Istanbul Stock Exchange has also taken action: it excluded 27 stocks from the BIST 100 benchmark index as of October 1.

The funds are being liquidated: Their assets will be sold and the proceeds distributed to investors in proportion to their fund shares. The deadline for this has been extended from three to six months. According to Aysel Gundogdu, an independent financial advisor in Istanbul, this is intended to reduce selling pressure so that stocks are not sold at fire-sale prices. However, this does not mean that the liquidation of each fund will take six months. Depending on market conditions, it may be completed more quickly.

Only, there is no guarantee. An investment fund is not a savings account and any loss in value affects the investor. Gundogdu expects that money market funds will largely be redeemed. With hedge funds and equity funds holding "junk stocks" that are virtually untradeable, however, the situation could prove more difficult.

It is important to distinguish between market losses and damages resulting from potential irregularities.

Economist and columnist Guldem Atabay emphasized that a distinction must be made between price losses and manipulation: Investors bear the market risk, but they do not have to accept false information, artificially inflated prices, or a lack of oversight.

If funds are closed or liquidated, the shares and other securities in the portfolio can be sold. The question is how much they are worth and how easily they can be sold.

Financial expert Gundogdu believes that the fund's assets will not cover investors' claims. Furthermore, she says there have been structural failures. "The crisis is far greater than a simple liquidity crisis. There are flaws in management and oversight."

She therefore considers it possible that the assets of the fund founders and managers could also be tapped to make up for the shortfall.

Economist Atabay calls for those responsible to be identified and for the damages to be recovered from them. Following the liquidation of the funds, it must be clarified who is responsible for any legal violations. "Liquidation is no substitute for accountability," Atabay said.

The crisis has already led to the resignation of a high-ranking female politician in the ruling AKP party. Fatma Betul Sayan Kaya, deputy party chair and former minister of family affairs, asked Turkish President and AKP leader Recep Tayyip Erdogan to "relieve her of all her posts." This has been a phrase frequently used within the AKP for years when someone is forced to resign.

She was facing allegations regarding stock transactions by her and her husband, Ilyas Kaya, in connection with the fund crisis. Political responsibility, she wrote on social media, goes beyond legal responsibility. She said her position should not hinder the investigation.

The opposition now intends to bring the issue before Parliament. Selcuk Ozdag, deputy leader of the Yeni Yol parliamentary group, announced that as soon as Parliament reconvenes, he will submit a motion for a parliamentary investigation. The investigation is intended to determine whether politicians are involved in the manipulation scandal.

📅 26 Oct 2026 | other | Source

Nature (2026) Cite this article

Magnetic tunnel junctions based on ferromagnets are canonical devices in spintronics, with wide-ranging applications in data storage, computing and sensing. They simultaneously exhibit mechanisms for electrical detection and control of magnetic order through the tunnelling magnetoresistance (TMR)1,2,3,4,5 and spin-transfer torque6,7,8,9 effects, respectively. It was long assumed that neither of these effects could be sizeable in all-antiferromagnetic tunnel junctions (AATJs), considering that they have no net magnetization. However, it was recently shown that AATJs based on chiral antiferromagnets do exhibit TMR due to their non-relativistic momentum-dependent spin polarization and cluster magnetic octupole moment, which are manifestations of their spin–split band structure10,11,12,13. However, the reciprocal effect, that is, the antiferromagnetic counterpart of spin–transfer torque, has been assumed non-existent due to the total electric current being spin-neutral. Here we report nanoscale AATJs that display this reciprocal effect, which we term octupole-driven spin-transfer torque (OTT). We demonstrate current-induced OTT switching of PtMn3|MgO|PtMn3 AATJs, with a TMR value of 363% at room temperature and switching current densities of the order of 10 MA cm–2. Our theoretical modelling explains the origin of OTT in terms of the imbalance between intra- and inter-sublattice spin currents across the AATJ, and, equivalently, in terms of the non-zero net cluster octupole polarization of each PtMn3 layer. This work establishes a new materials platform for antiferromagnetic spintronics and provides a pathway towards deeply scaled magnetic memory and room-temperature terahertz technologies.
