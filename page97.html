Asked about countering the threat of the 34-year-old goal machine at his team’s

eve-of-match press conference at Papara Park, Vrancken said: ‘For sure, you have to make it a team effort.

‘You cannot focus only on Salah. He is not the only one with a lot of qualities.

Mohamed Salah has been in prolific form for Trabzonspor since signing from Liverpool

‘But at the same time, we have to also believe in the things we are doing. Like in the example from Benfica, they had also a lot of quality players who can decide a game.

‘We did that also with a team environment and team effort (in a 1-1 draw at Tynecastle in August). That will be necessary here.’

Trabzonspor are fourth in the Turkish Super Lig and have won each of their last four matches in all competitions, scoring 13 goals in the process.

Vrancken, whose side drew 1-1 in their Conference League opener at home to Nordsjaelland last week, is braced for an opponent of a higher standard than anything Hearts will encounter domestically.

‘Yeah, I think the quality of their team is European top level,’ he said. ‘You see that in their results now.

‘They beat Galatasaray 4-0, without any problems. It will be very, very difficult.’

Hearts boss Wouter Vrancken knows his side will have to be vigilant against Trabzonspor

‘I have to say, I’ve never experienced in my career injuries all in the same position,’ he said, alluding to the fact he is now without four of his preferred central-midfield options in Blair Spittal, Tom Renaud, Ageu and McEntee. ‘But at the same time we have to cope with it. I’m trusting the guys who will play tomorrow and who will take these positions. They have our full support there.’

📅 26 Oct 2026 | other | Source

Michael Carrick is at risk of the sack at Manchester United and the usual names are being bandied about as potential replacements.

Eddie Howe? Antonio Conte? Julian Nagelsmann? They’re all available and better managers on paper than who United currently have. But would going for one of them just be too obvious?

We’ve taken a look at some more leftfield suggestions for who could replace Carrick if the axe falls.

Bit of a funny one, Motta. Reputations can change quickly at the top level of management.

Once a source of minor ridicule for his bold idea of a 2-7-2 formation, Motta worked wonders at Bologna to get them into the Champions League, taking his stock higher than ever.

But rather than lead Bologna in that European campaign, he made the step up to Juventus. It was a disaster.

Motta was sacked by the March of his first season with Juve and has been out of work for over a year and a half since.

But while his time in Turin may have been a misstep, Motta had too bright a future as a manager to just disappear from the touchline.

Bringing his ideas into a new league could be what he needs to get back on track. It’d be a gamble, no doubt, but sometimes you need someone who thinks outside the box.

Now this wouldn’t go down too well over at Leeds, but that’s not really Man Utd’s problem.

One thing’s for sure, Bielsa would get United running – and that’s what they need right now.

His three-year Uruguay reign ended after their World Cup exit in the summer. At 71, he’s earned the right to put his feet up. But he’s the kind of manager who always has that itch to contribute to the game in some way.

And he had the Sir Alex seal of approval back in his Athletic Club days.

Like Motta, Conceicao is a coach needing to get back on track.

The 51-year-old enjoyed a seven-year reign with Porto, winning three league titles, but he only lasted half a year in his next job at AC Milan and failed to even get them into Europe.

A venture into the Saudi Pro League followed, which normally doesn’t bode too well for a future back among the European elite.

Conceicao left Al-Ittihad at the end of last season and is waiting for his next opportunity.

Favouring a high-pressing approach, Conceicao could bring some intensity to United. He’s also previously expressed an admiration for at least one of their players, when his Milan side were linked with Marcus Rashford in 2025.

There was a time when United chiefs seemed obsessed by the idea of bringing Gareth Southgate back into club management after his England exploits.

But Southgate has pretty much ruled out ever returning to a dugout at all. United might have been too big a gig for him anyway, but that does feel like a shame.

Not to worry, INEOS. Want someone else who fits the bill of being in charge for one of FIFA’s highest-ranked countries for a long time but hasn’t tested himself with a club for ages? Step forward Deschamps.

The architect of France’s World Cup win in 2018, Deschamps made way for Zinedine Zidane after the 2026 edition.

Somehow still in his fifties, there’s plenty of time for the former Monaco, Juventus and Marseille manager to take on a new challenge if he fancies it.

“I’m available, and everybody knows it,” he vowed in May. “I won’t retire after the World Cup.”

He managed some big egos during his time in charge of France and consistently got them to go far in tournaments.

The parallels between Carrick’s struggles after an impressive interim reign and Solskjaer’s are obvious.

United projects post-Fergie just never seem to properly take off. But, ignoring Ralf Rangnick, they’ve had some good fortunes with interim managers.

What if they just bring back Solskjaer for a 2019-esque boost and dispense of him when all’s said and done? Then switch back and forth to caretaker Carrick ad infinitum?

We’re only half joking. What could possibly go wrong?

To stand any chance of qualifying again for the knockout round, Martin O’Neill’s men will need to get off the mark against Celta Vigo tonight.

Celtic’s recent results in Europe are the main bone of contention for many supporters - with the Green Brigade boycotting the clash with the Spaniards as part of an ongoing protest against the running of the club.

With the 60th anniversary of the Lisbon Lions arriving in May, assistant manager Maloney feels it’s imperative that the current players recognise the footsteps in which they tread each time they enter the continental arena.

Celtic players trudge off after being soundly beaten 4-1 by Benfica in Lisbon

‘Our reputation in Europe is really important,’ he said.

‘We felt that last year. There was a moment before the Feyenoord game when we weren’t sure whether we were going to pick our strongest side to win that game.

‘We had some really important games coming up.

‘We were at the point where we’d had a really difficult start.

‘But the European games are so important to the club and the history of the club that you have to go for it.

‘This is now a really, really important game.

‘Looking at our away games, people have already paid and booked up to follow the team so we have to try our best to have an impact on the European scene.

‘That is ingrained in you here. It’s hard, but we have to try.’

Celtic beat Celta on away goals 24 years back - a result which opened the door to O’Neill’s team of that time reaching the Uefa Cup final in Seville.

Parkhead No2 Maloney wants to see pride as the team tries to salvage their Euro campaign

Asked if it was realistic for Celtic fans to feel they could see their side go as deep in European tournaments again, Maloney added: ‘It’s difficult for me to say we are close after being beaten by Benfica and Ferencvaros.

‘As a team we have a lot of work to do to beat Celta Vigo.

‘But there have been teams throughout Europe even without the biggest budgets who have shown they can have deep runs in Europe.

‘That should be what we are aspiring to do. To do that we have to get everything right. Obviously us as coaches, recruitment, all departments of the club have to be bang on to get those runs.

‘We’re obviously not there now but that is what we should aspire to.

‘Even with our budget in terms of the European stage, we should be trying to get as far in European competition as we possibly can.

‘Recently clubs with a similar budget have done it so that has to be our motivation.’

A large section of the support remains convinced that the club’s hierarchy doesn’t share their ambitions to see Celtic reach the heights they previously did under O’Neill.

Maloney was asked if he believed the club had the same level of ambition as the fanbase.

'I hope so,’ he said. ‘I haven’t heard anyone saying anything different. In fairness, I don’t speak to the board very often, that’s for the boss.

'But I hope so. Every supporter is desperate for that. They are desperate to get back to the Champions League, which will be hard as we have to win our own domestic league first.

'But it’s something I know the fans are desperate for and while we are in the Europa League, how deep can we go?’

Maloney said he respected the stance of any supporter who stays away tonight while remaining hopeful that full houses will become routine once again.
