The history of World Series MVPs is littered with legends: Yoshinobu Yamamoto in 2025, Stephen Strasburg in 2019, Manny Ramirez in 2004 and as far back as Don Larsen in 1956. Bob Gibson won after throwing three complete-game shutouts in 1967. Reggie Jackson hit five home runs in 1977, including three in Game 6. David Freese hit the game-tying triple in the ninth and the walk-off home run in the 11th in Game 6 in 2011.

This year, the Fall Classic's hero will come from either the Milwaukee Brewers or the Tampa Bay Rays, two franchises vying for their first World Series title. The names are just as talented, though perhaps not as famous: aces like Jacob Misiorowski and Drew Rasmussen, sluggers like Jackson Chourio and Junior Caminero. Will one of them join the vaunted list? Or will someone else step up and shine under the brightest lights of October?

Here are your CBS Sports MLB experts' predictions for World Series MVP, plus odds.

Mike Axisa: Aaron Ashby had a case for NLCS MVP. He threw 6 ⅔ scoreless innings in three appearances during the four-game sweep of the Dodgers. Brewers manager Pat Murphy leans on Ashby so much -- SO MUCH -- that I can see him throwing something like nine innings in the World Series, which would be an enormous workload for a reliever. Ashby is so good and he's going to be asked to get the biggest outs of the game multiple times in the series. A reliever has not been named World Series MVP since Mariano Rivera in 1999. The stars are aligned for Ashby to end that drought this year.

Kate Feldman: If the Rays are going to win this thing, they're going to do it on the back of Junior Caminero and, well, that's usually the guy who wins MVP. The 23-year-old star is not just one of the most fun players to watch in MLB -- he's also one of the best. He's hitting just .273/.351/.455 in the playoffs this year, but he heated up in the back end of the ALCS: 4 for 12 with a double, a home run and 3 RBI. Julian McWilliams: There was a legitimate argument to be made that William Contreras deserved NLCS MVP honors, but who cares about that now? Contreras has been on an absolute tear at the plate this postseason, batting .367 with three home runs, five RBIs and a 1.157 OPS. He's been the heartbeat of Milwaukee's lineup, and I don't see him slowing down now.

I'll say that changes in the World Series and that he shoves in two starts against the Rays. Yes, the Rays are much better against right-handers like Misiorowski, but I think the downtime -- assuming he starts Game 1 on Friday, he'll be coming off 11 days of rest between starts -- will be the refresher he needs, and I think we'll see him in something much closer to vintage form in this series.

#3358. Victor Wembanyama on his dismal opening night performance vs. OKC: 'This is not the sport I like right here'

The San Antonio Spurs got off to a disappointing start to their 2026-27 season in a 116-104 loss at home to their hated rivals, the Oklahoma City Thunder.

While there was plenty of blame to go around for a lackluster showing from last year's Western Conference champions, Victor Wembanyama's brutal offensive effort stood out. Wembanyama shot just 1 of 13 from the field, looking uncomfortable and at times lost as the Spurs try to operate a new lineup with their three star guards.

Wembanyama finished the game with 12 points, 13 rebounds, six blocks, three assists and a steal, but for a player expected to take the title of Best in the World this season, he looked far from it compared to the current owner of that crown across the way, Shai Gilgeous-Alexander.

After the game, Wembanyama offered his thoughts on his performance and thought there was a simple explanation for his struggles.

"I feel like it's obvious for me. I need to go back to playing the basketball I like. This is not the sport I like right here," he said. "I got no reason to be hesitant, no reason to not be myself. For me, it's as simple as that. I'm good when I have fun."

Wembanyama has not been shy about his disdain for the Thunder and, in particular, Chet Holmgren. However, while that channeled its way into something incredible last year, in this year's opener, Wembanyama seemed to be pressing, desperate to make another statement rather than simply playing basketball. On offense, he was out of control and on defense, even as he piled up six blocks, there were times when he ignored responsibilities to chase blocks -- especially when Holmgren had the ball.

After a humbling opener, it's back to the drawing board for Wembanyama and the young Spurs, where the answer may run counter to human nature.

"I might be overthinking it, and I feel like it's a chance for me that what I have to do is really let go," Wembanyama said. "I don't need to do more. I need to think less, probably."

As a team, the Spurs could probably all use to adopt that mindset. The only starter who shot better than 50% from the field on Tuesday night was De'Aaron Fox (6 of 9) as the Thunder sagged off Stephon Castle and Dylan Harper and clogged the paint. The young guards weren't able to fully crack the code of how to navigate that strategy -- which they'll undoubtedly see all season -- and Wembanyama failed to alleviate the pressure on his teammates.

Against lesser opponents, the Spurs will find more gaps and have more opportunities, but the three games against OKC will be their measuring sticks this season. In the opener, they proved they weren't good enough, and perhaps that'll be a good thing for a young team coming off a Finals run.

San Antonio now knows there are still mountains to climb, and this opening night loss should fuel the fire until they meet the Thunder again on Feb. 11.

#3356. Jared Goff vs. Packers' stifling secondary: How this crucial NFC North showdown will be won

📅 26 Oct 2026 | other | Source

If not listed, please contact your TV provider.

Thus far, there is only one division in the NFL with all four teams making the playoffs if the season ended today: the NFC North.

The Minnesota Vikings lead the way with a 5-0 record, which would make them the No. 2 overall seed in the NFC bracket at the moment. Then, the rest of the division makes up the three wild card teams, seeds five through seven in the following order: the Green Bay Packers (4-2), the Chicago Bears (4-2) and the Detroit Lions (3-2). Every inter-division game between those four squads holds high-stakes ramifications on the conference's playoff picture, with both division and conference tiebreakers up for grabs.

That makes the Packers' Week 7 trip to Ford Field to face the Lions one of the league's most important matchups of the week. So, where could this showdown between two high-flying offenses, led by Green Bay quarterback Jordan Love and Detroit quarterback Jared Goff, be decided? Let's take a closer look.

Wide receiver Christian Watson and wide receiver Matthew Golden are the first duo in Packers history with 500-plus receiving yards each in the first six games of a season. Along with Love, they wouldn't seem to have too much of an issue with the Lions' leaky defense, which is allowing the third-most points per game (29.6) this season. Detroit is also allowing the highest rate of opponent touchdowns per drive (37%), and its defensive passing expected points added (EPA) per game (-19.0) is the second worst by any team through the first five games of a season, ahead of only the 2005 San Francisco 49ers, who went 4-12.

That's why the matchup between Goff, wide receiver Amon-Ra St. Brown, wide receiver Jameson Williams and tight Sam LaPorta against the Packers defense will likely decide Sunday afternoon's game. It's a battle that could tilt either way.

Goff is off to a historically great start to the season as just the second quarterback in NFL history with 1,400-plus passing yards (1,485) and no turnovers through the first five games of a season, along with Drew Brees in 2018. However, Goff will be challenged by a Packers defense that is allowing the sixth-fewest passing yards per game (193.3) in the entire league and is playing its best ball right now.

Green Bay's offense, hitting its stride with a patched-up offensive line, has allowed its defense to play with more energy since it doesn't have to be on the field as often, and it's paying off. The Packers lead the NFL in scoring defense (11.3) and takeaways (9) during the team's three-game winning streak since Week 4.

Where they will really pose a challenge to Goff is on tight-window throws (less than one yard of target separations). Per the NFL's Next Gen Stats, Goff is throwing into a tight window on just under a fifth of his throws, 19.4%, which is the fifth-highest rate in the league this season and his highest since his rookie year with the Rams in 2016, when he threw when an NFL-high 25.9% of his throws were into tight windows. What's interesting about Goff's willingness to squeeze the football into tight windows this season hasn't really changed when the Lions are tied or leading (19.6%) or trailing (19.2%).

Goff has completed 13 of his 37 tight-window throws for 115 yards and two touchdowns, with St. Brown making up the bulk of that production. The duo has connected on five of 16 tight-window throws for 34 yards and both of Goff's tight-window throw touchdown passes this season. Green Bay's defense is tied for the most tight-window interceptions so far, with three, so it would be more than happy for Goff to try them when a tight window opportunity arises.

There's also a chance Goff may be attempting a higher volume of tight window throws on Sunday because Lions three-time Pro Bowl running back Jahmyr Gibbs has historically struggled against the Packers, more so than any of his other NFC North opponents. In six games against Green Bay, Gibbs is averaging 48.2 rushing yards per game with a 3.9 yards per carry average and just one touchdown. Compare that to his 12 games against the rest of the division, and his numbers look a lot different: He has a 75.3 rushing yards per game average with a 5.2 yards per carry average and 12 rushing touchdowns in NFC North division games not against the Packers.

Whoever wins the battle on Goff's tight-window throws between St. Brown and the rest of the Lions' pass catchers and the Packers' secondary will likely decide the outcome of this crucial Week 7 showdown in the NFC North.

#3355. Knicks call plan to use fake championship rings as employee phishing test 'an unfortunate mistake,' will issue rings to employees

📅 26 Oct 2026 | other | Source

The New York Knicks appear to be pivoting after a report that the team used the promise of championship rings as a cybersecurity test. In a statement to Yahoo Sports on Friday, a spokesperson for Madison Square Garden (MSG) confirmed a report from The Athletic that the company had sent emails last month offering employees championship rings as a phishing test.

The company said it had recently undergone a hack, which led to the test, likely under the urging of Knicks owner James Dolan, who is known to be worried about cybersecurity.

"The company had previously sent out an email to its corporate team as a simulation of a phishing attack designed to strengthen our security against hackers," the spokesman said. "MSG was recently hacked and extorted using an employee login and password obtained through a similar phishing attack. Using the rings as a lure to gain employees attention was an unfortunate mistake."

Part of the oddness of the targeted phishing test is that, as The Athletic reported, many longtime MSG employees were not going to be receiving rings at all. That includes some who work in the Knicks' business operations — a role that would typically result in receiving a ring.

The Knicks also appear to be walking that back as well: The spokesman told Yahoo Sports that the company will be giving "more than 275 championship rings and jewelry to players, coaches, employees and other members of the Knicks family." The jewelry will total more than $3.7 million, per the company.

Individual teams typically get to decide who, in addition to the players, will receive rings after a championship, but many owners will give out more simple versions of the players' rings to employees within the organization.

Knicks players received their massive, diamond-encrusted rings during the team's home opener on Tuesday, with the championship banner also getting raised into the rafters at MSG. The rings feature a removable top that reveals a miniature Madison Square Garden.

#3353. Draymond Green Sees a LeBron James Connection in Luka Doncic’s Heated Clash With Yaxel Lendeborg

📅 26 Oct 2026 | other | Source

Yaxel Lendeborg scored 12 points in the Golden State Warriors' 127-105 loss to the Los Angeles Lakers on October 21, but his NBA debut drew attention for his heated exchange with Luka Doncic. The No. 11 pick received a technical foul in the second quarter after going back and forth with the Lakers star, who ended the half with a half-court buzzer-beater.

The exchange continued to draw attention after the game. Lendeborg said he had "lost all respect" for Doncic, while Warriors coach Steve Kerr said the rookie had received "the rookie treatment."

Draymond Green has now defended his young teammate. Speaking on his podcast, he took issue with the technical foul.

"For Yaxel to get a tech for saying something on one play that Luka doesn't say something, I thought was the weakest s**t ever," Green said.

Green also made clear that he understands the hierarchy between established NBA players and rookies. However, he does not believe a player's reputation should stop a newcomer from competing on equal terms.

"I respect the hell out of Yaxel for willing to stand toe-to-toe," Green said. He later added, "If you can't rock with a guy like that, who compete like that, who willing to fight like that, you're probably a loser."

His argument comes down to how players respond when their confidence is challenged. Green believes the criticism should be directed at someone who starts talking but backs away once the exchange intensifies, rather than someone who stands by what they say.

"The guy you criticize is the guy that does that and then tuck his tail and run. That's the guy who should be criticized, not the guy who do it, stand 10 toes on it, continues to go forward," Green said.

Green has some experience in this department. He said he came into the league talking, and pointed to his rookie-year game-winner against LeBron James and the Miami Heat, when the two were already trading words.

"He tell me I'm too little. I score, tell him he too little. Then I get to walk off," Green recalled. "When you see the respect and love that Bron and I have for each other now, it starts there from a guy not backing down."

That shot came on December 12, 2012. With 0.9 seconds remaining, Green finished a pass from Jarrett Jack to give Golden State a 97-95 win in Miami. Green scored seven points, while James finished with 31 and congratulated the rookie after the game.

The moment explains why Green believes rookies should not be expected to stay quiet simply because they are facing established stars. In his view, competition and mutual respect can coexist, and a player's standing in the league should not prevent a younger opponent from standing up for himself.

Draymond Green, now a four-time NBA champion, expects the same kind of respect he got from James to follow for his rookie teammate.

"I'll guarantee you one thing," Green said. "He keep going toe-to-toe, regardless of how Luka feels, he going to have to respect him."

Green's message to Lendeborg's critics is that confidence should not be mistaken for disrespect. For him, the willingness to compete and stand by one's words matters more than the superstar status.

The Milwaukee Brewers have acknowledged they "did not live up to the spirit of the program" of a 2015 promotion that granted fans admission to any future game. Fans who purchased the "Timeless Ticket" promotion for $1,000 gained access to any future game, and now that the Brewers are in the World Series, fans are looking to cash in, but the team has run into logistical challenges.

"We subsequently erred in our internal communications on the logistics of fulfilling the requests," Brewers president of business operations Rick Schlesinger told the Associated Press. "... While we were able to accommodate all with seats, we received feedback from some that they were unhappy with their seat locations. Clearly, even with extraordinary limitations on available seats, we did not live up to the spirit of the program, and we have been working through a resolution."

The Timeless Ticket program allowed purchasers to redeem that ticket for "the best available seat locations, subject to ticket availability at the time of redemption," and also purchase three additional tickets at full retail price to that game. Understandably, some fans who purchased the Timeless Ticket had designs on using it for a future World Series game.

Anything the season ticket holders don't have, you'll have dibs at it for the World Series.' That's kind of the story they sold."

📅 26 Oct 2026 | other | Source

Cristiano Ronaldo and Lionel Messi are once again at the center of soccer’s greatest debate as France Football introduces a special award celebrating the most iconic Ballon d’Or-winning performances in history. The new initiative brings together legends from different generations, allowing supporters to revisit some of the sport’s most memorable individual achievements and decide which deserves the ultimate recognition.

The announcement comes as the prestigious award celebrates its 70th anniversary in 2026. However, the format introduces an unusual twist that could make the competition far more unpredictable than a conventional vote for the greatest player of all time.

France Soccer, the magazine behind the Ballon d’Or since 1956, has unveiled the Ballon d’Or Of All Time (B.O.A.T.), a public vote designed to identify the greatest individual Ballon d’Or-winning year in soccer history. Rather than simply selecting a player, voters must decide which specific winning campaign deserves to stand above the rest.

The distinction matters because a player with multiple Ballon d’Or trophies has several eligible seasons competing for votes. Messi, for example, has eight winning years represented, while Ronaldo has five, potentially dividing support between their individual achievements.

FIFA Ballon d’Or nominees Lionel Messi of Argentina and FC Barcelona (L) and Cristiano Ronaldo of Portugal and Real Madrid (R)

The shortlist features 10 legendary players who have won the Ballon d’Or at least twice during their careers.

Messi leads the historical standings with eight Ballon d’Or victories, followed by Ronaldo with five. Platini, Cruyff and Van Basten each collected three trophies, while the remaining five nominees won twice.

The organizers restricted eligibility to players who have won the Ballon d’Or at least twice, immediately excluding famous one-time winners such as Zinedine Zidane, Ronaldinho and Eusebio. Pele and Diego Maradona are also absent, but for a different historical reason.

During their playing careers, the Ballon d’Or was restricted to European players, meaning the two South American icons were not eligible under the award’s original rules. The shortlist therefore reflects the history and eligibility criteria of the traditional prize rather than a comprehensive ranking of every player who has ever played the game.

Fans can vote through the official Ballon d’Or website until October 27, 2026, at 11:59 p.m. Paris time. Each vote costs €1, and supporters can submit multiple votes rather than being restricted to a single choice.

According to the published details, participants receive access to an iconic Ballon d’Or photograph and an opportunity to win two tickets to the 2027 Ballon d’Or ceremony. The B.O.A.T. results are scheduled to be announced on October 28, two days after the main 2026 ceremony at London’s Palladium on October 26.

Lionel Messi’s attendance at the Ballon d’Or gala in London remains unconfirmed less than two weeks before the October 26 ceremony.

The 39-year-old remains among the leading names in the 2026 Ballon d’Or conversation after another productive season for club and country, but his relationship with the individual prize appears to have changed considerably over the years.

Lamine Yamal says the Ballon d'Or should reward style over goals, pointing to Messi and Ronaldinho as his model.

Pitcher Trevor Megill brought the song to the Brewers dugout sometime before Game 2 of the National League Championship Series. Fellow pitchers Shane Drohan and Chad Patrick have mandated it stays in every post-victory playlist.

Everything in Foenem's sound has a Milwaukee influence.

#3342. How the Brewers and Rays have fared in the World Series before as both vie for first title

📅 26 Oct 2026 | other | Source

If not listed, please contact your TV provider.

The 2026 World Series will see the Milwaukee Brewers and Tampa Bay Rays square off, much to the chagrin of people who love complaining about how it's just "the same teams every single year."

People who love variety should be ecstatic with this matchup. Since we last had a first-time winner (the 2023 Texas Rangers), five MLB teams remain title-less: these same Brewers and Rays, plus the Colorado Rockies, San Diego Padres and Seattle Mariners.

We are guaranteed to knock the answer to that trivia question down to four after this Fall Classic. There will be a first-time winner by Halloween.

This is not, however, the first time these franchises have played in the World Series. Only the Mariners (who came tantalizingly close last year) have zero pennants. Here's how the previous World Series appearances went for the two 2026 pennant winners.

Though it's very possible the 2026 Brewers have at least one Hall of Famer, maybe multiple, it's a great bet they won't match the 1982 group, which had five: Robin Yount, Paul Molitor, Ted Simmons, Rollie Fingers and Don Sutton.

"Harvey's Wallbangers" was the affectionate nickname for this group under manager Harvey Kuenn. They won 95 games in the regular season before taking down the Angels (yes, the Brewers were in the American League at the time), three games to two in the ALCS. They then played in their first-ever World Series against a decorated franchise in the Cardinals, which at the time had 17 pennants and eight World Series titles.

The Brewers announced their presence early and with authority. Yount and Molitor combined to go 9 for 12 and Simmons homered as the Brewers took Game 1, 10-0, behind Mike Caldwell's shutout. It was total domination.

The Cardinals won Games 2 and 3 before the Brewers evened things up in Game 4 with a monster comeback. They were down 5-1 heading into the seventh inning and put up six runs. Yount and Gorman Thomas each had two-RBI singles in the big inning. The Brewers also took Game 5 behind Yount's 4 for 4 effort, which included a double and a home run.

The Cardinals returned home for a blowout 13-1 win in Game 6 and then ripped out the Brewers' hearts in Game 7. It was a 3-1 Milwaukee lead in the middle of the sixth inning. A Keith Hernandez bases-loaded single was followed by a George Hendrick single and all of a sudden it was 4-3 Cardinals. They tacked two more on in the eighth and Hall of Fame reliever Bruce Sutter finished off his two-inning save in the ninth.

The Tampa Bay Devil Rays started play in 1998 and were, for the most part, a laughingstock of a franchise through 2007. They finished in last place in the AL East each year except for 2004, when they snuck into fourth with 70 wins. That was their best record (70-91). They went 66-96 in 2007. A new ownership group, looking to tie in sunshine "rays" and for better vibes, dropped the "Devil" before 2008 and everything changed (no, not because of the name change).

The Rays improved by 31 wins, going 97-65 and shockingly winning the AL East. They then beat the White Sox in the ALDS and took down the Red Sox in seven games in the ALCS. They were void of Hall of Famers, but had All-Stars Dioner Navarro, Evan Longoria (also AL Rookie of the Year) and Scott Kazmir, along with quality players like Carlos Peña, Carl Crawford, B.J. Upton and James Shields. They also got a boost late in the season and in the playoffs from a young lefty by the name of David Price. Perhaps you've heard of him.

This World Series, unfortunately, might be most remembered outside of Philadelphia for the rain-suspended Game 5. Game 3 started after 10 p.m. ET due to rain as well.

The Phillies won Game 1, but the Rays bounced back to win Game 2. They struck early, getting two runs in the first and another in the second. Shields threw well, and then Price finished the game, though he did allow two runs for a final score of 4-2.

The rain-marred Philly portion of the series saw three Phillies wins. Game 3 was a heartbreaker. The Rays had tied it in the eighth when Upton stole third and the throw got away, allowing him to score. The Phillies walked it off in the ninth. Jimmy Rollins and Ryan Howard went off in Game 4, a 10-2 Phillies win. Then there was the suspended Game 5. It was 2-2 in the middle of the sixth when everyone packed up and returned the next night. When play resumed, the Phillies took the lead quickly. A Rocco Baldelli home run in the seventh tied it, but the Phillies responded right back with a Pedro Feliz RBI single in the bottom half. Brad Lidge closed things down in the ninth, giving the Phillies their first title since 1980.

We don't need to rehash how awful the year 2020 was, do we? Nah. Just remember that these were all neutral-site games.

The Rays were the No. 1 seed in the AL after a 40-20 regular season. They beat the Blue Jays (2-0) in the Wild Card Series, the Yankees (3-2) in the ALDS and the Astros (4-3) in the ALCS. Brandon Lowe, Yandy Díaz and Willy Adames were their muscle on offense, while the pitching staff was led by Blake Snell, Tyler Glasnow and a great back-end of the bullpen.

The Dodgers took Game 1, 8-3. The Rays responded with a 6-4 win in Game 2 thanks in large part to Lowe's two homers. The Dodgers won Game 3 and then the Rays survived a wild back-and-forth Game 4, 8-7, with a Brett Phillips walk-off single that also included an error at home plate to close things out.

The Dodgers took Game 5 and then won it all in Game 6, most memorable for the Snell controversy. The lefty had a one-hit shutout with nine strikeouts through five innings and the Rays were clinging to a 1-0 lead. Snell recorded the first out of the inning and then gave up a single to Austin Barnes. Rays manager Kevin Cash elected to pull Snell in favor of shutdown reliever Nick Anderson. Anderson gave up a double to Mookie Betts, a wild pitch brought Barnes home and then Betts scored the go-ahead run on a Corey Seager groundout. Betts provided some insurance with a home run in the eighth and the Rays' season finished with a 3-1 loss.
