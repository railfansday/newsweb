#1800. Estoril Praia vs Moreirense - prediction, team news, lineups

📅 26 Oct 2026 | other | Source

By Joshua Ojele | 23 Oct 2026 21:33

Seeking to bounce back from their midweek Taca de Portugal exit, Moreirense journey to the Estadio Antonio Coimbra da Mota to face rock-bottom Estoril Praia in round nine of the Portuguese Primeira Liga on Sunday.

Having failed to win the last six meetings between the two teams, Estoril head into the weekend looking to get one over Os verdes e brancos and finally get off the mark in the league.

While Estoril Praia have endured a woeful start to the new league campaign, they avoided an early upset in the Taca de Portugal last Sunday when they secured a penalty-shootout victory over Campeonato de Portugal outfit Salgueiros after a 1-1 stalemate in normal time.

Rafik Guitane came up clutch for Estoril when he netted in the 89th minute to cancel out Deven’s 81st-minute opener and force extra time, and with nothing to separate the two sides after 30 minutes, the game went on to penalties, where the visitors secured a 4-1 victory.

While it was another shaky team display, head coach Leandro Pires will hope the cup victory can inspire a much-needed turnaround in the Primeira Liga, where the Canarinhos are the only side yet to taste victory this season, claiming two draws and losing six so far.

With 12 goals conceded already, defensive frailties have been Estoril Praia’s biggest concern this season, highlighted in their last Primeira Liga outing on October 10 when they fell to a 2-1 defeat against newcomers Academuco Viseu at the Estadio Municipal do Fontelo, a result which leaves them rooted to the bottom of the league standings.

While Estoril will look to stop the rot this weekend, next up is the challenge of an opposing side whom they have failed to get the better of in their last six attempts, losing three and claiming three draws since a 1-0 victory in May 2022.

On the other hand, Moreirense were dumped out of the Taca de Portugal in their opening game last weekend when they fell to a 1-0 defeat against AVS in their third-round clash at the Estadio do CD das Aves.

Rodrigo Pinho’s third-minute strike proved decisive after Maracas received his marching orders on the stroke of half time, with the hosts making the most of their numerical advantage to see out a third straight victory across all competitions.

This was in keeping with their struggles in the Primeira Liga, where Moreirense have failed to win six of their opening eight matches, claiming two draws and losing four, including a 1-0 home defeat at the hands of Gil Vicente on October 8, to sit 12th in the league standings.

Breaching opposing defences has not been a worry for Vasco Costa’s men, who have made the net ripple eight times in their eight league matches, but with a fourth-highest 15 goals conceded already, they must tighten up defensively if they are to get back on track this weekend.

However, given their record at the Estadio Antonio Coimbra da Mota, the odds appear stacked against Moreirense, having failed to win nine of their most recent 11 away games against Estoril, losing five and claiming four draws since February 2011.

The attacking duo of Fabricio Garcia and Moroccan striker Yanis Begraoui have been sidelined through injuries and are out of contention for Estoril Praia.

Israel Salazar continues his long road to full recovery after sustaining a cruciate ligament tear in August 2025, while 31-year-old midfielder Xeka has missed the last five matches since coming off injured against Benfica on August 31.

As for Moreirense, Costa will be unable to call upon the services of veteran defender Maracas, who is currently suspended after rebinding his marching orders in the cup defeat against AVS last weekend.

Gambian international Gilberto Batista, who has started all eight Primeira Liga games this season, should return to the XI, forming the back four with Kiko, Kevyn Monteiro and 26-year-old Dinis Pinto.

Alexandre Parsemain picked up an injury while in action for Martinique against Suriname in September, and the 23-year-old striker is set to sit out a third consecutive game for Os verdes e brancos.

#1799. Moggi points to Juventus leadership after 3-0 Lazio loss

📅 26 Oct 2026 | other | Source

Former Juventus general manager Luciano Moggi said the Bianconeri lacked a coach and failed to perform in their 3-0 home defeat to Lazio, sharpening the focus on the team’s leadership.

In comments reported by Football Italia and Gazeta Express, Moggi argued that Juventus had not simply played badly: in his view, they had not taken to the field at all. He pointed to Gleison Bremer’s public remarks as a reflection of the team’s situation and also criticised the defender’s part in Lazio’s third goal.

Moggi’s assessment followed a bruising night at the Allianz Stadium. Oliver Provstgaard, Gustav Isaksen and Fisayo Dele-Bashiru scored as Lazio won 3-0, with Juventus jeered off by their supporters. Football Italia reported that the result left Juventus eight points behind Inter and Lazio.

Juventus’ defeat to Lazio also unfolded with Luciano Spalletti watching from the stands while serving a ban. After the match, captain Bremer criticised his teammates; Spalletti responded that Bremer shared responsibility for the loss.

Moggi, who previously served as Juventus general manager, framed the performance as a problem of coaching authority rather than a poor display on the pitch. He said the team’s situation could be understood through Bremer’s comments, while directing questions about responsibility beyond the players to those in charge.

Spalletti said after the defeat that he was no longer sure the squad were following him. Moggi challenged that uncertainty, arguing that the team’s circumstances could not be excused by injuries. His criticism landed amid an already public exchange between the coach and his captain over individual responsibility for the defeat.

Asked whether Spalletti should be at risk, with some supporters calling for Antonio Conte, Moggi declined to assess the coach’s position from outside the club and directed the question back to the fans. The exchange leaves the focus on the squad’s response and the relationship between players and coach after a damaging home result.

Spalletti’s comments and the dispute over Bremer’s remarks have kept attention on the dressing-room mood, while Moggi’s intervention has added a former club executive’s criticism to the reaction. Juventus now face the task of addressing the concerns raised in the aftermath of the Lazio defeat, with the next performances set to shape the conversation around the team’s direction. The response from Spalletti after the loss has already become part of that debate.

There are several takeaways from the game, as you would expect after such a poor result, and we have highlighted five of these below.

1. Silly mistakes prove costly: It doesn’t matter if Milan had played as well as Barcelona. With such major mistakes at the back, they likely would have lost anyway.

2. Europa League importance unclear: How Milan have treated the competition so far leaves us wondering what their objective actually is. Right now, top 8 is in great doubt and rotation is being made in each game.

3. Opportunity squandered by everyone: Thursday’s clash was a great opportunity for several players but none of them made the most of it. In fact, all players were rated below 6 in our player ratings.

4.  Cisse and Camarda questions: Amorim has always made it clear that Camarda is and must feel like the deputy striker, yet he opted for Cisse in the Bournemouth clash. The youngster’s role is now far from clear.

5. Wing-back indications: With Chukwueze and Saelemaekers injured, Amorim opted to field Moreira on the right at Vitality Stadium, even if everyone thought he would be on the left. We should probably get used to seeing him on the right more.

Milan could have played as well as Barcelona for all I care, but when you make such major individual mistakes, then you are almost always going to lose the game anyway. There were obviously other flaws, but that was my main reaction last night.

I criticised Koni De Winter for his defending in my player ratings, simply because it should be defending 101 not to extend your arm like that inside the box. Especially not if you are actively trying to reach the ball. You are just asking for trouble and as we saw, trouble did indeed arrive.

Of course, as that would probably be categorised as clumsiness or a lack of skill more than anything, it’s nothing compared to Mike Maignan. He knew exactly what he was doing in the moment and the referee did well to act immediately.

Could you argue that it was a soft penalty? Probably, but the fact that the goalkeeper put both hands in the opponent’s face and pushed means the referee didn’t exactly have a choice. And if he hadn’t given it himself, VAR most likely would have stepped in to recommend the red card anyway.

It’s just incredibly silly and there is no good reason for doing that, no matter how you frame the situation. As the captain of the team, he should be the one encouraging others to avoid making those mistakes.

It was always going to be near-impossible to come back from two penalties and a red card, but it didn’t help that Strahinja Pavlovic made a mockery of Lorenzo Torriani’s free-kick wall. He jumped away from it and the Bournemouth player capitalised on the gap, scoring the third.

In short, three fatal mistakes in one game. Not that Milan were looking brilliant anyway, but they didn’t even give themselves a chance.

I have been racking my brain since the win against Salzburg, which also included a lot of rotation in the starting eleven. What is the Europa League to Milan this season? A path to an important trophy, a way to cash in more money, or a nonessential commitment?

The schedule is very hectic right now, so the rotation perhaps makes somewhat sense, but it’s starting to feel like Milan don’t prioritise the competition at all. Three games in and they only have one win, now far away from the top 8 finish that supposedly was the goal at the start.

Last night, Amorim left the likes of Mario Gila, Luka Modric, Adrien Rabiot and Christian Pulisic on the bench for an away game that he knew was going to be difficult. If you ask me, regardless of the game, at least one of them has to play to guarantee some consistency.

The irony is that Amorim is citing the packed schedule for his rotation, but if Milan finish outside the top 8, then they will have an additional two games to play at the start of next year. They will also get less money, so it’s bad on multiple fronts.

We can also use the old excuse  “It’s only the Europa League, who cares”, but the reality is the games still get a lot of eyeballs. Especially the one against Bournemouth, so Milan could have used it as a way to boost their image and confidence.

Instead, they once again proved how far away they are from returning to the upper echelons of the football world.

It was a poor performance from a collective point of view, no two ways about it, but the players also failed to perform at their usual standard. Excluding Torriani, Gazzetta dello Sport didn’t rate a single player at 6 or above and not even the goalkeeper got a 6 in our ratings.
