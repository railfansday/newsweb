The New York Knicks are signing Dalton Knecht to a two-way contract, ESPN reported on Friday. To make room for Knecht, who was waived earlier this week by the Los Angeles Lakers, the Knicks waived Kevin McCullar Jr., according to SNY.

Knecht, the No. 17 overall pick by the Lakers in the 2024 NBA Draft, was in and out of Los Angeles' rotation the last two seasons and ended up being the odd man out on roster cutdown day last week. Knecht, a 6-foot-6 forward, appeared in just 54 games during the 2025-26 campaign, averaging 4.2 points over 10.2 minutes per contest. It was a major step back in production and playing time for Knecht, who showed some promise as a rookie.

By signing Knecht to a two-way deal, the Knicks are taking a low-risk, high-upside swing that Knecht, 25, can regain some of his rookie-year form, which saw him become a key rotational piece during JJ Redick's first season in Los Angeles. Knecht had seven games with at least 20 points during his rookie season, including a 37-point outburst against the Utah Jazz in the NBA Cup.

However, less than three months later, Knecht was included in a trade package to land Mark Williams from the Charlotte Hornets. Days after the move was reported, the Lakers rescinded the trade for Williams after the big man failed his physical. That sent Knecht back to the Lakers and he finished his rookie season averaging 9.1 points in 19.2 minutes per game while shooting 46.1% from the field and 37.6% from distance.

The defending champion Knicks have had success by making moves on the margins like this in recent years. The Knicks re-signed Landy Shamet to a one-year contract ahead of last season after making 50 appearances with the franchise the year before. Shamet became an integral member of Mike Brown's rotation that saw the Knicks win their first NBA title in over 50 years.

At the trade deadline last season, New York added Jose Alvarado for two second-round picks and Dalen Terry. Alvarado had several key moments throughout the Knicks' playoff run as a defensive ace who could knock down shots. New York signed Shamet and Alvarado to multi-year contract extensions this offseason.

Another move the Knicks made this offseason was signing veteran Andre Drummond to a one-year minimum deal to replace Mitchell Robinson, who signed with the Celtics. Drummond played extended minutes during the Knicks' season-opener against the 76ers due to Karl Anthony-Towns being in foul trouble and finished with 12 points, 14 rebounds and two blocks.

Knecht's path to immediate playing time with the Knicks will be tricky. Knecht joins Tyler Nickel and Jaden Akins as New York's two-way players. However, given the Knicks' recent success with low-risk moves that paid dividends in the playoffs, there is a chance Knecht finds a second wind with the Knicks. If he gets back to how he was playing as a rookie before the botched trade with Charlotte, Knecht can contribute as a shooter and shot-creator.

The 122nd World Series begins Friday night at American Family Field in Milwaukee. The National League-best Milwaukee Brewers will host the American League-best Tampa Bay Rays, and one way or the other, baseball will have a first-time champion this season. Neither the Brewers (established 1970) nor Rays (established 1998) have ever won a World Series.

"The 2020 World Series was kind of a blur. The whole postseason was a blur. I'm a heck of a lot happier we're in this situation now coming out on the other side of that," Rays manager Kevin Cash said on Thursday. "Those games, they were intense, there was a lot of anxiety in them, but I felt that same intensity and anxiety in the ALCS just two or three days ago. Excited to go and watch our guys do their thing in the next week to ten days."

Historically, the winner of Game 1 of a best-of-seven has gone on to win the series 65% of the time. It's 64% in the World Series, so basically identical. Just last year, though, the Los Angeles Dodgers lost Game 1 of the World Series at home, then rallied to beat the Toronto Blue Jays in seven games. Winning Game 1 certainly gives you a leg up, but it does not mean the series is over.

Here now are three key questions heading into Game 1 on Friday night.

Jacob Misiorowski, the likely NL Cy Young winner, has not been himself this postseason. He failed to complete five innings in either of his two starts, not necessarily because of ineffectiveness, but because of high pitch counts. Misiorowski threw 88 pitches en route to allowing two runs in four innings in Game 1 of the NLDS. In Game 1 of the NLCS, it was one run on 94 pitches in 4 ⅓ innings.

Misiorowski's high-octane fastball has played down this postseason. The velocity is plenty good. He averaged -- averaged -- 101.0 mph with his heater in the NLDS and NLCS. Hitters are not swinging and missing nearly as much as they did during the regular season, though, and the resulting foul balls have run up his pitch count.

The Rays had the lowest strikeout rate (18.8% of plate appearances) and lowest whiff rate (20.7% of swings) during the regular season, by a lot too. The Blue Jays were a distant second with a 22.2% whiff rate. Misiorowski has had trouble missing bats with his heater this postseason and he'll now face the best contact-hitting offense in the sport. That could be a problem.

"He hasn't been as crisp," Brewers manager Pat Murphy said about Misiorowski on Thursday. "I don't think people are up there thinking it's going to be 104, and I think they're elated if it's anything less than that. They're obviously looking at an area. They're trying to exploit everything. He's exposed, like all these guys get at some point. He's exposed. They're going to attack a certain way, and we're going to try to counter that attack the way we counter it."

The Brewers are expected to carry five lefties in their World Series bullpen: Aaron Ashby, Shane Drohan, DL Hall, Kyle Harrison, and JoJo Romero. Ashby, Hall, and Romero are the trusted late-inning guys. Drohan started a bullpen game in the NLCS. Harrison is more of a break-glass-in-case-of-emergency option. He'll get the ball deep in extra innings, something like that.

The Rays have shown little power against lefties this year, which is hard to believe with right-handed thumpers Junior Caminero and Yandy Díaz anchoring the lineup. Ryan Vilade, Tampa's go-to righty platoon bat, will undoubtedly get several key pinch-hit at-bats in the World Series, and perhaps as soon as Game 1. The Rays must solve Milwaukee's lefties to win the title.

The Brewers swept the Dodgers in the NLCS and have not played since last Thursday. They've played only eight games in the last 25 days. Rest is good. Believe me, everyone on the Brewers is happy to take it. Still, the fact of the matter is they haven't played much baseball the last three weeks, or any baseball over the last week.

"You can't recreate real, you know what I mean?" Murphy said about his team's downtime. "It's a long layoff, and if we want to use it as an excuse, then let people use it as an excuse, but I refuse to do that. We needed a little bit of a break. It was an emotional series in L.A. Cross-country trip, all that. We had a few days where it was a little lighter, but then we've picked it up and gotten a lot of live ABs, but nothing simulates the game the way it is."

In the three-wild-card era, teams that have five or more days off going into a postseason series are 13-10 in Game 1. Teams have so many tools available to them (Trajekt hitting machines, etc.) that it is easier to navigate that downtime than it was even five years ago. Murphy's right and you can't recreate real, but you can come pretty close. The layoff is definitely a subplot going into Game 1.

#3166. Connor Hellebuyck trade: Jets send star goalie to the Devils in exchange for four players, two draft picks

📅 26 Oct 2026 | other | Source

If not listed, please contact your TV provider.

Connor Hellebuyck is a now a Devil. The star goalie for the Winnipeg Jets is on the move after being traded to New Jersey on Friday in exchange for four players and two future draft picks. the teams announced the move with the Jets receiving Dawson Mercer, Nick Bjugstad, Nico Daws and Alexander Command, plus a 2027 seventh-round pick and a 2029 second-round pick.

Hellebuyck expressed his desire to be traded in the offseason when he formally requested a move in August. He said at the time that he had made the request months earlier and tried to keep the situation private.

Hellebuyck has spent his entire career with Winnipeg after being drafted by the Jets in 2012 at No. 130 overall. He's recognized as one of the best goalies in the league as a three-time Vezina Trophy winner. He also helped carry the United States to its first gold medal at the 2026 Olympics in February with some incredible saves against Canada in the final game.

After trading Jacob Markstom in the offseason, the Devils had a clear need in goal, and this splashy move certainly fills that need. With a talented young core already in place, New Jersey's Stanley Cup window is wide open.

RAISE HELLE!Connor Hellebuyck is a New Jersey Devil: https://t.co/HCehRGOQI5 pic.twitter.com/dZGNQR16m1

For the Jets, it must be a hard pill to swallow when a franchise icon decides to walk out they door, but this trade package should help them as the enter a new era and revamp the roster. Winnipeg got a former first-rounder in Mercer to try and add to a rebuild as well as a veteran presence in Bjugstad. They also acquired a younger goalie in Daws who could fill the role of Hellebuyck at just 25 years old.

Goaltending has been an issue in New Jersey for a while now -- even after the team acquired Markstrom from the Calgary Flames in 2024. Markstom never really panned out, and his 13.6 goals allowed above average, per Natural Stat Trick, were a big reason why the Devils missed the postseason last year.

In the early portion of this season, it was clear that New Jersey needed to make a move to upgrade in the crease. Daws and veteran Jake Allen simply aren't a tandem suitable for a Stanley Cup contender, and the numbers backed that up. New Jersey ranks 24th in team save percentage at .877, and the duo of Daws and Allen combined to surrender 2.99 goals above average.

Once Hellebuyck gets up to game speed, that shouldn't be a problem anymore.

In the three-season stretch from 2023-26, Hellebuyck led the NHL with 73.42 goals saved above average, which is 20 goals higher than the next closest netminder. Over that span, Hellebuyck also posted a standard save percentage of .914 and an astronomical high-danger save-percentage of .832.

Hellebuyck carried less talented teams to the playoffs multiple times in his career. Now on a team loaded with weaponry in their prime -- Jack Hughes, Jesper Bratt and Nico Hischier to name a few -- Hellebuyck could be the final piece to the championship puzzle.

In terms of the acquisition cost, it was probably an easy deal for Devils general manager Sunny Mehta to make. The 2029 second-round pick is useless for a team that should be all-in now, Bjugstad is simply a depth piece and Daws now has no path to a starting role with Hellebuyck in town.

The most interesting trade chips are Mercer and Command. Mercer flashed major upside with 56 points in his second NHL season, 2022-23, but he hasn't really come close to hitting those heights again. Still, even if he has plateaued, Mercer remains a strong middle-six forward rising into his prime.

Although Command had his detractors -- and may have been over-drafted at No. 12 overall -- he was a consensus first-round pick last summer. That gives Winnipeg a potential franchise forward it can build around long-term, but New Jersey already has one of the deepest forward groups in the league, especially with the emergence of Arseny Gritsyuk and the acquisition of Luke Evangelista.

If we're looking for a 3-D chess angle of this deal, it only makes the Devils more appealing for a certain Minnesota Wild defenseman whose brothers just happen to play in New Jersey already. Grade -- A+

Let's get the obvious out of the way first: The Jets were never going to get fair value for Hellebuyck because, at his best, he's the No. 1 goaltender in the world with no equal. That type of excellence can't be replaced.

That said, Winnipeg did fairly well given the circumstances. Hellebuyck made a public trade request, and he wasn't even with the team after sitting out for the entirety of training camp. The entire league knew the Jets had to trade him.

Hellebuyck's talent created a decent market anyway, but most of the suitors were bona fide Stanley Cup contenders with little in the way of valuable draft capital, and they weren't going to knee cap the roster by sending a key player back in return.

With all that taken into consideration, Kevin Cheveldayoff got a solid return. Although Mercer's ceiling may be lower than initially thought, he's a 24-year-old forward who banks roughly 20 goals and 20 assists every season. The kicker with Mercer will be Winnipeg's ability to re-sign him before he hits restricted free agency next summer.

The addition of Command means Winnipeg now has two forwards selected in the top 12 of the 2026 NHL Draft after taking center Viggo Bjorck themselves. If both of those players deliver on their first-round pedigrees, they could be a terrific one-two punch for the team long-term.

Daws was a third-round pick in 2020, and despite playing just 13 NHL games since the start of the 2024-25 season, he should give Winnipeg a solid back up option behind Stuart Skinner. It certainly helps that Skinner is off to a great start with his new team -- and if Daws develops into a true No. 1 -- it'll be gravy.

If there's any reason to critique this deal from the Jets' side of things, it's that they didn't get premium draft capital or a surefire up-and-coming prospect. Maybe Command develops into a star, but there's no guarantee he becomes a top-six stud in the NHL.

The Jets got a lot of good stuff, but they didn't really get anything great, at least not based on what we know in the moment. Because of that, I have to ding Winnipeg a little bit. Grade: B

The New York Knicks went on one of the more euphoric championships in NBA history earlier this year. It was an unforgettable playoff run that ended a 53-year title drought. But a lot of the news surrounding the team since then has had a different tone.

Whether it's been owner James Dolan's hard-line stance against going into the second apron costing the team key reserve Mitchell Robinson or contentious extension negotiations with multiple starters getting tabled before the season, the vibes surrounding New York's championship defense are significantly more dour than they were even a few months ago.

And, on Thursday, The Athletic reported that the issues haven't just been confined to the roster.

While Knicks players received their championship rings on Tuesday before their season-opening loss to the Philadelphia 76ers, the rest of the organization appears to still be waiting. Knicks assistant coaches reportedly did not receive their rings on Tuesday night despite being handed (apparently empty) boxes during the ring ceremony. While the assistants' reactions to not getting their rings reportedly ranged "from genuine anger to annoyance," The Athletic noted that not all assistant coaching staffs, including last year's Oklahoma City Thunder, get their rings on opening night.

However, in an atypical move by NBA standards, many longtime Madison Square Garden employees reportedly will not receive a championship ring at all. While the most expensive and ornate championship rings tend to be reserved for the players, most employees of NBA teams receive less expensive rings when their team wins a title. The New York Post reported Friday that the plan is to distribute a total of 275 championship rings "to players, coaches, employees and other members of the Knicks family, worth more than $3.7 million."

And yet, as a means of testing the organization's cybersecurity, Madison Square Garden employees received an email in September asking them to reserve their preferred ring style. The email, however, was reportedly a phishing test. Employees who clicked on the link were forced to attend a mandatory cybersecurity class "to help satisfy some of Dolan's growing cybersecurity concerns." It is not clear how many of the employees who received this email will eventually be awarded a championship ring.

On Friday, an MSG spokesperson called the decision "an unfortunate mistake" in a statement to the New York Post:

"The company had previously sent out an email to its corporate team as a simulation of a phishing attack designed to strengthen our security against hackers. MSG was recently hacked and extorted using an employee login and password obtained through a similar phishing attack. Using the rings as a lure to gain employees attention was an unfortunate mistake."

The Knicks kicked off their title defense with a loss to LeBron James and the 76ers, and while the season is only just getting underway, much of the goodwill that followed last spring's magical run appears to have dissipated. Though off-court controversies have been minimal since Leon Rose was hired to run the team, the Knicks are seemingly courting a pretty avoidable one in how they've handled the dispersal of their championship rings.
