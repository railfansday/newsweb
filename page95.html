Real Madrid star Vinicius Junior was strongly tempted by Arsenal in the summer, his agents have revealed.

But the Brazilian superstar hopes to spend the rest of his career with the LaLiga giants, who brought him to Europe in 2018.

Arsenal enquired about Vinicius Jr and reports suggested they were willing to offer him a mammoth £18.5million per season - more than any player at the club.

The Gunners still hold on to hope, with Vinicius Jr yet to renew his contract, which expires at the end of the season.

'Arsenal were very involved. They were very aggressive,' Michael Yormack, one of his agents, told Sports Boom.

'Vini’s heart is in Madrid, that’s where he’s always wanted to be, and that’s where he is, and that’s where he hopes to be for the rest of his career. I don't think there was ever a question.

Vinicius Junior seriously considered moving to Arsenal from Real Madrid in the summer

'Did he ever seriously consider Arsenal? I mean of course. When a team like Arsenal rolls out the red carpet for you and makes you feel really wanted, then obviously you have to listen.'

'With Vini, he went to Madrid at a very young age, and I think there’s an element of comfort, wanting to be there and wanting to build something at Real Madrid,' said Grant Veitch, another of his agents at Roc Nation Sports International.

'There were other opportunities that arose. But I think the level of comfort he has, and him already being a star at Real Madrid, it kept him there, and he wanted to stay.

'You can branch out, but I think the comfort level of him being there since such a young age and wanting to build up his reputation and wanting to continue that and win more titles with Real Madrid, that’s the main reason he’s stayed.'

Vinicius Jr has won La Liga three times and the Champions League twice with Madrid, scoring 129 goals in 386 appearances.

His contract expires at the end of the season and the club's position is that he must lower his demands if they are to agree an extension. Madrid would still be willing to make him their joint-highest earner alongside Kylian Mbappe.

The balance of power shifted to Mbappe when he joined from Paris Saint-Germain in 2024 and Vinicius Jr's future has become a subject of increasing speculation.

He has often been a target of racism in Spain - there have been more than 20 such cases of alleged abuse towards him since he joined from Flamengo in 2018. The situation has reduced him to tears before, but he has insisted on staying in La Liga so that he doesn't 'give the racists what they want'.

Christos Tzolis is the only attacker Arsenal signed in the summer and he is currently injured

Jose Mourinho admitted last week that he would have left in the summer if he were the 26-year-old, saying: 'If I were in his shoes, I probably wouldn't be here. I would have forced a move to a country where he'd be respected, but out of loyalty to Real Madrid and love for the club, he stayed.

'He believes things can improve, and he still has that smile. Nothing and no one can take it away from him.

'He's a fantastic guy, and it's easy to get along with him. I spend a few minutes alone with him every day.

'I value him immensely because he always manages to live and survive with a smile.'

Kyle Walker has admitted he would probably wipe his 'four in a row' leg tattoo if Manchester City are stripped of their Premier League titles.

The Man City legend won six league titles with the club and last year revealed his inked tribute to their record run of consecutive crowns between 2021 and 2024.

Daily Mail Sport has reported that City are primed for a huge points reduction which would relegate them to the Championship after they were found guilty of breaking financial rules between 2009 and 2018.

It remains to be seen whether the club will be stripped of their titles after they manipulated their financial accounts to the tune of £900million via 'sham' contracts.

Asked by talkSPORT if he would get rid of his tattoo, Walker said: 'I'm probably going to have to do that.

'But you look at the likes of the great Man United squad, and they never managed to get four in a row. So there's only a select few of us that can say that we won four.

'I'll try and keep it on, but hopefully we don't get the things taken away.'

'I feel that what we achieved in that space of time was probably incredible and probably won't be done ever again.

'From the outside looking in, it doesn't look great and we have to address that.'

Walker was on the books with City for eight seasons, having joined from Tottenham for £45m in 2017, and was elected captain after winning the Treble with them in 2022/23.

'It’s something we don’t want to talk about because we want to be looked upon as winning them trophies, winning them the right way,' he said earlier this month.

City insist they are innocent and are appealing the verdict by an independent panel, which found them guilty of all the serious alleged financial breaches.

In a lengthy statement, the Premier League say City arranged 'sham contracts (which misrepresented the true agreement between the parties) with a number of its commercial partners, as well as relying on "sham" agreements with others, to artificially inflate the club's revenues and reduce its costs'.

It also says the club falsely altered the financial picture at the Etihad to the tune of £900m. The club had agreed exorbitant sponsorship deals with a number of companies, but those sponsors only paid part of the fee.

The rest was propped up by the Abu Dhabi United Group Investment & Development Ltd (ADUG), which owned the club.

As the Premier League note, this allowed City to 'artificially inflate the club’s revenues' and therefore spend more under Premier League rules. The higher the revenue a club records, the more they are permitted to spend.

Moreover, further 'sham' deals, funded by ADUG, allowed the club to 'record lower operating expenses than it actually incurred'.

The Premier League say that revenues were inflated and costs reduced to the tune of £900m in an attempt to comply with spending rules. The result was that the club 'filed misstated accounts and concealed the true state of its finances from its auditors and football regulators'.

It added that the club was 'significantly in breach of both the Premier League's and UEFA's spending limits' during the time period in question, 2009-2018 when it won eight major trophies.

Had City reported accurately 'it would have been in breach of both the League's and UEFA's spending limits by a very substantial amount'.

The panel also found that City committed multiple breaches of its duties of co-operation and good faith in that it failed to assist the Premier League's four-year investigation, triggered by the emergence of club emails obtained by a hacker.

Indeed, City had 'made concerted efforts to stop and frustrate the PL investigation'.
