#2379. Stefan Borson drops Michael Carrick sack verdict - 'He may not survive the week'

📅 26 Oct 2026 | other | Source

Michael Carrick may have lost the Manchester United dressing room as pressure builds on the manager at Old Trafford.

That is according to former Manchester City financial adviser Stefan Borson, who exclusively told Football Insider that there is a slim possibility of the 45-year-old saving his job.

The Red Devils are winless in their last seven games across all competitions, most recently being embarrassed by Como in the Champions League.

A 4-1 defeat to the Serie A side was made even worse by the fact that Como were fighting for promotion from Italy's third tier in 2020-21.

After the game, just days after Lisandro Martinez's outburst, Bruno Fernandes criticised the team's tactics, which instantly stole the headlines.

It is understood that Carrick will be in charge for Man United's clash with Bournemouth on Sunday, 25 October, but that might not last much longer.

Speaking exclusively to Football Insider, Borson believes that Carrick no longer has the trust of the dressing room, with the end coming for United's manager.

"I think that there is an inevitability that the end is coming quite soon. There aren't many managers that have managed to turn around a situation similar to Carrick's right now," he said.

"There's certainly a possibility of having issues in the dressing room, and that's problematic because it's not just a case of what's happening on the pitch game by game.

"If you've also got players who are saying negative things in the aftermath, that just creates a big problem for the ownership.

"So they're obviously going to be thinking about whether now is the right time. Carrick might now survive this week or next week."

The situation has, understandably, brought Fernandes' new contract into disrepute, with many believing that he should be sold as soon as possible.

Fernandes' contract will expire this summer if Man United decide not to trigger the one-year extension, with talks over a new deal at an impasse.

Despite the situation, former United scout Mick Brown believes that losing the 32-year-old next summer would still be a "huge blow" at Old Trafford.

Fernandes has been one of the most consistent playmakers over the last few seasons, and very few offer what he can in the final third.

#2373. ‘To this day…’ – Jurgen Klopp makes heartbreaking confession over Diogo Jota’s death

📅 26 Oct 2026 | other | Source

Jurgen Klopp has said that he’s yet to ‘come to terms’ with the tragic loss of Diogo Jota in a car crash in July 2025 which also claimed the life of his brother Andre Silva.

The siblings were killed when, whilst en route from Porto to Santander to catch a ferry back to England, the car in which they were travelling suffered a tyre blowout before going off the road and catching fire.

In the days which followed the tragedy, tributes poured in for both men throughout the world of football, with Anfield becoming a shrine to Liverpool’s number 20 as thousands of scarves and other memorabilia were laid outside the stadium.

In a newly-published book titled ‘Diogo Jota: Forever In Our Hearts’, Klopp recalled the sense of shock at hearing of Diogo’s sudden death and admitted that he’s still trying to process the tragic loss.

The former Reds manager said (via Liverpool Echo): “I’m 58 years old. I know that people die, that this is part of life. Once you reach a certain age, we have to accept that these things happen, but when I heard about Diogo’s accident, I was in shock and I couldn’t even speak.

“I didn’t ask how, I didn’t ask where; I just stopped what I was doing and tried to come to terms with it. To this day, I must admit, I still haven’t really managed to do that.

“When my assistant Danielle asked me if I was available to have this conversation – to contribute to this book – I said yes, that I wanted to talk about Diogo, to think about him. However, now that we’re talking, I can see that I’m not ready for it.

“I’ve spent most of my life talking, but now I’m just hanging by a thread, lost for words – which is not at all normal. It’s crazy…”

Want more Empire of the Kop coverage? Add us as a preferred source on Google to your favourites list for news you can trust

The footballing world might continue to trundle on, but the depth of heartbreak suffered by those at Liverpool was felt for months after the tragedy, with Andy Robertson admitting that the players ‘didn’t care about football’ for much of last season amid their enduring grief.

It was Klopp who signed Diogo for the Reds in 2020 and worked with the Portuguese forward for four years before he left Anfield, and the German’s tribute to our forever number 20 highlights the extent to which the fatal car crash has (and continues to have) on the ex-LFC manager.

Time will pass and those representing the Merseyside club on the pitch will get on with their core task of trying to win football matches, but the heartbreak of losing Diogo and Andre will never fully heal.

In the rush to criticise players when they make mistakes during a match, it’s easy to forget just how much mental fortitude it must’ve taken for the Liverpool squad to simply get through last season, never mind securing Champions League football.

For that, everyone at the club has our utmost respect.
