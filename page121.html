#1259. South Park satirist pushes for accountability in Cornell gang-rape lawsuit

📅 26 Oct 2026 | other | Source

Writer Toby Morton is using a new website to amplify allegations in a high-profile sexual assault lawsuit involving seven Cornell students. The reopening of the case is causing an outcry on social media.

Toby Morton, who wrote several episodes of the animated comedy series South Park, launched a website seeking accountability in connection with an alleged 2024 sexual assault case involving seven fraternity members at Cornell University in the US. The site names seven men accused of sexually assaulting a 20-year-old woman who was allegedly unconscious after being given drugs and alcohol at a Cornell fraternity house, and collects personal data and photos of the members involved.

"I've received messages reminding me I'm not a journalist, I'm 'just a satirist,'" Morton wrote in a September 27 Instagram post regarding the creation of his website TheCornell7.org. "This is true, but it makes me more dangerous," he added, saying he has "nobody to tell me I've gone too far."

The case has recently drawn a surge in public attention after the accuser, identified as Jane Doe to protect her identity, filed a lawsuit in mid-September. Her complaint alleges that she was brutally sexually assaulted in 2024 by seven members of the Chi Phi fraternity while unconscious after being pressured to take ketamine, smoke marijuana and drink alcohol at the fraternity house on Cornell's campus in Ithaca, New York. The university, she said in the lawsuit, failed to protect her. Cornell conducted an internal investigation after the incident, but no one was charged.

Since the lawsuit was filed on September 16, the case has generated widespread commentary on social media. Some have criticized Cornell and the district attorney's office, while others have questioned the allegations.

Tompkins County District Attorney Matthew Van Houten, who handled the original case, said his office has reopened it and plans to present evidence to a grand jury amid public questions about why no criminal charges were initially filed.

Van Houten said in a statement that his office is considering bringing criminal charges related to the incident, though the allegations in the civil complaint are "dramatically different" from the woman's statement to police two years ago — in her statement to authorities, she did not allege she was drugged without her knowledge or that she did not consent to engage in sexual acts.

Cornell's Chi Phi chapter was barred from campus after the alleged incident in 2024. The woman who accused the men of sexual assault dropped out of Cornell University, according to her attorney.

One of the most chilling allegations of the lawsuit is that a message was sent to a Chi Phi Snapchat group at about 1:42 a.m., inviting members to the room to abuse Doe, who was heavily under the influence of drugs and alcohol. The lawsuit alleges that more men then entered the room and that the assault continued for several hours until Doe lost consciousness.

"There were so many people that could have prevented it and they didn't. I've never seen anything like it," an attorney for Jane Doe, Thomas Giuffra, told CBS.

Giuffra pointed out that two of the seven accused fraternity members were expelled, while the others received lesser disciplinary measures.

"Not one of these guys was arrested," Giuffra told the network.

Many people, from US politicians to Hollywood actors, have spoken out about the case in recent days. US Representative Alexandria Ocasio-Cortez, a New York Democrat, condemned what she described as a "culture of rape" protected by elite institutions.

"When I read what happened at Cornell, those men posted in a group chat. That means it was normal to them. That means it was acceptable to them. This is a culture, and what we must do is understand that we cannot protect those spaces anymore," she said Sunday at a forum near Cornell's campus.

British actress Florence Pugh described the allegations as "horrific" on social media and expressed her support for the student involved.

Pugh, who is currently on a promotional tour for her upcoming Netflix series "East of Eden," wrote a lengthy statement on Instagram, saying that "Colleges/schools/universities repeatedly fail women around the world. You build men that get taught through time and time again that they can do extreme harm and that they won't be harmed in return."

"Those who protect and impose light sentences on men who rape and destroy lives should be punished and closely scrutinized. Why are you protecting someone so fiercely?" she wrote.

American actor Josh Gad, best known as the voice of Olaf in Disney's "Frozen" films, also commented on the Cornell case, criticizing what he described as insufficient disciplinary consequences for the students accused of sexual assault. In an Instagram post, Gad said sexual violence should not be treated as a mistake or addressed with limited disciplinary action by a university.

He argued that the alleged conduct had "destroyed a life" and called for greater accountability. Edited by: Joshua Lang

#1253. Why red-hot St Johnstone striker Josh Fowler is Scottish football's answer to Jamie Vardy

📅 26 Oct 2026 | other | Source

By CALUM CROWE, SCOTTISH DEPUTY CHIEF SPORTS WRITER

Published: 17:12 BST, 23 October 2026 | Updated: 17:26 BST, 23 October 2026

Had things turned out differently a few years ago, Derek McInnes could have been the manager who gave Josh Fowler his first big break in professional football.

Back in the summer of 2023, whilst in charge of Kilmarnock, McInnes took the young striker on a pre-season trip to Northern Ireland.

This was at a time when Fowler had just scored 49 goals in 78 games across two seasons with Beith Juniors.

Killie were not the only top-flight club keeping tabs on him, with Fowler also going on trial with Motherwell during a pre-season trip to the Netherlands.

In the end, neither club decided to take a punt on a young man who was still only 21 years old and who had been working as a joiner.

It could easily have been a sliding doors moment, the kind which sees a young player perhaps start to feel like their chance of making it as a professional has gone.

St Johnstone striker Josh Fowler is joint-top scorer in the Scottish Premiership

But Fowler chose a different route. He was a man in demand. He would eventually leave Beith to embark on spells with Gulf United in Dubai then St Albans Dinamo in Australia before heading back to Dubai City.

He returned home in 2025 for a spell at Queen’s Park which saw him score 19 goals in 25 matches and become top scorer in the Championship, before eventually signing for St Johnstone in January earlier this year.

The goals are now starting to flow in Perth as well. Fowler hit the winner in a 1-0 triumph over Aberdeen a fortnight ago and netted again in a 1-1 draw with Dundee United last week.

He has five goals in his last six matches. All in all, six goals in nine matches so far this season sees him arrive at Ibrox this afternoon to take on McInnes and Rangers as the league’s joint-top scorer.

It is quite a tale. A striker with electric pace plucked from obscurity further down the leagues, before eventually going on to score goals by the bucketload in the top flight, he could well be Scottish football’s answer to Jamie Vardy.

Reflecting on Fowler’s journey, Chris Irving, the club secretary at Beith for the past decade, said: ‘The first thing I would say about Josh is that he is a really humble guy. He doesn’t forget his roots.

Fowler's nomadic career thus far has taken him to Dubai and Australia before his move to Perth

‘Even now, he still keeps in touch and takes an interest in how Beith are doing. I think there is a lot to be said for that kind of humility.

‘I can vividly remember Josh arriving at Beith as a young boy a few years ago. To be honest, the signing kind of came about by chance as much as anything.

‘Josh was working as an apprentice joiner and his lecturer at Cardonald College was Danny Shearer. Danny was our club president at the time, so Josh just asked him if he could come down for a trial.

‘I think he trained for a couple of nights then his first game was a pre-season friendly against Lugar Boswell Thistle which we won 8-0.

‘Josh played out wide on the left wing in that game and I remember speaking to our manager Bryan Young, who seemed to be a bit unsure whether we should sign him.

‘I basically said to Bryan: “Listen, we’ve got to sign him before someone else does”. I think the fact he then went on to score nearly 50 goals over two seasons probably vindicated that decision.

‘From what I could gather, I think Killie and Motherwell both liked him when they had him on trial. Why they didn’t sign him, I don’t know. You would have to ask them.

On his return from a second spell in Dubai, Fowler immediately caught the eye at Queen's Park

‘But, just generally, I think there is maybe still a slight reluctance from Premiership clubs to take a chance on a player from lower down the leagues.

‘Some people still have this idea about the Juniors from 20, 30, 40 years ago that it’s all just long ball and kick-and-rush. But it’s not like that at all.

‘I’ve been involved with Beith for a long time now and I can tell you the overall quality and the level is far, far better than most people would give it credit for.

‘Josh is the perfect example of how you can find a hidden gem if you are willing to look down the leagues and take a chance on someone.

‘Listen, you never know, if he can maintain this form and finish the season as top scorer in the country, Sebastien Pocognoli could have a look at him for Scotland. I don’t see why not. What a story it would be.’

Given the way that Fowler has continually thrived every time he’s moved up the ladder to a higher level, it would be foolish to rule out a Scotland call-up.

Could Sebastien Pocognoli be tempted to cap Fowler's rapid rise with a Scotland call-up?

He is a striker with blistering pace, clever movement, and has an eye for goal. In case anyone hasn’t noticed, Scotland are not blessed with many strikers who can tick those boxes.

Since taking charge, Pocognoli has spoken of a desire to freshen the squad up and to try and bring through a newer generation of players.

Fowler is still only 24. All of his best years are ahead of him. There will be some, of course, who get sniffy about the idea of a St Johnstone player being called up to the Scotland squad. But Josh McPake would be a prime example of a player who has continued to blossom, having shone for Saints as well.

Conner McGlinchey was a close friend of Fowler’s during their time together at Beith and he believes it’s only a matter of time before clubs from England start taking notice.

‘My first impression of Josh was: “Wow! He is rapid”,’ said McGlinchey. ‘Don’t get me wrong, he was quite raw. But he was so quick and he was confident as well.

‘He was quite off-the-cuff with a lot of the things he did. In his first season, he was playing on instinct a lot of the time.

‘Then, in his second season, he went on to another level. In that second season he was easily the best striker in the league by a mile. He was lethal.

Fowler will lead the line for Simo Valakari's St Johnstone against Rangers at Ibrox this weekend

‘I was vice-captain at the time and me and a couple of boys used to car share with him. We knew there was interest in him.

‘Given the start he’s had to the season with St Johnstone, top scorer in the league, I would guarantee you there are clubs looking at him right now.

‘Especially if he goes and scores against Rangers at Ibrox this weekend, an English Championship club will come in for him in January. Clubs down south will always take a chance on someone who can score goals.’

Fowler has emerged as one of the feel-good stories in Scottish football over the past year or so.

A rough diamond plucked from the lower leagues, now thriving in the top flight.

His path has been anything but conventional. But he has proven he can cut it at the top level in Scotland. A young man who continues to take everything in his stride, it will be fascinating to see just how far he can go.

#1249. El Nino is a 'postcard from the future' — and could cause 451,000 excess heat deaths

📅 26 Oct 2026 | other | Source

The current El Nino could cause 451,000 excess heat deaths. Researchers warn it offers a glimpse of temperatures that may become normal within 20 years.

Peruvian fishermen gave El Nino its name — "the little boy" or Christ Child — after noticing unusually warm Pacific waters around Christmas that caused the fish they depended on to suddenly become scarce.

But what those fishermen observed locally can have consequences thousands of kilometers away. El Nino can shift temperature and rainfall patterns around the world, bringing extreme heat and drought to some regions and heavy rain and flooding to others.

Researchers now estimate that the unusually high temperatures linked to the current El Nino could cause around 451,000 excess deaths worldwide from June 2026 to February 2027.

The event is a "postcard from the future," Tamma Carleton, head of research at the University of Chicago's Climate Impact Lab, told DW. Land temperatures over the coming months are forecast to run about 1.2 degrees C (2.1 F) above normal.

"For the average person, that's roughly the warming climate change is projected to bring about 20 years from now, arriving all at once," Carleton said.

Normally, winds blowing west across the Pacific push warm surface water towards Asia and Australia, allowing colder water to rise near South America. During El Nino, which occurs erratically every two to seven years, those winds weaken. Less cold water then rises, and much more of the Pacific stays unusually warm.

The warmer water heats the air above it and fuels rainfall over the central and eastern Pacific. The rising air spreads high in the atmosphere, sending waves that disrupt weather worldwide.

"It is like throwing a stone into a lake," climate researcher Fred Kucharski of the Abdus Salam International Centre for Theoretical Physics in Trieste, Italy, told DW. "Initially the surface perturbation is local, but then the energy spreads all over the lake through wave propagation."

Kucharski said the effects can cross the tropics within one week and reach higher latitudes in about two. Where the waves travel determines whether a region becomes hotter, cooler, wetter or drier.

The current El Nino developed in June and has strengthened rapidly since. It is now on course to become an exceptionally strong — sometimes called "super" — El Nino.

The Climate Impact Lab says its estimate covers only the first nine months of El Nino, as far as the current seasonal forecasts allow. The event is expected to continue into Northern Hemisphere spring, and its effect on global temperatures could last longer.

"We expect the final number to be meaningfully higher than what we are currently reporting here," Carleton said.

The estimate of 451,000 excess deaths covers deaths linked to the direct health effects of unusually high temperatures associated with El Nino.

Extreme heat strains the heart and kidneys, makes it harder for the body to maintain a safe temperature and can worsen cardiovascular, respiratory and other illnesses. As a result, the underlying condition is often recorded as the cause of death rather than the heat that aggravated it.

"Most heat deaths never show up in official statistics as heat deaths," Carleton said.

El Nino can also alter rainfall, contributing to drought in some regions and heavy rain and flooding in others.

Floods can kill, contaminate drinking water and spread disease. Drought can damage harvests and cause water and food shortages.

Deaths from these other indirect effects are not included in the 451,000 projection.

The Climate Impact Lab expects some of the largest increases in deaths in the Sahel, South and Southeast Asia and parts of Latin America. The highest numbers of fatalities are predicted for Nigeria, Sudan, Niger, Chad, India, Indonesia and Brazil.

Many are projected to occur between December and February, meaning much of the expected toll still lies ahead.

"Heat headlines usually focus on Europe and North America," Carleton said. "This event's toll is overwhelmingly in lower-income, already-hot countries, where people have the fewest ways to adapt and where these deaths are least likely to be counted."

Some people who die during extreme heat are already frail. This raises an important question: Is the heat cutting their lives substantially short, or merely bringing forward deaths that would have occurred days or weeks later — an effect known as mortality displacement?

The underlying research uses annual rather than daily death rates, which largely filters out deaths brought forward by only days or weeks.

Ben Armstrong, an emeritus professor at the London School of Hygiene and Tropical Medicine, said he could not verify the 451,000 figure. But because the estimate is based on annual mortality data, it largely excludes deaths brought forward by only days or weeks. If the figure is correct, he said, most of those counted "would have lived at least six months longer."

Scientists have long estimated the death toll from extreme temperatures retrospectively. Better seasonal forecasting is increasingly allowing researchers to look ahead instead.

To produce the estimate, researchers used earlier work showing how death rates change during hot weather in different parts of the world. They combined it with seasonal forecasts showing how hot each place is expected to become.

"Putting the two together lets us estimate the human cost of this event while there's still time to do something about it, rather than counting the deaths after the fact," Carleton said.

Heat warnings, cooling centers and hydration stations, home visits to vulnerable people, protections for outdoor workers and additional health care capacity could all reduce mortality.

The researchers believe targeted interventions could save tens of thousands of lives.

For Carleton, the forecast is a warning to prepare now for the heat that climate change could make commonplace.

"This El Nino gives us a look at future weather in the 2040s," Carleton said. "These temperatures will become normal in 20 years, giving us time and reason to begin investing in adaptive capacity now."

#1247. Harry Kane shows Arsenal what they're missing at striker as Bayern hold Arsenal in Champions League draw

📅 26 Oct 2026 | other | Source

If not listed, please contact your TV provider.

Arsenal have long since moved past the stage where they want any more lessons from Harry Kane, and yet he insists on delivering them anyway. Though one of his counterparts in Arsenal blue might have played a critical role in the equalizer, this 2-2 draw came with indications aplenty of the value of a striker who can do it all.

Like the North London derbies of old, Kane played the role of spoiler supreme for the Gunners, his 16th goal in 21 meetings an expertly struck penalty that capped an outstanding display from Bayern Munich's No. 9. The penalty he scored was the relatively easy part. Kane did plenty else. He had won that spot kick by finding space to meet Konrad Laimer's deflected drag back, Jurrien Timber making the fatal error of having arms while defending his penalty area in a UEFA-organized match.

Scoring was not the half of what Kane brought tonight. Shorn of his primary running mates -- Luis Diaz fit enough only for the bench, Michael Olise suspended and Jamal Musiala still unavailable -- Kane took on even more of the creative burden. It is sometimes easy to think that any false nine can put up a dozen-plus assists to go alongside his goal a game when he is aiming for the best wide forwards. Today he was putting it on a plate for repurposed left back Alphonso Davies, his through ball delivering the sort of opening you'd back even the most agricultural defender (which Davies is not) to roll it in.

With no Joshua Kimmich either, it fell on Kane to help out a youthful midfield with the ball progression too. Kimmich, Toni Kroos, Luka Modric: even the best ball-playing midfielders of their generation would have done well to ping what was effectively a goal kick following an Arsenal corner, a 70-yard pearler that pierced the best defense in the world in a flash.

This truly was a striker who could do it all, a worthy Ballon d'Or winner. As for his counterparts at Arsenal, they did their part. It should be noted that the Gunners would likely have suffered their first defeat in the league phase of the Champions League had it not been for a magnificently measured touch by Viktor Gyokeres with his back to goal, easing the ball into the path of Declan Rice, who thundered in a merited equalizer for the Premier League champions.

Kai Havertz too offered value of a sort. Arsenal were insistent that they would not play through Bayern's man-to-man press and would instead go over it. Havertz proved to be a valuable outlet in that regard, ensuring that Arsenal had a bit more of the territory, possession, shots and non-penalty xG. Mikel Arteta's game plan was a well-executed one, the sort that if replicated in the away leg of a big knockout tie at this ground, would have Arsenal firmly in the contest.

Arsenal have proven once more that they belong in the inner tier of Champions League contenders, that a knockout tie between them and any of Bayern, Barcelona and Paris Saint-Germain might well be decided by the sort of "magic moment" that Arteta spoke of when eliminated at the Allianz Arena in 2024. Eberechi Eze and Rice delivered those, drilled efforts from tight angles to beat an otherwise impressive Manuel Neuer. Martin Odegaard was on song too with a brilliant assist for the opener. Bukayo Saka started brightly before what appeared to be a thigh injury.

And maybe Gyokeres' assist had a little bit of the supernatural to it. Stil, it is not the sort of magic you expect from the striker of a Champions League contender. Arsenal's strikers need to be scoring. Scratch that, they need to be shooting. Across 16 games in all competitions this season, the starting center forwards for the English champions have delivered 17 shots, 15 in 677 minutes for Kai Havertz and two in 330 for Viktor Gyokeres. On a per 90 basis, Arteta is getting slightly less shot production from his senior strikers as Nottingham Forest get from center back Murillo. It took Kane four games to take his 17th shot. Against admittedly inferior Bundesliga opposition to the average Premier League backline, Kane has 50 shots in 802 minutes.

Arteta has always viewed his team as one where the shooting and scoring burden is shared around. When he challenged a player to score two or three goals a game it was Saka, not his center forwards. Arsenal's idealized striker was probably the fully fit Gabriel Jesus they had in the second half of 2022, a player who can drift from one flank to another, offering no fixed reference point and bringing others into play. Still, at peak fitness, Jesus averaged over three and a half shots per 90. He's a center forward. That's what they're supposed to do.

Partly this may be a question of service. Arsenal's moves to goal have slowed right down this season, an average of 7.3 passes per scoring sequence, the longest in Europe's top five leagues. That is great for a late arriving Rice or even Odegaard, less so for the reference-point center forward.

Arsenal will never play in a way that best suits Gyokeres in particular. Their opponents are generally packed so deep that there is not the space in the channel he craves. When they don't it is usually because they are applying the sort of suffocating press that makes it impossible to accurately release a striker over the top. Gyokeres might have loved Bayern's worst moments in the first half, when Odegaard got space to look up and ping passes in behind.

Then again, no opponent plays in a way that suits Kane too. Ousmane Dembele and Raphinha will have every tactical adjustment imaginable put in place to negate them. Does anyone feel compelled to do that for Arsenal's attackers?

Tonight Bayern's No. 9 had Ezri Konsa or Bruno Guimaraes tracking him high and low, ready to apply the blitz on one heavy touch. It never came. That is the difference between the best and what Arsenal have leading their line. They make the game suit them.

Wearable computing promises to transform human interaction with information, yet conventional graphical user interfaces (GUIs) rely on planar, rigid displays incompatible with textiles, which are composed of one-dimensional fibres. Despite recent progress1,2 in flexible and fibre-based displays, bridging this fundamental dimensional mismatch remains challenging because existing approaches do not naturally provide the digitally addressable, high-throughput, information-rich interfaces required for interactive GUIs. Here we present a digital pixel fibre integrating addressable pixels, a common propagation-axis interconnection for scalable in-fibre circuitry and a coaxial sensing layer for fibre-level touch localization. Microelectronic components and a touch-gated resistive path system form a linear circuit that acts as both an interactive display backbone and a computational bus, while peripheral multimodal fibre sensors and actuators enable physiological, inertial, optical, and acoustic input and output. We integrate these fibres into the forearm portion of a garment sleeve to realize a GUI with a 96 × 54-pixel display, 30-frame-per-second refresh rate and 8-bit colour depth. It supports dynamic graphics, integrated audio–video playback, wireless data exchange and interactive computing functions, while maintaining the flexibility, washability, breathability and mechanical robustness of everyday textiles. By merging display, sensing and computation within a single fibre, this work establishes a scalable platform for intrinsically interactive textiles, opening pathways toward garments that operate as fully integrated computational systems.

#1242. Martin O'Neill insists Celtic are no Jekyll and Hyde team after latest European no-show

📅 26 Oct 2026 | other | Source

By JOHN MCGARRY, SCOTTISH CHIEF SPORTS FEATURE WRITER

Published: 18:00 BST, 23 October 2026 | Updated: 18:00 BST, 23 October 2026

Martin O’Neill has hit back at the mounting suggestions that Celtic are now a Jekyll and Hyde team under his management.

Thursday’s home defeat to Celta Vigo in the Europa League left the Parkhead men without a win in their three group matches, with a dismal run on the continental stage extending to the collapse away to LASK in the Champions League play-off.

O’Neill’s men are now sitting in 35th place in the 36-team group – with FC Ararat-Armenia the only team beneath them.

Celtic, though, remain top of the Premiership heading into the weekend, with a home loss to Rangers the only reverse they’ve suffered in nine matches.

Such contrasting form has led to many observers questioning why a side which remains the team to beat domestically is repeatedly failing to turn up in Europe.

O’Neill, though, feels the disparity is less to do with his side’s mentality and more to do with the step up in the standard of opposition.

Martin O'Neill was frustrated after Celtic suffered a third consecutive Europa League defeat

Celta Vigo defender Javi Rodriguez celebrates after claiming his second goal of the evening

‘We are up against reasonable opposition. I thought the other night was the night to get off the mark.

‘Is Europe a step too far for us? I’m hoping not. It might be right at the end of it all but I don’t think so.

‘You want to compete. I’m not saying it should be an absolute joy, but it should be a spur to play in these games against really decent opposition.

‘There’s no question the European games are a step up. They (Celta) are playing in the Spanish top division and have some decent players.

‘They have some younger lads and then the experience of (Iago) Aspas and sometimes you need a bit of experience around you.

‘They are decent, but I believed they were beatable the other night, and that’s the disappointment. But you do learn, even I am still learning.’

Celtic next face Besiktas at home with trips to face Portuguese outfit Torreense and Omonia arriving before Christmas. The group campaign concludes with a home match against Marseille and an away game with Union SG in January.

Although aware that his side will probably need to pick up ten points from those five matches to stand a chance of progressing, O’Neill is not without hope.

‘I know it’s easy for me to say, but Celtic will be back,’ he stressed.

‘You can definitely learn a lot from the European games. Like last season against Midtjylland, we were physically blown away. But we fought back and got some good results. That’s what we want to try to do again.

‘It’s easy for me to say, but if we can get up and running there’s still a chance. It didn’t happen the other night, but we’re still not out of it in terms of the points we can gain.

‘But each game that passes when we don’t get on the scoreboard, it becomes more difficult.’

O'Neill insists his Celtic side can still qualify but concedes they need positive results soon

Celtic now need to dust themselves down and go again at Easter Road on Sunday, O’Neill’s men bounced back in style from the loss to Benfica with a 7-1 demolition of Hearts and the manager is looking for a similar response.

‘You can’t have excuses going into Sunday,’ O’Neill stressed. ‘That’s how the club is built. You need to be competing.

‘We have to physically recover and get ourselves right for the game at Hibs. I don’t think the other night will cause us any serious psychological damage.

‘You never know, it’s a tough old schedule for us. It’s been a tough week with going to Portugal and so on. But no excuses. We are where we are and we have to try to win. Sunday becomes a big game for us.’

Not included in the European squad, Jota will be available for Easter Road.

Cameron Carter-Vickers and Alistair Johnston are in contention to return after injuries although Auston Trusty will be absent for a spell following the death of his father.

‘I think having those two lads and Jota will definitely be a real plus for us in the coming weeks,’ said O’Neill.

‘Cameron and Alistair are coming back from hamstring injuries so we will have a look and see if they are available. Hopefully we’ll have Yang (Hyun-jun) back in a few weeks as well.

‘Obviously, you are aware of Trusty’s situation. He’s very, very down, which is totally understandable.

‘We’ll have to see what he wants to do. I don’t want to talk out of turn but I think there will be a celebration of his father’s life that he’ll want to go out to. We’ll give him time and see how he is.’
