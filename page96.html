#1757. Vignola Double Seals Victory For Blues

📅 26 Oct 2026 | other | Source

Two outstanding goals from Ornella Vignola guided Everton to a comfortable win in the Subway Players Cup, as the Blues beat Newcastle United 2-0.

After a goalless first half, Vignola came to the rescue for Everton with a long-range effort and curling strike being the difference at Goodison Park.

Everton now have consecutive wins in the cup after beating Birmingham City in their opening group stage game.

Scott Phelan made nine changes to the team that faced London City Lionesses on Sunday, with Rion Ishikawa and Vignola being the two to keep their place in the starting XI.

Noemie Mouchon and Jutta Rantala both made their long-awaited starts for the Club after the duo signed from Leicester City over the summer.

In between the sticks, Canada’s Rylee Foster-Inman replaced Courtney Brosnan, making her second consecutive appearance in the Cup.

Club captain, Megan Finnigan, returned to the pitch after missing Sunday’s clash in the capital, and was partnered alongside Hannah Silcock.

After impressing from the bench in the last game, Aurora Galli started as well as Hannah Cain, who earned her spot in the lineup following her magnificent goal on the weekend.

The Blues threatened early on down the left-hand side, and it was Cain who had the first opportunity of the match. Her effort whistled past the far post after she was picked out superbly by Silcock’s lofted, long-ball.

15 minutes into the first half, Everton were denied the lead after Mouchon’s first-time finish was ruled offside. The France-youth international was first to the rebound after Silcock tested Anna Tamminen from close range.

Shortly after, Mouchon continued to cause problems for the Lasses, as she held up the ball in the center of the box and played a short pass to Cain, who struck the post with a side-footed effort.

Scott Phelan was able to talk to his players 28 minutes into the game after Newcastle’s Leanne Kiernan suffered a knock, and the pressure continued to pile on the away side for the remainder of the first 45 minutes.

Minutes before the end of the first half, Tamminen was yet again called upon as she parried away Vignola’s long-range strike.

The first half drew to a close, with the Toffees looking to break the deadlock in the second 45 minutes.

Newcastle were forced to substitute Tamminen for Kayla Rendell after the break, whilst Blues keeper, Foster-Inman, did not have to make a save until 52 minutes.

Whilst Newcastle began to counter-attack effectively, The Blues were still creating the more clear-cut chances.

Cain was again at the forefront of Everton attacks, and her whipped cross found Galli on the edge of the box, but her effort blazed over the crossbar on the hour mark.

With the game still goalless, Phelan looked to the bench for reinforcements and brought on Clare Wheeler, Maz Pacheco, and Yuka Momiki.

But it was Vignola who took the lead for the Blues. Her fantastic long-range effort from outside the box rifling into the top-right corner, leaving Kayla Rendell rooted to the spot.

With 10 minutes to go, the Spain international added a second after Wheeler’s cut-back cross found Everton's number 18 unmarked.

Her tidy finish into the bottom right-hand corner eliminated any chance of a nervy end to proceedings, as Everton continued to pass the ball around nicely for the remainder of the game.

Following six minutes of stoppage time, the final whistle sounded to confirm an impressive second-half display from Everton.

After two consecutive wins in the Player Cup, and five on home soil in all competitions, the Blues will now have their eye on automatic qualification for the next round.

Phelan's side return to the Grand Old Lady on Sunday afternoon (2pm) as Aston Villa make the journey from the second city.

Former Chelsea and England star Ryan Bertrand is the central figure behind a new investment group pitching to buy into AFC Wimbledon – with plans to pump £87million in to take the fan-owned club to the next level.

League One Wimbledon announced last week that they had identified a preferred investor in their effort to raise new capital, after admitting their ownership structure is 'not sustainable'.

The south-west London phoenix club, founded in 2002, rose from the ninth tier of English football to the third but narrowly escaped relegation in their tenth season in the summer, a year after announcing an operating loss of £600,000 and that projected finances 'will become very tight'.

Now, Daily Mail Sport can confirm Bertrand, 37, is the central football figure of the 'preferred investor' group – Sloane Street Capital (SSC), which is backed by British tech entrepreneur Omar Mir.

SSC plan to oversee football operations at Wimbledon with Bertrand in place as senior football adviser. They are offering £16m for 34 per cent of the shares in AFCW PLC and promise £71m of investment over five seasons while allowing fan ownership and control to continue by never gaining majority voting control.

'Wimbledon is a club with real football heritage,' Bertrand told Daily Mail Sport. 'There's the story of what happened before but what's happened since is truly unrivalled. This is not "The Ryan Bertrand Show". It's about more ideas and empowering the club by partnering with the fans.'

Ryan Bertrand is central to the preferred AFC Wimbledon investment offer (Source: Ryan Bertrand/Instagram)

Bertrand retired from football in 2024 and has been working in player representation and talent identification after selling the Fintech investment firm he co-founded. Sources close to the former left-back claim players he has identified for various clubs now have a market valuation of £332m.

A Champions League winner with Chelsea, he has long dreamed of entering football club ownership and held talks over a move to buy Charlton Athletic previously. This summer he completed an in-depth course at Harvard University on the Business of Entertainment, Media and Sports.

SSC's proposal is that £8m of the first £16m invested is in football operations and infrastructure. Bertrand believes his expertise can both unearth talented youngsters and help identify senior performance players with proven experience.

Mir, the backer behind the group's funding, is the Global Strategic Advisor to the Co-Founder/CEO of World Wide Technology, the largest privately-owned technology company in the US with $20billion (£15bn) in annual revenue, and an adviser to the King's Trust on AI and Advanced Technology. He is the group's sole funder.

Stuart Fiertz, co-founder of the investment manager Cheyne Capital, and football agent Oliver Ward complete the SSC group.

Mir owns 95 per cent of SSC and is its only funder. The other five per cent would be split equally on completion between Bertrand, Fiertz and Ward.

Bertrand, 37, retired from football in 2024 after playing for Chelsea, Southampton and Leicester

Wimbledon say they spoke to more than 20 interested parties and Bertrand's group emerged as the 'strongest fit'.

The offer will now move to a vote by the 6,266 members of The Dons Trust, which closes on November 17 with an announcement on the result a day later. For the vote to pass, at least 50 per cent of eligible members must vote, 75 per cent of votes cast must be in favour and 40 per cent of all eligible members must vote 'yes'.
