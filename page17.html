#3336. Should the Chiefs trade for Garrett Wilson? Why blockbuster deal makes sense, and why it probably won't happen

📅 26 Oct 2026 | other | Source

If not listed, please contact your TV provider.

The NFL trade deadline is less than two weeks away, and several stars could be moved prior to that Nov. 10 date. Sometimes the trade deadline is more "bark than bite," but we've already seen a team punt on their former No. 4 overall pick wide receiver in Marvin Harrison Jr. Could we be in for some fireworks?

Among the hypothetical buyers at the trade deadline are the Kansas City Chiefs. They are right back to being a contender following that 6-11 season in 2025. In fact, they are the first team since the 2019 San Francisco 49ers to start 5-0 following an 11-loss season. It's in large part due to the play of new running back Kenneth Walker III, who has rushed for more yards (665) than any other player in their first five games with a team in NFL history.

Imagine adding another playmaker to this Chiefs offense. CBS Sports' Russell Wilson has one in mind, and that's New York Jets wideout Garrett Wilson.

"If I'm the Kansas City Chiefs, I got the all-star running back in the backfield, Ken Walker, which we've never had before," Wilson said on "Set Hut."  "All these years we've never had a running back like this. And guess what that does? Play action opens up. What Mahomes does better than arguably anybody in the world is throw the ball down the field. So if I'm thinking about this, who would I go get, I'm going to go get Garrett Wilson.

"If the Jets keep losing, I'm going to go get Garrett Wilson from the 'New York football Jets,' because I believe that Garrett Wilson down the field -- with his ability, he's a smart guy he learns quickly, he'd be great in an Andy Reid style of offense. Patrick Mahomes and him, how he gets in and out of his breaks, they haven't had a receiver like him. "

Garrett Wilson to Chiefs? @DangeRussWilson thinks the Chiefs should trade for Garrett Wilson pic.twitter.com/PRHRrVVLpq

So, should the Chiefs swing a massive trade for a WR1 like Wilson? Let's take a look.

The benefits of adding Wilson are easy to identify. He enters Week 7 ranked 14th in receiving yards (418) despite having average quarterback play. Well, at least not Mahomes-level quarterback play. In fact, Geno Smith is one of the best quarterbacks Wilson has ever had. Wilson had to begin his NFL career with Zach Wilson and Mike White. Yet, he found a way to cross 1,000 yards receiving in each of his first three seasons!

Wilson actually became the fifth player in NFL history to record 80 receptions and 1,000 receiving yards in each of his first three seasons, joining Ja'Marr Chase, Justin Jefferson, Michael Thomas and Odell Beckham Jr. And again, did it mostly with Zach Wilson. The Jets' quarterback situation last year with Justin Fields, Tyrod Taylor and Brady Cook was so bad, that Wilson ended up leading the Jets in receiving with 395 yards -- in SEVEN games played. It was the fewest receiving yards by a team leader since 1976, according to CBS Sports Research, excluding the 1982 strike season. Wilson has already surpassed that total in six games this year.

So, this playmaker can be productive no matter how bad his situation is. Imagine giving Wilson a three-time Super Bowl MVP and two-time NFL MVP at quarterback in an Andy Reid offense.

That brings me to my next point, which Russell Wilson brought up about play action. When the Chiefs signed Walker, the first thing I thought about was how he would open up the passing game. Granted, I didn't know he was going to come in and be Barry Sanders, but I did think he was going to be the best running back Kansas City has had in quite some time, which was really going to open up things for everyone else.

The Chiefs passing offense currently ranks in the top 10 with 254.2 yards per game, but I'm not sure it's really felt like that. No offense to a legend, but 37-year-old Travis Kelce probably shouldn't be leading your team in receiving through five games. His 299 yards receiving rank 35th in the NFL. Behind him is Tyquan Thornton with 219 yards, who is expected to miss extensive time after suffering a serious ankle injury earlier this month, then Xavier Worthy is third with 206 yards receiving. Those 206 yards are tied for 76th in the NFL. Rashee Rice has caught 13 passes for 190 yards and one touchdown through four games in what has statistically been his worst season thus far, and he's dealing with a hamstring injury.

The Chiefs have a legitimate need at wide receiver, and a legitimate need for a WR1 as well. If Kansas City added a talent like Wilson, the Chiefs would definitely be looked at as the favorites in the AFC -- and for good reason.

Are you willing to trade at least a first-round pick for Wilson? That's probably what it would cost. Another hurdle to trading for Wilson is his contract and the Chiefs' cap situation.

Some believe the salary cap is a myth, and in certain circumstances, it can feel like that. But Wilson just signed a four-year, $130 million extension that includes $90 million guaranteed 15 months ago. He's technically under contract for five more seasons, including 2026. Currently, the Chiefs have just $2.2 million in available funds, per Over The Cap, which ranks No. 29 in the NFL. Sure, you can move money around, but the Chiefs are also projected to be nearly $12 million over the cap in 2027. His cap hit goes up to $23.3 million in 2027, and then north of $35 million in 2028-2030.

While we are talking about finances, the Jets would take on a dead cap hit of $31.2 million to trade Wilson, while also losing $21.2 million in "savings." So, the Chiefs would have to REALLY sweeten the offer for the Jets to make this kind of move. I'm not really sure it makes sense.

Do the Chiefs really need to give up premium draft capital and take on a massive contract with their financial situation while already boasting what is statistically the best offense in the NFL?

Ultimately, I don't think the Jets are going to trade Wilson. They had their fire sale last year with Sauce Gardner and Quinnen Williams, while Wilson got paid. The dead cap hit/loss in savings don't make sense for New York, which would make a potential deal even tougher for the Chiefs.

Maybe if the Jets fall to 2-7, they could reconsider their situation?

Wilson in Kansas City would be a dream fit, but the Chiefs could use a cheaper option. Maybe that Tyreek Hill reunion is in the cards, although the NFL's investigation complicates that. The names are less sexy, but perhaps Brian Thomas Jr., Jauan Jennings or Jerry Jeudy could be potential trade targets for the Chiefs if they want to go that route.

Pitcher Trevor Megill brought the song to the Brewers dugout sometime before Game 2 of the National League Championship Series. Fellow pitchers Shane Drohan and Chad Patrick have mandated it stays in every post-victory playlist.

"I have never suggested that a season like this season is a success," Steinbrenner said. "I am often asked, 'Was the entire season a failure?' Winning 93 games is not a failure. Playing in the Division Series is not a failure. It became a failure because we did not do well. I don't believe I've ever called a season that we didn't win a world championship successful."

New York's offense went in the tank after captain Aaron Judge was placed on the injured list with a rib fracture in early June. Judge's last game before going on the shelf was May 31, which was effectively the end of his season. He returned in September, played seven games, then suffered a calf strain that sidelined him the rest of the year. Judge did not play in the postseason.

"The players didn't live up to their end of the bargain in the playoffs," Steinbrenner added. "... We're going to look at everything (this offseason). I was disappointed with the offense. They were inconsistent for long periods of time."

No decisions regarding the future of the coaching staff, including hitting coach James Rowson, have been made yet, and neither Cashman nor Steinbrenner would get into offseason roster-building strategies. The Yankees have one of the game's best pitching staffs, but the offense was lacking, and Judge's absence was only part of that. As good as he is, he is just one player.

"Do I like sitting here trying to explain why? No, but I am going to analyze, in a very objective way, what we think went wrong," Steinbrenner said about falling short of the World Series again. "... I'm upset sitting here and having to answer these questions. Absolutely, and I know how upset our fans are, but believe me, we're going to be doing everything we can."

The Yankees have not won the World Series since 2009 and their 17-year championship drought is tied with 1979-95 for the longest in franchise history. Here now is what you need to know about Cashman's and Steinbrenner's postmortem press conference.

As expected, Cashman will return for what will be his 30th season as general manager in 2027. Cashman's contract was set to expire this offseason, but he confirmed the two sides have agreed to a new deal, and they just need to finalize details before putting pen to paper. "We're locked and loaded," Cashman said about his contract status.

"Cash is great every season," Steinbrenner said. "I mean, I think the roster he put together, the team that we rolled out of spring training with, was as good as any in baseball, in my opinion."

The Yankees have not had a losing season during Cashman's tenure and they've made the postseason nine times in the last 10 seasons. At the same time, they've won three division titles in the last 14 seasons, and the World Series drought is tied for the longest in franchise history. Cashman's recent teams win a lot during the regular season and not much in October.

"The first step of the process is making sure you can punch a ticket and get there," Cashman said about the team's postseason failures. "Then, everything is reset. You need to have as many talented players as you possibly can on both sides of the ball."

Manager Aaron Boone will return for a tenth season in 2027 and Steinbrenner said there was a "weeklong conversation" about Boone's future and whether he should return. Next year is the final season on Boone's contract and there have not been any talks about a contract extension. He will manage next year as a lame duck.

"The inability to score on a consistent basis throughout the course of the entire season, not on the shoulders of Aaron Boone," Steinbrenner said. "... Talking about this season, I thought Boone managed a good season, especially with the pitching staff. We had a lot of additions and subtractions in the rotation throughout the course of the season. Our bullpen was absolutely exhausted by the end of the season. He got through all that and he got us to the playoffs."

Boone also spoke Wednesday and said he's "very comfortable" managing next season without a contract extension.
