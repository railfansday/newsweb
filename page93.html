Published: 12:29 BST, 21 October 2026 | Updated: 15:12 BST, 21 October 2026

Barcelona have reported Paris Saint-Germain to UEFA for the ‘chainsaw massacre’ banner displayed during Tuesday night’s Champions League match in the French capital.

The banner carried the slogan ‘Massacre, Parisien style’ and depicted a blood-spattered masked man in a PSG shirt cutting through a Barcelona crest with a chainsaw.

Pyrotechnics gave the effect of sparks flying as the saw made contact, while the chainsaw also appeared to be covered in blood.

Barcelona have also reported offensive chants being directed towards the club during the game.

Daily Mail Sport understands UEFA will wait to see if the banner is mentioned in the match delegate's report before deciding whether to take action.

As for on the pitch, PSG ran out 4-2 winners in a thrilling game between two of Europe's most in-form sides.

The banner carried the slogan ‘Massacre, Parisien style’ and depicted a blood-spattered masked man in a PSG shirt cutting through a Barcelona crest with a chainsaw

Desire Doure, Lucas Hernandez, Vitinha and Fabian Ruiz were all on target as PSG made it three wins out of three in the Champions League this season, and firmly established their lead at the top of the league standings.

PSG manager Luis Enrique was delighted with what he saw from his players against his former club.

'What I loved is how the players were capable of playing a sensational first half defensively, winning a lot of balls with the high press and creating chances to score more goals,' he said.

'And then we overcame difficult moments in the second half when Barca had 15 minutes when they dominated and were better. But we are a team that adapts to whatever happens on the pitch.

'I think we played aggressively, strong. You can see how difficult it is to press players of this level: Pedri, Lamine, Olmo, Raphinha, (Anthony) Gordon, Espart, (Pau) Cubarsi. They are talented as individuals and as a team. This is the best level we could come up against.'

For Barca, who twice levelled through Xavi Espart and Lamine Yamal, this was their first defeat of the season, bringing an end to their 11-game winning streak.

PSG have been in scintillating form in Europe this season since defending their title in May, beating Manchester City and Barcelona by the same 4-2 scoreline this month.

But Tuesday’s latest victory has come at a cost, as winger Khvicha Kvaratskhelia is set for a long-term lay-off after rupturing his anterior cruciate ligament.

The European champions confirmed Kvaratskhelia, who received extensive treatment early in the second half of the 4-2 triumph at Parc des Princes following a challenge with Xavi Espart, will have surgery 'in the coming days'.

The 25-year-old Georgia captain’s injury setback is a major blow for PSG, who are in pursuit of a third straight Champions League crown.

Kvaratskhelia has emerged as one of the world’s leading forwards since joining the French champions from Napoli for £59million plus add-ons in January 2025.

Atletico Madrid winger Alex Baena has now emerged as a possible target, according to fresh reports.

The Spain international previously attracted Arsenal’s attention before moving from Villarreal to Atletico Madrid, and the north London club are believed to have renewed their interest ahead of the winter window.

According to FootballTransfers, Arsenal have identified Baena as a potential signing for January, with sources in Spain indicating that the Premier League side are revisiting their interest in the 25-year-old.

Given his importance to Simeone and the timing of the proposed move, Arsenal could face demands considerably above that valuation.

Atletico have previously demonstrated a willingness to resist interest in key members of their squad, meaning the Spanish club’s stance would be central to any potential negotiations.

For now, Arsenal’s reported interest does not mean a bid has been submitted or that an agreement is close. The coming weeks should provide a clearer indication of whether the Gunners are prepared to turn their renewed admiration for Baena into a formal January pursuit.

#1815. Rangers transfer news: Nicolas Raskin issues latest update on future, contract decision

📅 26 Oct 2026 | other | Source

By Oliver Thomas | 23 Oct 2026 21:06

Nicolas Raskin is “really happy” at “big club” Rangers, but he is unsure over his long-term future at Ibrox.

The 25-year-old midfielder, who joined the Gers from Standard Liege in January 2023, was tipped to seek pastures new in the summer after representing Belgium at the World Cup.

However, a permanent exit from Glasgow failed to materialise, with Rangers rejecting multiple bids from Turkish giants Besiktas in the closing stages of the transfer window.

After missing three matches at the start of the campaign amid speculation about his future, Raskin has since become a mainstay in Derek McInnes's Rangers team, helping the club win five and draw one of their last six Scottish Premiership fixtures.

Raskin has amassed 156 appearances in total for the Gers across all competitions, and he scored in his most recent outing for the club – a 2-2 league draw with Dundee last weekend.

Ahead of Rangers’ next Premiership match at home to St Johnstone on Saturday, Raskin has opened up on a summer of uncertainty and whether he expected to leave the club.

"Of course, when you're an ambitious player, you want to keep going higher and higher and higher, so I thought there was a point where maybe I could try to go and get a new challenge after the World Cup,” Raskin said as quoted by Sky Sports.

"But like I said, I've never been pushing for something because I'm really happy here, it's a big club.

"This club helped me to join the Belgian national team, which is a big thing, so I'm really calm with that.

"If something comes and everybody would be happy with it, then I would have looked to see if I could do it or not, but I don't need to push anything.

"I'm happy; the manager has been amazing with me since he arrived, so it was good this year to have that support from the manager.

"I was quite calm in the game, just taking it day by day, training after training to see where that would take me."

Asked whether he could sign a new contract with Rangers, with his current deal running until May 2028, Raskin said: "I don't know, it's not up to me. It's up to the club.

"I'm just trying to work every day as hard as I can and try to give a good performance on the pitch and see what happens."

McInnes is also unsure whether Raskin will commit his future to Rangers, adding: "I normally leave those sort of contract discussions between the club, the player and the agent.

"But if there was an opportunity to work with Nico beyond that, then obviously it's something that I would not be against.

"I'm enjoying working with him, but equally I understand players may well have other aspirations.

"Nico said to me that he could have had one or two things that he could have jumped at in the transfer window, but he's keen to be here and be part of a successful team.

"Unless something comes up that really turns his head and really turns Rangers' head, then we keep working together.

"And how long that remains, whether it's to the end of the season or whether it's beyond that, too many things can happen."

Raskin is yet to win a major piece of silverware with Rangers, coming closest in February 2023 – just a month after joining the club – when they lost the Scottish League Cup final to rivals Celtic.

📅 26 Oct 2026 | other | Source

Luciano Spalletti said Juventus were inferior to Lazio after their 3-0 home defeat on Sunday, 18 October, admitting the performance had surprised him. The assessment, reported by Football Italia, came just days after the Bianconeri had beaten Celta Vigo in the Europa League.

Juventus rarely threatened to recover in Turin as Oliver Provstgaard headed Lazio ahead, Gustav Isaksen added a second with an angled finish and Fisayo Dele-Bashiru sealed the result on a late counter-attack. Spalletti said his side had returned from Vigo at 4am on Friday after their 2-1 comeback win, then had time to recover with the performance staff.

He had made six changes, but felt Juventus were slow in both body and thought. His criticism covered the lack of tempo, ideas, running and willingness to work, as well as the team’s weakness in duels and its response when things went wrong. Those concerns followed a previous Juventus defeat that had also prompted scrutiny of the team’s competitive response.

Spalletti was absent from the touchline because of a ban following his altercation with Cagliari’s Fabio Pisacane; assistant Marco Domenichini deputised. Juventus have 11 points after seven league rounds, eight behind the leaders. Football Italia noted that Igor Tudor’s team had one more point at the same stage last season, while Thiago Motta’s side had collected 13.

Supporters who stayed until the final whistle jeered and insulted the squad. Spalletti said the response now had to come on the pitch, rather than the players giving in to events, and identified two areas for review: how he communicates with the team and the working method. He said he needed to examine his relationship with the players and decide what must change.

Speaking to DAZN Italia after the match, Spalletti said Juventus had neither controlled the game nor shown a clear idea of how to shape it. He considered the performance unacceptable and said he would reflect carefully on it. His next task is to determine whether the adjustment lies in his approach to the group, the work itself, or both.

#1808. Declan Rice- the hero that Arsenal need

📅 26 Oct 2026 | other | Source

‘I see him like a lighthouse, he will light (up) the others and improve the others, and make the team better. The way he talks, the ambition he has and the passion for the game is exactly what we need.’ These were the words of Mikel Arteta upon signing Declan Rice in the summer of 2023.

However, this season, it seems as though Arteta has revoked Rice’s lighthouse license. ‘I went to him and I said: ‘You are not the lighthouse anymore. You are something else.’ These were Arteta’s words after the 2-1 victory over Leeds. In this game, Rice assisted his midfield partner Bruno Guimaraes with a deft flick.

A few weeks ago, I wrote about how there had been a clear and obvious attempt to get Bukayo Saka into more optimal shooting positions. I deduce that having failed to secure any one of Rogers, Vinicius or Alvarez during the summer, Arteta and his staff had to consider how to juice more goals from the Arsenal orange with existing personnel.

Christos Tzolis was Arsenal’s only attacking signing and if he simply replaces the output of Trossard and Martinelli from last season, he will have been a wildly successful signing. I think getting Saka into more shooting positions is a simple case of banking on the team’s best attacker to do a little bit more.

When Arteta talked about Declan Rice no longer being a ‘lighthouse’, I think it is an acknowledgement of two things. 1- that Rice is being tasked with doing more in the final third this season and 2- a midfield of him, Bruno and Odegaard ought to operate more like a carousel where all three players are eights in the aggregate.

What I mean by this is that they are all capable of playing as sixes, eights and tens simultaneously within games. There was a move towards greater flexibility in midfield when Martin Zubimendi signed last summer. Whereas Thomas Partey and Jorginho were players who had to sit at the base of midfield for mobility reasons, Zubimendi and Rice offered the opportunity for both players to sit or push forward depending on the phase of play.

I think the signing of Bruno Guimaraes has furthered this sense that the midfield operates flexibly and the upshot is that Declan Rice is becoming far more creative this season. It’s not the first time that Arteta has asked Rice to be more impactful in the final third.

Towards the end of the 2024-25 season, with Arsenal suffering with attacking injuries and playing Mikel Merino upfront, Rice was asked to push forward more often. This season so far, Rice’s data is shape shifting yet again towards creativity and away from his more rumbustious qualities.

As you can see from the table (all data taken from Fotmob and expressed per 90 minutes), Rice’s creative statistics are all trending upwards compared to last season. He has four assists already this season. But as well as his creative data trending upwards, some of his more out of possession duties are reducing ever so slightly too.

In short, Rice is doing a little less scurrying and collecting and a little more passing and creating. I suspect the logic for this is the same as the clear decision to ask Saka to take up more shooting opportunities. Arsenal have to create more threat this season without having really improved their final third personnel in the market.

That being the case, Mikel Arteta has asked two of his best players to adapt their games in pursuit of that aim. It reminds me somewhat of that Simpsons episode where Marge finds a cheap Chanel dress and she keeps redesigning it so as not to let on to some rich friends that she cannot afford to buy another Chanel dress.

The signing of Bruno Guimaraes also represents another change for Rice, Partey and Jorginho were very similar players and that gave Rice a higher physical burden. The addition of Martin Zubimendi changed that somewhat and both players adopted a mix and match partnership whereby they would consistently swap between deeper and more advanced positions.

The rise to prominence of Myles Lewis-Skelly offers a very different and very useful variety in midfield too with his press resistance and ability to progress through dribbling. Bruno Guimaraes is a relatively similar player to Rice, capable of playing deeper and in more advanced areas, as well as being highly attuned to the physical demands of being a Premier League central midfielder.

We are yet to see exactly how a Rice, Odegaard and Bruno midfield will operate in practice; but we can see that Rice’s role has evolved yet again. It speaks to his quality and his intelligence- at West Ham the iteration of his partnership with Tomas Soucek evolved season on season too. Sometimes he would sit deeper and allow Soucek to push on and some seasons he would play in a more advanced position with Soucek behind him.

Rice has always shown a high aptitude for adding bows and whistles to his game season on season. Once again, he is being asked to do something a little bit different by Mikel Arteta and he is responding in kind. His capacity for learning and application is, for my money, unrivalled by any English midfielder I have seen in the last 25 years.

#1805. How two tobacco tycoons, Cesc Fabregas' unique link, a former England hardman and the 'Disney model' inspired Como's remarkable rise ahead of Manchester United clash

When Michael Carrick arrives in the tranquil surroundings of Lake Como for Wednesday night's Champions League clash against the Italian side, he may find little respite from Manchester United's troubles.

For all its picturesque beauty, Como is home to one of European football's most ambitious and remarkable footballing projects.

Just seven years ago, they were playing in Serie D, the fourth tier of Italian football, having suffered three bankruptcies between 2004 and 2017. Now, they are rubbing shoulders with Europe's elite.

The transformation began in 2019, when Indonesian billionaire brothers Robert Budi Hartono and Michael Bambang Hartono, who originally made their fortune in the tobacco industry, bought the club for $850,000 (£644,427) through their Djarum Group.

With a combined net worth then estimated at $48billion (£36.4billion), they instantly made Como the richest club in Italian football.

The money mattered, but so did the strategy. Rather than pursue instant success through lavish spending, Como built steadily, investing in infrastructure, recruitment and a clear footballing identity.

Manchester United take on high-flying Italian side Como in the Champions League tonight

Como are managed by Cesc Fabregas, who has become one of Europe's top young coaches

'They didn't just do an Abramovich and buy anything that would move on two legs,' Italian football expert Daniele Verri tells Daily Mail Sport. 'They just went step-by-step and they’ve done it really well... they are still doing it well.

'They climbed the ladder of Italian football pretty quickly, but they never overspent. It’s not like they bought Roberto Baggio or mega-fancy names.'

The results have been striking. Promotion from Serie D in 2019 was followed by a Serie C title in 2021, before a second-place finish in Serie B three years later secured Como's return to Serie A after a 21-year absence.

Having come tenth in 2024-25, they finished fourth last time out to qualify for the Champions League.

After a rapid summer renovation of their Stadio Giuseppe Sinigaglia, which sits on the shores of Lake Como and will have a capacity of around 11,000 for the United game, they are now welcoming one of the biggest clubs in the world.

The fact they were able to complete the necessary stadium improvements to ensure they could host Champions League games sums up their efficient management, with renovation of grounds typically difficult in Italy due to bureaucratic issues.

At the heart of the project is Cesc Fabregas, the former Arsenal, Barcelona and Chelsea midfielder who has emerged as one of Europe's brightest young coaches. Also a minority shareholder, he has developed a side built around possession, intensity and an attractive style of play.

His connections to Spanish football have proved particularly valuable in the transfer market. Although significant investment has helped Como's journey, they have always spent smartly and brought in top young talents.

That is best reflected in Nico Paz, who joined from Real Madrid aged 19 but is now an Argentina international and one of the most exciting young players on the continent. Greek forward Anastasios Douvikas, a 2025 arrival from Celta Vigo, is another good example.

Como's current squad includes other young stars like Maximo Perrone and Jacobo Ramon, while summer additions that included Moise Kean, Robert Sanchez and Trevoh Chalobah underlined the club's growing appeal.

'The idea of being part of a rising club with big-money involved and young players under a former champion like Fabregas is very attractive,' Verri adds.

#1802. Arsenal handed transfer update as Bournemouth make January decision on injured star

📅 26 Oct 2026 | other | Source

Over the years, he's had the pleasure of interviewing some of the biggest names in world sport, including Usain Bolt, Rafael Nadal, Christian Pulisic, and more.

Arsenal could face a difficult task if they decide to revive their interest in Bournemouth forward Eli Junior Kroupi, with the Cherries reportedly determined to keep the Frenchman beyond the January transfer window.

Mikel Arteta’s side were linked with the 20-year-old ahead of the summer window after his impressive debut Premier League campaign.

Kroupi scored 13 league goals after joining Bournemouth from Lorient for around £10 million, attracting interest from several leading clubs across Europe.

However, Arsenal may have to wait if they intend to make another move, with Bournemouth maintaining a firm position on the forward’s future despite his recent injury setback.

According to the Standard, Bournemouth are not prepared to entertain offers for Kroupi during the January transfer window.

The report states that the south coast club believe another season at the Vitality Stadium would benefit the young attacker, particularly with the team competing in Europe.

Arsenal were among the clubs linked with the Frenchman during the summer, when reports suggested a bid approaching £100 million might be required to test Bournemouth’s resolve.

That figure would represent a substantial investment from the Gunners, although it is a reported valuation rather than a confirmed asking price.

Kroupi’s performances last season explain why he has attracted such attention. His ability to contribute goals while operating across attacking positions makes him an intriguing prospect for Arteta, who has previously shown an interest in versatile forwards capable of developing into key first-team players.

From Arsenal’s perspective, his age and Premier League experience could make him an attractive long-term option. However, Bournemouth’s reported refusal to sell in January means the Gunners may need to consider alternative targets if they want to strengthen their attack during the winter window.

There is more encouraging news surrounding Kroupi’s fitness. The forward underwent surgery on a fractured metatarsal in July and was initially expected to miss up to four months of action.

He has since returned to full training, raising the prospect of an earlier-than-expected comeback.

Bournemouth manager Marco Rose provided an update on his availability, saying: “Junior is back into team training but not on the UEFA list, but maybe we can have him for some minutes in Manchester.”

His return would be a welcome boost for Bournemouth, who have been without the forward since the beginning of the campaign.

For Arsenal, Kroupi’s recovery provides an opportunity to assess his progress before potentially revisiting their interest in a future window. Nevertheless, there is currently no confirmation that the north London club intend to submit a bid.

The London Standard report also highlights a contrasting situation involving Bournemouth midfielder Alex Scott. The Cherries are reportedly more open to considering a January departure for Scott, provided their Premier League position is secure and a suitable offer arrives.

Chelsea previously had a reported £64 million bid rejected for the midfielder, while Bournemouth are said to value him at around £100 million.

Kroupi’s situation appears considerably more straightforward for Bournemouth: the club want to retain him and believe continued development on the south coast is in his best interests.

What this means for Arsenal: Arteta’s side may continue monitoring Kroupi, but Bournemouth’s reported stance makes a January transfer difficult to envisage. A future move could depend on the player’s development, Arsenal’s attacking priorities and whether the Cherries eventually reconsider their position.

#1801. CM: Milan among Serie A teams watching on as Chiesa could leave Liverpool

📅 26 Oct 2026 | other | Source

Federico Chiesa could leave Liverpool in January and several Serie A clubs are keeping tabs on his situation, including AC Milan.

It’s been a little over two years since Chiesa decided to leave Serie A to join Liverpool, then eager to test his wings in the Premier League. However, with just 424 minutes in the competition (two this season), it hasn’t exactly been successful.

If Chiesa decides that his time at Liverpool has run its course, then there will be plenty of suitors in Serie A. As the report continues, the likes of Milan, Inter, Lazio, and Atalanta are keeping tabs on his situation and would be exploring a move if he’s open to joining.
