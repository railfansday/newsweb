Macron arrived at the scene of the drive-by shooting in Trappes, a town west of Paris, on Friday night, saying he was deeply saddened by what he called the “premeditated” attack. The 12-year-old girl, who was shot eight times, is being treated in hospital.

Rabeh, whose family has Moroccan roots, said: “The people targeted today could just as easily have been my mother, my brothers or my sisters-in-law.

The prosecutor said in a statement on Friday night that the medical status of the five wounded victims was not stable. Rabeh said the man killed was “of African origin” and that another of the wounded was “of north African origin”.

Nicolas Dainville, the mayor of La Verrière, told Agence France-Presse that two of the wounded people in his town were cleaners of African origin attending to a school Friday on morning when the shooter got out of his car to open fire.

Seoul's military also concluded that North Korea had buried mines south of the Military Demarcation Line (MDL) which caused the recent blast. Pyongyang has rejected those findings.

#193. Surveillance and 'constant paranoia': Western diplomats tell BBC of life in war-time Moscow

📅 26 Oct 2026 | other | Source

Ex-US and French ambassadors Lynne Tracy and Pierre LÃ©vy were among those to speak to the BBC of their time in Moscow

"Being the ambassador to Russia was probably the hardest assignment of my career," Lynne Tracy, who represented the US in Moscow from 2023 to 2025, tells the BBC.

US, UK and European diplomats once played a crucial role in Russia, defusing tensions even at the worst moments of the Cold War.

But now, deemed "unfriendly" by President Vladimir Putin's government, Western ambassadors are under constant surveillance, isolated and increasingly cut off from the country they were sent to understand, as the war in Ukraine grinds on through its fifth year.

The BBC has spoken to five diplomats who have served in Russia since it launched its full-scale invasion.

They compare current pressure levels to the Cold War era - when Western embassies were monitored through physical surveillance, listening devices hidden inside gifts, and "spy dust" - a near invisible chemical agent used to track officials.

Hundreds of Russian diplomats were expelled from countries in Europe and the US after the war began in 2022. Moscow responded in kind, declaring hundreds of Western diplomats persona non grata and leaving many embassies short-staffed.

Tracy's 30-year career took her to countries like Pakistan, where she survived an armed attack in 2008. But she says that even under constant physical threat, her posting in Peshawar was, in a sense, easier than Russia.

"There, I still felt that there were things within my control. What made Moscow more difficult were the high levels of uncertainty."

The constant pressure from Russian counterintelligence was the primary source of such uncertainty, she recalls.

Tracy pictured leaving Russia's Foreign Ministry after being summoned by Moscow in 2023

Russian diplomats in the West also complain of such unfair treatment. But according to the diplomats who spoke to the BBC, the scope and tactics of Moscow's intelligence services are far greater.

"Expect listening devices, expect cameras in every single room of your house," a former European diplomat says of the warning he received before leaving for Moscow.

Another ex-ambassador says they were forced to dismiss two Russian employees after Moscow's domestic security service, the FSB, tried to recruit them, demanding that they report on everything going on at the diplomatic mission.

The pair told the embassy instead, who let them go - "otherwise, they could have faced some sort of criminal prosecution".

Meanwhile, Russians who would ordinarily build relationships with diplomatic staff - such as those working in academia and culture - are also being intimidated into avoiding contact with Western officials.

One former ambassador says his guests were once "tailed" by FSB agents right up to the doors of the embassy.

Just this June, guests invited to the British ambassador's residence in Moscow for the King's birthday celebrations said they had been harassed by pro-Kremlin activists and state-media journalists, and then questioned by the police.

One of the guests says he has since declined several invitations to events at Western embassies out of fear.

One former ambassador says "constant paranoia" has driven a number of officials to quit their jobs. Even after leaving Russia himself, he adds, he was looking over his shoulder for months.

In the immediate aftermath of the invasion, there were discussions across Europe about whether to recall ambassadors or stop working in Russia altogether, says former French ambassador Pierre LÃ©vy.

He remembers calling Paris immediately to say pulling their envoys would be a mistake, feeling that the worse relations became, the more important they would be.

The invasion quickly transformed their work, says another ex-European ambassador; the co-operation required for their usual efforts on bilateral relations, trade and joint projects was now impossible.

And for those who stayed in Russia, the war left them with a host of new priorities beyond supporting their own citizens and companies.

Firstly, countries viewed as unfriendly by Moscow still wanted at least some channel of communication with Russian officials to get an accurate picture of what was happening inside the country.

But they also had to protect their staff and keep their embassies functioning.

One diplomat recalls discussing staffing shortages with a Russian official, who advised him not to get too upset. "He said, 'at least you still have the chef'."

Financial sanctions imposed by Western governments to support Ukraine created different day-to-day difficulties. After Russian banks were cut off from the Swift payment network, and Mastercard and Visa suspended operations in Russia, many embassies struggled with basic operating expenses.

A former ambassador recalls simply running out of cash and being unable to pay his staff - until another diplomat, who was selling his car after being expelled, brought the rubles to the embassy in a suitcase.

"It's completely insane," the diplomat laughs. "But you had to look at it with a sense of humour."

Russian officials make little secret of their attitude towards Western diplomats. They have openly accused those from "unfriendly countries" of engaging in "espionage".

Moscow has been particularly critical of those who show an interest in expressions of dissent against the Kremlin and, according to Russian journalist Alexei Venediktov, has effectively banned most Russian officials from speaking to Western embassies.

This has only worsened relations with the West, says Venediktov, who recalls telling Russian officials: "You are being misunderstood precisely because of your own actions - because ambassadors cannot do their jobs."

Today, Moscow has little to no contact with many Western governments, says one ex-European ambassador. "There are no calls, no meetings at ministerial level."

But they say this has made diplomats all the more important, left with the responsibility to communicate their governments' positions to Moscow.

"Generals prove what they can do during a war. The same is true for diplomats. When things get really difficult, every single thing you do actually matters."

Lynne Tracy left Moscow in 2025 feeling she had accomplished her primary mission: the US Embassy had held firm.

But she is also proud of the work she and her team did to maintain connection between Americans and ordinary Russians.

"Because at some point, government-to-government relations will have a chance to improve - and those ties between people will be there to help speed the improvement."

#192. Uh-oh: iPhone Duo displays are reportedly cracking along the hinge on launch day

📅 26 Oct 2026 | other | Source

While the iPhone Duo has been mostly showered with praise on its launch day, there has been some criticism of the device. Reviewers aren't fans of the FaceTime camera's output quality, many apps have not yet been optimized for Apple's first foldable device, and there's a new report showing that the inner display's crease at the hinge becomes more visible as users fold and unfold the device over time.

However, just hours after the iPhone Duo was released, some users are going home to find what appears to be a major defect that makes those previous critiques pale in comparison.

The inner display of the iPhone Duo is cracking from top to bottom along the entire hinge — right where the device folds — for some very unlucky first-day iPhone Duo users.

It's unclear exactly what the issue is, but the result appears to be the same each time, according to photos of cracked iPhone Duo displays spreading across social media. A large crack appears on the inner display right along the hinge. In addition, the cracked hinge causes a pixel issue on the right-hand side of the inner display, resulting in a bright white line.

Reports of faulty iPhone hinges don't seem to be contained to any specific market, either. While it's also unclear just how widespread the defect is, users from the U.S. to Australia to China have all reported cracked iPhone Duo displays at the hinge.

A Reddit user in Australia shared his customer support experience while contacting Apple about his defective iPhone Duo. Over the phone, the company offered to provide an express replacement; however, the user would have to "put down a hold for the full cost of the phone." Apple also couldn't guarantee a timeframe for when the replacement would arrive. The user decided to go straight to the Apple Store instead, where they "apologized and replaced it with a brand-new one on the spot." It seems Apple may have some extra iPhone Duo devices in-store as replacements for when problems arise.

The Australian Redditor said the crack occurred in less than 6 hours after picking up the iPhone Duo and after they folded the device fewer than 20 times.

As MacRumors points out, Apple may be looking to collect broken devices to investigate what went wrong and fix the issue when manufacturing iPhone Duo devices going forward.

Consumers should always expect some issues and bugs with first-generation devices such as the iPhone Duo. But a huge crack down the middle of the inner display is certainly an extreme defect. Apple will undoubtedly fix the issue for unlucky customers, but it's certainly a big launch-day inconvenience for the first iPhone Duo owners.
