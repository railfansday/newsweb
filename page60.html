Arsenal legend Nigel Winterburn has had enough of the constant wait around Manchester City’s 115 charges. He wants the Premier League to deal with it this season and thinks it’s a serious possibility that they will be relegated.

An independent commission concluded that Manchester City was guilty of 114 out of 115 charges, and the Premier League confirmed the finding on 29 September.

They claimed that the club had violated their financial rules systematically for almost a decade.

Manchester City responded that they were disappointed and surprised and that they would look into their options for appealing the ruling. Sanctions will be decided during a separate hearing.

Winterburn won three league titles with Arsenal. He told Casino Kings:

“I’ve been talking about this a lot, and I’m fed up with it. I want it resolved. It’s frustrating that they’ve been found guilty, but we don’t know the full details. Everyone is waiting for that.

His frustration is understandable. The outcome has raised as many questions as it has answered, and City has continued to win.

They have taken maximum points from their first five matches of the season under new manager Enzo Maresca. Winterburn doubts an appeal will overturn the commission’s work:

“My gut feeling is that you can appeal, but can you really get off 114 charges? I don’t think so. Otherwise, people would question the competence of the panel. I think there would be serious scrutiny if City got off.

“Some charges might be dropped, but if they stand, my feeling is that City could face relegation through a severe points deduction that effectively keeps them out of the league.

Reports suggest that the commission has the power to impose fines, points deductions or even expulsion. Winterburn should know about the feeling of heading into a drop. He ended his playing days at West Ham when they were relegated in 2003.

He believes it will be important that the timing of any punishment is considered as much as the punishment itself:

“I think it has to be dealt with this season. How bad would it look for the Premier League if City won? It already looks bad when journalists talk about their wins without knowing what it means.

“City are top of the league but could potentially be at the bottom. That doesn’t work. The decisions and the charges need to be dealt with quickly.

He accepts the process may not be simple, but he has a warning for the league if it drags on:

“I don’t know how easy or difficult that will be, but if it isn’t resolved by the end of the season, I think the Premier League will look very silly.”

For now the sanctions hearing is still to come, and the city is ready to fight on as Winterburn insists that the Premier League simply cannot allow a ruling to be delayed to next season.

Fighting to distance themselves from the Bundesliga's relegation zone, promoted club Schalke 04 will travel to face FC Koln at RheinEnergieStadion on Saturday.

The Billy Goats are hoping to bounce back from defeat last time out, while Die Knappen are desperate to avoid a third consecutive loss.

Rene Wagner's Koln have produced a mixed start to the campaign, and a win this weekend would give their season a major boost, pushing them towards the European spots.

The Billy Goats kicked off their Bundesliga calendar with a 3-2 victory over Hoffenheim, but lost two of the following three matches, drawing the other.

Wagner's side were able to beat Borussia Monchengladbach 2-1 on October 10, but once again failed to build on the success, falling to a 1-0 defeat to Eintracht Frankfurt most recently.

That inconsistent run has left Koln 12th in the league with seven points to their name, a tally that puts them a mere two above the relegation zone.

However, securing all three points on Saturday could move the Billy Goats level with fifth-placed Mainz 05, a realistic possibility considering the hosts are unbeaten at RheinEnergieStadion this term, winning twice and drawing once.

Meanwhile, Miron Muslic's Schalke earned promotion to the top flight by winning the second-division title last season, but have found the step up challenging.

Die Knappen currently sit 15th with five points and are only above the relegation zone on goal difference, a stark contrast to fellow promoted club Elversberg, who are sixth.

Last weekend, Muslic's men were beaten 1-0 by Mainz, failing to respond to Phillip Tietz's first-half opener in a dire performance that is perhaps best summed up by the fact former Liverpool goalkeeper Loris Karius was named man of the match - his third such award this season.

To make matters worse, that defeat saw Schalke's 13-month unbeaten streak at Veltins Arena broken, alongside their failure to score for the fourth time in the league just six games into the campaign.

That attacking strife has seen Die Knappen rack up only four goals so far, the fewest of any Bundesliga side, and it remains to be seen whether they can overcome that record on the road against a team that have played their best football at home.

Koln are without striker Thijs Dallinga as he recovers from a cruciate ligament injury, while fellow centre-forward Ragnar Ache is unlikely to feature as he works his way back from a thigh problem, so expect to see Marius Bulter and Said El Mala start up top.

At the opposite end of the pitch, centre-backs Timo Hubers and Rav van den Berg are out due to respective knee and muscle injuries, and versatile defensive midfielder Ellyes Skhiri is dealing with an ankle issue.

In their absence, Luka Lochoshvili and Jahmai Simpson-Pusey should start at the heart of the hosts' defence, shielded by Tom Krauss in the number six role.

As for Schalke, they are missing striker Moussa Sylla with a leg injury he sustained against Freiburg, though manager Muslic is confident that Edin Dzeko will be ready to play.

Captain Kenan Karaman and Dejan Ljubicic are also ruled out as they recover from knee and muscle issues, but Junior Adamu and Adil Aouchiche should be on hand to support the veteran centre-forward up top.

Elsewhere, centre-back Maximilian Wober remains unavailable with a thigh injury, and the visitors could opt for a three-man backline featuring Timo Becker, Nikola Katic and Hasan Kurucay.

#2473. Why must Manchester United move beyond the Glazers?

📅 26 Oct 2026 | other | Source

The Glazers took control of Manchester United in 2005. That takeover has since caused so many disruptions and protests by the fans over massive debts and insufficient backing of the team in the transfer market, despite successful periods in the Premier League and Europe.

Now Manchester United need to think about who should control the club in the future. Laurie Whitwell has now shared his views on why Manchester United need an ownership change. He simply wants the Glazers gone from Old Trafford. However, he also recognizes that replacing the current ownership doesn’t automatically mean things will improve for United.

The journalist believes the change in ownership has had a lasting effect on United, with the club still feeling its consequences today. A fresh start might be welcome, but the identity of the next owners would matter just as much as the departure of the existing ones.

“Personally, I would like to see the Glazers gone from Manchester United,” Laurie Whitwell said on The Rest is Football podcast with Gary Lineker.

“That change, you know, just really affected and still impacts United now.”

The desire for different ownership is one thing, but finding people capable of taking the club forward is another.

Whitwell admitted there is no perfect answer when it comes to who should take charge.

That leaves United facing a difficult choice. A sale could offer the chance to change how the club is run, but simply bringing in a new owner would not guarantee better decisions or a brighter future.

Whitwell also raised the idea of United supporters having a greater say in the club’s ownership and decisions, much like the Barcelona model where fans own the club itself. However, he highlighted that the Barcelona model isn’t ideal either and has its own shortcomings.

Changing the names at the top would only be one part of the change. The club would also need an ownership structure capable of making sound decisions and providing a direction.

The issue of ownership goes beyond whether the Glazers should stay or leave. Manchester United needs to find the right people and the right model to secure the club’s future. Getting that decision wrong could leave them facing the same circus the fans are enduring under the Glazers.

📅 26 Oct 2026 | other | Source

West Ham United v Southampton EFL Championship London Stadium Saturday 24 October 2026, 3pm BST

West Ham United’s victory at Swansea City was absolutely massive. Not only did we beat the Championship leaders on their own ground, but the result also removed almost all of the immediate pressure from Nuno Espírito Santo following a difficult run of performances.

There are still reservations about Nuno’s tactics and questions remain over how slowly West Ham begin matches. However, even his fiercest critics must admit he is doing a decent job this season, with the Hammers second in the table and unbeaten in nine league games.

Southampton’s visit now presents an excellent opportunity to strengthen that position. The Saints are genuine promotion rivals, but victory on Saturday would put significant daylight between the two clubs and make three consecutive West Ham wins following the international break.

The 1-0 win at Swansea was a genuine promotion statement. Unlike the 6-0 victory against nine-man Lincoln City, this was achieved away to the league leaders in a competitive match against one of the division’s strongest teams.

It was not a dominant performance, and Swansea created enough opportunities to make the afternoon uncomfortable. However, West Ham defended extremely well, competed physically and found the decisive goal through Divine Mukasa.

That result moved the Hammers into second place and completely changed the atmosphere surrounding Nuno. Having faced understandable questions after the poor performances against QPR and during the first half at Lincoln, he has now delivered consecutive away victories and placed West Ham firmly inside the automatic promotion positions.

The numbers support the sense that the situation has changed quickly. As Claret & Hugh’s latest promotion projections demonstrated, the outlook has improved dramatically following those two wins.

Nuno has not answered every question, but his critics have certainly been silenced for now.

West Ham’s position is healthy, but there are still issues which need addressing. We continue to start matches too slowly, allowing opponents to settle and grow in confidence before showing the required urgency.

That happened against QPR and again at Lincoln, despite the eventual six-goal winning margin. Even against Swansea, the Hammers had to withstand significant pressure after taking the lead and were fortunate that the hosts failed to take their opportunities.

Nuno deserves credit for the results and for finding different ways to win. However, West Ham cannot continually rely on improving during matches or on the superior quality of the squad eventually making the difference.

Southampton have lost only twice in the Championship this season. If West Ham begin Saturday’s game passively, they will face a team capable of taking advantage.

Kante has become an important player for Nuno

His energy and pressing were crucial at Swansea. Kanté helped make the home side uncomfortable in possession and was directly involved in the move which ended with Mukasa scoring the winning goal.

He is beginning to look like a player Nuno trusts in important matches. While there is enormous competition for places in West Ham’s midfield, Kanté’s performances are becoming increasingly difficult to ignore.

I expect him to start in the attacking-midfield role behind Taty Castellanos on Saturday, with Mukasa dropping deeper alongside Arne Engels.

Edvin Austbø deserves to start on the left ahead of Manor Solomon. The Norwegian was a menace after coming off the bench at Lincoln, scoring an excellent goal and repeatedly carrying the ball towards the opposition defence.

Solomon still looks knackered after international duty. There is little value in continually starting a player who appears fatigued when Austbø is producing energetic and dangerous cameos from the bench.

The youngster now deserves an opportunity from the beginning. Southampton are likely to be organised and difficult to break down, so Austbø’s pace and willingness to run directly at defenders could be particularly valuable.

There have already been calls for Nuno to make changes against Southampton, and this is the most obvious one. Austbø has earned his chance rather than simply receiving it because another player needs resting.

Southampton should be considered genuine promotion rivals. They began the season with a four-point deduction but have still climbed to eighth after losing only twice in their opening Championship fixtures.

That makes their league position slightly deceptive. Peeping Tonda Eckert’s side would be even closer to West Ham without the punishment and should remain involved around the promotion places as the season develops.

Saturday therefore offers West Ham an opportunity to do more than collect another three points. Victory would create significant separation from one of the teams capable of challenging for promotion while strengthening our own position in the top two.

Former Hammer Flynn Downes is doubtful after damaging an ankle ligament against Norwich City. His absence would be significant because he is an experienced and effective Championship midfielder, even if allowing him to leave West Ham was the correct decision.

Nuno’s team remains difficult to predict because of the options available in midfield and attack. However, I expect him to retain the back four which performed well at Swansea, restore Castellanos as the central striker and reward Austbø with a start on the left.

Southampton’s record indicates this will be a more difficult match than their league position suggests. They are genuine promotion rivals and have been beaten only twice, so West Ham will need to start far more quickly than they have in recent games.

However, the Swansea victory should have removed much of the tension surrounding Nuno and his players. West Ham return to the London Stadium with momentum, growing confidence and the opportunity to put meaningful distance between themselves and Southampton.
