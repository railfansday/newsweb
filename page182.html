#39. Nigeria confirms Ebola case as outbreak spreads into western Africa

📅 26 Oct 2026 | other | Source

Case in Africa’s most populous country was detected in a woman who had travelled from Uganda

Nigeria has confirmed an Ebola case in its capital, Abuja, after a woman who arrived from Uganda tested positive for the virus, the health ministry said.

The woman began showing symptoms on 7 October and was admitted to a hospital in Abuja four days later, where she was later diagnosed with Ebola. It is unclear when she arrived in Nigeria, Africa’s most populous country.

The health minister, Muhammad Pate, said authorities had begun tracing 44 people identified as contacts of the patient. “We are doing everything possible to contain this case by tracing all the contacts the individual may have had,” he said.

Uganda’s ministry of health said the patient had only transited through Uganda and that she is a Nigerian woman who had been on a business trip to Congo’s eastern region. The Nigerian authorities have not disclosed her nationality.

“Travel through Uganda does not establish that infection occurred here. The location of exposure remains under investigation,” the ministry said in a statement. The ministry insisted Uganda has “no confirmed active Ebola cases”.

Nigeria is the latest country to report an Ebola case as the outbreak spreads across the region. Kenya confirmed its first case earlier this month and has since recorded nine infections. Uganda had previously reported Ebola cases, but it later declared itself free of the virus.

Dr Jean Kaseya, the director general of the Africa Centers for Disease Control, said on X that “we are facing an extremely serious threat,” with a fatality rate of nearly 50% of cases.

“This Bundibugyo Ebola outbreak has already become the most geographically extensive in history, affecting several countries spread across three regions of Africa,” he said.

The Nigeria Centre for Disease Control said two weeks ago it was stepping up surveillance following the detection of Ebola in Kenya and strengthening the country’s preparedness for a potential outbreak.

“These efforts have focused on early detection, isolation and referral, laboratory diagnosis, infection prevention and control, contact tracing, risk communication and protection of health care workers,” the agency said.

Nigeria established an Ebola taskforce in June after the outbreak in Congo in mid-May.

The spread of Ebola beyond Congo has heightened fears among health authorities about the rapid transmission of the rare Bundibugyo virus, which has infected more than 9,500 people and killed more than 4,600 people.

The latest Ebola outbreak is the fastest-spreading on record and is on track to exceed the deadliest on record, the 11,000 people killed in West Africa’s regional outbreak between 2014 and 2016, WHO has said.

#35. Russia's attacks on Ukraine's capital, Kyiv, are intensifying. DW asked people on the ground what life is like

📅 26 Oct 2026 | other | Source

Russia's attacks on the Ukrainian capital are hitting harder and more frequently, making 2026 one of the long war's bloodiest years for Kyiv. DW asked people on the ground what life is like.

Until a few months ago, life was relatively peaceful in Kyiv when compared with the situation near the war's front lines elsewhere in Ukraine. Now, as Russia expands its attacks on the capital, Kyiv has increasingly become a front-line city.

With too few shelters for the 4 million residents of Kyiv, subway stations double as makeshift dormitories. Starting at about 10 every night, people create sleeping areas with mattresses and small tents. Children snuggle up to adults; pets seek comfort from their owners or find a spot among strangers between the cold walls.

"We're alive," Halyna told DW, holding her 5-year-old son, Roman, close. "We've been lucky once again." Most evenings — even when no air-raid siren has sounded — she takes the boy to the nearest subway station. Her apartment is located in the Lukyanivka district, which Russia has systematically targeted over the war's four years and eight months.

In September, 14-year-old Daniel Umanskyj was doing homework in his room when a Russian drone struck the apartment building in the central district of Pechersk. A neighbor named Vlad happened to be in the hallway with his daughter at the time of the strike. "We were late that day — and this saved our lives," Vlad said. "Daniel's death is a tragedy." He added: "These sick monsters don't care who they kill." Flowers have been planted in front of the damaged apartment building in memory of Daniel.

Russia also targets places where children gather. In early October, a drone struck a school building  in southwestern Kyiv. Fortunately, pupils and teachers were already in shelters at the time.

"We've been teaching all the pupils in person because we have a large shelter that can accommodate more than 500 students," Valentyna Chorna, the school's principal, told DW. As there is enough space in the shelter for everyone, students in the first through ninth grades are able to go to school, she said.

Whether classes take place depends not only on the decision of the parents and the school administration: It also depends on whether shelters are available and how long the air-raid siren continues to sound. If an alarm sounds in the morning while children are still at home, they may not be able to go to school that day.

"This is an enormous burden for parents," said a colleague from the DW bureau in Kyiv, whose daughter attends school. "You have to go to work — and the child stays home alone."

"Even the walk to school is dangerous because an air-raid alarm can go off at any time," she said. Her daughter attends a school not far from the Antonov aircraft factory. "One day she texted me: ‘You can't imagine what a huge explosion that was!'" she said. "I don't know if something was shot down or if there was a strike somewhere." She added: "Things like that really scare children." And staying home brings its own risks.

In September, a Russian drone struck their building. The apartment itself wasn't damaged, and her daughter was at school that morning, but it was another reminder of just how much survival has become a matter of luck. When an air-raid siren sounds, no one knows who will be spared, she said.

Russia's attacks have become deadlier in 2026. According to the military administration in Kyiv, 140 people were killed in the capital during the first eight months of the year, compared with 172 for all of 2025. If this trend continues, 2026 could become Kyiv's deadliest year since the start of the Russian invasion in 2022.

Attacks on warehouses have led to shortages in supermarkets on a scale not seen since the early months of the war. At the end of September, emergency power outages resumed in Kyiv. The subway was paralyzed by a Russian attack. Given the strikes on the energy infrastructure, it is unclear how the capital can reliably supply residents with heat, electricity and water during the upcoming winter.

Despite Russia's attempts to render Kyiv uninhabitable, 72% of the city's residents said in September that they are prepared to endure the war for as long as necessary, the Kyiv International Institute of Sociology reported in early October. The authorities are striving to maintain normal life in the city, where the curfew has been shortened and now runs from 1 a.m. to 5 a.m.

Some facilities have even been allowed to continue operating during "yellow" alerts. During "red" alerts, however, people must immediately seek shelter: This  could mean ballistic missiles, cruise missiles or massive combined attacks.

#33. Israeli forces begin demolishing Palestinian buildings in Hebron after two Israeli soldiers shot dead

📅 26 Oct 2026 | other | Source

Diggers were seen demolishing Palestinian homes on Friday afternoon

The Israeli military has begun demolishing Palestinian buildings in the city of Hebron, after two Israeli soldiers were killed in an attack by two Palestinian gunmen.

Prime Minister Benjamin Netanyahu said the military would "destroy" the neighbourhood where the two attackers came from, and that Israel was ready for "extensive action" in the occupied West Bank.

Three other soldiers were wounded in the attack at a checkpoint outside Hebron, before the gunmen were shot dead, officials say.

It comes at a time of high tensions, with extremist Jewish settlers targeting Palestinians during the annual olive harvest, and Israel's general election taking place in four days' time.

Footage verified by the BBC shows an intense gun battle between soldiers and the gunmen. Other footage shows two gunmen exit a car armed with what appears to be a handgun and an assault rifle and start firing on soldiers at the checkpoint, before coming under return fire.

Photos of one of the suspected attackers were shared by AFP news agency

The incident has already begun to influence the ongoing election campaign, with Netanyahu - who has built his campaign on the promise of security - saying that his Likud party would cancel campaign events which had been set for Friday morning.

Speaking later from the scene, Netanyahu said that the military would "demolish this place and completely transform it," referring to the Hebron neighbourhood where the attackers are said to have come from.

It is common for Israeli forces to demolish the homes of Palestinians who are suspected of carrying out attacks on Israelis - a practice Israeli rights groups have criticised as collective punishment.

Netanyahu likened his plans to previous operations in West Bank refugee camps where residential buildings were demolished and thousands were displaced.

He also said he had reviewed plans for the largest military operation in the West Bank in decades - though did not say when it could be carried out.

He added that he had imposed cordons around the city of Hebron - which has around 200,000 residents - and the nearby city of Halhul.

Netanyahu's far-right Finance Minister Bezalel Smotrich called for Israel to launch a campaign in the West Bank "exactly like in Gaza or Lebanon," while far-right National Security Minister Itamar Ben Gvir called for Israel to dismantle the Palestinian Authority.

On Thursday, Foreign Minister Israel Katz accused opposition leaders of undermining the government by criticising Netanyahu's warnings of an attack ahead of the election, saying they would be "responsible" for any future attack on the country.

Opposition chiefs, including frontrunner Gadi Eisenkot and former Prime Minister Naftali Bennett, offered their condolences to the families of the killed troops and praised those who killed the attackers.

The Israeli military said it had found a large quantity of ammunition in the attackers' vehicle, and that they believe the gunmen had intended to carry out a much larger attack on the nearby Route 35, but ran into the checkpoint - so instead decided to open fire there.

At least 96 Palestinians have been killed by Israeli forces or settlers in the West Bank since the start of the year, according to the UN.

Four Israelis, both security forces and civilians, have been killed by Palestinians there over the same period, the UN says - not including those killed in Friday's attack.

Jack Dorsey’s Block announced an AI model called Dessa that’s trained “from scratch on nothing but human conversation,” and to show off the model, Block shared chats it has had with humans that can occasionally be a little awkward and uncanny.

Take this one, for example, shared by Dessa lead Ragavan Thurairatnam. The conversation between the model and the speaker does have a mostly natural back and forth (though for some reason, the model initially mistakes the speaker for somebody named “Ronaldo”).

Here’s a video of Dessa playing a guessing game over voice:

The Dessa website features a few other examples, like a back-and-forth about Christopher Nolan’s films (including Dessa going on an unexpected tangent about Bryan Singer’s Superman films), a person telling Dessa about taking it on a surprise trip to Paris (to which Dessa, as the website describes, “then answers with what sounds like teary gratitude”), and a conversation between OpenAI’s GPT-Live-1 voice model and Dessa (where Dessa comes across as more natural). You can hear clips from some of those conversations in this video.

On the website, the Dessa team says that Dessa is a “true speech-to-speech model, and everything it knows came from human speech,” and it can run locally on a MacBook Pro M4 Max “or better.” As for why they made it, the team gives the three reasons “curiosity (aka fun), better understanding between humans and AI, and safety,” and provided some more detail:

Babies learn to talk without ever seeing a word written down. We wanted to see if a model could too. It can. It’s just very hard.

Speech is the most natural interface to any intelligence, super or otherwise, and today’s voice AI wastes it. It flattens what we say into text and can’t keep up with human timing.

AI keeps gaining power and independence, and it’s still nearly deaf to the emotion in our voices. Imagine being in serious pain while a voice AI has no idea and just keeps going. That’s the safety problem. Dessa is a step toward fixing it.

The Dessa team says it will be giving “a small group of AI safety researchers” early access to the model.

#30. Severely ill ICE detainees face imminent deportation to unknown African states, advocates say

📅 26 Oct 2026 | other | Source

Flights to third countries where the detainees have no ties are scheduled as early as Wednesday, relatives detail

Send us your questions about immigration and the midterms

Relatives of gravely ill detainees at two Florida immigration jails say authorities are about to deport their loved ones to unknown countries in Africa despite worsening medical conditions.

Flights are scheduled as early as Wednesday, according to the families and the immigration advocacy group the Workers Circle, which has been monitoring conditions and speaking with detainees inside two Immigration and Customs Enforcement (ICE) facilities in south Florida.

The group said multiple sick detainees with varying conditions had been targeted for imminent deportation, and learned about the operation only in the last few days.

“I received a call from several detainees letting me know that men were being pulled in to interviews with deportation agents, where they were being informed that because of their medical conditions, Mexico would not be accepting their entry, and they will be sent to Africa,” Arianne Betancourt, the group’s immigrant rights organizer, said at a Tuesday-night press conference.

“We know that once they are deported and sent to these third countries, they are not receiving medical care in detention. They have difficulties getting medicine.”

A Department of Homeland Security (DHS) spokesperson, in a statement to the Guardian late on Wednesday, said it was “a false story designed to smear the brave men and women of ICE as they are facing a more than 1,600% increase in assaults against them”.

“No sick detainees are being deported. All detainees are medically cleared before travel and leave with any necessary medications.”

The affected detainees are at the Krome North processing center in Miami, which was found to be experiencing severe overcrowding by a government inspector’s report last month, and the Broward transitional center in Pompano Beach. Both facilities are privately run on behalf of ICE.

Anisley Cortés, the wife of Noslén Sendra, a 41-year-old Cuban national, said her husband had been incarcerated since last November, first at the now closed “Alligator Alcatraz” immigration jail, and at Krome since April. She said her husband has uncontrolled diabetes and acute pancreatitis that resulted in a weeks-long hospital stay earlier this year.

“He lost over 50 pounds. They wouldn’t give him his medication, and they would not give him enough food. It was a nightmare of a time for all of us,” she said. “He is very sick and needs the right healthcare.

“Cuba will not accept him as a deportee, and we now have proof that Mexico will not accept him as a deportee because he is so sick. And so they told him that because he could not be accepted at either of those two countries, ‘Oh well, we’re going to have to send you to Africa.’

“They told him that Mexico would not accept him because he has too many medications that he requires, and so they said, ‘Well, we have to deport you anyway.’ We’re left with the uncertainty of not knowing how he is, or if we’ll see him again.”

Shalyn Fluharty, an immigration rights attorney, said information from the detainees indicated deportation flights were imminent.

“We understand that just this week, within a couple of days, likely Thursday and Friday, there will be two flights going to Africa, in particular to the Central African Republic, and we also believe Burundi,” she said.

Fluharty said previously deported immigrants were shackled at their hands, ankles and waists during lengthy flights, and some who complained were placed in full body wraps with no ability to move.

“This is what we anticipate will be happening this week for these individuals, based upon what we’ve seen in the past,” she said.

“Travel can last sometimes up to 24 hours. What if you’re somebody who has a heart condition? Who has a high risk of stroke? Who is experiencing diabetes and requires regular access to food or insulin? These conditions take your already vulnerable medical state and make them, unfortunately, often life-threatening.”

The DHS statement said Sendra was “a criminal illegal alien from Cuba convicted of fraud, larceny, and property crimes” and “has been consistently cleared and discharged from the hospital following medical evaluation”.

He was ordered removed from the US in September 2015, it said.

“Third country removals are an essential public safety tool that remove the most dangerous criminal illegal aliens from our streets,” the spokesperson said.

“Illegal aliens being sent to a third country receive notice and assurances prior to their removal, and the Department of State is given diplomatic assurances from destination countries that the illegal aliens being removed there will be safe.”

Betancourt said families risked retaliation for their relatives by coming forward. “This is not a single one-off story,” she said.

“The inspector general’s report confirmed that there is a lack of medical care inside of Krome detention facility. Families have been demanding oversight, and every day they are experiencing real fear to speak up and advocate for their loved ones.”

During two unannounced visits to Krome in May, government inspectors found multiple violations of noncompliance with detention standards, including severe overcrowding. Holding 1,102 detainees in a facility with a maximum capacity of 882, the report said, “jeopardizes the safe and orderly operations within the facility”.

Other listed violations included an inadequate number of operational toilets, sinks and showers, and previous complaints included female detainees made to use toilets in full view of men, and arriving detainees held for more than 24 hours in a bus in the parking lot because of a lack of space.

Advocates say Donald Trump’s sweeping second-term immigration crackdown has caused overcrowding in detention centers nationwide that the DHS has attempted to alleviate by ramping up deportation flights to so-called third countries.

The controversial practice, approved by the US supreme court last month, has seen hundreds of detainees deported to at least a dozen African countries where they have no ties.

A Human Rights Watch report earlier this month found countless examples of abusive treatment during and after the flights, and detainees subjected to persecution, arbitrary incarceration and torture following their arrival.

#25. Freed US citizen was tortured in Russian jail, former Ukrainian prisoners say

📅 26 Oct 2026 | other | Source

Stephen Hubbard (second from left in the first row) was flown back home on a US plane earlier this week

Ukrainians held alongside freed US prisoner Stephen Hubbard have said they witnessed him being beaten and humiliated by Russian guards during his four-year detention.

Dmytro Khyliuk first met Hubbard at the Novozybkov prison in Bryansk, not far from Russia's border with Ukraine, in September 2022.

"We heard it from the guards. They came during inspections and were asking: in which cell is the American? And then they used obscene words. They threatened him, they mocked him from the first days."

Ihor Shyshko also remembers Hubbard's arrival at the jail and says his nationality marked him out to the officers.

"It attracted such attention because they mocked him, forced him to learn the Russian language, sing the national anthem and repeat the rules of the prison schedule, which he could not read."

Hubbard was captured in April 2022, when Russian soldiers occupied the eastern city of Izyum, where he was living with his Ukrainian partner.

He was accused of serving in Ukraine's armed forces and convicted in a trial in Moscow before being sentenced to six years and 10 months. Shyshko and Khyliuk were held alongside Hubbard as he awaited trial.

Ihor Shyshko alleges that Hubbard was subjected to sexual humiliation and abuse for the entertainment of Russian prison staff

Shyshko said he witnessed and heard Hubbard being beaten by prison guards and electrocuted with a stun gun.

"They beat him on the legs, on the back, on the arms," he said. "Of course, they didn't give him food, they didn't give him medical care. They kept him in the cold.

"He was always treated more harshly, because he was an American. They claimed he was a mercenary, and said 'let's beat him'."

Shyshko, a captured Ukrainian soldier, alleges Hubbard was subjected to sexual humiliation and abuse for the entertainment of the prison staff.

Accounts from the two former Ukrainian prisoners corroborate statements made by Hubbard's family that he was tortured in prison.

The 74-year-old was released by the Kremlin after four-and-a-half years, on the day US special envoy Steve Witkoff met Vladimir Putin in St Petersburg to discuss the war in Ukraine.

Witkoff praised the Russian President as a "humanitarian", despite the ongoing full-scale invasion and daily Russian attacks across Ukraine.

US President Donald Trump said he had asked Putin to free Hubbard during a phone call the week before.

Dmytro Khyliuk says Hubbard was hidden in a Russian prison to conceal his condition from a team of International Red Cross visitors

Khyliuk, a journalist, was held in the earliest days of the invasion, when Russian troops advanced towards the capital Kyiv.

He said he knew Hubbard's health was declining over the months they were initially held in nearby cells because he heard his complaints and the guards calling him more often to see the prison doctor.

Khyliuk described broken windows and biting cold, with detainees using time out of their cells to huddle together in the bathroom for warmth.

"It was very difficult conditions in Novozybkov, worse than in the next prison. It was very cold there in winter, terribly cold," he said.

Both men encountered the retired English teacher again at their next prison, Pakino, where Shyshko says Hubbard's condition worsened.

"There was not a single spot on his knees or on his feet without rot," he said.

"Everything was rotten. We all had scabies. It was very, very difficult for him. I even bandaged him once at the medical centre, because the doctor didn't want to do it. He just couldn't do it himself, and I had to help. He was quite weak then and he almost couldn't move."

They detailed a harsh regime and a meagre diet of porridge, bread and potatoes, with a daily routine of beatings and humiliation, being forced to run or hold stress positions.

"There's a handler with a dog, who lets go so it can bite you on the buttocks, on the elbow, or bite your leg. Then these wounds fester very badly," Shyshko said.

Khyliuk claimed that soon after their transfer to Pakino, Hubbard was hidden to conceal his condition from a team of International Red Cross visitors.

"I would like to say that an international humanitarian organisation that receives a lot of funding could do better. They saw only what was shown to them and they were happy with that," he said.

The International Committee of the Red Cross said in a statement that "in any situation where the ICRC has been granted access, we request to see every individual and assess the conditions they are held in. We continue our discussions with parties to the conflict on this issue."

Khyliuk said Hubbard disappeared from the prison in 2024 and he thought he must have been released. Khyliuk was returned to Ukraine in August 2025 in a prisoner exchange.

He likened their treatment to the regime of Soviet leader Joseph Stalin.

"Russian captivity is a torture machine. From the first second of your detention, they do everything to suppress you, to intimidate you. This is torture in all its manifestations, from psychological to physical. Beatings, hunger, cold, lack of any information, lack of the opportunity to communicate even with other prisoners".

Khyliuk said he was pleased to see Hubbard finally released and heading to the US.

"I understand what it is like to be in terrible captivity. Moreover, it was not his war. He ended up in the wrong place and at the wrong time," he said.

But he described the timing as "shameful PR of the US president, who is losing votes in elections to Congress".

Shyshko said he hoped one day their torturers would face justice.

"Evil must be punished, and so it will be. I have hope for that".

#24. Trump eases Canada tariffs ahead of US midterms

📅 26 Oct 2026 | other | Source

The move comes as Trump's Republican Party scrambles to maintain control of the US Congress, with a Republican candidate in a key Senate race saying she had lobbied to lift the tariffs on Canadian products.

US President Donald Trump has lifted some of the tariffs imposed on Canada amid an economic row with the US' neighbor to the north, which peaked this summer.

The White House on Friday said the easing of tariffs will apply to products including  "plywood sheets, some paper products, certain machinery and refrigeration equipment."

The changes will take effect on October 30.

The White House said the president was seeking to offset "the burden to US commerce while better serving the public interest.''

The move comes less than two weeks ahead of the November midterm elections, where Republican candidate Susan Collins is locked in a tight race with Democratic challenger Troy Jackson in Maine.

Collins said on Friday she'd addressed the tariffs with Trump's top trade negotiator, Jamieson Greer, and Commerce Secretary Howard Lutnick, adding that she raised with the due the issue of "the toll tariffs are taking on Maine families, businesses, farmers, and growers.''

Washington and Ottawa have been locked in a trade dispute since Trump returned to the White House last year.

Trade tensions intensified when talks broke down in August, with Trump announcing a 50% levy on some Canadian goods and Canadian Prime Minister Mark Carney announcing retaliatory tariffs.

On Friday, Trump told reporters that "Canada is working very hard to make a deal with us."

What old and vulnerable software might be running on your PC right now?﻿

What old and vulnerable software might be running on your PC right now?﻿

Remember Popcorn Time, the app that served as an easy-to-use front end for streaming torrents of movies and TV shows? Development of the MPAA’s least-favorite app of the 2010s has been abandoned for years, but the “Netflix of Piracy” software is still installed on millions of devices that are just waiting to join a bot army. Researchers at Eye Security found the updater for a Popcorn Time fork called “Time4Popcorn” still running on tons of Windows, macOS, and Android devices located around the world, phoning home to now-abandoned web domains over unencrypted HTTP and running an outdated, insecure version of Chrome.

Eye Security’s Niels Teusink says the updater could have been used to install and run basically any kind of software with full privileges, easily enabling someone to create a botnet just by taking over one of the domains, using a man-in-the-middle attack to target specific devices, or perform surveillance by identifying the machines running the software.

Their solution was to register one of the domains and use it to send their own update that instructed the updater to disable itself. Despite ethical concerns about the fix, the researchers say their only alternative “was to do nothing,” pointing out, “The update process wasn’t doing anything functional anyway, it was only creating risk for users.”

Eye Security said on Windows devices, their update “simply stops and disables the service via the Service Control Manager” without deleting any files or anything else. On macOS, it uses a shell script to execute a command that blocks the update process from starting. They didn’t address the software on Android devices, where installing the update would still require actual user interaction first.

They had to tweak their update after it was uploaded to VirusTotal, causing security programs to flag it as malware and block it while leaving the vulnerable updater running. It’s unclear exactly how many devices were at risk, but Eye Security says it “sent out over 2 million update downloads” by March, when they stopped sending the update and transferred the domains they used to the Shadowserver Foundation, a web security non-profit.
