#1560. Lucas Digne faces PSG test as Lamine Yamal threat looms

📅 26 Oct 2026 | other | Source

Paris Saint-Germain have a selection problem on the left of defence before their Champions League meeting with Barcelona: Nuno Mendes is injured, and Lucas Digne is the natural alternative against Lamine Yamal. Mundo Deportivo reported the situation on 19 October, describing Mendes as Luis Enrique’s preferred answer to the Barcelona winger.

The timing matters for the Catalans, with Yamal in excellent form. His impact in Barcelona’s comeback against Galatasaray underlined the threat PSG will need to manage on Tuesday.

Digne’s recent outing against Strasbourg did little to strengthen his case. Mundo Deportivo assessed the Frenchman’s display in PSG’s 3-1 defeat as laboured and lacking in decisiveness, and reported that Luis Enrique substituted him as he changed course after starting several reserves.

That leaves the coach weighing a natural replacement whose latest performance raised questions, rather than simply making a like-for-like switch without concern. Digne is also a former Barcelona player, having spent two seasons at the club from 2016 to 2018.

Mendes, by contrast, had been a regular in PSG’s European starting side. He featured in the 6-1 home win over Slovan Bratislava and the 4-2 victory away to Manchester City, bringing physical strength, defensive force and forward runs from the left.

Luis Enrique has largely relied in Europe on the core of the side that won the Champions League in 2024-25 and 2025-26, making the loss of a regular starter a notable disruption. PSG’s flexible 4-3-3 is built around possession, a high press and quick attacking movement, with the full-backs providing width.

The structure asks for both intensity and technical precision, while Luis Enrique is particularly wary of turnovers that allow opponents to counter. Achraf Hakimi supplies vertical runs on the other flank, Vitinha organises the midfield and Fabián Ruiz offers control; Khvicha Kvaratskhelia and Désiré Doué bring individual creativity in attack.

Barcelona’s right-sided threat makes the left-back decision especially prominent. Yamal’s role on the right flank of Barcelona’s attack puts him in the channel PSG must cover, although the matchup will depend on more than the defender selected.

PSG face Barcelona on Tuesday, 20 October, in the third matchday of the Champions League. Luis Enrique must decide how to cover the left side, with Digne the alternative identified as most natural in the available reporting.

Sport1 also reported on 18 October that Mendes was expected to be unavailable, citing French newspaper Le Parisien’s account of his examination, while noting PSG had confirmed only that he left the Strasbourg match injured. Neither report establishes a recovery timeline, so the immediate question is who starts against Barcelona.

#1558. Can a human survive in a whale’s belly? The science behind the thriller <i>Whalefall</i>

📅 26 Oct 2026 | other | Source

In the film Whalefall, Jay Gardiner (played by Austin Abrams) attempts to escape a sperm whale that has swallowed him. Credit: 20th Century Studios/Everett Collection

The film Whalefall, released on 16 October in the United States, takes viewers into the belly of the beast — quite literally. The survival thriller, based on Daniel Kraus’s 2023 novel, follows university student Jay Gardiner (played by Austin Abrams) as he is accidentally swallowed by a sperm whale when scuba diving. Viewers watch with apprehension as Jay treks through the whale’s multi-chambered stomach, looking desperately for a way out — all the while reckoning with the anniversary of his father’s death.

The making of both the novel and the subsequent film relied heavily on collaboration with scientific advisers. One of them — Joy Reidenberg, a comparative anatomist at the Icahn School of Medicine at Mount Sinai in New York City — says that she and the storytellers discussed everything, from which whale species would work best for the “modern-day Jonah” story to what damage Jay might incur inside various stomach chambers.

Here, Nature speaks to Reidenberg and Ilias Foskolos, who researches deep-diving toothed whales at Aarhus University in Denmark and Stanford University in California, to help separate science from fiction.

Foskolos: There are a few problems with this. Sperm whales are usually not in coastal areas. They’re oceanic predators. They won’t feed on something that is at shallow depths. Most evidence from sperm-whale stomach contents suggests that they consume quite small prey. They mostly consume squid of about 30 to 40 centimetres long — so nothing even close to a person’s size. Sometimes they go for bigger squid, but that’s not very common.

Reidenberg: Sperm whales don’t have much of a mouth to carry anything. But the throat passageway is actually quite large. And if it’s a very large sperm whale, which is the premise for this movie and for the book, then it’s physically possible.

Behaviourally, could this ever happen? You have to realize that the premise of the whole thing is that this sperm whale is pulling [Jay] in accidentally [with a squid]. If you were eating a gummy bear and someone put a pebble next to it, and you put the pebble in your mouth with the gummy bear, you’d probably spit everything back out again because that’s not really what you want to eat. So how likely is it for a sperm whale to ingest a human? About as likely as you are to ingest a rock.

During the film, Jay grabs a glowing jellyfish that was also swallowed by the whale to light his way as he looks for an exit.Credit: 20th Century Studios/Everett Collection

Reidenberg: I think so, if they had enough of the right protection.

The first chamber is supposed to be a mechanical mushing chamber. What that stomach is trying to do is break [Jay] into smaller, digestible units. If you’ve got something that’s punching the walls against the person, or constricting like a boa constrictor might around someone’s chest, would it stop that person’s heart? Usually, these stomach muscles are trying to mash up something that doesn’t have bones. Humans have bones. So now we’re dealing with a form of resistance that this whale’s not used to encountering. And then add the scuba tank, which is pretty incompressible. It’d be pretty unlikely that the stomach would constrict all of the chest, because you still have that area where the ribs meet the tank.

If the scuba diver has a dry suit on, then stomach acids [in the second chamber] are unlikely to get into that dry suit. Only the areas that are exposed would start to burn. And you don’t necessarily die from burns, but that would not be a pleasant experience.

Foskolos: What we call whale fall is just the remains of a dead whale along the sea bottom. It creates entire ecosystems because the remains can, at times, fuel different communities of animals — depending on the state of decomposition of the whale — which is amazing.

Reidenberg: There isn’t anything else really growing down there that can provide nutrients. There are lots of scavengers around waiting for something. And when a whale fall comes down, it’s like manna from heaven. Everything comes from miles around to feed on this carcass and it develops its own community.

Real Madrid forward Vinicius Jr. will miss El Clasico against FC Barcelona on Oct. 25 after being suspended for four games for the red card he received in Real Madrid's last home game, against Villarreal at the Bernabeu. The Brazilian forward came on in the 52nd minute and was sent off 20 minutes later. Vinicius Jr. thought he had scored, and Real Madrid coach Jose Mourinho celebrated alongside his team and ran over to him a few seconds before VAR ruled out the goal and sent him off. The VAR check showed Vinicius Jr. had pulled Nathan Saliba's hair before scoring. The Spanish Football Federation's rules state that pulling an opponent's hair is violent conduct, and Vinicius was suspended for the upcoming four Real Madrid matches against Sevilla, Barcelona, Racing Santander and Valencia. Mourinho, who skipped Saturday's post-match press conference after the episode, spoke ahead of the Champions League match against AS Roma on Wednesday and said, "After the previous match against Atletico Madrid in which Vinicius started, I decided to take a different approach and to protect him a little and then bring him on when the game called for a player like Vini. I think that worked out well: the stadium, which had sometimes voiced disapproval, immediately rallied behind him the moment he started warming up. I believe that was a crucial turning point for him. He came on and played very well, the goal and the sending off were a shame, of course, but it seemed like something clicked for him, reigniting that joy and reminding him of the kind of player he truly is." The ban applies only to domestic competitions, so Vinicius will be able to play against AS Roma on Wednesday, then against RB Leipzig and on Nov. 4 against AEK Athens. He will be eligible to play in LaLiga again only after the November international break, when Real Madrid meet Celta Vigo on Nov. 22.

#1551. Real Madrid’s 4-1 win yielded little from set pieces

📅 26 Oct 2026 | other | Source

Real Madrid beat Sevilla 4-1 at the Santiago Bernabéu on Sunday, October 18, but the set-piece analysis published by Managing Madrid found little threat from either side’s dead-ball situations. Kylian Mbappé’s penalty was the only set-piece-related goal; neither team scored from a delivery.

The figures underline how little the restarts contributed to the scoreline, rather than offering a complete account of Madrid’s attacking play. Sevilla recorded 1.17 non-penalty expected goals (npxG) overall, but only 0.07 from six attacking restarts.

Sevilla had three free kicks, two corners and a throw-in delivered into the box. Two led to shots, while Madrid’s seven restarts-six free kicks and one corner-produced only one attempt: Endrick’s effort after Arda Güler’s corner shortly after half-time, worth 0.03 npxG.

Five of Madrid’s seven restarts came after they had taken the lead, and Güler took three. The total set-piece output was a small part of the match’s chance tally: Madrid recorded 1.22 npxG overall, while Sevilla’s restarts accounted for just a fraction of their total.

Madrid dealt with three of Sevilla’s deliveries by winning first contact, while Thibaut Courtois claimed two more. Sevilla’s best opportunity came in the 73rd minute, when Manu Bueno met Gabriel Suazo’s free kick and sent his shot wide; the chance was valued at 0.06 npxG.

At the other end, Sevilla won first contact on two of Madrid’s seven deliveries. Madrid lost its only first-contact aerial duel, and goalkeeper Odysseas Vlachodimos dealt with one delivery. The analysis therefore depicts a quiet match for set pieces, not a full statistical breakdown of how Madrid created its four goals.

Madrid’s win came after Barcelona had beaten Real Betis, and the two sides are due to meet at Camp Nou next weekend. Infobae reported that Los Blancos would arrive six points behind the league leaders; AFP also placed Barcelona six points clear before the Clásico. Barcelona’s result has added immediate weight to the fixture, which will test Madrid against the side currently ahead of them in the table.

The 4-1 scoreline also followed a match in which Mbappé scored twice and Yan Diomande added Madrid’s fourth, according to AFP. The next league meeting with Barcelona is scheduled for Sunday, October 25.

In some ways, it is not a surprise that back-to-back UEFA Champions League winners Paris Saint-Germain would use Wednesday's game as another opportunity to assert their dominance with a 4-2 win. Their unique brand of dynamism is still a defining feature, one that is still hard to beat two years after manager Luis Enrique's tactical reinvention. Ousmane Dembele and Desire Doue are still impressively clinical, Vitinha still amongst the world's best in midfield. What was genuinely impressive, though, is that they were unflinching on a day that tested City's sporting construction.

The hosts ultimately outshot PSG 27 to 15, racking up 2.5 expected goals while the European champions only tallied up 1.83. City's end product matched their efforts, Erling Haaland once again assuming cheat code mode with a brace that kept his side in the game while becoming the club's top goalscorer in the competition. Their defensive resolve failed the tests PSG posed time and time again on Wednesday, though, defined by a mistake-ridden performance from goalkeeper Gianluigi Donnarumma.

The Italy international has always been a respectable option in goal but at 27 years old, he does not seem on pace to become one of his generation's top options. His inability to respond to Doue's opener in the 13th minute and Dembele's goal two minutes later was surprising, almost single-handedly digging his team into a hole they could barely dig themselves out of. It was a recurring theme over the course of 90 minutes, even as he saved Ferran Torres' tame penalty in the 90th minute.

Donnarumma was hardly the only person to blame for City's woes on Wednesday. Head coach Enzo Maresca opted to use Elliot Anderson as the lone player in midfield while just about everyone in front of him took a more advanced position, an imbalanced approach that was misguided from the start. It did not get the best out of anyone, PSG taking a 3-0 lead by halftime while City took just six shots and mustered 0.79 expected goals at that point.

Maresca's halftime decision to bring on Ayyoub Bouaddi for Jeremy Doku and offer Anderson some assistance in midfield was to City's benefit but at that point, it was far too late. There was not much left to salvage despite a valiant effort, headlined by Haaland.

Guardiola made a quick trip to Manchester to be a supportive figure but in the end, his shadow loomed large. The generous read of Maresca's tactical mistakes to start is that he borrowed Guardiola's famous overthinking habit for Wednesday's marquee matchup but in actuality, the new City boss does not have the reputation just yet to afford that kind of benefit of the doubt. Even then, Guardiola's ability to overthink was not the type of thing that would prevent someone from pointing out when he got something wrong and even if Maresca is the right successor, there is no reason to stop short of saying his choices were costly on Wednesday.

It was not the only way Guardiola's legacy hung over the Etihad, though. He receives a hero's welcome time and time again in that part of Manchester because he stewarded a superteam with his new position in the stands rather than on the pitch, the clearest sign as any that those days are a thing of the past. City's post-Guardiola rebuild means the team is still very much a work in progress, the team not favorites to win the biggest titles in front of them for the time being.

It makes City fans' defiance all the more intriguing. While opposing fans expertly spent the prolonged international break successfully making Oasis puns to sell the point, a sign reading "What's the story? Tainted glory" at Anfield on Sunday, all City supporters could come up with was worship for club chairman Khaldoon Al Mubarak. They have rallied around an executive and a series of businesspeople rather than the players on the pitch, all while erasing their actual case as victims – these are the very people who tarnished what should be the happiest sporting experiences, and ultimately what should be a storied chapter in the English game.

The funniest part, though, is that for all of City's advantages, Guardiola's tenure looks more and more like magic in a bottle. Watching Donnarumma's slow response to Doue, another beneficiary of a nation-state's backing, it was hard not to ask: This is all City could come up with after all their efforts to work around the Premier League's financial systems?
