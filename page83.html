#2020. Dejan Kulusevski thanks Tottenham fans as star completes 'impossible journey' after 526-day absence with serious knee injury... and admits he told loved ones 'it might not work out' just two months ago

📅 26 Oct 2026 | other | Source

Published: 12:00 BST, 20 October 2026 | Updated: 20:00 BST, 20 October 2026

Dejan Kulusevski completed his ‘impossible’ and ‘beautiful’ journey back to the Premier League and promised to repay Tottenham fans for their support in the difficult times.

Kulusevski returned as an 87th-minute substitute on Monday. It was his first appearance since a serious knee injury in May 2025 and coincided with an upturn in fortunes for Spurs.

Roberto De Zerbi’s strugglers beat Coventry 3-1 to secure their first league win of the season at the seventh attempt and climbed off the bottom of the table.

James Maddison’s first goal for almost 18 months after his own injury nightmare and Kulusevski’s long-awaited return served to fuel the celebrations.

'The journey felt impossible,' said Kulusevski, 26. 'It was the most difficult it could be, but I had a feeling I was going to come back to play football. I just had to believe and that is what I do.

'I had to tell my closest ones it might not work out, just a couple of months ago. But in my heart, I always knew it would work out. I kept working. For 10 hours every day when no one is watching.

Dejan Kulusevski has vowed to repay Tottenham fans support after making his first appearance in 526 days after a serious knee injury

Kulusevski admitted the injury, sustained in May 2025 against Crystal Palace, led to him telling his loved ones a couple of months ago that his return might not work out

'Imagine when everyone gives up on you. And football for me is like breathing. Imagine you can’t breathe for two years but still you have a family, so you show up, you do it with a smile, you go with your team-mates, you hype them up and that takes a lot of love, a lot of courage and a lot of belief. That is what I had to practise.

'So, when everything was impossible and everyone gave up, I never gave up.'

Kulusevski damaged a kneecap in a game against Crystal Palace. He had surgery but found he was still in pain and discomfort when he returned to training seven months later.

There followed a course of injections and further rest and when that did not solve the problem he underwent another operation in March.

It has been a long and difficult road with dire warnings about the delicate nature of the injury, and the popular Swede told how his Christian faith helped him through darker parts of his recovery.

'God, he gives me all the power,' said Kulusevski. 'I can’t take credit for what I’ve done because I haven’t done it alone. Every day he has been doing it for me.

'I will tell everyone about the journey. It has been an amazing journey even if it was hard sometimes. Hard is beautiful and I am so grateful that I had to experience it.

'Now I am ready to play football. I see myself a little different now. I think I am a different man now. Same guy, same guy that wants to play football but a little bit smarter.'

Tottenham fans gave Kulusevski a rapturous ovation when he came on in the closing stages of their 3-1 victory over Coventry

Spurs fans gave him a rapturous ovation as he peeled off his tracksuit to replace Mohamed Kudus.

'I am so grateful that they miss me play,' said Kulusevski. 'It means a lot and I am here to show them a better me. And a better me will be a better Spurs so we will be good.'

He played in an internal practice match at the Tottenham training centre at the start of October and travelled with the squad but didn’t make the bench for the first game after the international break at Manchester United.

By the time of the Coventry game, he was straining at the leash to get back on the pitch.

'I was more emotional in the morning,' said Kulusevski. 'I wasn’t nervous. I was ready to play those 10 minutes and make a good impact. It was amazing. It was fun. A beautiful day and I’m so happy we won.'

Ashley Young has claimed Antonio Conte would be a 'disaster' managing Manchester United - after the Italian revealed his desire to manage a fallen giant.

Conte, 57, is out of work after leaving Napoli at the end of last season following a two-year stint where he won Serie A and the Italian Super Cup.

Michael Carrick is still in post at Old Trafford and there are no immediate suggestions of that changing, but their struggles so far this season have raised alarm.

Young played under Conte at Inter Milan, where they won a Serie A title together, and says that Manchester United's players would be unhappy with his love for running and lengthy meetings - even if he is grateful personally to the Italian.

United's lack of running in matches this season has left them exposed and led to Gary Neville branding them an 'absolute disgrace'.

And Young told the Overlap Daily show: 'A lot of talk has been about Man United’s running stats. If they’re not going to run and Antonio Conte goes in there, it’s going to be a disaster, I think.

Antonio Conte would be a 'disaster' at Manchester United, Ashley Young has claimed

Young says Man United's players would not appreciate the level of running and long meetings

'He is throwing his name in the hat. Whether that is something that’s going to happen or not, I’m not sure whether the Man United players will be happy. I think you saw that when he went to Tottenham Hotspur.

'Speaking to a lot of Tottenham players – and I had spoken to Chelsea players before I went to Inter Milan and was like, "So what’s he like?" And they were like, "You’re gonna run a lot, you’ll be fitter than you’ve ever been."

'But do you know what? That was one of the things where I think that I was able to continue my career for so long because of the running, although it was crazy.

'The training sessions were a lot of 11 v 11, at times a little bit boring. Meetings were long. When I say long, I’m talking, if we lost the game, we could go in and do the meeting after the game and it’d stop literally everything.

'And you could be in there for three, four hours. I think that’s one of the reasons why he doesn’t last long at places, because not just the players – he tries to test mentally – but the board as well, and the owners, and I think that people get annoyed with him.'

Conte has a dazzling CV, having won league titles at Chelsea, Juventus, and Inter, but he is also known for his combustibility and not lasting long in jobs.

His longest gig was with Juventus between 2011 and 2014 but he is often too honest with people to retain relationships.

He was sacked by Tottenham in 2023 after a savage 10-minute rant where he branded them selfish, accused them of lacking 'fire in the eyes', and admitted the collective spirit had regressed.

'They don't play for something important. They don't want to play under pressure. They don't want to play under stress. It is easy in this way. Tottenham's story is this,' he said.

'Until now, I try to hide the situation but there are 10 games to go and some people think we can fight. Fight for what? With this spirit, this attitude, this commitment? What? For seventh, eighth, 10th place?'

Conte's name is being mentioned around United after he did an interview with The Athletic earlier this month and told them of his ambition to restore a fallen giant.

He has been linked with the managerial role at Manchester United in the past and told The Athletic: 'If you go to a club where the history speaks for itself - a history made up of trophies, league titles and victories - you can only honour that history in one way: by having the ambition to bring that club back, if it isn't there today, to fight for those goals.

'I know full well that only one team can win every year, but I must carry in my heart, and instil in the hearts of the fans, my players and my directors, that spark, that hope that makes you say, "f***, we can win". You only make history by winning.

'There are many clubs that have somewhat lost sight of their history. I'd like to go to one of these clubs to dust off that history and make it shine once more.

'Clubs where there's pressure, where, ultimately, you know full well that you're not going there just to take part, but to compete.'

United continue to endure a tough period, with the club having not won the Premier League title since Sir Alex Ferguson's last season in 2012-13.

In the subsequent 13 years, United's only trophies have been the FA Cup (in 2016 and 2024), the EFL Cup (in 2017 and 2023), and the Europa League (in 2017), and they have rarely threatened to challenge for the biggest titles.

There does not appear to be much chance of that this season, with the Red Devils sat 13th in the Premier League.

#2006. Sheffield Wednesday owners determined to realise club's huge potential - David Bruce

📅 26 Oct 2026 | other | Source

While many Wednesday fans celebrated the end of Dejphon Chansiri’s deeply unpopular reign as owner on October 24 last year, it was also a day of huge uncertainty over what would come next.

An American consortium, Arise Capital Partners, led by David and Michael Storch along with Tom Costin, ultimately took control of the club in May of this year, crucially managing to avoid a 15-point deduction to ensure they started life in League One with a clean slate.

One of their first moves was to bring in Bruce, formerly the chief business officer at Sunderland and a Major League Soccer marketing chief before that.

He grew up at a time when Wednesday were a Premier League force and a challenger for domestic cups, graced by the likes of Chris Waddle, David Hirst and John Sheridan.

He sees no reason why, with responsible leadership and patience, the club cannot reclaim that status.

“This club was always a top 10 club in this country, and it can be a top 10 club again. This club is like a rocket ship,” he said.

“One of the first things (new sporting director) Simon Wilson said in his interview for the job was ‘Sheffield Wednesday is probably the OG of sleeping giants’, and he’s right.

“This is about having a go, but doing it in the right way. I can’t think of another club like Sheffield Wednesday anywhere in Europe where there’s this opportunity to get your hands on it, try and shape it in a way which hopefully is going to be incredibly beneficial in the long term for so many people.

“As English people we have this tendency not to talk about the journey till it’s really almost done, and let’s wait until it’s formed or it’s almost out in the world. But I’ve worked so long in America, and the owners are Americans. They want to talk about journeys as soon as an idea is out of their mouths. And Americans are so good at that, and I don’t see anything wrong with that.

“We need to articulate to people, ‘it’s going to be bumpy, but it’s going to be fun as hell’.

“And it definitely isn’t going to be linear, because football is not linear. It’s full of ups and downs and all kinds of unknowns. But it’ll be amazing, and we’ll always intend to do the right thing by everyone.”

On-field success had been the obsession of the previous owner too, but the new owners found a club which had been neglected off it.

Bruce said: “You pick up a stone and you see something (under it) you need to resolve, but then you see something else, and you’re like, ‘I can’t just put the stone down now. I’ve seen it. I’ve got to do this right’.

“What happened in the past was you just never picked up the stone, or you saw (underneath) it and put it back down.”

Leaving no stone unturned has resulted, Bruce estimates, in £11m worth of work being done at the stadium and the training ground since May.

“It’s been things like fixing stands, fixing roofs, so fans can go to a game and not have water leak on them,” he said.

“This stadium has water and electricity coming into it, but it’s not distributed around the building in the right way that can enable us to do things like flush toilets in the second half of a game, have warm showers after a certain number of players have had a shower, get water just in the areas you need to get water into.

“So we put big new water tankers and piping around the whole stadium which enables us to have water in the building in the way we need, whether cold, whether hot. None of that stuff is making anyone any money. It’s just stuff that had to be fixed.”

Bruce is adamant the Storch family see Wednesday as a “long term” investment, adding: “The club has really got under their skin – really, really quickly.

“One of the things that really got them motivated about this football club early on was around the health of the region, the feeling that the health of the region was affected by the fact this club could disappear.

“That’s because of the belonging and the connection these fans have with this club that’s multi-generational, that’s been in their family forever. Their families’ identity is wrapped up in this football club. (The new owners) realised how important this club is as an absolute pillar of a community.”

#2003. From ticket fiasco to transfer chaos: INEOS' biggest Manchester United mistakes as crisis strikes again

📅 26 Oct 2026 | other | Source

Michael Carrick is under serious pressure as Manchester United manager after losing 4-1 to Como in the Champions League on Wednesday night.

United currently sit 20th in the Champions League table and 13th in the Premier League after a disastrous start to the season, and despite only being appointed full-time in the summer, Carrick is already facing calls for the sack.

If Carrick were relieved of his duties, he would become the third manager to depart since INEOS bought a minority stake in the club in February 2024.

INEOS and Sir Jim Ratcliffe’s agreement to buy a 27.5 per cent stake in United - since increased to 29.4 per cent - was supposed to usher in a new era after more than a decade of underperformance under the Glazer family, yet results have been inconsistent and controversy has followed off the pitch.

Carrick will likely be the fall guy for United’s poor start to the season, but the club’s issues extend far beyond the manager’s responsibilities, and supporters have become increasingly disillusioned.

From an all-time low Premier League finish to major redundancies, Standard Sport has assessed the biggest mistakes INEOS have made since arriving at Old Trafford.

Ratcliffe has overseen two major restructuring waves since taking over day-to-day control of United.

The club made losses in five consecutive years from 2019 to 2024 before INEOS arrived and cuts were seen as an inevitable, if unpopular, measure.

Redundancies were viewed by some as par for the course after years of financial mismanagement and poor results on the pitch.

But the scale of INEOS’ cuts has been vast with 322 jobs made redundant and a number of staff perks, including free travel to the 2024 FA Cup final and free lunches for non-playing employees at Old Trafford and the club’s Carrington training ground, removed.

Managerial flip-flopping and poor player recruitment were issues at United long before INEOS came to the club.
