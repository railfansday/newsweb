#2256. Where did it all go wrong for Colin Kazim-Richards at Crawley Town?

📅 26 Oct 2026 | other | Source

One win from ten league matches has seen Kazim-Richards dismissed from his role as Head Coach. He was axed after the Reds suffered their seventh defeat of the league campaign losing to Shrewsbury Town.

That brought an end to the 40-year-old’s short tenure at the Broadfield Stadium after a wretched start to the season - in fact the worst start since The Reds joined the EFL in 2011.

Director of football Erdem Konyar has also gone, along with Kazim’Richards’ No2 Julian Gray, while the Reds have appointed former Leeds and Sheffield United boss Kevin Blackwell as interim manager while they conclude their search for a permanent appointment.

So what went wrong for the former Derby County and Sheffield United man?

When Kazim-Richards was appointed as the Reds new permanent Head Coach, having saved the club from relegation and expulsion from the Football League at the end of last season, the decision split the fan base.

Those who were sceptical remained doubtful, and soon those who felt he had justifiably been given the role joined a rapidly growing contingent of the fanbase that were not convinced he was not the right man for the job after all, and pressure started to mount on him.

The defeat to Grimsby Town on September 19 seemed to be a real turning point, with supporters adamant that Kazim-Richards had to go, especially as at the time Crawley did not have a league game for another three weeks because of the international break.

Chants for Kazim-Richards to be sacked came at home to Chesterfield on October 10 when Crawley were 2-0 down. They did however fight back from two goals down to rescue a point – but this was a result which to many fans effectively felt like paper over the cracks.

The following Saturday the Red Devils were defeated by league leaders Salford City and the pressure was mounting.

It soon became a ‘when not if’ situation, so when the news arrived that he had gone, with the traditional corner flag picture part of the club’s announcement, fans were left rather unsurprised.

When the former Turkey international arrived at the club in late March, he subtly criticised what had gone on before him, explaining that one of the downfalls of Crawley’s season to date had been that players had been played in the wrong positions.

Fans were intrigued when they first saw Ade Adeyemo moved to a full-back role by Kazim-Richards in the latter stages of last season and this season brought some even more puzzling shifts in positions for personnel.

Charlie Barker was moved into a defensive midfield role from centre-back, Jonny Russell popped up at centre-back from his more usual left-back role and Dylan Morgan operated as a lone striker.

Some would argue that the boss was somewhat hypocritical in his initial comment about players being used in the wrong positions – especially when there were players on the bench whose natural positions were the ones that others were filling.

It is of course all down to personal opinion, but a lot of fans will say, particularly with regards to Adeyemo, who they had seen the most of playing at full-back as opposed to as a winger, that when he was moved to more of an attacking role in the later stages of games, he looked far more comfortable – and ultimately Crawley were able to get the most out of him.

What Kazim-Richards did particularly well when he first came in was stripping it back down to basics, but when things got tough this season it could be said he was guilty of over-complicating it.

There were a lot of concerns surrounding a lack of experience in the squad but also around the coaching staff, which lacked bodies, with just Julian Gray and player-coach Vito Mannone alongside the main man. Some extra managerial nous and a level head in the dugout to help guide Kazim-Richards would have been very beneficial.

In his final interview before his dismissal Kazim-Richards said “I think we need a little bit more experience, a little bit in terms of game management.”

It was something which he hadn’t referenced very much previously as he normally defended any questions surrounding a lack of experience within the side.

This is potentially quite revealing in terms of what Kazim-Richards felt he was lacking from the summer transfer window that he had desired and was not provided with, mentioning that he had given “nine league debuts” – rather accentuating the fact that those players needed more help around them on the pitch.

Kazim-Richards had an even more candid approach than usual to the post-match interview after the loss to Shrewsbury, one by which he all but knew his time was up.

It was very suggestive of the fact that he maybe didn’t have as much control over recruitment as he would have hoped – and so with the departing of Erdem Konyar, does this mean that he can also be seen as a culprit in the Reds’ woeful start?

Konyar returned to the club in November last year and oversaw what can be concluded as two disastrous transfer windows under two different managers with a total of nigh on 30 players – but one cannot deny he definitely had the best interests of the club at heart.

There are certain aspects on the pitch that are completely out of a manager’s control such as unforced defensive errors, which have certainly cost Crawley points this season – as well as not punishing teams when they have had the chance to, and also failing to produce any kind of consistency from a performance perspective and in both boxes.

It is always a culmination of different variants as to why it doesn’t work out for a manager at a club but ultimately it is a results business and so the responsibility lies on the shoulders of the boss.

On a personal note, Colin was a respectful human being and would always shake your hand before and after interviews. He was an honest person and in everyday life – and as a journalist – that is a quality that I find commendable, particularly when you are in a pressurised environment like he was.

The press conferences with him were always interesting and it was a great experience for me when he used to fire questions back at me.

As the first Crawley Town manager that I have ever had the pleasure of interviewing, it was very much a memorable experience and I wish him all the best in his future endeavours.

Now the search begins again for the club’s ninth manager in four seasons and it is so desperately important that they get this appointment right, making sure they bring in someone quickly but not so much that they rush the process and fail to carry out a thorough search for a new Head Coach.

#2255. Europa League preview: Bournemouth vs. AC Milan – Team news, opposition insight, stats and prediction

📅 26 Oct 2026 | other | Source

AC Milan resume their Europa League campaign on Thursday night with a trip to the south coast of England, to take on AFC Bournemouth.

The last couple of games have generated a more positive feeling around Ruben Amorim’s men, given they yielded six points and seven goals. It allowed the Rossoneri to kick start their European adventure properly, and to jump into the top four in Serie A.

Game date: Thursday, October 22 Kick-off time: 21:00 (CEST) Venue: Vitality Stadium Referee: Vassilis Fotias (VAR: Higlar)

As mentioned above, there seems to have been a swing in the pendulum from pessimism to optimism when it comes to Milan’s capabilities this season. The team are buoyed by the 4-0 win at RB Salzburg last midweek, and the 3-2 victory over Atalanta on Sunday.

Nonetheless, that game against La Dea showed both the ceiling and the floor of this team. Having raced out to a three-goal lead it looked like being a real statement of intent, until a late collapse saw the result thrust into doubt inside the final 10 minutes.

So, Amorim’s side are far from the finished article, though they have another opportunity here to prove that they can go toe-to-toe with one of the Premier League’s most exciting teams in recent years: AFC Bournemouth.

In terms of injuries, the absences of Alexis Saelemaekers and Matteo Gabbia were known about well in advance. The former has undergone surgery on an ankle injury sustained in training, while the latter suffered muscle and tendon damage, with both out until the new year.

However, two further blows came on the eve of the game. Firstly, Samuel Chukwueze missed the final session before the team fly over to England because he complained of hip discomfort after the Atalanta game. A specialist check-up is required for him, with a lengthy absence feared.

Then came the second bit of damage: Goncalo Ramos pulled out of the session early after feeling pain in his calf. The medical staff plan to check him over but it seems at the moment like a muscle overload, which will see him miss at least this game and possibly more.

The big question mark from a selection standpoint concerns whether Amorim plans to rotate heavily again, as has been the trend so far when having multiple games in a short stretch of time. He made seven alterations to the XI in Salzburg, then seven again versus Atalanta.

The match in Austria saw the likes of Fikayo Tomori, Ardon Jashari, Davide Bartesaghi, Omari Hutchinson, Alphadjo Cisse and Francesco Camarda all given a change in what was a youthful side. Sunday’s 65-minute performance demonstrated the energy levels needed to carry out the coach’s style effectively, so reports are suggesting more rotation is coming.

As happened last week, Amorim will make some changes. In defence, there will be room for Fikayo Tomori at right-back, while on the left Pervis Estupinan is expected to start. In midfield, a chance will be given to Ardon Jashari alongside Yunus Musah and Ruben Loftus-Cheek.

In attack, the duo of Omari Hutchinson and Diego Moreira will operate behind Alphadjo Cisse. Last week’s hat-trick hero – who turns 20 on Thursday – is expected to lead the line over Francesco Camarda. The bench, after the two latest injuries, looks a tad lighter than before.

Predicted Milan XI (4-3-3): Maignan; Tomori, De Winter, Pavlovic, Estupinan; Jashari, Musah, Loftus-Cheek; Hutchinson, Moreira; Cisse.

Predicted bench: Torriani, Bouyer, Vladimirov, Gila, Diawara, Bartesaghi, Modric, Rabiot, Comotto, Pulisic, Camarda.

Injuries & suspensions: Terracciano, Saelemaekers, Gabbia, Chukwueze, Ramos.

It was a summer of big change for AFC Bournemouth after a historic season, one which saw them qualify for a European competition for the first time ever. The Cherries have spent most of their existence in the lower leagues in England, so this past few years have been a real fairytale.

Without doubt the biggest pivot (and blow) over the summer was in the head coach position. Andoni Iraola chose to accept Liverpool’s advances – having also been linked with Milan – and so they turned to the seasoned German coach Marco Rose to lead them into 2026-27.

An expansive summer window followed his arrival. Antonio Silva and Alvaro Rodriguez arrived from Benfica and Elche CF respectively for €25m, while Juanlu Sanchez joined from Sevilla for €11m, and Michele Di Gregorio signed from Juventus on a loan deal.

It was expected that Bournemouth would make some big sales, with the likes of Alex Scott and Eli Junior Kroupi being linked with moves to top clubs for serious money. In the end though the most expensive exit was Hamed Traore to Marseille for €7.7m, though they did lose Marco Senesi on a free and Alex Jimenez headed to Fiorentina on loan.

It has been a bit of a rough start to the campaign domestically for AFCB under Rose. They lost three and drew three of their opening six Premier League games which had them in the relegation places. However, Sunday’s 2-0 victory over fellow strugglers and Milan’s future European opponents Sunderland helped ease some of the pressure.

The Cherries eased into the fourth round of the EFL Cup with a 4-0 win over Lincoln City, while they have started well in Europe with a 2-1 win away against Real Sociedad and a 3-0 home victory over Sturm Graz.

In terms of injury news, Rose will be without a couple of his most important players. Alex Scott suffered a hamstring injury on England duty and is expected to be out until next month, while Kroupi needed surgery on a foot injury and is also out for a few weeks yet. The other absentee is Araujo, with a hamstring problem.

Rose spoke before the game in his pre-match press conference about how his side will not ‘underestimate’ the Rossoneri, but they will go into it feeling they have a real chance of a historic result at home.

The current reports suggest the 4-2-3-1 will be spearheaded by Evanilson, with Tavernier, Kluivert and Gannon-Doak as the trio behind him. Hill and Silva will anchor the defence; Adams and Christie will be the midfield duo; while Smith and Truffert are the leading full-back candidates.

Predicted Bournemouth XI (4-2-3-1): Petrovic; Smith, Hill, Silva, Truffert; Adams, Christie; Tavernier, Kluivert, Gannon-Doak; Evanilson.

Injuries & suspensions: Kroupi, Scott, Araujo.

It perhaps goes without saying that this will be the first ever competitive meeting between the two sides that share red and black stripes as their colours. Milan have a long past against sides from England, but Bournemouth have never played an Italian team in a competitive game.

The Rossoneri have played 50 games in their history against English teams, and they have won just 14 of them. Narrowing this down to the Europa League, Milan have drawn one and lost one against Manchester United, and lost twice against Arsenal.

The most recent match against a Premier League side was the Liverpool Champions League game at San Siro under Paulo Fonseca early in 2024-25. An early Christian Pulisic goal inspired hope, but the Reds eventually prevailed 3-1.

➤  Bournemouth’s 2–1 win away to Real Sociedad was the first European match in the club’s 127-year history. They followed it with a 3–0 home victory over Sturm Graz, scoring five goals in their first two European outings.

➤ While this is the first competitive meeting, the two clubs played a friendly in 2016, which Milan won 2–1.

➤ Alphadjo Cisse scored a hat-trick in the 4–0 win away to Red Bull Salzburg, becoming the youngest Italian ever to do so in a European competition. He celebrates his 20th birthday on the day of the game.

➤ Milan lost 2–0 at home to Benfica before responding with their 4–0 win in Austria. Their goal difference therefore improved from −2 to +2 in one match.

➤ Marcus Tavernier has scored four Premier League goals before this fixture. The winger has also started both of their Europa League matches.

➤ Evanilson scored his 100th career goal in the 2–0 Premier League victory over Sunderland last weekend.

➤ The Cherries have scored twice against Real Sociedad and three times against Sturm Graz. That’s 2.5 goals per game in their opening two European fixtures. Milan, meanwhile, are averaging two goals per game, making this a battle of two top attacks.

➤ Bournemouth have six points from six available, compared with Milan’s three. A home victory would take the Cherries to nine points from three matches, and put them six clear of the Rossoneri.

➤ Ruben Amorim made seven changes to the line-up before the Salzburg win, then seven changes for the Atalanta victory. He could make seven changes again for this one, especially with injury issues.

For Milan, the opportunities lay at the feet of the youth once again. After impressing in Austria, Cisse and Hutchinson will once again be asked to show the same positive signs in Dorset, ideally giving Camarda a bit more service too.

However, in a game like this against an intense, fast and physical side, on a small pitch in a crowded stadium, the feeling is that the experience of the likes of Maignan, Tomori, Pavlovic and Rabiot will be key in managing the moments of the match and keeping composure.

In terms of attacking production, Evanilson has stepped up for Bournemouth in Kroupi’s absence, racking up seven goal contributions (3G, 4A) in his nine starts across all competitions. Marcus Tavernier is the top scorer on four goals, while Rayan and Ryan Christie each have three.

The summer signing Silva – who Milan were linked with – has become a fixture in defence alongside the talented James Hill. Truffert at left-back is a big attacking threat, while Musah and Pulisic’s compatriot Tyler Adams is the midfield engine.

An obvious assumption ahead of this one is that there will be goals, though picking a winner feels hard despite Bournemouth’s tough start. If Milan play like they did in Salzburg they should have enough, but we expect this to be a much tougher test.

Barcelona and Real Madrid will lock horns in La Liga on Sunday night.

Real Madrid, meanwhile, are currently third in the table, boasting a record of seven wins and two draws from their nine matches.

Barcelona's lead over Real Madrid is currently six points, demonstrating the importance of this match to Los Blancos, even at this early stage of the season.

Both teams will enter the match off the back of Champions League matches, with Barcelona suffering a 4-2 defeat to the holders Paris Saint-Germain, while Real Madrid were 4-1 winners over Bundesliga outfit RB Leipzig.

Two of the leading attackers in world football will go head-to-head in this match, with Barcelona's Lamine Yamal and Real Madrid's Kylian Mbappe locking horns.

Here, Sports Mole looks at the head-to-head record and past meetings between the two sides.

One of the biggest rivalries across the world in any sport, El Clasico is a simply giant football match, with the contest pitting the two biggest clubs in Spain against each other.

These two sides have locked horns on 264 occasions throughout history, and it is Real Madrid that just about lead the head-to-head record, recording 107 wins to Barcelona's 106, while there have also been 51 draws, with their first-ever league game dating back to February 1929.

Lionel Messi is comfortably the highest goalscorer in this fixture, with the Argentine netting an incredible 26 goals against Real Madrid during his glittering career at Barcelona, while a certain Cristiano Ronaldo managed 18 goals for Los Blancos against Barcelona.

Real Madrid had been on a four-game winning run against Barcelona ahead of El Clasico in October 2024, and they overcame the Catalan side three times during the 2023-24 campaign, posting 2-1 and 3-2 victories in La Liga, in addition to a 4-1 success in the final of the Spanish Super Cup.

However, Barcelona stunned Los Blancos at the Bernabeu by recording a 4-0 victory in their La Liga meeting in the first half of the 2024-25 campaign, with Robert Lewandowski scoring twice.

Barcelona then beat Real Madrid 5-2 in the final of the 2025 Spanish Super Cup to lift their first trophy under the management of Hansi Flick, before recording a 3-2 victory in the Copa del Rey final between the two teams in April 2025.

The pair next locked horns at Estadi Olimpic Lluis Companys in La Liga in May 2025, with Barcelona recording a 4-3 victory, but Real snapped their losing Clasico run with a tetchy 2-1 triumph in October 2025.

The first contest in 2026 between these great rivals went the way of Barcelona, who became the first team to retain the Supercopa de Espana in 15 years with a 3-2 victory in the final courtesy of a brace from Raphinha either side of a Lewandowski chip.

The triumph maintained Flick's 100% record in finals - the German boss has won all eight during his managerial career - and also meant that La Blaugrana had won five of the last six Clasicos.

Barcelona went on to make it six wins in seven El Clasicos with a comfortable 2-0 victory against Real Madrid on May 10, 2026, which saw the Blaugrana seal the La Liga title.

Despite the defeat, Real Madrid just about lead the head-to-head record in La Liga, posting 80 wins to Barcelona's 77, while there have also been 35 draws, which is low considering how well-matched the pair have been throughout history.

Real Madrid are 36-time Spanish champions, while Barcelona have lifted the famous domestic trophy on 29 occasions, including last season.
