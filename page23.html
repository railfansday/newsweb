Boerne Champion took its first loss of the season to League City Clear Creek in August, but the Chargers weren't going to let the Wildcats get past them a second time.

Boerne Champion downed Clear Creek 17-12 in Friday afternoon's UIL water polo state semifinals at Josh Davis Natatorium. The Chargers face Austin Westlake for the boys state title at noon Saturday.

"We lost by one goal in August, but it really helped shape what our offense is going to be, how we're going to move defensively against them, and help me as a goalie learn how they shoot," Boerne Champion senior goalkeeper Jackson Hankins said about facing Clear Creek on Aug. 15.

Boerne Champion held a 4-3 lead at the end of the first quarter, but the Chargers took control of the match by halftime. Eight Boerne Champion goals in the second quarter proved to be too much for Clear Creek to overcome.

"I believe we played as a team," Boerne Champion senior utility player Julius Hoxhaj said. "We played team defense. We played team offense way better than we did every year, and I think that really benefited us in today's match."

Hoxhaj, the reigning Express-News Boys Water Polo Player of the Year, led the match in scoring with six goals. Boerne Champion senior attacker Ethan Altermatt followed with five goals. Hankins recorded 13 saves in the afternoon.

While Boerne Champion has made five consecutive state tournament appearances, the Chargers have yet to bring home a state title, something they hope will change Saturday. This year's seniors last played in a state final match as freshmen.

"Hopefully we can keep up tomorrow against Westlake or Hebron," Hoxhaj said. "We'll see what happens."

Boerne Champion's Nicolas Mendoza throws the ball during the Water Polo State Tournament semifinals at the Josh Davis Natatorium in San Antonio on Oct. 23, 2026. (Blaine Young/For the Express-News)

Although Clark girls water polo saw its season end with a loss, the Cougars exceeded head coach Jeffrey Segrest's expectations.

"I can't be happier with the way the girls handled that game, talked to each other, supported each other throughout, kept building each other up and pushing each other for more," Segrest said. "It's a great result after a long season."

Clark fell to Lewisville Hebron 25-15 Friday morning at Josh Davis Natatorium in UIL state semifinals. Clark ends its season 26-4 overall, while Hebron takes on Clute Brazoswood in Saturday morning's state final.

Clark's Haley Harris sprints to the ball during the Water Polo State Tournament semifinals at the Josh Davis Natatorium in San Antonio on Oct. 23, 2026. (Blaine Young/For the Express-News)

Segrest said his young team was tested early due to multiple exclusions in the first quarter. The coach added that Clark has multiple athletes who just started playing water polo this season.

"They did it well," Segrest said of his team's efforts.

Junior Ali Maldonado led Clark with seven goals. Freshman Maddie Halliburton recorded five goals for the Cougars. Hebron's Ryann Renfroe led all scorers with 11 goals.

Speaking after the match, Kompany acknowledged that Karl is yet to return to the level he reached last season but stressed that the youngster continues to enjoy the full backing of the coaching staff.

“The rhythm and performances will come. He’s not performing at the level he had last season, but that’s also okay,” Kompany explained.

“He knows that it happens, he knows we’re patient with him. At some point his moment will come and he will show us the Lennart Karl that we all know.”

Injury setback has disrupted Karl’s progress

Karl enjoyed a remarkable rise last season, establishing himself as one of Bayern’s most exciting young talents thanks to his technical ability, direct attacking approach and eye for goal.

However, a serious muscle injury sustained in June disrupted his progress and ruled him out of the 2026 World Cup. The forward subsequently spent around 12 weeks on the sidelines before returning to action towards the end of August.

Since his comeback, Karl has been working to regain his match sharpness and the form that made him such an important option for Kompany during his breakthrough campaign.

The Bayern coach acknowledged that the youngster’s injury problems have played a role in his current situation, urging patience rather than expressing concern over his development.

“He’s still young, he had a few injuries. It will come, I’m not worried,” Kompany added.

Karl’s return to full fitness has coincided with considerable competition for attacking positions at the Allianz Arena.

Mikel Arteta's men stayed within touching distance of Manchester City courtesy of a 1-0 success at Nottingham Forest last weekend, while the Toffees were subjected to their first loss of the top-flight campaign, a 2-0 reverse vs. Chelsea.

The all-too common City Ground stalemate was on the cards for Arsenal last weekend, when the English champions looked set to settle for a 0-0 draw away to Nottingham Forest for the third year running, until Arteta's game-changing changes came to the fore again.

After Eberechi Eze's first half left a lot to be desired, Noni Madueke made the most of his second-half cameo with a critical 80th-minute strike - albeit a deflected one - to ensure Arsenal would remain just three points below unblemished Manchester City in the Premier League rankings.

The Sky Blues can go six points clear again - for at least a couple of hours - by getting the better of Aston Villa in the lunchtime kickoff, but the Emirates evidence suggests that Arteta's side will quickly cut the gap back down again.

Indeed, the Gunners have won each of their three Premier League home games this season and have prevailed in each of their last six top-flight matches at the Emirates all told, shipping just two goals in the process.

Amid ongoing and increasing criticism of their perceived 'boring' playstyle, Arsenal scored two or more goals for the fifth time in seven games on Wednesday evening, salvaging a creditable 2-2 Champions League draw with Bayern Munich thanks in no small part to Eze's rapid redemption arc.

Scrapping hard to end their nine-year spell without any form of European football, Everton share Arsenal's standout record of just one Premier League loss all season, although that solitary defeat arrived in dampening circumstances last weekend.

Either side of a distressing head injury to Emiliano Martinez that overshadowed the result, Pedro Neto breached the Everton backline twice to end the Toffees' unbeaten start to the campaign and raise doubts about their continental credentials.

However, Everton were undone by an Arsenal late show in March's 2-0 Emirates loss - in which Max Dowman became the youngest Premier League goalscorer in history - and the Toffees are winless in seven against the Gunners since James Tarkowski propelled them to a 1-0 Goodison Park victory in February 2023.

Arsenal's point at the Allianz Arena came at a significant cost, as Bukayo Saka hurt his quad while passing the ball in the 10th minute and had to be substituted for Eze, who made up for his City Ground disappointment with the opening goal in midweek.

Saka is now feared to be facing six to eight weeks on the sidelines, so Eze will undoubtedly return to the first XI on Saturday, as Madueke switches over to the right flank again.

The Gunners boss also ought to consider some non-enforced changes, bringing in Myles Lewis-Skelly and Viktor Gyokeres for Bruno Guimaraes and Kai Havertz after the latter two struggled to perform in Munich.

Piero Hincapie will recover from an ankle problem in time to make the cut, but William Saliba (back) and Christos Tzolis (hamstring) remain sidelined for the hosts.

Meanwhile, Everton are blessed with a fully-fit squad - a major fillip for Moyes given his already-limited options - after ex-Arsenal midfielder Christian Norgaard recovered from a groin injury to make his debut against Chelsea.

However, another erstwhile Gunner in Ainsley Maitland-Niles endured a torrid time against Chelsea - finding himself at fault for Neto's opener - so Jake O'Brien may be restored to right-back in North London.

Concern was sparked among Toffees fans earlier this week when Jarrad Branthwaite was missing from training footage, but Moyes kept his cards close to his chest in his pre-game press conference.
